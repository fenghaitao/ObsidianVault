## Definition

A GTM Engineer (GTMEN) is an emerging role that applies technical and AI capabilities to go-to-market workflows. They systematically break down GTM functions (inbound, outbound, install-based sales, etc.) and encode them into AI agents, enabling dramatic efficiency gains. The role has risen in the last 18-24 months.

## Key Information

- At Vercel, the first three GTM engineers were former sales engineers (front-end developers with CS degrees who moved into sales).
- The ideal profile has GTM experience — someone who understands the "art and science" of sales, not just pure engineering. This ensures agents incorporate best practices, not just the habits of junior reps.
- A single GTM engineer built Vercel's lead qualification agent in ~6 weeks (spending 25-30% of their time), replacing 10 inbound SDRs with one human QA reviewer.
- The lead agent costs ~$1,000/year to run vs. well over $1M in SDR salaries — a 90%+ cost reduction.
- The agent maintained flat lead-to-opportunity conversion rates while reducing touches needed to convert (faster response times, including nights).
- Workflow: shadow the highest-performing person in a function → document their workflow → encode it into an agent → keep human-in-the-loop for QA → iterate.
- Beyond prospecting, GTM engineers also work on install-based sales (next-best-action from internal data), outbound (lower end of market first), and deal analysis bots.
- The role forces companies to be more rigorous about their sales process earlier — you can't automate what isn't documented.
- A founder could combine AE + GTM engineer into one hybrid role early on, especially with a technically-minded general athlete.
- Jason Lemkin's alternative model: promote someone internally — "a nerd, someone that likes marketing and sales and is quant" — to be the chief AI/agent orchestration officer. Odds they come from regular sales approach zero.
- The ideal profile: someone who raises their hand and says "I've already done this" — has already built apps in Replit, tried AI agents on their own, and wants to manage them.
- This person must spend 1-2 hours daily managing agents, reviewing outputs, and iterating — "they operate autonomously but not without constant oversight."
- At SaaStr, Amelia (chief AI officer) fills this role: she knows the product cold, understands all the agent quirks, and spends 20% of her time on orchestration.
- Lemkin's view: "everyone's posting on LinkedIn that they want to hire GTM engineers — I don't think that role exists today" (as a hireable role; you have to grow it internally).
- "We need veterans. We don't have veterans yet" — the field is too new for experienced external hires.
