## Definition

Truth seeking is the practice (and, in Brian Attwell's redesigned ladder, a core competency) of actively hunting for what is actually true — questioning foundational decisions and industry norms — rather than optimizing for being loud, well-positioned, or convincing.

## Key Information

- Attwell's ladder emphasizes "how you perform" (truth seeking) instead of "what you perform" (scope/influence)
- Contrast with Meta's leveling guide ("influence other teams at E6 with good ideas"): Attwell's ladder asks that "you have to be right most of the time when questioning a foundational engineering and product decision or industry norm"
- He assumes "everybody including myself is wrong just all the time," and gets excited when someone questions a "religious" long-held practice
- Example: an integrations team maintaining 1,200 integrations stopped supporting low-value ones after quantifying each integration's value — fewer pages, more time, better aggregate product quality
- His biggest regret is not truth seeking: going along with a decision he knew was wrong mid-project at ~2 years into his career, while owing the team honesty
