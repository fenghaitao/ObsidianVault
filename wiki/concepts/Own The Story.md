## Definition

Advice given to [[Jeetu Patel]] by [[Cisco]] board member [[West Bush]]: never treat your company's narrative as a marketing exercise to delegate, but personally, repeatedly tell it yourself — since each layer of an organization that retells a message adds unintentional distortion (a "telephone game").

## Key Information

- Bush's framing: the story is the "most intrinsic foundational exercise of the company," and the leader should always be its custodian, even if that means personally repeating the same message on stage for 90 minutes across many events.
- Patel's networking analogy for this: "packet loss" — just as data packets can be lost/corrupted crossing a network, messages lose fidelity crossing organizational layers; a leader telling the story directly is like "a direct Ethernet Cat 5 connection" with no packet loss.
- Unexpected benefit Patel discovered: forcing himself to personally own and simplify the story massively simplified Cisco's sprawling, hard-to-summarize business, because if he couldn't explain the story clearly himself, there was no chance thousands of salespeople or customers could understand or repeat it.
- Reframes storytelling: not something that happens after a product is built (a marketing afterthought), but the reason the product is built in the first place — the story comes first, and the product supplies its evidence/proof.
- Practical execution note: give the team the maximum context legally/practically possible (accounting for constraints like public-company quiet periods) and treat people like adults rather than being sanitized/sterile with facts.
