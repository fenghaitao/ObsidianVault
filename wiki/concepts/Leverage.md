## Definition

Leverage is the principle of achieving outsized impact by building tools, platforms, or data sets that other engineers or teams depend on, rather than by individual output alone. It is a core mechanism for staff-level engineering impact.

## Key Information

- The panel discussed how building at the "bottom layer" makes everyone better — this is leverage
- Rahul's framework: when evaluating projects for promotion, consider three characteristics: size of workstream (number of engineers, duration), complexity (can someone a level below do it?), and impact (does it span multiple teams?)
- Infrastructure teams have more leverage opportunities because "if you're building that bottom layer, you're making everyone better"
- The math of leverage: "1% for a thousand people, 10% for 100" — that is where you make outsized impact
- At L9+ (three levels above Staff), engineers make compounding improvements that make the entire company faster
- Zach's Groovy script at Netflix: saved 1-7 minutes per deploy, 30-60 minutes per engineer per day, across potentially hundreds of engineers
- Rahul's debug-ability tool: started as a single tool, became a platform where other engineers could add their own tooling, multiplying impact
- Data engineering leverage: creating Master Data sets that 30+ other pipelines depend on
- Both Ryan and Evan experienced leverage through working with interns: Ryan had an intern who effectively doubled his output, showing him the power of working through others
- Evan's manager had to repeatedly tell him to delegate — "you don't have to do everything yourself, in fact at the higher levels you can't do everything yourself"
- The key feedback Evan received when going from senior to staff was "scaling yourself" — you need to increase your scope through others
- Working through others allows you to take on more scope than you can physically deliver yourself, which is the name of the game at staff level and beyond
