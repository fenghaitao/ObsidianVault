## Definition

[[Jason Cohen]]'s term (named for the shape it traces — a rising "trunk" followed by a sagging "butt") for how marketing/acquisition channels actually behave over time: not a simple S-curve that plateaus, but one that eventually declines as the channel saturates and audiences become fatigued or move on.

## Key Information

- Contrasted with the commonly assumed "S-curve" (grow, then plateau at a stable ceiling) — Cohen argues channels instead sag downward after the plateau, citing magazine ad circulation (self-reported growth right up until the magazine folded) and conference attendance (record turnout right before the event shut down) as examples of channels that looked healthy until they weren't.
- Every acquisition channel has a hard practical ceiling ("inventory": finite searches, finite ad slots, finite audience attention) even before accounting for decline — Cohen's diagnostic question: "do you know right now which channels are saturated and which aren't?" Most companies don't, and default to "add a feature, hope marketing pushes harder," which doesn't work once a channel is genuinely saturated.
- Prescribes seeking genuinely *new* channels (not incremental tweaks to existing ones) once saturation sets in — citing [[Constant Contact]]'s in-person small-business workshops and [[HubSpot]]'s pivot to an agency-partner channel (grew to ~50% of revenue over 4-5 years) as real examples, plus [[WP Engine]]'s own reliance on agency-sold hosting.
- Frames new-product/new-market expansion as a similarly valid "new channel," recommending a "plant one foot in an existing strength, risk the other foot" approach rather than expanding on pure speculation.
- Notes AI is actively reshaping which channels are viable (e.g., ChatGPT's app store as an emerging distribution channel) but that the underlying dynamic — most channels eventually saturate and decline — isn't new and shouldn't be assumed away.
