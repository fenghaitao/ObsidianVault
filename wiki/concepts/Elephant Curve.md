## Definition

The Elephant Curve is Jason Cohen's model for how marketing channels perform over time. Unlike the traditional S-curve (which plateaus), the Elephant Curve has an initial trunk-like rise, then a sagging decline — channels don't just saturate, they deteriorate.

## Why Channels Sag (Don't Just Plateau)

1. **Audience saturation** — People have seen the message too many times; they either bought or they won't
2. **Channel decline** — The channel itself loses effectiveness (magazine circulation drops, conference attendance falls, algorithm changes reduce reach)
3. **Competitive crowding** — More competitors enter the channel, driving up costs and reducing returns
4. **Channel dishonesty** — Channels often hide their decline (magazines inflate circulation numbers, conferences claim attendance is great until they go out of business)

## Implications

- You can't just "flog the marketing department" — adding one feature and hoping AdWords will work is futile when channels are saturated
- You need to know which channels are saturated and which aren't (most companies don't know)
- Creative channel expansion is necessary: Constant Contact's in-person workshops, HubSpot's agency channel, WP Engine's agency partnerships
- Direct and indirect channels (SEO, social) often don't cross-pollinate well — a product that sells well direct may not do well in SEO, and vice versa
