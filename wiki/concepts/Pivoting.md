## Definition

Pivoting is the decision to fundamentally change a company's direction. Stewart Butterfield, who pivoted both Flickr and Slack from video games, advises that pivoting requires coldly rational decision-making after exhausting all realistic possibilities, despite the emotional humiliation involved.

## Key Information

- **When to pivot**: after you've exhausted all the realistic possibilities (not necessarily all possibilities)
- **Default advice is wrong**: the default advice is always "persevere" — hang in there, kitten on the branch. But there are countless survivorship bias stories
- **Coldly rational**: you must create distance from the emotional investment to make an intellectual, rational decision. The decision should be based on expected value diminishing to the point where alternatives look more attractive
- **Why it's hard**: pivoting is humiliating — you convinced investors, employees, press, and users to believe in something, and now you're admitting you were wrong
- **Real costs**: shutting down Glitch meant people lost their community, identities, jobs; people who moved cities for the job were affected
- **Not a pivot if**: you're 3 people, 6 months in, and just messing around trying to figure out what to build — that's not a real pivot
- **Slack's advantage**: the team worked on Slack for years without explicitly working on it, only doing minimum features that were absolutely guaranteed to be successful, because they were using it themselves
- **Andy Grove's framework**: used Glitch → Slack as an example of a "smart fold" — when expected value of the current path drops below the alternative
- **Matt McGinness's counterpoint**: pivoting success stories are extremely rare. Companies that hit it big hit big pretty quick. If you're at year 4-5 without "screaming rip-roaring growth," it's extraordinarily difficult to pivot to success. The "never quit" Silicon Valley mantra is VC propaganda — VCs are incentivized to keep founders grinding because they can't get their money back. McGinness advises founders to quit, reset the cap table, and start fresh rather than persisting through multiple pivots.
