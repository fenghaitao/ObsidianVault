## Definition

Engineering Culture refers to the shared values, practices, and norms that define how engineering teams operate. Philip Su's experience building Meta's London office from scratch demonstrates how to intentionally establish and preserve engineering culture at a remote site.

## Key Information

### Building Culture at a Remote Site
- Philip brought a landing team of 12 people who were specifically selected as culture carriers — he interviewed their peers and managers to assess this
- The two-year commitment from the landing team was critical for maintaining culture during early growth when the percentage of new people was high
- Google's experiment with landing teams confirmed a minimum two-year commitment was necessary
- Philip had observed mistakes from Meta Seattle's growth (from 4 desks to 120 desks) and learned from them

### Strategic Thinking for Culture
- As the site grew, Philip looked at the "pyramid of seniority" — whether the right mix of seniority existed and whether people were growing at commensurate velocity
- Strategic decisions included things like intern hiring volume based on H-1B projections 18 months out
- These decisions were about "strategic things rather than tangible concrete things to deliver"

### Weak Teams
- At a company the size of Microsoft (140,000-240,000 people), there are "always weak teams"
- On a weak team, you may think you're awesome because you work with bad peers, leading to over-leveling
- You never want to be the "most expensive house on the block" — you have no room to grow
