## Definition

Net Dollar Retention (NDR) measures how much revenue existing customers generate over time, accounting for expansion, contraction, and churn. An NDR above 100% means existing customers are generating more revenue than they did previously, which is a "superpower" for getting higher valuation multiples.

## Key Information

- Elena Verna calls NDR a "superpower" metric for getting bigger valuation multiples from investors
- Lovable's NDR is "quite good" because when people build, they want to buy more credits
- NDR is different from subscriber retention and engagement retention
- Engagement retention is the leading indicator for paid retention
- Lovable prioritizes engagement retention over paid retention because the north star is maximum usage, not revenue optimization
- Lovable's paid retention is on par with benchmarks of other B2B SaaS products Elena has worked at (Miro, Dropbox, SurveyMonkey, Netlify, Amplitude)
- **Jason Cohen's perspective:** NRR is essential because it's the only growth lever that scales automatically with company size. Unlike marketing (which grows linearly), existing customer expansion grows proportionally with the customer base.
- **Median IPO NRR is 119%** for public SaaS companies. Only ~2 of 100+ public SaaS companies have NRR below 100%. Those that do have "horrible financials."
- **The math:** a 20% loss from cancellations requires a 25% gain from upgrades just to break even (not 20%). This is because percentage gains are on a smaller base after losses.
- **NRR alone is insufficient.** Logo churn (N) can still be too high even if NRR is positive — if too few customers remain, there aren't enough left to upgrade. Both metrics are needed.
- **The right mindset:** "How do we create more value for the customer and then split it with them?" — not just "add a feature and make them pay."
