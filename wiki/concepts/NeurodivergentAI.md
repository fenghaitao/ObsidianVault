## Definition
Neurodivergent AI is an analogy comparing AI models to neurodivergent individuals: both can be extremely capable in specific domains while struggling with a world designed for neurotypical humans. The analogy explains why AI excels at benchmarks but fails at real-world tasks.

## Key Information
- AI models are "effectively neurodivergent individuals" — the world was not built for them
- Everything in our world is shaped and sized for humans: military pack weights, tax complexity, software interfaces
- Like neurodivergent individuals, AI models have rough edges where their capabilities don't align with the world's expectations
- They need human assistance to accomplish anything real in our world — "it's just too hard for them currently"
- The analogy connects to benchmark performance: "you ever met a neurodivergent individual who wasn't terribly good at something? Completely useless at getting through life? They're all very good at reading books"
- This explains the gap between AI beating GPQA experts on hard science questions while failing at practical tasks
- Two paths to resolution: models must get "really, really good" or "our world will have to change"
- Joel Becker endorsed this analogy as capturing something important about AI's current limitations
