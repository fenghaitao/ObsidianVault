## Definition

Owner's delusion is the cognitive bias where product builders overestimate how much attention, intent, and patience users bring to their product. They design as if users are "subjects who paid money to go to your play" rather than regular people who will bounce in a fraction of a second.

## Key Information

- The name was coined by an anonymous Twitter user in reply to a Butterfield tweet about restaurant websites
- **Classic example**: restaurant websites that show slow-loading photos with Ken Burns effects, auto-play music, and make the phone number/address/hours/menu/reservation info hard to find — despite the owner having surely experienced the same frustration on other restaurant websites
- **Why it happens**: the builder thinks "my thing is so important" and doesn't recognize that users are distracted, rushed, need to go to the bathroom, worried about their kids, etc.
- Users arrive with the absolute minimum threshold of intent — just barely enough to take action
- If the product doesn't immediately make sense, they bounce
- **Solution**: consciously recognize the delusion, get feedback from others, and train yourself to "take a breath, pretend you're a regular person, and look at this again"
- Related to Apple's "Sleep" feature naming — incomprehensible to users but made perfect sense to the people who built it
