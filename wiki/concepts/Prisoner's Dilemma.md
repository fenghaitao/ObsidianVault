## Definition

The prisoner's dilemma is a game theory concept used by Stewart Butterfield to explain why generosity in business is strategically advantageous. Acts of generosity signal cooperation in the iterated game, leading to mutual benefit.

## Key Information

- **Butterfield's framing**: acts of generosity demonstrate "I am going to cooperate as we iterate in this game"
- If you cooperate, others will also cooperate, and both benefit
- If you never reveal whether you'll defect at the first opportunity, the other party's best bet is to defect
- This applies to real business situations that are "much much much more complicated than the prisoner's dilemma"
- **Application**: generous policies (fair billing, generous SLA, employee-friendly IPO structure) signal that Slack is a cooperative partner, leading to better long-term outcomes
