## Definition

A counterintuitive signal of product-market fit: when your biggest customer complaints are not "I don't think this is useful" but rather "it's broken" or "it doesn't work as well as I want." This indicates that users want the product to work — they see the value — but the execution hasn't caught up to the demand.

## Key Information

- Claire Vo's description of OpenClaw: "It has so many sharp edges. It's a pain to set up. You got to feed and maintain your claws. It is not hands-off. But, the value is so high. I am willing to go through the pain."
- "Any good product manager has felt this experience of launching a product and it's just hot garbage. It's buggy, it doesn't look great, and somehow your biggest complaints from customers are not 'I don't think this is useful.' It's that it's broken or it doesn't work as good as I want."
- "That's when you know you have product market fit."
- "I like the complaints of like, 'it's buggy, it doesn't remember, I want it to do X but it can't.' That's not not product market fit. That's a product that hasn't caught up to its product market fit."
- This was Claire's initial experience with OpenClaw: her calendar got deleted, but she also felt "that really ugly and apparent feeling of product market fit" — enough joy and utility to know something was there.

## Why It Matters

- Reframes "buggy product" complaints from negative to positive signals
- Helps product teams distinguish between "no demand" and "unmet demand"
- Validates the "ship early, fix later" approach when the core value proposition is strong
