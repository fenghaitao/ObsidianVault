## Definition

Molly Graham's Six Rules for Goal Setting is a framework for creating clear goals and alignment on teams. It is built on the premise that goals are fundamentally a communication tool designed to create clarity and help people know what to work on each day.

## The Six Rules

1. **No company needs more than three company goals.** Facebook had three goals for five years: growth (monthly active users), engagement, and revenue. If you can govern Facebook with three goals, you can govern any business with three goals.

2. **One goal needs to win in a fight.** When prioritizing time on a given day, everyone must know what the most important thing is. At Facebook, engagement won — acquiring users who would use the site all the time drove both revenue and the heart of the product.

3. **Explain it to me like I'm five.** "An intern that started on Monday should be able to look at your goals and understand them." If they can't, the goals are failing as a communication tool. Explain acronyms, use numbers that make sense to average people.

4. **Strategy should hurt.** Inspired by Claire Hughes Johnson's phrase "strategy should hurt." If your goal-setting process is not painful, you're not prioritizing heavily enough. Make it clear what you're not going to do. If you can't cut things off the list, people will prioritize for you — and you may not like their choices.

5. **One goal has one owner.** A number has a name next to it. "Two people owning a goal is no one owning a goal." It's not the CEO — it's someone who works for the CEO. If you can't do this work, you haven't done the most important organizational work.

6. **Goals alone are not enough.** James Clear: "Winners and losers have the same goal." You must have a process to follow up, hold people accountable, and learn from goals. Goal setting is about learning: "You set a goal. Can we do it? How hard is it to move this number?" You might be wrong all the time, but you're learning what it takes.

## Key Context

- Graham has "a bone to pick with OKRs" — when she asks leaders about their goals, she often gets back a 100-line spreadsheet that "feels like it's written in Greek" and creates no clarity
- Goals are a communication tool, not a management bureaucracy
- What's right for a seed-stage company is not what's right for an established business — annual goals for early-stage companies are "just a waste of time"
