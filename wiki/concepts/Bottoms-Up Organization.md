## Definition

A bottoms-up organization is one where ideas, initiatives, and direction emerge from individual contributors rather than being dictated top-down. OpenAI is described as "truly, truly bottoms-up" — a structure that requires exceptionally high talent caliber.

## Key Information

- Alexander Imbiricos describes OpenAI as "truly, truly bottoms-up" — more so than most companies that claim to be
- This is a counterintuitive approach for a PM who previously believed in "rallying the ship" and pointing it in the right direction
- The rationale: because OpenAI doesn't know what capabilities will emerge, what will work technically, or what will land with users, it's more important to be humble and learn empirically
- The structure requires exceptionally high talent — "very few companies have the talent caliber to be able to do that"
- The approach: "ready-fire-aim" more than "ready-aim-fire" — get things out quickly, see how users behave, then double down
- There's a fuzzy long-term aim (what futures to build toward, alignment problems) combined with empirical short-term execution
- The "awkward middle ground" of 3-11 months out is difficult to reason about; conversations are either about 1+ years out or weeks out
- This approach may not be portable to companies without OpenAI's talent density
