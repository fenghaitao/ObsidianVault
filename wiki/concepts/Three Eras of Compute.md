## Definition
The Three Eras of Compute is a framework presented by [[Adrian Bertagnoli]] describing the evolution of compute paradigms: the first era was dominated by the CPU (making compute quicker), the second by [[Nvidia]] and massively parallel GPU compute, and the third — now emerging — is heterogeneous compute mapping optimally onto multi-agentic workloads across different chips.

## Key Information
- **First Era**: CPU-dominated — making compute quicker (serial processing)
- **Second Era**: GPU/parallel-dominated by [[Nvidia]] — making compute massively parallel
- **Third Era (emerging)**: Heterogeneous compute — mapping multi-agentic workloads onto different chips optimally, with co-evolution of models, workflows, and silicon
- The third era represents a fundamental shift from homogeneous scaling to heterogeneous intelligence
- New generations of silicon are coming to market, but currently lack interfaces for unified, constructive integration with the existing compute stack
- [[Callosum]] and [[Aria UK Institute]] are building the first heterogeneous collocated cluster in the UK to spearhead this era
