## Definition

"Pods," as described by [[Adam Mosseri]] (Head of [[Instagram]]), are small, generalist mini-teams that Instagram/[[Meta]] adopted in 2026 to replace the traditional large, specialist-heavy product team structure.

## Key Information

- The prior canonical team ("for the longest time") was roughly a "baker's dozen": 2-3 Android engineers, 2-3 iOS engineers, 2-3 server engineers, a generalist, a PM, a designer, a data scientist, and (if lucky) a researcher — driven by the need for every engineer writing code to have a peer reviewer familiar with that codebase.
- Pods are call it 4-6 engineers who are more generalist, centered on one "[[Product Staff|product staff]]" role (an evolution of the PM absorbing some design and data-science work), plus whatever senior specialist the specific work actually requires (e.g., a senior data scientist for pricing strategy, a senior product designer for a genuinely novel experience).
- Net effect: a core team on the order of 6-7 people, roughly half the size of the old model.
- Mosseri attributes part of the productivity gain not just to AI tooling directly but to team size itself: smaller teams have less coordination overhead, move faster, and make better decisions with "a little bit less design by committee."
- Underlying enabler: internal tools now let generalists perform work that used to require a specialist — e.g., a "product staff" person can now run a basic funnel/waterfall analysis (previously bespoke data-scientist work) using internal tools that pull the data automatically.
- Mosseri expects specialist functions to persist but be "shaped differently" — fewer purely senior-IC specialist headcount, less large-organization management, but continued investment in growing tomorrow's senior specialists (not just today's), since an org with only senior specialists and no pipeline behind them will run out of expertise.
