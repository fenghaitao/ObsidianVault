## Definition

Prototype as Highest Fidelity (Sash's framing) is the idea that a working prototype is the highest-fidelity communication medium in product work — above a written doc or mockup — because it leaves the least ambiguity for others to interpret.

## Key Information

- Fidelity ladder: text → image → video → prototype; each step leaves less room for mismatched mental models
- Someone can click a prototype and see the real thing, shrinking the ambiguity that causes meetings where you realize you were describing different products
- Early in a project, one working prototype outshines 500 docs — Sash's Mastodon-to-Instagram demo created the momentum that got Threads staffed
- Prototypes also build internal reputation: Sash posted working demos on Workplace and became known as "the prototyping person" people reached out to
- Start unconstrained ("everything works the way you want"), then trim to real-world limits (thermal envelope, compute, network)

### David Fowler's "Code Is Currency"
- Fowler describes himself as "code first, ask questions later": show a working sample, not a PowerPoint or document — "Here's a working example of something. We should start there."
- His pattern was to see a customer problem, spike a prototype, then show an advocate/architect/PM and ask "is this useful? can we turn it into something?"
- He built many prototypes knowing that maybe one in a thousand would become a real product; SignalR started this way
