## Definition

[[Cat Wu]]'s principle that an automation which works 90-95% of the time isn't really an automation at all — you still have to check its work, which defeats the purpose — so it's worth the extra effort to close the gap to fully reliable.

## Key Information

- Wu's framing: people often build something to 90-95% accuracy and then give up on it, not realizing that the remaining 5-10% is disproportionately where the real value sits; building the automation itself is often slower than doing the task manually, so stopping short wastes that investment.
- Her prescribed fix: scope a smaller, more specific automation, then put in the "elbow grease" to teach Claude your preferences and give it feedback until it reliably reaches 100%, rather than settling for a broad but unreliable one.
- Both she and [[Lenny Rachitsky]] admit to being personally guilty of stopping at ~95% (e.g., an inbox-triage workflow that occasionally misfiles an important email).
