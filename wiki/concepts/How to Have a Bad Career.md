## Definition
"How to Have a Bad Career" is one of David Patterson's talks that teaches by negation — explaining how to do something badly, then telling you not to — and is the umbrella for the career and life advice he shares at the end of his talks.
## Key Information
- Origin: with a friend he devised "How to give a bad talk" to teach grad students; he later did "How to have a bad career" (aimed at academia), "how to build a bad research center/lab," and recently "how to give AI a bad carbon footprint."
- The lessons crystallize in his paper "Life Lessons from the First Half Century of My Career" (16 lessons), split into career and personal advice:
  - Family first — a senior colleague told him "I wish I'd spent more time with the family," and "nobody on their deathbed says I wish I'd spent more time in the office."
  - Optimize for happiness over wealth — "wealthy and happiness" are different goals; the world is full of unhappy billionaires.
  - Make time for fun and hobbies even as an adult.
  - Protect time for important-but-not-urgent work (the Covey quadrant) against the urgent-but-unimportant noise of email and texting.
  - "It's not how many things you start, it's how many things you finish."
  - Hennessy's related advice: you're remembered for five or six things, not hundreds of little ones.
  - Courage: "fortune favors the bold," but "friends come and go but enemies accumulate" — stand up when it matters, in a marketplace of ideas.
  - Optimism (his going-steady story — a misread "no" led to a 59-year marriage).
  - The "nine magic words" for relationships: "I was wrong. You were right. I love you." — no substitutions.
  - Work with and help people; psychology's findings on happiness (a job you like, friends and family, helping others, contact with nature).
  - His one regret: as SIGARCH chair in the 1990s he was unaware of harassment of young women at the conferences and would have intervened.
