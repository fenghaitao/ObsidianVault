## Definition

Micro-specialization is the tendency in large organizations for product development roles to fracture into increasingly narrow sub-specialties (e.g., interaction design, animation design, content design, research), each with dedicated personnel, creating process and organizational complexity.

## Key Information

- Tomer Cohen identifies micro-specialization as a key driver of the bloated product development process at scale.
- In design alone, sub-specialties include interaction design, animation design, content design, and research — each with people dedicated to those sub-steps.
- While each specialization has a valid reason to exist, the cumulative effect is organizational bloat and slow velocity.
- The full-stack builder model aims to collapse these specializations back, enabling individuals to span multiple domains.
- Cohen notes that some people prefer specialization and that's okay — the model doesn't require everyone to become a full-stack builder, but fewer specialized roles will be needed.
- The shift mirrors the difference between a Navy SEAL (cross-trained, mission-focused) and a traditional large military unit.
