## Definition

Framing (credited by [[Mark Pincus]] to Peter Thiel's style of thinking) for why copying proven ideas is an underused competitive edge: founders resist copying because school taught them "copying is cheating," creating a moral/ego resistance that leaves the opportunity more available to those with less ego invested in appearing original.

## Key Information

- Pincus: "It's almost a moral arbitrage... you became a founder, an entrepreneur, because you wanted to go be an innovator, so it can feel like a beatdown that your path to innovation starts with copying other people's work."
- The antidote is defining ambition "in the eyes of your consumer, not your peers" — e.g., building for "nurses in Indiana" ([[FarmVille]]'s real audience), not for industry-peer approval. Pincus: "if you're truly ambitious, burn your resume."
- Consumer taste itself resists overly derivative products (as in movies/TV/books), so the craft is in copying in a way the end user doesn't consciously register as derivative.
- Directly supports the "Proven" step of [[Proven Better New]] by removing the psychological barrier to mastering an existing pattern before innovating.
