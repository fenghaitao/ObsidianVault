## Definition

Impact-Driven Culture is Meta's engineering culture that rewards engineers based on the impact of their work rather than the difficulty or complexity of the solution. This creates incentives to find the simplest path to the largest impact, but can also encourage a "ship it and run" mentality.

## Key Information

- "At Meta, if you're the guy who made the one-line change and it had huge impact, you're going to be rewarded proportional to the difficulty... which is zero. Whereas at other companies that wasn't the case"
- This creates a culture of "go find the impact, it doesn't have to be the hard crazy thing, it doesn't have to be a lot of work — find the impact and have positive impact for the company and you're rewarded"
- Ryan described this as "the number one thing that I enjoy about Meta culture" and said "no matter where I'm working, I'm always going to take that with me for the rest of my career"
- The downside: "it encouraged this culture of just like ship it and run and keep going, impact impact impact impact"
- Because of this culture, Evan "didn't need to ask those questions of like how does [the caching layer] actually work — because it didn't matter to me"
- The culture disincentivizes deep understanding in favor of fast iteration and measurable results
- Both Evan and Ryan acknowledged this as a cultural negative even while praising the impact-driven reward system
- Evan's advice: combine the impact-driven approach with curiosity — ship fast, but also take time to understand why things work
