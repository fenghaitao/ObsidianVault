## Definition

Jeetu Patel's counterintuitive leadership philosophy that directly contradicts the conventional management wisdom of "praise in public, criticize in private." Instead, he argues for establishing enough trust so that teams can debate and critique in public, and then using private moments to build trust and show support.

## Key Information

- Articulated by Jeetu Patel
- "Every management book that you read will tell you praise in public, criticize in private. I fundamentally disagree with that notion."
- In public: be respectful but direct — "This is not working. Let me tell you why it's not working. We got to face the facts."
- In private: "be very clear with people that you got their back. Don't be stingy with words on that front."
- "Conflict is a necessary condition of business, but the only way you can have productive conflict is if you've established trust"
- If you only give perfunctory compliments, those compliments feel hollow — "you didn't mean them"
- References Ben Horowitz's "shit sandwich" from Hard Thing About Hard Things (praise, criticism, praise) and rejects it: "just treat people like adults. Tell them the facts. Watch your tone."
- The goal is to avoid posturing and instead focus on problem-solving
