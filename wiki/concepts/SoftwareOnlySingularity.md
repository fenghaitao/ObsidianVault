## Definition
Software-Only Singularity is the idea that AI could automate AI research and drive recursive self-improvement using only software advances, without also needing to automate chip design and chip production. Skeptics argue that hardware bottlenecks would quickly constrain such a scenario.

## Key Information
- Joel Becker is skeptical of software-only singularity, arguing that fixed hardware limits the number of productive experiments that can fuel further progress
- If software-only singularity is not possible, compute growth and capability growth should be causally proportional
- The alternative scenario involves full automation of chip production by robots, creating a self-sustaining robots-plus-AI economy
- Robotics capabilities currently lag LLM capabilities by ~2 orders of magnitude in compute investment
- Chip production is extremely difficult to automate: fabs cost billions, iteration is glacial compared to software, and human expertise remains critical
- Becker notes it's "not obvious" whether superintelligence inside today's hardware could build chip production facilities
- The debate connects to whether AI progress will slow as compute constraints bite, or whether AI will find ways to dramatically improve capabilities relative to fixed compute
