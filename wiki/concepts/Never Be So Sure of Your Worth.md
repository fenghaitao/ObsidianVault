## Definition

A core negotiation principle attributed to Chris Voss: never be so sure of what you're worth that you wouldn't accept more. When you anchor with a number, you create a ceiling — and you might be worth far more than you think. The principle advises against self-limiting in compensation discussions.

## Key Information

- Attributed to Chris Voss (former FBI hostage negotiator)
- When you share a number first, you create a ceiling: "When you anchor, that's the ceiling"
- The natural tendency of counterparties is to split the difference — so anchoring low means you settle even lower
- Jacob Warwick: "You should never be so sure of what you're worth that you wouldn't accept more"
- This applies both to not sharing your number first and to not anchoring to a low ceiling
- Example: a Hollywood writer's attorneys asked for $1.3M on a $700K offer and settled at $1M — but if they'd asked for $1.7M, might they have settled at $1.2M? The ceiling wasn't found.
- Jacob: "Those attorneys weren't aggressive enough. We didn't find the ceiling there, did we? That was lazy negotiating."
