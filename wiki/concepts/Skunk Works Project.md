## Definition

A pattern Tony Fadell describes of quietly continuing to develop a feature or capability behind the scenes — even against the current opinion-based leader's explicit wishes — because the team believes it will be needed once the market/technology catches up.

## Key Information

- **iPod Windows connectivity**: Steve Jobs initially refused Windows support ("over my dead body... this is going to help us sell more Macs"), but Fadell's team pursued a "skunk works" project behind the scenes; it eventually shipped and became core to the iPod's third-generation breakout success.
- **iPhone stylus**: Steve Jobs was firmly against a stylus ("your finger is good enough"); it was developed as another skunk works project and later became a valued feature for professional/creative use cases without displacing finger-first interaction.
- Fadell's takeaway: "sometimes you have to have those skunk works things that even if the opinion-based leader doesn't like it, you're like, that seems like the right thing... maybe not right now, but it's going — you can see it on the horizon."
