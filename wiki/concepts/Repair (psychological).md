## Definition

Repair is the #1 relationship strategy: going back to a person after a moment you didn't feel proud of, taking responsibility for your part, acknowledging the impact it had on them, and talking about what you would do differently next time. Secure attachment is defined by the presence of repair, not the absence of mistakes.

## Key Information

- "The thing that really differentiated secure attachment is the presence of repair" — Dr. Becky learned this in clinical psych grad school
- Components of repair: (1) take responsibility for your part, (2) acknowledge the impact, (3) talk about what you'd do differently
- The thing that prevents repair is the false belief that there's a goal to be perfect
- "Perfect is creepy" — only non-humans can be perfect; the human condition is wanting to do well and messing up over and over
- "There's nothing to reestablish trust and connection like repair. When trust and connection are reestablished, they cooperate better."
- With a kid: "Hey, sorry I yelled. I had a stressful day at work. That wasn't your fault, and I'm working on staying calmer even when I'm upset."
- At work: "Hey, earlier in the meeting, I totally cut you off. I used a really harsh tone. Honestly, I did disagree with what you were saying, but that's no excuse for me to talk to you the way I did. I'm sorry. I'm going to work on that."
- Repair is hopeful: it's something you can realistically get good at, unlike perfection
- If you didn't have a parent who repaired with you, "no wonder it feels awkward — I'm kind of the first person in my whole lineage to give a true apology to my three-year-old"
