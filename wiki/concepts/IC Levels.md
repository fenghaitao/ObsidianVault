## Definition

IC Levels (Individual Contributor levels) define the engineering ladder at companies like Meta, ranging from IC3 (new grad) through IC9 and beyond. Each level represents increasing scope, impact, and leadership expectations.

## Key Information

- Meta's levels: IC3 (new grad/entry), IC4 (mid-level), IC5 (senior), IC6 (staff), IC7 (senior staff), IC8, IC9
- Evan King's trajectory: IC3 to IC6 in 3 years (IC3-IC4 in 1 half, IC4-IC5 in 2 halves, IC5-IC6 in 3 halves)
- At Meta, IC levels are not publicly visible — other engineers don't know your level, which forces influence without authority
- The jump from IC5 to IC6 (senior to staff) requires "scaling yourself" — you can't do everything yourself anymore
- Ryan Peterman felt that going to IC7 would make him a "snowflake" — narrowly useful only at very large companies with limited transferability
- Evan noted that IC8+ engineers at Meta are "handcuffed" to the company — too much equity to leave, and other companies wouldn't match the level for someone with their years of experience
- The IC track is viable all the way up — you don't need to become a manager to advance
- The transition from IC to management (like TLM) is a separate decision that changes the nature of your work from individual execution to people management
