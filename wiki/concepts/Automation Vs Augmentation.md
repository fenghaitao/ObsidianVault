## Definition

The "automation versus augmentation fork," as named by [[Max Schoening]] (Head of Product, [[Notion]]), is the open question of whether faster, near-instant AI inference will push interaction back toward direct manipulation (augmentation — a human still molds the work in real time) or toward fully automated, queued/delegated agent work (automation — a human hands off a task and reviews later).

## Key Information

- Schoening frames it through inference speed rather than model intelligence: "If the inference is slow, then you're queuing up a bunch of jobs and then you're walking around the building thinking about other things and then come back and review versus if it's nearly instant... do we get back to direct manipulation? Do you instantly sort of mold the clay that is the code?"
- Ties this to whether current multitasking/"frenetic" work patterns are a genuine flow state or just an artifact of slow inference forcing batch/queue behavior.
- Connects to his "retina display" analogy for model intelligence: past a certain threshold of capability, more raw intelligence stops mattering for most cognitive tasks ("after I can't see the pixels, I can't see the pixels, I don't need you to make them smaller") — at that point what matters is a different modality and faster response, not a smarter model.
- Raises this directly in the context of [[Figma]] usage trends at Notion: some designers are moving away from direct-manipulation tools toward agent-driven workflows, while others still prefer Figma — Schoening says it's genuinely hard to predict whether direct manipulation is disappearing or merely being mediated by agents.
- Explicitly skeptical of labs' operating assumption that everyone always wants the smartest/frontier model; argues most knowledge-work tasks will hit "good enough" and then optimize for other things (speed, cost, running locally) rather than raw intelligence — echoing economist [[Tyler Cowen]]'s point (as Schoening paraphrases it) that "society is largely not capped by intelligence."
- Frames his own interest as being in the "exoskeleton" (augmentation) rather than "a god in a box in some data center" (pure automation) framing of AI's future.
