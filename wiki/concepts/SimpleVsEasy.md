## Definition
The distinction between "simple" and "easy" as articulated by Rich Hickey in his 2011 talk "Simple Made Easy." Simple means structurally unentangled — one fold, one braid, each piece doing one thing without intertwining with others. Easy means adjacent — what is within reach, what can be accessed without effort.

## Key Information
- **Simple**: About structure. Requires thought, design, and untangling. You cannot make something simple by wishing.
- **Easy**: About proximity. You can always make something easier by putting it closer — install a package, generate with AI, copy from Stack Overflow.
- Jake Nations argues that AI is the "ultimate easy button" — it makes the easy path so frictionless that developers no longer consider the simple one
- Every time we choose easy, we choose speed now and complexity later
- Historically, complexity accumulated slowly enough that teams could refactor; AI has destroyed that balance by making easy too frictionless
- The distinction is central to understanding why AI code generation leads to tangled, unmaintainable codebases
