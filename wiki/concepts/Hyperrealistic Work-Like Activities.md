## Definition

Hyperrealistic work-like activities are superficially identical to real work — people sitting in conference rooms, presenting slides, discussing and reviewing — but create no actual value. They arise when organizations have more people than "known valuable work to do."

## Key Information

- **Linked to "known valuable work to do"**: at a startup's beginning, everyone has abundant known valuable work (open a bank account, create a users table, etc.). As the company grows, the supply of known valuable work shrinks relative to the number of people
- **Root cause**: not because people are stupid or evil, but because they naturally want to be recognized, get promoted, and demonstrate wins
- **Example**: Slack threads case — a team spent thousands of person-hours (engineering, QA, analytics, product management, user research, meetings) to A/B test a tiny feature change (pre-populating @username in thread replies) that could only possibly make a 0.03-message difference in thread length. The cost of the analysis alone guaranteed it was a loser
- **Parkinson's Law connection**: every product manager wants to hire a junior product manager; every new hire creates demand for work; when there's not enough real work, people create fake work
- **Leader's responsibility**: it's on managers, directors, executives, and the CEO to ensure sufficient supply of known valuable work, create clarity around priorities, and explicitly say no to things up front
