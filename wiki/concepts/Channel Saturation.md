## Definition

Channel Saturation is the state where a company's marketing and acquisition channels have reached their practical limits — they can no longer drive meaningful incremental growth. It is step 4 of Jason Cohen's Stalled Growth Framework and forces companies to consider creative expansion or entirely new approaches.

## Signs of Saturation

- ROAS (return on ad spend) declining over time
- More effort/money yields proportionally less growth
- The same channels that drove early growth are now flat or declining
- Marketing team is being "flogged" to do more of the same with diminishing returns

## Why Channels Saturate

- **Inventory limits:** There are only so many searches for your keywords; you can only appear once per search result
- **Audience fatigue:** People have seen the message 7+ times already; they either bought or they won't
- **Channel decline:** The channel itself deteriorates (magazine circulation, conference attendance, algorithm changes)
- **Competitive crowding:** More competitors enter, driving up costs

## What to Do About It

1. **Add new channels.** But direct and indirect channels often don't cross-pollinate — a product that sells well direct may not do well in SEO/social, and vice versa.

2. **Get creative with existing channels.** Examples:
   - **Constant Contact:** In-person workshops in cities, teaching small businesses email marketing — cost-effective for a $20/month product
   - **HubSpot:** Selling through agencies instead of direct — became 50% of revenue after 4-5 years
   - **WP Engine:** Selling through agencies that create WordPress sites

3. **Develop a second product.** Sell to the same customer segments so existing customers can buy it. High risk, high reward.

4. **Change target market.** Plant one foot in a strength/asset you have, move the other foot into a new market.

## The Elephant Curve Connection

Channels don't just plateau (S-curve) — they decline (elephant curve). This makes channel saturation inevitable and means you must proactively find new channels before the old ones sag.
