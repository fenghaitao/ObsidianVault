## Definition

Amazon's Promotion Process is the formal system for advancing engineers through the SDE leveling ladder. Unlike Google, Amazon's promotions and performance reviews are decoupled, running on separate schedules. The process is notoriously difficult at the SDE3 to Principal (L7) level because Amazon effectively skips a staff level.

## Key Information

- Promotions and performance reviews are decoupled — two independent processes that may happen to line up but have no dependency on each other
- The promotion process changed three times during Steve Huynh's 4-year attempt (2016-2020) to get from SDE3 to Principal
- Steve's key advice: don't wait to start — put your packet in early and get actual feedback about gaps
- After a rejection, focus on the delta (weaknesses) rather than doubling down on strengths
- The promo panel looks at what changed since the last review — they focus on outliers and the delta
- A common failure pattern: engineers focus on principal-level work and neglect SDE3 responsibilities, then get flagged as low-performing at SDE3
- Engineers need to "do two jobs" during the promotion push — maintain SDE3 performance while also demonstrating principal-level scope
- Steve described the process as "becoming an expert in the process of getting promoted" rather than upleveling himself
- The SDE3 to Principal gap is like jumping two levels because Amazon skips staff
