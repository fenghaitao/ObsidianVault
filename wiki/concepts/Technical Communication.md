## Definition

Technical Communication is the ability to explain complex technical topics clearly to both technical and non-technical audiences. It is a critical skill for staff-level engineers, as the people making promotion decisions are often from both backgrounds.

## Key Information

- Carly's promotion was directly triggered by a presentation to an executive leader and the CTO where she demonstrated this skill
- "You should be able to recite the impact that you've had, and you need to be able to do this to technical and non-technical audiences"
- "The people who are making the decisions about whether or not you're ready to move to the next level are both [technical and non-technical]"
- "It's not just the fact that you can do some crazy algorithm — it's that you can actually speak to it to a layman, make them understand the value in your work"
- Rahul's framework: research who's in the audience (standup, meeting, workplace group), understand how much they know and care, and tailor the level of depth accordingly
- Both extremes are bad: too deep in the weeds alienates non-technical audiences; too light on detail fails to convince technical audiences
- Zach's tip: posting on LinkedIn daily trains conciseness — the LinkedIn algorithm rewards dense information delivered in the most concise way possible
- Lee's technique: asking questions rather than making declarative statements — "one of the highest measures of intelligence is not what you say but what questions you ask"
- Being able to ask the right question at the right time can be the difference between successful and failing projects
- Lee: "20 minutes of a senior person's time could have prevented two or three Dev years of sunk cost"
