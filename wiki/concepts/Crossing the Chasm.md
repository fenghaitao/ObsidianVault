## Definition

The classic technology-adoption-curve model (early adopters, later adopters, laggards); Tony Fadell says he updated/adapted this graphic in *Build* to reflect how his teams tailor marketing language to each adopter segment across product versions.

## Key Information

- Fadell's application: marketing language must be tailored to the customer segment being targeted at each product stage — early adopters need different messaging than "later adopters," who need different messaging than "laggards."
- Concrete example (iPod in Europe): reusing identical US marketing messaging in Europe failed, because European buyers were "slower to adopt technology" and needed their own tailored early-adopter and later-adopter messaging — not a copy of US messaging crafted for a different adoption stage.
- Fadell frames "gestation of your customer" — understanding where a given market/segment sits in its own adoption journey — as essential to deciding what marketing message to use, separate from the product itself.
- Notes that software/digital products can sometimes "go global right away," compressing distribution time, but awareness and intelligent understanding of the product by a new market still can't be fully accelerated — you can't skip the adoption psychology just because distribution is instant.
- Per [[Jason Cohen]] (episode 30): recommends the original book itself (by [[Geoffrey Moore]], a prior Lenny's Podcast guest) over secondhand summaries, since most people have only seen "the little picture" (the chasm diagram) without reading the book's deeper argument — particularly its definition of a market as a group of people who respect each other's opinions, which explains why early adopters spread word only to other early adopters, and why jumping to a new market can't be done via case studies from the old one.
