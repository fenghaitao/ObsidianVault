## Definition

Down-Leveling is the practice of offering a candidate a lower title/level than their current one when they join a FAANG or big tech company, typically because their current scope doesn't match the new company's leveling expectations. It is often accompanied by a significant compensation increase.

## Key Information

- Titles outside big tech are generally inflated — a "principal engineer" at a startup might map to a senior engineer at FAANG
- Down-leveling is not really a downgrade — it's "correct leveling" against the new company's standards
- The panel advised focusing on scope and work rather than titles: "if you're getting paid three times as much and your title is two steps lower, what do you care?"
- Zach's personal experience: was a mid-level engineer at a startup, got a junior role at Facebook, but received an 80% pay bump. Zach was also told he would be down-leveled if he switched from Data Engineer to Software Engineer at Facebook, despite writing 90% of the JavaScript on his team of 15 data engineers. Facebook's standard practice at the time: going from DE to SWE triggered a down-level, but not the other way around — revealing a "Software Engineer Supremacy" culture. The contrast with up-leveling through job hopping: Zach was denied a level increase internally but got Staff at Airbnb through interviewing, demonstrating the asymmetry between internal promotion and external hiring.
- The advice is to look at the scope of work you're doing and compare it to what the target company expects at each level
- A "vice president" at a small company might lead a small team, which maps to a senior engineer at FAANG
- Down-leveling is a reflection of the candidate's demonstrated scope, not a punishment

- Boris Cherny came in under-leveled at Meta and called it lucky: lower expectations gave him space to explore and build a strong first impression, and he cautions against pushing for level+1 on job switches ([[summary-20251215 - Boris Cherny (Creator of Claude Code) On What Grew His Career And Building at Anthropic]])

### David Ronca on Leveling

- "When in doubt, go low": joining Meta or Google as a 5 and executing as a 6 earns better bonuses, multipliers, and RSU refreshes; an over-leveled hire has no mechanism to fix it except leaving.
- Netflix's lack of levels made compensation un-rationalizable — a 2x market offer for a great engineer couldn't be justified in context, which is why he argues levels are needed at scale.
