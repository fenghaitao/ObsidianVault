## Definition

Research concept (from 1980s analysis of the Space Shuttle Challenger disaster) applied by [[Simon Willison]] to AI security: every time an organization gets away with an unsafe practice without consequence, it institutionally grows more confident that the practice is actually safe — even though the underlying risk hasn't changed.

## Key Information

- Original context: engineers knew the Challenger's O-rings were unreliable, but repeated successful launches without failure made the organization progressively more comfortable launching despite the known risk, until the risk materialized catastrophically.
- Willison's AI-security application: the industry has been using AI agents in increasingly unsafe ways (see [[Prompt Injection]] / [[Lethal Trifecta]]) without yet suffering a headline-grabbing, large-scale attack — and he argues this absence of disaster is itself making people more reckless, not more careful.
- His prediction: there will eventually be an AI security "Challenger disaster" — a genuinely catastrophic, widely publicized prompt-injection exploit — that may be the only thing that forces the industry to take the problem seriously. He notes he has made a version of this same prediction roughly every 6 months for 3 years without it yet occurring (compared by Lenny to the "black swan turkey" problem — false confidence growing right up until the event that disproves it).
