## Definition

Saying No in the context of engineering career growth is the deliberate practice of declining work that does not contribute to promotion readiness or personal growth, even when that work comes from peers or managers. Ricky described it as an essential skill for protecting an accelerated promotion timeline.

## Key Information

### Ricky's Strategy
- Ricky explicitly said no to "a lot of projects that I felt like were not at the next level"
- He acknowledged these projects "might have impact, but they weren't going to help me grow"
- The key distinction: impact alone is not enough — the project must also stretch you toward the next level

### When to Say Yes
- If it's a bug you caused: fix it — it's your responsibility
- If it's a bug in your scope of area: fix it — you're the owner
- If it's a new project that genuinely is at the next level of scope: consider it

### How to Say No to Peers
- If someone else assigns you work you don't want, escalate to your manager immediately
- Ask: "Why is this being handed to me?" — it might be a legitimate bug you caused
- Your manager can help deflect or clarify the expectation

### How to Say No to Your Manager
- Push back respectfully: "Is this something I have to do? I have my own projects here that I feel like would have more impact and also help me grow more. Can I just do these projects instead?"
- A good manager's job is to find projects that reports actually want to do — forced work leads to poor quality
- The manager should want you working on projects that help you grow and succeed
