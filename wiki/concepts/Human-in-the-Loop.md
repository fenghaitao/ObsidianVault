## Definition

Human-in-the-loop (HITL) is a design pattern where humans remain in the decision pathway of AI systems, reviewing, approving, or overriding AI actions. It is a key mechanism for building trust, logging behavior data for the AI flywheel, and preventing dangerous autonomous decisions during the calibration phase.

## Key Information

- **Purpose**: Keep humans in control while the AI system earns trust. Prevents AI from making dangerous autonomous decisions before it's proven reliable.
- **Flywheel role**: When humans review AI outputs (e.g., drafts, suggestions), you log what they keep, change, or omit — this is free error analysis data.
- **Agency progression**: As the AI proves itself, human involvement decreases. V1 = human reviews and approves everything, V2 = human reviews samples, V3 = human monitors exceptions.
- **Pre-authorization pattern**: In insurance, low-risk procedures (MRIs, blood tests) can be auto-approved by AI, while high-risk procedures (invasive surgeries) go through human review.
- **The orchestrator role**: Human-in-the-loop isn't passive — it's an active, exhausting role. At SaaStr, managing 20 AI agents required constant attention, including nights and weekends.
- **Air Canada example**: Without human-in-the-loop, an AI agent hallucinated a refund policy, and the company was legally forced to honor it.
- **Current state**: Most AI products today focus on productivity (high human-in-the-loop) because 74-75% of enterprises cite reliability concerns — they're not ready for full autonomy.
