## Definition

[[Sam Lesson]]'s principle that good conversation is a reciprocal exchange, not a one-sided extraction or monologue — modeled explicitly as a game of ping-pong, where each party alternates offering something and responding.

## Key Information

- Origin framing: "a conversation is a give and a get and it should be an exchange." Coming in only to ask questions "kind of misses the point" if it becomes one-sided extraction — "sometimes I feel this: you'll meet someone and you feel like it's the inquisition."
- Explicit ping-pong analogy: "hit the ball back... if you hit 10 balls at them in a row... that's kind of not the vibe you want to go for." Applies equally to asking questions and to telling stories — "they tell a story, you have a story to tell."
- Balances with (but is distinct from) [[Abundance Mindset]]'s "leave them wanting more": the goal is neither delivering your full personal "four-minute spiel" nor extracting the other person's; it's trading manageable, engaging turns.
- Extended to matching vocabulary/register: meeting someone at their level of formality/vocabulary (a university professor vs. a child) is framed as another form of "give" — making the other person comfortable rather than making a point about yourself.
- Extended to graceful conversation exits: recognizing the signs that a conversation is winding down (e.g., someone saying "I'm going to go grab a drink") and respecting them rather than continuing to extract more attention, or actively "giving" the other person a next conversation partner ("have you met Steve?").
