## Definition

[[Jenny Wen]]'s three preferred designer hiring profiles for the current AI-accelerated era: strong generalists ("block-shaped"), deep T-shaped specialists, and "craft new grads."

## Key Information

- **Strong generalist / "block-shaped"**: not a merely competent generalist, but someone who is ~80th-percentile skilled across several core disciplines simultaneously (design, some engineering, some PM) — rare and hard to hire, but valuable because the design role itself is stretching to cover more adjacent skills. Related to the "F-shaped"/"E-shaped" framing [[Marc Andreessen]] has separately described.
- **Deep specialist**: classically T-shaped, but with the vertical stroke of the T going unusually deep — e.g. a designer who is effectively half a software engineer, or a designer with elite visual/icon craft. Valuable specifically because it lets someone differentiate work in an era where AI makes baseline execution accessible to everyone.
- **"Craft new grad"**: early-career, humble, and an unusually fast learner, without entrenched process assumptions from a pre-AI design career — Wen argues this profile is undervalued industry-wide (most companies over-index on senior hires), but is especially useful right now precisely because they aren't burdened by outdated process rituals.
- Wen's advice to aspiring designers: build and ship real things rather than theorize, and find/join a community of people doing the same (she cites her alma mater's "Socratica" project-showcase culture as a model).
- Connects to [[Role Collapse]]: as design increasingly overlaps with PM and engineering work, these three archetypes represent different ways to be valuable inside that convergence rather than staying narrowly within traditional design scope.
