## Definition

Internship Success refers to the rubric and strategies for performing well as a software engineering intern, with the goal of securing a return offer. It is measured across three dimensions: productivity, code quality, and initiative.

## Key Information

- The three factors that determine internship success: productivity (speed of project completion), code quality (decreasing review revisions over time), and initiative (proposing improvements beyond the assigned project)
- A strong intern completes their project in half the internship's time, starts with code needing many reviews but ends with one or zero revisions, and proactively proposes new projects or improvements
- Lines of code is not a reliable metric — Ricky wrote only 800 lines of code during his Google internship, far less than peers writing 11K, but still got a return offer because he produced a complex design doc exploring pros and cons of different approaches
- Internship projects are usually well-scoped with a defined end, unlike the ambiguous problems at higher levels
- The biggest differentiator between high and low performers: asking questions and proposing improvements. High performers have the "audacity" to challenge senior engineers; low performers are quiet and struggle alone
- Ryan mentored five interns at Meta and observed that the quiet ones who tried to figure everything out alone made no progress over time and didn't get return offers
