## Definition

A technique for presenting to executives: give three options with the "Goldilocks" middle option as your recommendation. This demonstrates that you've considered alternatives, preempts the "did you think of X?" pushback, and makes the executive feel like a co-creator rather than a rubber stamp.

## Key Information

- **The mistake:** Giving only one option. "People think you missed something."
- **The solution:** Present three options with the middle one as your recommendation — classic pricing and packaging strategy.
- "What that actually allows you to do is say: hey, we considered. We are not dumb. We did not miss something."
- Have backup options ready in an appendix or Figma draft. "It's not necessary to put all 15 options you considered, but at least be ready to show them."
- **Example:** Jessica's team at Webflow initially brought a single option to a product review with Rachel Woolen. It didn't go well. They turned around a new doc in two days showing all options considered, and Rachel understood why their proposed solution made the most sense.
- **Related: "Stuart plus two more"** — after Stuart Butterfield gave design direction, the Slack team would deliver exactly what he asked for plus two more versions they felt good about, creating a forum for debate.
- This connects to the **Mento Pyramid**: start with your recommendation, then the options explored, then the evidence.
