## Definition

[[Sherwin Wu]]'s engineering-management philosophy, borrowed from a prediction in *The Mythical Man-Month* (1970s): treat top-performing engineers like a lead surgeon in an operating room, with everyone else (including the manager) as support staff who anticipate needs and hand over the right tool at the right moment.

## Key Information

- The original book predicted software engineering might organize this way, with one person doing the core work while others (nurse/resident/fellow equivalents) support them — a prediction Wu says didn't play out literally (work is more collaborative than one-person-does-everything), but the underlying spirit stuck with him.
- Applied concretely as a management practice: spend the majority (Wu says "more than 50%") of management time with the top ~10% of performers, "looking around corners" to spot and clear organizational blockers before they become bottlenecks — the manager effectively keeps a mental "scalpel" ready before the engineer even asks.
- Wu argues this matters even more in the AI era: as engineers ship far more PRs via agents, the main bottleneck shifts from individual coding output to organizational/process friction, which is exactly the kind of blocker a proactive manager can clear.
- Echoes [[Marc Andreessen]]'s framing (per Lenny) that "AI makes good people better and makes great people exceptional" — Wu's practice of doubling down on top performers is his direct operational response to that dynamic.
- Speculates AI tools (an internal-knowledge-connected chatbot) could help managers proactively surface blockers and even anticipate future ones, a use case he says he hadn't tried before the interview prompted the idea.
