## Definition

A core Jacob Warwick principle: moving too fast in a negotiation is risky. Slowing down the process — taking days to respond, not rushing to accept or counter — signals scarcity, thoughtfulness, and creates space to gather more information. The more information you have, the more leverage you possess.

## Key Information

- Jacob: "Too hasty equals risk. So, as you slow down, often times we want to take a couple of days to respond"
- Slowing down is not about being a jerk or manufacturing fake urgency — it "shows a little scarcity and thought process to your time"
- Benefits of slowing down:
  - Collect more information about the company's needs and pain points
  - Test the company's level of interest and urgency
  - Create space to strategize and prepare
  - Signal that you're not desperate
- When receiving an offer: "I want to review this over the next couple of days. Talk to my wife about it. Talk to an advisor."
- "The longer we can drag out the hiring process — and this sounds like pulling teeth, especially when you're unemployed — the more information you're going to understand about that company and how you could really help."
- Taking time to respond also helps sell the deal: "I imagine we start working together in a couple weeks" — communicates that the deal will get done
