## Definition

SPOTAK is a candidate-evaluation checklist used by [[Matt MacInnis]] (CPO, [[Rippling]]) — smart, passionate, optimistic, tenacious, adaptable, kind — that MacInnis credits to an unnamed originator ("a hat tip to somebody out there in the universe").

## Key Information

- MacInnis describes it as not a good top-down evaluation framework by itself, but a useful tool for *decoding* an intuitive negative reaction to a candidate after an interview — e.g., realizing in retrospect that a candidate who "talked shit about their previous manager" and framed themselves as a victim of past company performance scored poorly on "optimistic."
- Rests on the premise that hiring intuition, built from many thousands of interviews ("a lot of batting practice"), is valuable and should be trusted — but needs to be articulated in terms other people can act on, since raw intuition alone isn't transferable.
- Used alongside the [[Alpha Beta Hiring And Process Framework]] to separate "is this person good" from "is this person the right kind of good for this specific role."
- Complemented at Rippling by a separate interview practice: giving every product candidate, regardless of seniority, the same deliberately impossible case study (calibrated with a rubric for what entry-level vs. senior vs. VP-level performance looks like), evaluating candidates on how many "corners" they can see around and how they react to being challenged, rather than expecting anyone to solve it fully.
