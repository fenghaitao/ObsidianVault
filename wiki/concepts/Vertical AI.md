## Definition
Vertical AI refers to AI companies and products built for specific industries or domains (e.g., legal, finance, healthcare), as opposed to horizontal AI platforms. Vertical AI companies aim to make agents complete increasingly complex end-to-end work within their domain, requiring deep domain-specific interfaces and collaboration patterns.

## Key Information
- Vertical AI companies focus on making agents complete complex, domain-specific work end-to-end
- The goal has shifted in the past 6-12 months due to new economics of production: doing work is now extremely cheap, but planning and reviewing work are the new bottlenecks
- Different verticals have tasks at different points on the verifiability spectrum:
  - Legal: checking definitions (easy to verify), writing contracts (hard to verify), litigation strategy (impossible to verify)
  - Coding: some parts easy to verify, building a successful consumer app very difficult to verify
- Vertical AI requires domain-specific interfaces: high-bandwidth artifacts that match the industry (documents for legal, tabular reviews, etc.)
- Example: Legora serves over 1,000 law firms across 50+ markets with a collaborative AI workspace
- The right collaboration interface varies by vertical — what works for legal documents doesn't work for code or finance
