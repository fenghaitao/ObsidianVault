## Definition

A "professional vibe coder" is a new, full-time hireable job role — distinct from casual [[Vibe Coding]] as a prototyping technique — in which someone with little or no traditional software-engineering background is paid to build production-quality internal and external products end-to-end by directing AI coding agents. [[Lazar]], [[Lovable]]'s first official "vibe coding engineer," is the episode's central example.

## Key Information

- Distinguished from the general concept of [[Vibe Coding]]: vibe coding is the *technique* (building by prompting rather than hand-coding); "professional vibe coder" is the *job* built around doing that technique full-time, with production-grade rigor, security, and quality expectations rather than throwaway prototyping.
- Scope of the role (per Lazar): building both internal tools (e.g., feature-adoption dashboards, community/enterprise tooling) and shipped, customer-facing products (e.g., Shopify integration templates, a company merch store) — spanning nearly every department rather than sitting under one team.
- The job exists because "ideas" are now the bottleneck, not implementation: many people have good ideas but lack the bandwidth or technical means to build them; the professional vibe coder's job is to make ideas real fast, with adequate quality and security for production.
- Lazar reports the role isn't unique to Lovable — he describes at least one other company that hired multiple full-time vibe coders (used to migrate an old codebase and internal tooling onto Lovable) before Lovable itself created the title internally.
- Core skill mix required, per Lazar: clarity (specifying intent precisely for an AI agent), taste/judgment (knowing what "world class" looks like versus merely "good enough"), and self-awareness/delusional optimism (assuming a task is possible until proven otherwise, while still being honest about one's own gaps).
- Career path into the role does not require being hired first: Lazar argues "you don't need a company to hire you — you can hire yourself as a professional vibe coder first" by building in public (see [[Build In Public]]) and demonstrating the skill before a company formally hires you; he was doing the job (via YouTube, a paid course, and public building) before [[Elena Verna]] hired him at Lovable.
- Practical techniques associated with doing the job well: heavy upfront planning (roughly 80% planning/chat-mode time versus 20% execution), a stack of living planning documents to manage the AI's context window (see [[Token Budgeting]]), running multiple parallel prototype attempts before committing to a direction, and a structured debugging escalation framework (see [[4x4 Debugging Framework]]).
- Framed as part of a broader convergence of the PM/engineer/designer "venn diagram" into overlapping, AI-amplified roles — see [[Role Collapse]] and [[Forward Deployed Engineer]].
- Lazar predicts the specific term may not last (he calls himself a future "rapid engineer") since as agents improve, some of what a professional vibe coder does manually today (e.g., manually preserving context across sessions) will increasingly be automated by the tools themselves.
