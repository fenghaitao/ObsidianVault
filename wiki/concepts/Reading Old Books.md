# Reading Old Books

A philosophy advocated by [[Qasar Younis]] (inspired by [[Charlie Munger]]): read old books because time has filtered out the noise, leaving high-signal content.

## Core Idea

"Read old books. Don't read anything new. Time has filtered out a lot of the noise. So you get a lot of signal."

## Key Points
- Charlie Munger: "I've never met anybody very successful who doesn't read all the time"
- The average person will read maybe 50-100 books in a lifetime — don't waste that on low-quality content
- "There are true pillars of human ideas out there. You consume those ideas and then it's up to you to interpret how those ideas then reflect upon the business that you're leading"
- Qasar's method: identify areas you know nothing about (Roman history, Jainism, feudal Japan), then find the best book in that space
- Reading a book like Malcolm X's autobiography will make you a better founder — not directly, but through broadening your understanding of society and history
- "Being a well-rounded founder where you understand society around you and history around you somehow makes you build a better product. I don't know how and why, but I think it's absolutely true"
- Marc Andreessen's version: barbell strategy — consume today's news (X) and 10-year-old books
- Qasar's recommended books: "The Emperor of All Maladies," "Made in America" (Sam Walton), "Guns, Germs, and Steel," "Collapse," "My American Journey" (Colin Powell), "SPQR," "High Output Management" (Andy Grove)
