## Definition

Up or Out is a policy at some tech companies where engineers are expected to reach a certain level (typically senior) within a defined timeframe, or they will be managed out. It is not as intense as it sounds — most people make it to those levels.

## Key Information

- Some companies have an "up or out" policy where if you can't reach the target level (e.g., senior), you're let go
- Ryan explained that "up and out" policies are "not meant to be intense and scary" — it's like "90% plus" of people who make it to those levels
- It's more of an "upper bound of how quickly you'll grow" rather than a threat
- Google is more relaxed and does not have an aggressive "up or out" policy; other companies are more aggressive
- At Amazon, SDE1 is not a terminal level with an "up or out" expectation within ~2 years

- Nimit Sohoni: quant has an "up or out" culture plus trimming of low performers, and job security can be U-shaped ("an inverse parabola") because senior quants get expensive relative to their output even when skilled
