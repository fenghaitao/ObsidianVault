## Definition

[[Dr. Becky Kennedy]]'s name for a temperament pattern — in kids and, by extension, adults — whose core fear is being "too much," often labeled dramatic or oppositional, who push away the very people they need most in the moments they need them, thereby confirming their own fear.

## Key Information

- Kennedy calls these "the most misunderstood kids in the world" — often mislabeled dramatic or oppositional, with professionals sometimes wrongly advising parents to punish/discipline more consistently. Unlike [[Resilient Rebels]] (fear of losing control), deeply feeling kids' core fear is being too much.
- Behavioral pattern: they have big, explosive feelings and act out in ways that push away the people closest to them in the exact moments they most need connection — "leave me alone" is described as what they'll scream when completely out of control, which is their deepest fear speaking, not their actual wish.
- This creates a self-reinforcing cycle: acting out from the "too much" fear pushes people away, which confirms "I really am too much," which exacerbates the pattern — until the person learns to recognize and interrupt the cycle.
- Kennedy says this pattern resonates with adults too: several people told her the framing gave them insight into a partner's or coworker's fear of being "too much" that manifests as intense, explosive reactions which alienate the very people they need.
- Subject of Kennedy's second children's book, *[[Leave Me Alone (book)]]: A Good Inside Story About Deeply Feeling Kids*, framed as a companion for the deeply feeling child to see themselves, paired with Good Inside's app as the companion for the parent.
