## Definition

Performance Review (called PSC at Meta) is the formal evaluation process at large tech companies where engineers receive ratings and feedback on their work. Ratings determine promotions, compensation, and in some cases, continued employment.

## Key Information

- At Meta (Facebook), the performance review process is called PSC and runs on a half-year cycle
- Ratings include "Meets All" (meets expectations) and "Greatly Exceeds" (significantly exceeds expectations)
- Zach received two consecutive "Greatly Exceeds" ratings as an IC4 at Facebook, which is strong signal that someone is performing at the senior level
- Despite two "Greatly Exceeds" ratings, Zach was denied promotion to IC5 because his manager questioned whether his performance was sustainable (he was working 50-60 hours/week)
- At Netflix, the review process was different: quarterly color ratings (green/yellow/red) from your manager, where red means imminent firing, yellow means improvement needed, and green means you're doing well
- Netflix had no formal performance reviews for compensation — annual compensation discussions were based on market value, not past performance
- At Airbnb, Zach received an "Exceeds" rating at the staff level while working approximately 40 hours/week, demonstrating that high ratings don't require extreme hours
- The disconnect between performance ratings and promotions illustrates that ratings measure output, while promotions assess behaviors and sustainability
- At Amazon, performance reviews and promotions are decoupled processes — they run on different schedules and are independent of each other
- Amazon's performance management has historically targeted ~5-6% managed out per year, shifting from soft guidance to harder mandate
- Amazon's talent review meetings (described by Steve Huynh as "terrible") are multi-day locked-room sessions where people are discussed in terms of measurables and feedback, with focus on the edges of the distribution
- A common failure pattern at Amazon: engineers focus on principal-level work for promotion but neglect SDE3 responsibilities, then get flagged as low-performing at SDE3 during performance review
- Steve noted that in recent years, without backfills, the repeated cuts start cannibalizing solid performers who would have been fine in any other year
- The way to survive stack ranking: explicitly ask your manager about expectations and whether you're meeting them, ideally early in the cycle — "bad news delivered early is just news; bad news delivered late is terrible news"

### Bryan Cantrill's Perspective (Sun Microsystems)
- Argues formalized performance review never made him perform better: "performance review is not about you performing better, it's about us measuring your performance" — but measurement should not be the goal.
- The annual cadence felt broken: self-reviews produced manager reviews that looked "stunningly like my self review albeit with great grammatical errors introduced."
- Promotions were never the highlight of his progression — significant bodies of work, nailing a hard bug, or doing the impossible with a team were "catalytic"; review, even positive ones, was not uplifting.
- Sun's grades were superlative / excellent / good; a superlative grade forced a promotion, so managers apologized for giving "excellent" to extraordinary work they couldn't yet reward with a promotion ([[summary-20260302 - Distinguished Eng： Stack Ranking, Competing with Bezos, Regrets ｜ Bryan Cantrill]]).
