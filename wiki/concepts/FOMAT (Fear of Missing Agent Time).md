## Definition
FOMAT (Fear of Missing Agent Time) is the anxiety of being away from your development machine when an AI coding agent either needs human input, has been blocked waiting for a response, or has completed a task. It is the agent-era analogue of FOMO (Fear of Missing Out), introduced by Michael Richman.

## Key Information
- Introduced by [[MichaelRichman]] in his talk at the AI Engineer conference.
- Manifests in two primary forms:
  1. Having an idea for an agent task while away from your dev machine (on a walk, in bed) and having to wait to get back to issue it.
  2. Stepping away while an agent is running, only to return and discover it blocked after 2 minutes waiting for a question — idling the entire time you were gone.
- The problem is rooted in the reality that agents are not yet fully autonomous — they require back-and-forth interaction and babysitting, and you cannot predict when input will be needed.
- Current coding tasks typically take 5–45 minutes, but as agents improve, task windows will extend to hours and days, making "check back in a bit" increasingly infeasible.
- The solution requires systems that let you know when an agent needs you (notifications) and let you respond from wherever you are (remote interaction), regardless of whether you are at your dev machine.
- Richman argues that alleviating FOMAT requires embracing time away from agents — it is during breaks that the best ideas often emerge, and systems must support reaching agents during those breaks.
- The concept frames agent idleness as a form of waste: the longer the agent waits for you, the more agent time is missed.
- Related to the broader trend of agents becoming always-available resources that need always-available human oversight, shifting the developer's role from deep-focused coder to agent choreographer.
