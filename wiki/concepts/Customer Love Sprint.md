## Definition

A two-week initiative [[Jessica Fain]] ran on [[Slack]]'s core product team (after taking it over from her chief-of-staff role): the entire engineering team paused other work to fix small product rough edges/polish issues, with engineers choosing what to work on, the only rule being that each person had to ship something good for users.

## Key Information

- Motivated by executive-level concern that Slack had "lost its mojo" around product craft — Fain frames this as a direct response to leadership's perceived priority, aligning team execution with what leadership actually valued (see [[Politics Vs Influence]]).
- Run with heavy support from PM/design/customer support but engineer-driven choice of what to fix; framed positively as a fun, competitive "judging" event (rather than a dry "bug bash") that executives themselves participated in.
- Concrete output cited: 65 user-facing improvements shipped in the sprint — a legible, leadership-aligned result Fain used to demonstrate the team was executing against what mattered to the company.
