# Emotions and Decision-Making

The principle, articulated by [[Qasar Younis]], that emotions are generally not helpful in making rational business decisions. They act as a filter that distorts the "raw image" of the decision.

## Core Idea

"Emotions are generally not helpful in making rational decisions. They're almost like the opposite of it."

## Key Points
- An emotion is a framework imparted in your brain through life experiences — not optimized for product review decisions
- Qasar's heuristic: the same decision being made by multiple people in the company should get the same result — removing the emotional "filter"
- "Remove that filter, see things as honestly as they possibly can, create a culture that doesn't put any weight on who the idea came from"
- Lenny's practical version: "When you have to make a hard decision, pretend nobody's feelings would be hurt. What would you do? Then figure out how to help people feel okay about it."
- Passion and leadership are often conflated with being "magnanimous or emotional" — Qasar rejects this
- This is connected to the "Midwest roots" of Qasar and his co-founder Peter

## Counterpoint
- Qasar acknowledges emotions may be essential to consciousness and creativity
- "Technical mastery is mastering the complex. Creativity is mastering the simple."
- He admits he may be wrong about AI and art
