## Definition

A growth framework, originated by [[Bangaly Kaba]] while scaling Instagram's growth team past 1 billion users, cited by [[Elena Verna]] (Head of Growth, [[Lovable]]): the biggest low-cost growth opportunity for an established product is converting "adjacent users" — people just outside the current core user base with somewhat similar needs (different geography, slightly different use case) — rather than chasing an entirely new market segment ("next horizon").

## Key Information

- Adjacent users are aware of, and may have already tried, the product, but haven't become successfully retained users because of remaining friction or positioning gaps.
- Verna applies the theory as a lens on the current vibe-coding category: she worries the entire industry (Lovable included) is so focused on constantly re-engaging and retaining early "pioneer" adopters (chasing the [[Product Market Fit Treadmill|latest capability wave]]) that it has little bandwidth left to convert the much larger "latent majority" of adjacent users.
- Risk she names: if pioneers are taken "too far into capabilities" without the latent majority catching up, a category (or brand) can become permanently associated with a narrow use case — e.g., "just for prototyping" or "just for techies" — alienating the larger audience it needs to reach to become a mass-market category.
- She notes [[OpenAI]], given its much larger existing user base, may already be forced to address adjacent-user conversion in a way most other AI companies aren't yet.
