## Definition

Luck, Talent, and Hard Work are the three factors Philip Su identifies as the main contributors to fast career growth. He argues that all three play a role, and that hard work can help you surpass those who are equally talented and equally lucky.

## Key Information

- Philip's framework: "I think there are only three main things that contribute to fast career growth. One is luck. One is talent. One is hard work."
- **Luck**: being at the right place at the right time — e.g., being at Stanford when Sergey and Larry were working there, being picked to join a company
- **Talent**: Philip pushes back against the "everyone can be an astronaut" narrative — "the harsh truth is I am never going to be a great basketball player." Similarly, someone not naturally good at public speaking can improve to passable but won't become Cicero through practice
- **Hard Work**: "if you are willing to outwork everybody who is equally talented and equally lucky, you're just going to get further"
- Philip explicitly says he does not recommend his extreme work hours to everyone, but acknowledges that for a "growth at all costs" person, working more hours gets results
- This framework connects to his broader philosophy: career growth is not purely about working harder, but about the intersection of all three factors
