## Definition

Change management for AI adoption refers to the cultural and organizational work required to get teams to adopt AI tools and new ways of working, beyond simply providing the technology. It is the third and most critical pillar of LinkedIn's full-stack builder model.

## Key Information

- Tomer Cohen emphasizes that providing AI tools is a prerequisite but not sufficient for adoption — most organizations fail by just rolling out agents and expecting adoption.
- Only about 5% of talent (the "cutting edge") has a natural bias for change and will adopt new tools immediately. The vast majority need structured change management.
- Key tactics that worked at LinkedIn:
  - Changing performance evaluations and hiring criteria to include AI agency/fluency.
  - 360-degree evaluations for leaders across functions (PMs rated by designers, etc.).
  - Creating a formal career ladder (full-stack builder title) and training program (APB).
  - Celebrating wins in all-hands, showcasing success stories, highlighting people who transitioned roles.
  - Creating FOMO through limited access and pilot programs.
  - Over-communication of both vision and progress, with leaders holding themselves accountable via KPIs and OKRs.
- Cohen draws parallels to the desktop-to-mobile transformation at LinkedIn, noting that once cultural change picks up momentum, it maintains high velocity.
- Some people do not want to become full-stack builders and prefer specialization — the message is not that everyone must change, but the organization needs fewer specialists.
