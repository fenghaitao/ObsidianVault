## Definition

[[Jason Lemkin]]'s framework for why traditional go-to-market tactics feel broken in the AI era: the individual "plays" (outbound, webinars, podcasts, events) still work exactly as they always have — it's the "playbooks" (the blanket, high-volume, ROI-agnostic way companies historically applied those plays) that have stopped working.

## Key Information

- Lemkin's summary line: "All the plays work. It's the playbooks that are kind of broken in the age of AI." Outbound still works, webinars still work, podcasts still work, events still work.
- The apparent brokenness is a symptom of collapsed ROI, not tactic obsolescence: "growth has decelerated so much that nothing seems to work... it's working, it just works so much worse than 2021." There isn't enough budget or return left in "old school SaaS from 2021" playbooks to justify the same blanket application.
- Contrast case: hyper-growth AI-native companies ([[Vercel]], [[Replit]], [[ElevenLabs]]) still run these same plays (roadshows, podcast appearances) but with much higher ROI, because demand is so high they can afford to be selective about which prospects to even respond to — Lemkin cites [[Bolt.new]] closing a seven-figure deal "stolen" from [[Lovable]] simply because Lovable never called the prospect back.
- Practical implication for GTM books/courses: Lemkin says most sales/GTM books are "playbooks" and should be mined for a few useful ideas, not followed wholesale — "run from the playbooks, embrace the plays" — because he considers most current GTM literature too dated for the AI-era version of these dynamics.
- Connects to [[Everyone In Market At Once]]: rising in-market demand is why some companies experience the same "plays" as suddenly wildly effective while others experience them as dead.
