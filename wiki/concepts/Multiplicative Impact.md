## Definition

Multiplicative Impact is the concept of achieving outsized results by making other engineers or teams more effective, rather than by individual output alone. It is a hallmark of staff-level engineering work.

## Key Information

- Coined/emphasized by Rahul during the panel: "One way to think about getting promoted to these very high levels is to think about a portfolio of bets"
- Rahul described creating a platform where other engineers could plug in their own debug-ability tooling — this created multiplicative impact because many engineers could benefit from the foundation he built
- Zach described it as two directions: Vertical Impact (direct business impact) and Horizontal Impact (enabling the team to move faster)
- The math: "1% for a thousand people, 10% for 100" — that is where you make staff-level impact
- At L9+ (three levels above Staff), engineers make compounding improvements that make the entire company faster
- Zach's Groovy script at Netflix that cut deploy times by 1-7 minutes per deploy, saving 30-60 minutes per engineer per day, is a classic example
- Making other people faster in a large organization scales dramatically — if you have five figures of engineers, a 10% improvement is absurd impact
- A litmus test: if you ask a teammate "what is this person known for?" and they can't tell you, you haven't built enough visibility for your multiplicative impact
