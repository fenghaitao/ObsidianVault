## Definition

"Shots on goal" is [[Max Schoening]]'s (Head of Product, [[Notion]]) term — used internally at Notion — for deliberately increasing the number of parallel explorations/experiments a team attempts, on the premise that AI has made early-stage exploration cheap enough that you can now afford to try many paths at once rather than committing early to one.

## Key Information

- Schoening: "Shots on goal is a thing that we say internally a lot, which is like, great, how do you increase shots on goal? ... it's easier to experiment now, you're increasing the shots on goal."
- Grounded in his "first 10% of every project are now free" observation: it takes almost no effort to build a first version (or "version 0.8") of an idea, so teams "can now afford to say I'm going to send off 10 agents to explore 10 different things and then see if I was right."
- Connects to an older GitHub product-review habit Schoening cites: "demos not memos" — instead of writing a PRD, give people something concrete to react to (a working demo, or at minimum the changelog/blog post a user would read), which built iteration into the process much earlier than a waterfall-style spec-first approach.
- Paired at Notion with [[Incremental Correctness]] (the discipline of converging those many shots toward quality over repeated cycles) and [[Obviously Good]] (the bar the eventual output must clear).
- Schoening notes this is not free of cost/tension: increasing shots on goal generates more raw ideas and prototypes, but Notion still has to do the harder work of consolidating overlapping outputs (e.g., its six automation primitives) into one clean core — otherwise more shots on goal just produces more unreconciled fragments.
