## Definition

The 30-Second Meeting Opener is a tactic for starting meetings with busy executives: spend no more than 30-60 seconds at the beginning providing context — why you're here, what happened last time, what the goals are, and how the meeting will run — then stop talking. Going over 60 seconds risks losing the executive's attention.

## Key Information

- Spend 30-60 seconds at the top of a meeting with an executive to provide context
- Key elements: "We're here to discuss XYZ. Last time we met, we left it off here. The goals of today's meeting are ZYX. Here's how we're going to run this meeting."
- Also ask: "Was there anything else you were hoping to cover today?"
- If you go over 60 seconds, you've lost them — execs are context-switching from numerous urgent matters
- "If 60 seconds flies by, you may think you're talking for 60 seconds. It often ends up being 5 minutes."
- This is especially important because executives have "strobe light" calendars and haven't had time to center your problems
