## Definition

A rule [[Molly Graham]] attributes to [[Mark Zuckerberg]] (applied at both [[Facebook]] and the [[Chan Zuckerberg Initiative]]): when two people of roughly equal power or context reach a genuine impasse, escalating together to a shared manager is not "tattling" — it's the correct, fastest tool for getting unblocked, and should be used as soon as a team is stuck.

## Key Information

- Zuckerberg's diagnosis: people often mentally frame escalation as "A and B disagree, so I go over B's head to C and tell on B" — a failure mode people avoid because it feels like a betrayal or a loss.
- The reframe: real escalation is "we disagree, neither of us has enough power to make this decision, let's go together to someone who does" — going *together*, making the case jointly, rather than going around one another.
- Applies whenever you "lack context or lack power" to resolve something yourself — the manager's job is precisely to unblock these situations, so using them for that is not a failure, it's "what management is for."
- Payoff: escalating early saves the time otherwise lost to two people "bashing heads" back and forth for days or weeks over a decision neither actually has the authority to make.
- Cross-referenced by [[Lenny Rachitsky]] against a similar point made by a different guest, Matt MacInnis, about wanting to be told about blockers immediately rather than have people quietly stay stuck.
