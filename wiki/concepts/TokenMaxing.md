## Definition
Token maxing is the practice of artificially inflating AI token usage to avoid appearing at the bottom of internal leaderboards or missing minimum spend targets at large tech companies. It started as a joke — people enjoying AI tools and building cool stuff — but has turned into a culturally weird phenomenon where engineers game the system to protect their jobs.

## Key Information
- Observed at Meta, Microsoft, and Salesforce
- At Meta: internal leaderboard showed people's token counts; taken down after an article made them look bad, but token maxing continues
- At Microsoft: engineers run autonomous agents to build junk just to increase token count
- At Salesforce: minimum AI spend target per month (~$175); engineers token max at beginning of each month to hit it
- Token count is used as one data point in performance evaluations at Meta — can be "weaponized" (low performer + low tokens = "not even trying")
- Examples of gaming: asking agents to summarize documentation instead of reading it, running autonomous agents to generate meaningless code
- Engineers fear losing high-paying jobs over "something stupid" like low token count
- Gergely Orosz compares it to the old "lines of code" metric — equally stupid, but now happening at top-tier companies
- Root cause: leadership wanting engineers to "use freaking AI" and measuring what they can
