## Definition

[[Jeetu Patel]]'s stack-ranked framework for what a company needs to win: timing, market, team, product, brand, and distribution, in strictly descending order of importance — you need all six, but each one dominates the one below it.

## Key Information

- **1. Timing** — the most important factor and the one you control least; great products built at the wrong time in the right market still lose. Patel's example: Steve Jobs shelved the iPad because the iPhone's timing was better; the iPad only succeeded later, riding the iPhone's success.
- **2. Market** — you must be able to attack a large-enough market incrementally, with the addressable opportunity growing over time.
- **3. Team** — a well-rounded team where members' weaknesses are covered by others' strengths, not just people who like each other. Patel: "if you have a great market, mediocre team, the market pulls you up. If you have a shitty market and a great team, the market drags you down." Market beats team.
- **4. Product** — "the soul of a company," where value actually gets delivered; Patel considers it unethical to sell a mediocre product.
- **5. Brand** — hard to rebuild once lost (a mentor's line to Patel: "don't ever go to a company who's lost their brand mojo"); you can fix a bad product, but a lost brand rarely comes back.
- **6. Distribution** — "just because you build it, they will not come"; you need a scaled mechanism to reach many people, but it's ranked last because the other five determine whether distribution efforts pay off at all.
- Practical use: Patel asks himself whether timing is right before deciding whether to double down on an initiative, distinguishing a genuine [[Mega Trend Vs Hype Cycle|mega trend]] worth committing to from a hype cycle not yet (or never) worth chasing.
- When timing is wrong, the right move isn't to scrap an idea but to "put it on ice" until timing catches up — as Jobs did with the iPad.
