## Definition

[[Cat Wu]]'s term for the hardest calibration problem in AI product management: figuring out the "right amount" of designing for a hypothetical, fully-capable future model versus designing for what the current model can actually do well.

## Key Information

- Building for the "super AGI" model is comparatively easy: a single text box where the model figures out everything itself (tools, integrations, clarifying questions) — an appealingly simple design target.
- The genuinely hard, valuable skill is figuring out, for the *current* model, how to elicit its maximum capability and steer users onto its "golden path," compensating for its specific weaknesses without over-building scaffolding that becomes obsolete the moment the next model ships.
- Because model capability jumps every few months, being "too AGI pilled" (over-designing for a future model) and "not AGI pilled enough" (under-estimating near-term capability) are both live risks — Wu says it's genuinely hard to predict which way to lean more than a few months out.
- Connects to Claude Code's practice of periodically stripping out prompt/UI scaffolding (like the to-do-list crutch) that a newer model no longer needs — see [[Building Blocks Progression]].
