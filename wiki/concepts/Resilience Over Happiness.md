## Definition

[[Dr. Becky Kennedy]]'s parenting-philosophy chapter/thesis, stated as [[Good Inside]]'s guiding principle: optimizing for a child's (or employee's) immediate happiness/comfort produces anxiety and fragility in adulthood, whereas building resilience — tolerance for a wide range of difficult experiences — produces durable happiness later.

## Key Information

- Kennedy's formulation: "optimizing for happiness in childhood is the quickest way to build anxiety and fragility in adulthood... happiness in adulthood actually comes from our ability to manage the widest range of difficult situations. That's what resilience is." Explicitly not about tolerating toxic situations — the referenced difficult experiences are ordinary ones: disappointment, jealousy, anger, feeling less-than.
- Mechanism: kids can only learn to tolerate the feelings their parent (or leader) can tolerate in them; if the adult can't tolerate a child's upset, the child experiences that as confirmation the feeling itself is "unmanageable," which compounds into adulthood as anything less than comfortable/perfect feeling intolerable.
- Applied to work culture: "when we're thinking about a resilient work culture, we want people who can say 'this is hard, and I can do hard things,' not 'this is hard, so someone must be at fault and it should be easier.'"
- Feedback-timing application: comparing relationships to a building, the first months with a new hire lay the foundation; if a manager optimizes only for comfort/happiness early on, the relationship's "foundation" is fragile, so later necessary critical feedback lands as a shock ("I've never gotten a piece of constructive feedback since I started here"). Illustrated via a [[Kim Scott]] / [[Radical Candor]] anecdote about someone fired after nine months of withheld feedback, cited by [[Lenny Rachitsky]].
- Reasonableness caveat: this doesn't mean giving a brand-new hire harsh feedback on day one — but Kennedy argues leaders should examine, from early on, whether the nature of their conversations is building toward a culture where hard truths can be said and heard.
- Closely paired with the [[I Believe You And I Believe In You]] formula as the concrete phrasing for supporting someone through a hard task without either dismissing the difficulty or taking the challenge (and the growth it enables) away from them.
