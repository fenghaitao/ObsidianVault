## Definition

Product taste is the ability to make discerning judgments about design, experience, and craft. Stewart Butterfield argues it can be developed (like becoming a better chef), and that leaning into taste creates a real competitive advantage because most companies don't invest in it.

## Key Information

- **Can taste be learned?** Yes — the word literally comes from experiencing food. Some people have natural advantages, but everyone can practice and get better
- **Competitive advantage**: most people don't have good taste and don't invest in it, so leaning into taste creates a real advantage for your product and company
- **Connection to "tilting your umbrella"**: taste is demonstrated through small courtesies and empathic design choices that create emotional connections
- **Butterfield's view on his own standards**: "I feel like what we have right now is just a giant piece of [__]. It's just terrible and we should be humiliated that we offer this to the public." If you can't see almost limitless opportunities to improve, you shouldn't be designing the product
- **Counterpoint**: some people become obsessed with tiny details beyond the point of diminishing returns — taste must be balanced with utility curves
- **Culture of direct criticism**: building a culture where people are grateful for direct feedback because they genuinely want to improve, like Michael Jordan learning to ski (Ray Dalio's example)
- Lazar's perspective: taste is developed through "exposure time" — deliberately exposing yourself to great design, great products, and great people
- In the AI era, "good enough" is now the baseline. The gap between good enough and world-class has widened dramatically. Everyone can produce good enough with AI.
- "You better start learning how to produce magic cuz otherwise you're just going to end up in a crowd with millions and millions of others."
- Design is emotional decision-making: "We're training these tools to make better technical decisions. I don't think we will train them just yet to make better emotional decisions."
- Fonts are "60% in my mind, maybe even more in how your output's going to look" — an underappreciated aspect of taste in AI-built products
