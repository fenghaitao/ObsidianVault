## Definition

Entropy in organizations is the tendency for systems, teams, and processes to decay toward disorder over time. The only antidote is relentless energy injection from leadership. This concept, adapted from the second law of thermodynamics, is a core framework used by Matt McGinness at Rippling.

## Key Information

- From physics: entropy is the second law of thermodynamics — things tend toward disorder (potholes form, sock drawers get messy, aircraft want to fall apart).
- In organizations: every line of code increases system entropy; teams naturally optimize for local comfort over company outcomes; processes degrade without maintenance.
- The only antidote to entropy is energy — constant, relentless attention from leadership.
- Practical implications: every bug must be escalated publicly; every hiring shortcut must be caught; every process must be enforced at the 99th percentile.
- "Teams will always optimize for local comfort over company outcomes" — not maliciously, but because humans naturally seek comfort.
- Combined with the power law: if entropy drops you 10-20% on the X-axis, the Y-axis (outcomes) collapses entirely, not just by 10-20%.
- "Life itself is a temporary victory against entropy" — the sun provides energy that organizes matter, and we fight entropy until we lose (death).
- Founder CEO intensity is the purest form of energy; each concentric management circle can drop intensity by orders of magnitude — this is dangerous.
