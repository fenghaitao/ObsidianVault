## Definition

[[Marc Andreessen]]'s argument that human intelligence is capped by biology (~110-160 IQ, with 160 being Einstein/Feynman-level), while AI has no equivalent ceiling and will likely scale well past it (180, 200, 250+), making "human-equivalent" AGI a transient milestone rather than an endpoint.

## Key Information

- Rough human IQ calibration Andreessen cites: ~105 for a good small-business accountant, ~110 for a good line manager, ~130 for a good lawyer, ~140 for a top research scientist/doctor/bestselling author, ~160 for the rare individuals (Einstein, Feynman) who develop genuinely new physics.
- Current frontier AI models are already testing in something like the 130-140 IQ-equivalent range on relevant measures, per Andreessen, on a trajectory toward 160 and beyond relatively quickly.
- His framing: there's no reason to assume 160 is a meaningful ceiling for a machine the way it functionally is for biological brains — a world with more/higher-IQ "Einsteins" (biological or artificial) is unambiguously better, in his view, just as a world with more capable AI coders/doctors/lawyers than the best humans is a good outcome, not a threatening one.
- Distinguished from the "cosmic"/singularity definition of AGI (a self-improving intelligence explosion where human judgment stops mattering) — Andreessen doubts we're near that scenario, and treats "AI matches/exceeds human experts" as a much more mundane, already-in-progress milestone by comparison. See [[Determinate Vs Indeterminate Optimism]] for his broader epistemic caution about bold predictions.
