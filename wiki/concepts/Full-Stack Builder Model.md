## Definition

The Full-Stack Builder Model is [[LinkedIn]]'s restructuring of product development, architected by outgoing CPO [[Tomer Cohen]]: rather than siloed PM/design/engineering functions each owning a narrow slice of the product-development lifecycle, individual "full-stack builders" (from any function, including business development) take an idea from research through launch themselves, working in small, fluid, cross-functional pods rather than large teams.

## Key Information

- **Motivating diagnosis**: at scale, every step of the traditional product-development lifecycle (research a problem, spec it, design it, code it, launch it, iterate) gets subdivided into many individually-justified sub-steps (e.g., 10-15 research sources, multiple types of review — design, privacy, security), each of which then requires dedicated, increasingly specialized people to execute — producing compounding "process complexity" and "organizational complexity" that can turn shipping a small feature into a multi-team, multi-codebase, multi-sprint effort.
- **What builders keep doing** (deliberately not automated): five traits — vision (a compelling stance on the future), empathy (deep understanding of unmet need), communication (aligning and rallying others around an idea), creativity (possibilities beyond the obvious — Cohen doesn't think AI is there yet), and, most emphasized, judgment (high-quality decision-making in ambiguous, complex situations). Everything else is explicitly targeted for automation.
- **Pod structure**: modeled explicitly on a Navy SEALs analogy — broadly cross-trained operators, organized around a specific mission rather than a fixed functional role, assembled into small nimble pods and reassembled quickly as priorities shift (e.g., quarterly).
- **Three components of implementation**: (1) platform — re-architecting the core codebase/design systems so AI tooling can reason over them (no third-party tool worked well off-the-shelf against LinkedIn's stack without heavy customization); (2) tools/agents — a suite of custom internal agents (trust, growth, research, analyst, coding, maintenance/QA) trained on LinkedIn-specific proprietary context, not general world knowledge; (3) culture — explicitly identified as the hardest and most important piece, requiring incentive/performance-review changes, visible pilot wins, and patience, since good-enough tooling alone doesn't guarantee adoption.
- **New career track**: replaces LinkedIn's traditional APM (Associate Product Manager) program with an "Associate Full-Stack Builder" program teaching coding, design, and product management together from day one; a formal "full-stack builder" title and career ladder now exists internally.
- **Counterintuitive adoption finding**: LinkedIn's top-performing talent adopted the new workflow fastest and most enthusiastically — evidence, per Cohen, that AI tools tend to amplify already-strong builders rather than uniformly lifting weaker performers, which is part of why deliberate change management (not just tool access) is essential for broader rollout.
- **Explicitly not mandatory for everyone**: Cohen states some people genuinely prefer specialization and that's fine — the goal is not 100% full-stack-builder conversion, but reducing how many narrowly specialized roles are needed overall.
- **Rollout philosophy**: don't wait for a formal reorg or official program launch to start working in a full-stack-builder mindset ("prove you're a full-stack builder in mindset before anything else"); treat the transformation as continuous, ongoing progress rather than a fixed end state to reach.
