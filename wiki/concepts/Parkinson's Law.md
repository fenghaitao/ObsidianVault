## Definition

Parkinson's Law states that "work expands to fill the time available for its completion." Originated from a 1956 article in The Economist by C. Northcote Parkinson, it also describes how organizations naturally accumulate administrators over time regardless of the actual work to be done.

## Key Information

- **Original maxim**: work expands to fill the time available for its completion
- **Corollary**: if you want something done, give it to a busy person; the inverse is also true — if you're not busy, basic things take a very long time
- **Original example**: an elderly woman with all the time in the world takes much longer to write and post a letter than a busy executive
- **Organizational manifestation**: Parkinson analyzed the Royal Navy and found that while the number of capital ships and sailors declined, the number of administrators grew steadily
- **Same pattern** appears in universities (administrators vs. students/faculty), government, and any large organization
- **Root cause in companies**: the overwhelming majority of people you hire want to hire more people who report to them — not because they're evil or stupid, but because number of direct reports correlates with career trajectory, compensation, and authority
- **Example**: 27-year-old product managers at Slack immediately wanted to hire someone — "that person would do the product management, and I would do strategy"
- No head of engineering, sales, or any department ever comes back and says "we can hire fewer people next year"
