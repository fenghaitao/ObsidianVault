## Definition

The philosophy that when you negotiate for higher compensation, you're not just helping yourself — you're raising the precedent for everyone who comes after you. Jacob Warwick frames this as a moral imperative, especially for women and underrepresented groups: if you don't push, the next person in line will have a lower ceiling too.

## Key Information

- Jacob: "Push back because a rising tide raises all ships."
- "If companies want to rely on precedent, have the audacity to raise the precedent because the next people in line will do that."
- The principle applies particularly to women and underrepresented groups:
  - "If you want women after you to have better opportunities than you have, you have to have the guts to do that."
  - Jacob's response to a woman who asked if she should negotiate for 84% (the gender pay gap): "Could you look your little girl in the eyes and say, 'Sweetheart, just get 80% out there'?"
- Example: A CMO negotiated severance protections for the entire executive team by framing it as "wouldn't it make us look good to proactively give everyone six months?" — she became the hero who got everyone severance protections
- Example: A CPO and CEO collaborated to set a severance precedent against private equity, knowing the CEO would get 2-3x whatever precedent they established
- "There are folks that aren't going to take that challenge, and that's okay. Let's all push for those to help make it easier for everyone."
