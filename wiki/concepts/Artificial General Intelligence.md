## Definition

Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) is a general intelligence that acts like a human. Judea Pearl argues reaching it requires more than scaling LLMs: it needs causal reasoning plus an innate drive for control over the environment.

## Key Information

- Pearl is skeptical that the LLM technique alone will reach AGI; LLMs summarize human-interpreted knowledge on the internet rather than investigating raw data.
- The path he favors combines LLM-level learning (going from finite samples to properties of distributions, level one of the ladder) with the calculus of intervention and counterfactuals — "causal AI."
- Autonomy requires curiosity: babies are born restless, craving control over their environment until they can predict which toys make noise; humans (unlike reward-driven monkeys, who ignore the world without a banana) keep exploring.
- The danger of programming such curiosity is that a robot would treat humans as part of its environment to control, blackmail, or use.
- Test for real progress: expose the system to raw data (patients, cancer, smoking) and ask it to be an automated scientist, rather than letting it read internet articles.
