## Definition
The design concept is the ephemeral, shared understanding of what is being built that exists between collaborators. From Frederick P. Brooks' "The Design of Design," it is not an asset or a document — it is the invisible theory of the system that floats between the people designing it together.

## Key Information
- From Frederick P. Brooks' "The Design of Design"
- The design concept is not something you can put in a markdown file — it's the invisible shared theory of what you're building
- When multiple people (or a person and AI) design together, the design concept exists between them
- Matt Pocock identified that the core problem with AI coding is that the human and AI don't share a design concept
- The "Grill Me" skill was created to bridge this gap — making the AI interview the user until a shared design concept is reached
- The design tree (also from Brooks) is the process of walking down each branch of design decisions, resolving dependencies between decisions one by one
- Default AI plan modes are too eager to create assets (plans, code) before reaching shared understanding
- Reaching a shared design concept first produces better outcomes than immediately generating plans
