## Definition
Saying No is the product discipline of rejecting feature requests to maintain product coherence and quality. Steve Jobs famously said great products come from "saying no to 999 things and yes to one thing." In the age of AI, where shipping every feature request is trivially easy, saying no becomes the critical skill that separates tasteful products from convoluted ones.

## Key Information
- Steve Jobs' principle: "Great products come out of saying no to 999 things and yes to one thing"
- Tuomas Artman argues this is more relevant than ever with AI: the pendulum has swung too far toward shipping everything
- AI makes it trivially easy to ship every feature request, but doing so leads to convoluted, confusing software
- Linear receives tons of feature requests but "usually never ships them as such"
- Instead, Linear talks to customers, groups requests, finds root causes, and designs a single perfect solution
- Saying no requires understanding the actual customer problem behind the surface request
- AI can help summarize and group requests but cannot exercise the judgment of what to say no to
- The discipline of saying no is what prevents "feature factory" dynamics where quantity of features replaces quality of experience
