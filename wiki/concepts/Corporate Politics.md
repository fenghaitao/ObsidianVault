## Definition

Corporate politics is the set of behind-the-scenes behaviors — empire building, reorgs, promotion queues, and relationship-driven decisions — that determine career outcomes in organizations because impact is subjective and hard to measure, while proxies like headcount are easy.

## Key Information

- Politics exists because it is rewarded: "counting people is the easiest thing to do," so leaders judge readiness by measurable proxies rather than subjective impact.
- Influence and politics use identical skills; the difference is motive (working for the org vs purely self-serving vs negotiable in the middle).
- "The squeaky wheel gets the grease": quiet, dutiful people can end up behind because managers focus on retaining the vocal person who is at risk of leaving.
- Managers are either an umbrella (shielding the team from pressure) or a funnel (dumping pressure downward); you change the balance of safety to get them to push back.
- To minimize your political exposure: choose a leader who shares your disdain, work on the hardest problems where expertise matters, and build leverage (an essential skill no one else has).
- Growth changes the game: fast-growing companies are more positive-sum (Amazon grew ~100x in Ethan's 15 years), while stagnant orgs breed zero-sum cannibalism.
- "Very few humans wake up and say 'I'm going to be evil today'" — people construct self-narratives; understanding the other party's story helps you defend or redirect.
