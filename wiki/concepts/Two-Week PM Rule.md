## Definition

[[Amol]]'s heuristic for when a project needs dedicated PM ownership at [[Anthropic]]'s growth org: if a project is estimated at two engineering-weeks or less, the engineer is expected to act as their own mini-PM (including cross-functional coordination with legal, security, etc.), with the PM only advising; above two weeks, a PM remains squarely accountable, delegating execution to engineering but still owning the outcome.

## Key Information

- Adopted out of necessity — PM and design capacity is stretched thin relative to engineering's AI-accelerated output (Amol estimates engineering throughput has grown roughly 2-3x per engineer under Claude Code, without proportional PM/design headcount growth).
- Explicitly not a rigid rule: Amol notes exceptions where a technically small (sub-two-week) project is unusually controversial and still warrants PM ownership — "use your head."
- Elevates the value of "product-minded engineers" who can competently self-PM — Amol treats this skill combination as an "order of magnitude" more valuable under this model, paralleling the general trend (also discussed by [[Nikhyl Singhal]] and [[Keith Rabois]]) of interdisciplinary skill combinations becoming disproportionately valuable as AI compresses execution time.
- Lenny draws an analogy to the classic PM litmus test of whether a team can function smoothly for a few weeks without its PM present — the two-week threshold roughly matches that same intuition.
