## Definition

The Two-Week PM Rule is an operating principle used by Amol Avaisary's growth team at Anthropic: if a project requires 2 weeks of engineering time or less, the engineer is on the hook to effectively be the PM (talking to security, legal, cross-functional stakeholders). The PM is in an advisory capacity. If a project requires more than 2 engineering weeks, the PM defaults to driving it.

## Key Information

- Under 2 weeks: engineer is the PM — talks to security, legal, cross-functional stakeholders. PM is advisory only, steps in if things go wildly off track
- Over 2 weeks: PM is squarely accountable, though they delegate more to engineering
- Not a "fully clean cut" — use judgment. A 1-week project that is extremely controversial should still be PM-driven
- This approach emerged because PMs and design are "absolutely squeezed" — engineers are getting 2-3x productivity from Claude Code, but PMs and designers haven't gotten the same leverage
- A team of 5 engineers + 1 PM + 1 designer now effectively has 15-20 engineers' worth of output, straining PM and design
- "Product-minded engineers" — their value goes up "an order of magnitude" under this model
- Amol expects more companies will adopt similar approaches: "deputize the engineers to be mini PMs"
- Counterintuitive implication: as engineers get more productive, you may actually need to hire MORE PMs, not fewer
