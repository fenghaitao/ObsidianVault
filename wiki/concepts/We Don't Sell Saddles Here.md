## Definition

"We Don't Sell Saddles Here" is [[Stewart Butterfield]]'s famous internal memo (written when [[Slack]] was roughly 8 people, before public launch, and later republished verbatim) arguing that a product must be sold on the outcome/experience it enables — not on its literal feature.

## Key Information

- Core metaphor: instead of marketing "look at this amazing saddle we built," communicate "here's the incredible experience of horseback riding you can have" — the customer then concludes on their own, "I need a saddle to do that."
- Grounded in the classic marketing book *[[Positioning (book)|Positioning]]*: it's nearly impossible to implant a genuinely new idea in someone's head from scratch; it's far easier to combine ideas people already hold ("it's Jaws meets Star Wars," "Uber for pets"). If your product is meaningfully different from existing alternatives, you're not just building a product — you're building the surrounding mental category/market for it too.
- Related classic marketing parallels Butterfield cites: Harley-Davidson doesn't sell engines and leather quality, it sells "the open road and freedom"; Lululemon doesn't sell yoga pants, it sells aspiration and self-improvement; the old "instill a yearning for the sea" line (rather than trying to convince people to build a ship).
- Written specifically to instill shared language and alignment inside a very small pre-launch team early enough that it could survive as the company scaled — Butterfield notes losing access to Slack's own message history after leaving the company is why he still has this memo verbatim (it was written as an email, not in Slack itself).
