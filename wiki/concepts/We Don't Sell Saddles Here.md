## Definition

"We Don't Sell Saddles Here" is a legendary internal memo written by Stewart Butterfield at Slack (before the product launched). It argues that you must create the market and communicate the outcome, not just build the product — you're responsible for both creating the product and creating the desire for it.

## Key Information

- Written when Slack was still 8-10 people, before the product launched, during private beta
- Was originally an internal memo, posted verbatim as a Medium post — not a word was changed
- The purpose was to instill these ideas as early as possible and create alignment within the small team so it could survive as the company grew
- **Core metaphor**: instead of selling saddles (the product feature), sell horseback riding (the outcome and experience). If people want to go horseback riding, they'll figure out they need a saddle
- **Related metaphors**: Harley-Davidson sells freedom and the open road, not motorcycles; Lululemon sells health and aspiration, not just yoga pants; "don't instill a desire to build a ship, instill a yearning for the sea"
- **Connection to positioning**: it's almost impossible to create a net new idea in someone's head. It's much easier to combine existing ideas ("Jaws meets Star Wars," "Uber for pets")
- If your product is different in any significant way from alternatives, you're not just creating the product — you're creating the market. They're one and the same
