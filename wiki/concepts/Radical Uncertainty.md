## Definition

[[Benedict Evans]]'s stated epistemic stance toward AI: the shift is undeniably as big as the internet or mobile, but almost nothing about how it will specifically play out — which jobs get automated, who captures the value, which products win — is yet knowable, so the right posture is to "presume radical uncertainty" rather than confidently predict outcomes in either direction.

## Key Information

- Evans frames the current moment as the rough equivalent of "1997" for the internet: the scale of the shift is not in question, but specifics are.
- Positioned explicitly against both AI-doomerism and head-in-the-sand dismissal — instead, the practical response is to dive into the tools and learn what they can actually do today.
- Applied throughout his "AI is eating the world" presentation to capital (do labs have pricing power?), deployment (what happens to software/services?), and change ([[Task Vs Job]] — which jobs actually get automated).
