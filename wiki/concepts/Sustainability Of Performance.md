## Definition

Sustainability Of Performance is the principle that promotions are based not just on delivering impact, but on demonstrating that the impact can be sustained at normal working hours. Companies resist promoting people who achieve results through extreme hours because it doesn't prove they have the behaviors and leadership skills of the higher level.

## Key Information

- Zach called this "one of the most important lessons I learned at Facebook" — hours worked does not necessarily influence promotion ability
- Working more hours can help address skill gaps (covering a gap by working more), but it does not instill confidence and leadership that someone is promotion-worthy
- At Facebook, Zach received two consecutive "Greatly Exceeds" ratings as an IC4 but was denied E5 because management questioned whether he could sustain the performance at normal hours
- The manager's exact concern: "You are delivering as an E5, but we believe you would not sustainably deliver like that"
- This was frustrating for Zach because he felt the harder you work, the more you should be rewarded, but the company's perspective is different — they're looking for behaviors, not just hours
- The concept is a key reason why job hopping works: new companies don't see the hours worked, they only see the impact story
- At Airbnb, Zach proved the point from the opposite direction: he received an "Exceeds" rating at the staff level while working approximately 40 hours/week, showing that behavioral shifts, not more hours, enable higher-level performance
- Zach's advice to his younger self: develop soft skills (selling vision, deciding which problems to solve, working through others) rather than just grinding more hours
