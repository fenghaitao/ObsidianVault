## Definition

A core principle of etiquette: what matters in social interactions is often the signaling of effort as much as the actual execution. People notice when you're trying, and that effort goes a long way toward building trust — even if you don't execute perfectly.

## Key Information

- Sam Lessin: "One of the most important things about all this stuff is what matters in some ways is the signaling of the effort as much as the actual thing."
- Example with eye contact: "If you have trouble with this but you're really trying, that goes a long way versus just being like 'whatever.'"
- The goal is to show you're aware of the social context and making an effort to fit in
- "There should be this deep well of respect for anyone who's helping you" — making the effort to thank EAs, ask where to put coffee cups, etc.
- In dress: "Look like you cared, look like you made some effort"
- The opposite: showing up in a misfitting expensive item signals either lack of awareness or that you're trying to impress with money rather than effort
