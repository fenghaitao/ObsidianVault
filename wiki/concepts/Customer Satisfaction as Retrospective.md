## Definition
Customer Satisfaction as Retrospective is the practice of replacing formal sprint retrospectives with statistically significant customer satisfaction surveys as the primary feedback mechanism for engineering process improvement. Instead of discussing what went well or poorly in a meeting, the team relies on quantitative customer sentiment and immediate flagging of issues.

## Key Information
- **Origin**: Presented by [[MikeSpitz]] as part of the [[PostEngineer Engineering Org]] model at [[PFF]]
- **Replaces**: Traditional sprint retrospectives where teams discuss process improvements at the end of each sprint
- **Primary Metric**: Statistically significant customer satisfaction surveys — PFF scored 8.6/10 post-AI (up from 7–7.5 before AI)
- **Complementary Signals**: Deployment frequency, other development metrics
- **Issue Flagging**: Engineers are asked to flag issues immediately when they occur rather than waiting until the end of a sprint — prevents feedback from feeling stale or unheard
- **Rationale**: If customers are happy, the process is working; if scores drop, investigate. The retrospective becomes continuous and data-driven rather than periodic and anecdotal.
- **Controversial Aspect**: Mike Spitz acknowledged this may be controversial, as retrospectives are a deeply ingrained Agile practice
