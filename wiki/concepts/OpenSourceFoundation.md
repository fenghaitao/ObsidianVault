## Definition
An open source foundation is a vendor-neutral legal entity that governs an open-source project, ensuring no single company controls its direction. It enables hiring full-time maintainers, accepting donations, and providing legal protection while keeping the project truly community-owned.

## Key Information
- The Open Claw Foundation was created by Peter Steinberger, inspired by what Ghosty did with their governance model
- Key principle: for OpenClaw to be successful, it "cannot be under one company" — hence the foundation as "Switzerland"
- Peter described running the foundation as "like running a company on hard mode" — all the responsibilities but with volunteers you can't directly direct
- Almost complete as of April 2026; delayed by "the American banking system which is a little bit slow and very confused when you're not American"
- Will enable hiring full-time people to "keep up the pace, improve the quality, and free up some of my time that I can work on cool stuff again"
- Peter deliberately limits any single company's involvement to avoid perception of takeover — "I could get a lot more people from OpenAI to help with the project, but that would just make a picture that they could have taken over the project"
- Contributors span multiple companies: Nvidia, Microsoft, Red Hat, Telegram, Salesforce, Tencent, ByteDance, Alibaba, MiniMax, Kimi
- One goal is improving the "bus factor" — who does commits, reducing reliance on any single person
- OpenAI supports the foundation model: "they very much understand that in order for that project to be successful, it cannot be under one company"
