## Definition

"Don't Box the Model In" is a product design principle for AI: give the model tools and a goal and let it figure out the rest, rather than imposing rigid workflows or scaffolding. Boris's corollary to the Bitter Lesson.

## Key Information

- Boris: "Don't try to box the model in. I think a lot of people's instinct when they build on the model is they try to make it behave a very particular way"
- Anti-pattern: layering strict workflows (step 1, step 2, step 3) with fancy orchestrators
- "Almost always you get better results if you just give the model tools, you give it a goal, and you let it figure it out"
- A year ago you needed scaffolding, but now you don't
- The scaffolding approach: "maybe scaffolding can improve performance maybe 10-20% something like this. But often these gains just get wiped out with the next model"
- "It's almost better to just wait for the next one"
- Connected to the Bitter Lesson: bet on the more general model, not on specific scaffolding
- Boris's principle: "Ask not what the model can do for you" — think about how to give the model tools, don't over-curate, don't give context upfront (give tools to get context)
