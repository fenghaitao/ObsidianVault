## Definition

Jobs to be done (JTBD), the Clayton Christensen-originated framework for understanding what customers are really "hiring" a product to accomplish, is used by [[Max Schoening]] (Head of Product, [[Notion]]) less as a rigorous methodology and more as a discipline for staying honest about whether a team would actually want/use what it just built.

## Key Information

- Schoening is candid about not engaging deeply with the original Christensen theory: "I bet that if I reread all of the Clayton Christensen stuff I would also not identify super strongly with it."
- His practical use of the framework: "have you thought holistically about what the user wants to hire your product for, and are you honest about what the user wants versus what you want the user to want."
- Names a common organizational failure mode JTBD helps counteract: in larger organizations, people reviewing their own product often "turn off the brain" as a user and instead think as an employee who made the thing — losing the ability to honestly judge whether they'd hire it themselves.
- Notes the framework is a long-running point of debate on [[Lenny's Podcast]], citing a guest referred to as "Shiram" (name as transcribed) who is described as strongly anti-JTBD.
- Concrete recent example Schoening gives (from an unnamed upcoming Notion feature launch): watching a team's landing-page writing style shift into inflated "marketing speak" as soon as they started describing the feature, and using a JTBD-style gut check — "pretend you're standing in front of a whiteboard explaining this to a friend... are you telling me that [the marketing copy] and [the actual designed thing] are the same thing?" — to catch the drift.

- At [[Snapchat]], [[Evan Spiegel]] uses JTBD in a more literal, operational sense: listing simple jobs to be done for Snapchatters (download the app, add close friends, use lenses) and for advertisers (join the ad platform, configure a campaign), to decide where AI agents can help and to track progress against business outcomes for each job.
