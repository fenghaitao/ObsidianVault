## Definition

Hiring Committee is the consensus-driven body of engineers and engineering managers inside big-tech companies (like Meta) that reviews candidate packets to make the hire/no-hire decision and the leveling decision, before passing the result to a higher committee for a "gut check" on the company-wide bar.

## Key Information

- At Meta, the committee is built of influential engineers and engineering managers, recruited by a hiring committee chair.
- For a senior packet, the first thing members do is go to the behavioral interview to understand the candidate's past scope, influence, insight, communication, and organizational level.
- The committee can recommend hire at a level, add follow-up interviews (e.g., when a behavioral interviewer didn't acquire good signal), or pass on the candidate.
- After the committee, the decision typically goes to a group of two or three engineering directors who operate mostly by consensus but sometimes vote.
- The committee is first involved early: for a border phone screen it may decide whether to advance to on-site, and it may be consulted to choose appropriate system-design interviews for specialized mobile (e.g., low-level C++) candidates.
- Referrals that put a senior person's reputation on the line can meaningfully help bubble candidates — a VP/director who worked directly with the candidate can push a borderline reject into a follow-up, but not override a unanimous no.
