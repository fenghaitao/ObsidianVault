## Definition
Competition Through Quality is the strategic insight that when multiple products reach feature and price parity, quality becomes the slow but inevitable competitive differentiator. Unlike features or pricing, quality degradation is gradual and invisible to A/B tests — it erodes user trust over years until users quietly migrate to the higher-quality alternative.

## Key Information
- Tuomas Artman's theory, drawn from his Uber experience: when Uber Pool and Lyft Pool had the same features and price, users gradually migrated to the higher-quality app
- Quality doesn't affect revenue until it does — there's no A/B test that can measure the ROI of quality investment
- The danger: building a low-quality product "opens you up to be slowly overtaken by the competition"
- At Uber, revenue was the golden metric everyone optimized for. Quality was hard to measure and easy to ignore
- The migration happens gradually: users might try the competitor once a year, notice the experience feels better, and eventually switch
- This is why Linear invests heavily in quality despite AI making it easy to ship features quickly
- In an AI world where everyone can ship features fast, quality becomes the remaining differentiator
- The early Uber had engineers who cared deeply about quality (e.g., a PR rejected for being "off by two pixels"), but this culture eroded as the team grew and incentives shifted to revenue
