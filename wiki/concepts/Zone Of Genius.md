## Definition

[[Rachel Lockett]]'s framework for burnout prevention and career design: people have significantly more energy when operating in their natural gifts/strengths, and the aspirational target is spending roughly 80% of work time in that "zone of genius."

## Key Information

- **Diagnosis of burnout**: Lockett observed, coaching top executives at [[Stripe]], that even highly capable, deeply committed leaders become exhausted over years of pushing — often because they're spending large amounts of time outside their natural strengths, which acts as a "slow leak" in their energy tank rather than a single dramatic cause.
- **Diagnostic exercises**: (1) for two weeks, write down each night the 5 things that gave you the most energy that day and the 5 that depleted it most, then look for patterns; (2) ask 5-10 people who know you well what they see as your core strengths/gifts, especially if you don't already have a clear sense of them; (3) review your calendar for the last month and note which meetings/activities you dread versus look forward to, and why.
- **Design around the answer, don't just endure it**: once you know your gifts, you can restructure your role — tell your manager directly what energizes you and ask to shift toward it (your manager's job is to help you perform in the role you were hired for; navigating your own career trajectory is your job, not theirs); hire or partner with people who complement your gaps (a COO for a visionary CEO who dislikes people-management, a chief of staff, etc.); as a founder, you have the most latitude to design team structure around your own and others' strengths.
- **Distinguished from simply avoiding hard work**: Lockett is explicit that this isn't license to avoid all depleting tasks — some logistics and unavoidable friction remain even in an 80%-gifts life. It's about deliberately not spending the *majority* of your time fighting your own nature.
- **Concrete examples cited**: [[Rahul Vohra]] (Superhuman) hired a president to take on operational execution he wasn't suited for; [[Dharmesh Shah]] (HubSpot co-founder) made a rule from day one to never have direct reports.
- **Lockett's own origin story**: as a product manager at a company called Remind, colleague [[Zach Abrams]] told her he'd watched her motivate and align a skeptical engineering team far more effectively than he could, and that her actual zone of genius was people, not product strategy — a comment she initially resisted but ultimately acted on, leaving product management to become an executive coach.
- Practical starting point for those who can't redesign their whole role immediately: start small — skip optional, draining commitments; build in short recovery breaks (a walk) between depleting obligations; protect small daily rituals (e.g., reading before bed) that restore energy.
