## Definition

A disarming question Jessica Fain recommends using when an executive says something that contradicts your data or domain expertise. It shows genuine curiosity about their reasoning, co-creates with them, and helps unpack the deeper context behind their opinion.

## Key Information

- **The phrase:** "That's so interesting. What led you to believe that?"
- This question is grounded in actual curiosity — you're genuinely interested in what's behind their statement.
- "Execs are so good at seeming certain. They say things in an authoritative way with confidence because so much of their work is having to make ultra-fast decisions with little information."
- By asking this question, you co-create with the person who offered the opinion. You're saying: "I'm interested in what led you to that belief, your experience."
- Underlying reasons could be: a meeting they had last week, board pressure, customer feedback, past experience.
- "If this is someone who is also a learner, who also has a growth mindset, then if you are able to help them unpack why they believe something and then respond with your own domain expertise, you are able to get to a better solution together."
- Jessica's colleague does this super well: "He'll hear something that flies in the face of the data he's seeing, and he'll say, 'That's so interesting. What led you to believe that?'"
