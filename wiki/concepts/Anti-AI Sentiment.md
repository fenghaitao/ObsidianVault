## Definition

[[Benedict Evans]]'s framing of the public backlash against AI as a "big fuzzy mess" that mixes legitimate grievances with objectively false claims, rather than a single coherent position.

## Key Information

- Real/legitimate components: local electricity price effects from data centers, genuine career anxiety, and culture-war fights over AI-generated art and "AI slop."
- Objectively false components: e.g. the claim that US data-center water consumption is a major resource drain — Evans cites a Lawrence Livermore Lab study measuring it at roughly 0.017% of total US water use.
- New, genuinely serious harm categories also exist and deserve real concern rather than dismissal: AI-generated deepfake pornography of minors, and the [[Fujitsu]]/UK Post Office Horizon scandal as a precedent for the dangers of blindly trusting buggy automated systems.
- Evans compares this backlash to the earlier, similarly mixed public reaction against social media — some of it well-founded, some not.
- His practical advice for individuals: don't retreat into denial or online "moral superiority" about AI — dive into the tools, learn what they can actually do today, and use that to become a stronger hire.
