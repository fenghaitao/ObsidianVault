## Definition

Friction vs. comprehension is a product design framework arguing that the primary challenge for most products is not friction (making things too hard to do) but comprehension (users not understanding what the product is, what they're supposed to do next, or what features are available).

## Key Information

- **Core argument**: "remove friction" became an unquestioned assumption in product design, but for most products, the real challenge is comprehension
- **When friction matters**: cases where user intent is high and specific — e.g., checkout flows, user registration, authentication. Apple Pay and Shop Pay significantly increase purchase completion because they reduce friction
- **When comprehension matters**: cases where user intent is low and non-specific — e.g., someone visiting slack.com for the first time. They have the minimum possible intent and barely understand what the product is
- **Intent spectrum**: Taylor Swift ticket buyers have 100% intent and perfect comprehension (Ticketmaster's friction doesn't matter). Someone clicking an Instagram ad for a t-shirt has ~70% intent. Someone visiting slack.com after hearing about it months ago has ~0.1% above the threshold
- **Two types of comprehension needed**: (1) what is this thing? (2) what am I supposed to do next?
- **The trap**: "I want to get people to the sign-up form as quickly as possible" — but if they don't know what they're signing up for, they'll back out
- **Most unique product features**: for 70-80% of a product's design, the challenge is comprehension, not friction. Users rarely discover capabilities on their own
- **Shopify example**: first-time store openers don't know reports exist, what kinds of reports are available, or how to customize them
