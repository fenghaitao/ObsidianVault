## Definition

A Sales Development Representative (SDR) is a sales role responsible for generating pipeline — qualifying prospective customers to the point where it's worth investing time in a full sales process. SDRs come in two types: inbound (qualifying website leads) and outbound (proactively targeting companies with product-market fit).

## Key Information

- SDRs are "pipeline generation" — they get prospects to a point where an Account Executive (AE) should run a sales process.
- Inbound SDRs: Handle leads from website contact forms, first call to qualify.
- Outbound SDRs: Target specific parts of the market where you believe you have product-market fit, drumming up interest from people who haven't raised their hand.
- Jeanne DeWitt Grosser's view: "No one graduated from college and was like, 'Yes, I just went to college for four years to become an SDR.'" The role was often a forced starting point — many SDRs could have gone straight into outbound or SMB closing roles.
- AI is well-suited to replace much of SDR work: the average SDR wasn't doing overly sophisticated research, and the workflow is relatively deterministic and replicable.
- At Vercel, 10 inbound SDRs were replaced by one human QA reviewer + an AI agent (built by a GTM engineer), with the other 9 shifted to higher-value outbound work.
- The last SDR work to be automated: deep enterprise prospecting with multiple organizational layers, business line selection, and triangulation.
- Traditional SDR efficiency: salespeople spend only 30-40% of time actually talking to customers (consistent for 20 years); AI agents aim to flip this to 70%.
- Jason Lemkin's prediction: the classic email-based SDR (junior person hired out of college to send emails) will be "90% displaced by AI next year" — mostly extinct within 12 months.
- BDRs (folks who qualify inbound leads from "Contact Me" forms) should be "extinct next year" — there is no reason to wait 1-2 days for a human to qualify a lead when AI can do it instantly.
- The argument: AI qualification is better for the customer — "it doesn't feel good to be qualified" by a human, and AI can do it without the prospect even knowing.
- Lemkin's SaaStr replaced all SDRs and BDRs with AI agents (Artisan for outbound, Qualified for inbound), keeping only 1.2 humans (one AE + part-time chief AI officer).
- The future of the SDR role: a $250K/year professional managing 10 AI agents rather than 10 people — more valuable, better paid, but requiring AI orchestration skills.
- The "people person" defense is dying: "if you can close on a text message, AI can close it" — AI can be "people people" too.
- The real threat is not layoffs but backfill: when SDRs quit, they're replaced by AI agents rather than new humans.
- SDRs who knock on doors (field sales) are not being displaced — only the email-based cadence SDRs are at risk.
- Cold calling is a specialized skill that fewer startups have than people think — Rippling had to deliberately hire cold calling expertise later in its growth.
