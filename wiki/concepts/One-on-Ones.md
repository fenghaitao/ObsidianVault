## Definition

One-on-Ones are the recurring individual meetings between a manager (or mentor) and a report, and the practice of using that time well. Fiona Fung advises reserving them for live conversation rather than status reporting.

## Key Information

- Fiona's advice to individual contributors: move status reporting to an asynchronous form (e.g., Slack), or a living one-on-one doc that both people edit throughout the week
- Use the actual one-on-one time for conversations that benefit from being live — learning about something new, digging deeper into current work, or product discussions with your manager
- She tries hard to never cancel a one-on-one — they "are really important" to her
- As a manager, she asks new reports: "what do you look for in a manager partnership? What's worked well, what hasn't worked well?" and "what's important to you / what's motivating to you and why?"
- A report once let her cancel and reschedule a one-on-one at the last minute so she could FaceTime her grandmother — an act of kindness that had outsized impact on her
