## Definition

[[Eric Ries]]'s framework (the "Todd Park rule," which [[Todd Park]] learned from [[Howard Schultz]]) for how mission-aligned behavior is trained into employees as ordinary deposits into a shared "culture bank," rather than treated as exceptional individual heroism.

## Key Information

- The rule: principled sacrifices are "deposits" into an organization's trust; greedy or self-interested shortcuts are "withdrawals." The discipline is to only ever make deposits and never *intentionally* make a withdrawal (accidental mistakes happen, but should never be intentional).
- Illustrated by [[H-E-B]]: during an ice storm power outage, a store manager let customers take groceries home for free because the point-of-sale system was down. Ries argues this wasn't isolated individual courage but the product of deliberate training — H-E-B teaches employees that doing the right thing in such moments is "making a deposit in the culture bank."
- Ries reports everyone who first hears the rule says it sounds impossible, but frames it as genuinely achievable discipline rather than aspiration.
