## Definition

A critical distinction in negotiation and leadership: assertiveness is confidence, collaboration, and knowing your worth; aggressiveness is pushy, confrontational, and adversarial. Jacob Warwick emphasizes that effective negotiation requires assertiveness, not aggressiveness — and that product people, engineers, and designers (who tend to be more introverted) often struggle with this distinction.

## Key Information

- Jacob: "Assertiveness, not aggressiveness. There's a difference."
- Assertiveness comes across as confidence; aggressiveness comes across as confrontation
- Product leaders, engineers, and designers (more introverted types) "negotiate more poorly than the obnoxious marketers and revenue leaders and the more extroverted types"
- Jacob credits Radical Candor (Kim Scott) with helping him be assertive: "That radical candor really gave me the confidence to be assertive in the ways that I never had been before."
- The simplest assertive pushback: "What's the chance there could be a little more?" — not greedy, not aggressive
- "It doesn't have to be such an aggressive thing. It doesn't have to be confrontational."
- Being assertive means:
  - Saying no to a 6:00 a.m. meeting and requesting your optimal time
  - Asking "What's the chance there's a bit more?" without demanding
  - Pushing back on scope creep without being confrontational
  - Taking ownership of the conversation without steamrolling
