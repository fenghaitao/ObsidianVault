## Definition

A core reframing of negotiation from adversarial (fighting over slices of a fixed pie) to collaborative (expanding the pie so everyone gets bigger slices). Jacob Warwick advocates physically positioning yourself as solving a problem together — like whiteboarding side by side — rather than confronting each other across a table.

## Key Information

- Jacob: "This is a myth about negotiation that's adversarial. What I teach is how can we be more collaborative?"
- Visual advice: physically pretend you're in the same room, put your arm around the other person, and whiteboard a solution together
- On Zoom calls, the face-to-face format creates an adversarial position — Jacob suggests reframing the conversation as joint problem-solving
- Walking side by side (like OpenAI's interview approach) creates a collaborative dynamic: "instead of you and I being confrontational, we're walking side by side"
- "Make it about we, not about me" — frame asks as benefiting the group, not just yourself
- Example: instead of negotiating severance for yourself, propose severance protections for the entire executive team
- "I'm not asking for you to take your pie and give them a bigger slice. I'm asking for you to work with somebody to expand the pie so everybody gets bigger slices."
