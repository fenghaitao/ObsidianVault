## Definition
The Plan and Review Shift is the observation that as AI coding agents improve, the software engineer's job is shifting from writing code to planning work upfront and reviewing AI-generated output. Time saved from coding does not return as free time — it displaces into planning and reviewing activities.

## Key Information
- Articulated by Louis Knight-Webb at AIE CODE 2026
- The historical ratio of software engineering work was: mostly writing code, with smaller amounts of planning and reviewing
- As AI tools improved (GitHub Copilot → ChatGPT → Cursor → Claude Code), the coding portion progressively shrank while planning and reviewing grew
- "You get 20 minutes back for every half an hour you were spending coding" — the remaining time displaces to planning and review
- This is not a net time savings but a reallocation of effort to higher-leverage activities
- Two approaches emerge from this shift: plan-based (invest upfront in specs) and review-based (YOLO and iterate)
- The shift is uneven across work types: frontend feature development still favors review-based approaches due to stateful edge cases, while backend and migration work favors plan-based approaches
- The shift also changes the nature of the job: engineers become managers of multiple concurrent agent work streams rather than deep-focused individual coders
