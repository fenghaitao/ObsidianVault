## Definition

Framework developed by [[Jeetu Patel]] and [[Aaron Levie]] (who calls it "the right to win"): only build in markets where it's logical for your specific company to compete and where you have a real distribution route — otherwise even an excellent product won't reach scale.

## Key Information

- Test: is it "logical" for people that *your* company built this versus a competitor building it? Do you have a route to mass-scale distribution for it?
- Without permission to play, a company can build an amazing product and still fail to reach scale — leading to the common enterprise-software dysfunction where product teams blame sales for not selling well, and sales blames product for building something unsellable.
- Patel's Cisco example: networking GPUs together for AI is a natural extension of 40 years of networking the rest of enterprise infrastructure — a logical "permission to play," unlike, say, Cisco entering consumer tech, where it lacks a natural distribution channel.
- Practical implication: be highly selective about where you "expend your calories" — concentrated focus in an area where you have permission to play produces outsized, compounding returns; spreading effort across many markets without that advantage dilutes results.
