## Definition

The Stalled Growth Framework is Jason Cohen's five-question diagnostic sequence for understanding and addressing why a product's growth has slowed. The questions must be addressed in order because earlier problems, if unfixed, make later fixes irrelevant.

## The Five Questions (In Order)

1. **Are customers leaving?** (Logo churn) — The worst problem. Cancellations grow exponentially with company size while marketing grows linearly. Formula: `max customers = new customers/month ÷ cancellation rate`. If unfixed, nothing else matters.

2. **Is pricing and positioning correct?** — Pricing selects the market. Most startups underprice because they guessed. Raising prices often doesn't change signup rates because you enter a different market segment. The microeconomic demand curve doesn't apply in practice.

3. **Are existing customers growing?** (NRR) — Can existing customers expand? If NRR is below 100%, cancellations will eventually win. Median IPO NRR for SaaS companies is 119%. Ask: "How do we create more value for the customer and then split it with them?"

4. **Are acquisition channels saturated?** — Marketing channels follow an elephant curve (S-curve that sags), not a pure S-curve. You can't just flog the marketing department. Options: find new channels, get creative (like Constant Contact's in-person workshops), or develop new products.

5. **Do you need to grow?** — The existential question. Maybe stasis is fine (37 Signals). Maybe "if you're not growing, you're dying" applies to the person, not the company. Maybe it's time to change jobs, sell the company, or build something new.

## Key Insights

- The sequence matters: if churn is too high, fixing pricing or NRR won't help
- The framework is useful for both diagnosing stalled growth AND accelerating already-good growth
- The common thread across all five questions: is the customer genuinely getting value?
- The framework helps avoid the common trap of just adding features and hoping marketing can "flog" them
