## Definition

Terminal Level is the career level at which a person's growth asmtotically approaches its maximum. Philip Su warns about the danger of reaching your terminal level too early without a plan for what comes next — being "the dog that caught the car."

## Key Information

- Everyone has a terminal level — a phase first where growth is easy, then growth slows as you approach your final level
- Everybody's terminal level is different, and it's not necessarily a flatline — incremental growth can still happen
- Philip hit his terminal level (E7 equivalent) around age 30, which created a crisis: "the problem with peaking early is you're like a child actor. What are you going to do with the rest of your life?"
- Philip went through a period of serious depression because he had lost purpose — "life seemed to have so much meaning and direction when I had a clear goal. But I caught the car."
- His advice: be sure you actually want the thing you're pursuing; don't be the dog that catches the car and then has no plan for what's next
- In the big arc, getting to E7 at 30 vs 38 doesn't make any difference — you still have 30 years of work to go
- The concept applies to the "Senior Engineer (Terminal Level)" concept — many companies treat E5/senior as a terminal level where you can comfortably stay
