## Definition

"Dog That Caught the Car" is Philip Su's metaphor for achieving a long-sought career goal only to realize you have no plan for what comes next. It describes the crisis of purpose that can follow early career success.

## Key Information

- Philip describes himself as "the dog that caught the car" — he was convinced his goal was to become a dev manager, and for his first 8 years at Microsoft he took any job that moved him closer to that goal
- Once he caught the car (became a dev manager at a young age, hit E7 around age 30), he had "no mental model for what happens next"
- He went through a period of serious depression because "life seemed to have so much meaning and direction when I had a clear goal. But I caught the car."
- The metaphor applies to any career goal — "the problem with peaking early is you're like a child actor. What are you going to do with the rest of your life?"
- His advice: be sure you actually want the thing you're pursuing. "Would you want to be Warren Buffett? So many people love the billions of dollars, but he's also in his late 90s. Would you want a few billion dollars and be in your late 90s? Is that worth it?"
- Roy Disney: "Decisions are easy when your values are clear to you" — Philip's advice is to spend more time clarifying your values before pursuing goals
- In the big arc, getting to E7 at 30 vs 38 doesn't make a difference — you still have 30+ years of work to go
