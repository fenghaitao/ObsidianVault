## Definition

Building for Future Models is the product strategy of designing AI products for the model 6 months from now, not the model of today. Boris Cherny advises this approach for AI startups, acknowledging it will be uncomfortable because product-market fit won't be good initially.

## Key Information

- Boris: "From the very beginning, we bet on building for the model 6 months from now. Not for the model of today"
- For early versions of Claude Code, the model only wrote ~20% of Boris's code, then 30% by May 2025, and crossed 100% in November 2025
- "It's going to be uncomfortable cuz your product market fit won't be very good for the first 6 months. But if you build for the model 6 months out, when that model comes out, you're just going to hit the ground running, and the product is going to click"
- For external developers, Boris suggests: "just trace the trajectory" — see what the model is getting better at (tool use, longer run times, etc.) and bet on those trends continuing
- This is connected to the Bitter Lesson: scaffolding gains (10-20%) get wiped out by next model version, so it's better to wait for the model to improve
- Claude Code stayed terminal-based because that was the only form factor that could keep up with the model's rapid improvement
