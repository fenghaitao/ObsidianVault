## Definition

Causal inference is the task of answering causal queries — the effects of interventions and hypothetical changes — by combining data, experiments, and assumptions, formalized by Judea Pearl through the Ladder of Causation and the do-calculus.

## Key Information

- Pearl frames every causal query by its level on the ladder (association, intervention, or counterfactuals), which determines what knowledge source is required: data, experiments, or someone's explanation.
- Association (statistics) alone cannot answer intervention or counterfactual questions without higher-level assumptions.
- The do-calculus and structural causal models supply the formal machinery for the intervention level.
- Pearl calls the planned combination of LLM-level learning with the calculus of intervention and counterfactuals "causal AI," which he sees as the missing ingredient for AGI.
