## Definition

Just-in-time (JIT) planning is a lightweight, short-horizon planning methodology used by [[Fiona Fung]]'s teams at [[Anthropic]] ([[Claude Code]] and [[Claude Cowork]]), replacing the six-month roadmap documents she initially tried when she joined.

## Key Information

- Originated from Fung's own early misstep on the team: she initially introduced a lightweight six-month roadmap doc, useful for kickstarting alignment conversations, but found that only three months in, so much had already changed that the roadmap was no longer being referenced.
- Current process: roughly a month of planning at a time, captured very lightly — not even in formal docs, just alignment on a simple spreadsheet of that month's priorities, with weekly check-ins to confirm the priorities still hold.
- The team still holds a twice-yearly (every 6 months) all-team gathering to set broader themes/bigger bets, but treats those themes as subject to change as the landscape shifts, rather than as a fixed roadmap.
- Fung frames JIT planning as itself provisional and still evolving — at the time of this conversation, the team was actively discussing how to further automate the monthly spreadsheet process so it doesn't become "a tax" someone has to maintain by hand.
- Explicitly tied to her broader "kill processes that no longer serve us" philosophy: periodically ask whether a given process (planning included) is still serving its purpose, especially given how fast the AI-tooling landscape changes.
- Operates alongside [[High Agency High Accountability|high agency]]: given a short list of monthly priorities, individuals are expected to propose their own approach to addressing them.
