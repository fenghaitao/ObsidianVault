## Definition

Separating behavior from identity is the core skill underlying the "Good Inside" philosophy. It means distinguishing who someone is (good inside) from what they do (problematic behavior). Most difficult interpersonal moments come from collapsing the two. Dr. Becky uses a physical gesture: one hand is identity, the other hand is behavior — literally separating them in the mind and body.

## Key Information

- "The idea of being good inside requires us to separate behavior and identity."
- "Most difficult times we have personally or interpersonally come because we've collapsed behavior and identity."
- "It's easy to do because we infer a lot from people's behavior."
- Physical gesture: "One hand is identity, the other hand is behavior. And literally just separating them forces you to distinguish who someone is good inside from their behavior."
- "Ironically, that's what allows you to effectively change and improve their behavior."
- Defensiveness comes from thinking someone is attacking your identity, not discussing your behavior
- "The quickest way to have an unproductive conversation is to lose sight of the fact that someone's good inside."
- At work: "This is a good person who is late" rather than "that person is lazy"
- "We all know what it's like to feel defensive. Do you know why we're defensive? Because we think someone else instead of talking about our behavior is talking about our identity."
- When identity feels wrapped up in the behavior conversation: "Now we can't even talk about the reason for my lateness because my whole identity feels wrapped up in it."
