## Definition

Company Hopping is the strategy of achieving higher job levels by moving between companies rather than through internal promotion. It is a valid and sometimes necessary path to Staff Engineer when internal opportunities are limited by organizational scope or business need.

## Key Information

- Zach Wilson achieved every level beyond Junior-to-mid through company hopping — he was only promoted once internally in his career. His IC3-to-IC4 promotion at Facebook was internal (5 months), but IC4-to-IC5 (Senior) came through moving to Netflix, and IC5-to-IC6 (Staff) came through moving to Airbnb. Zach argues that interviewing is the highest-leverage career skill because new companies only see your impact story, not your hours worked, sustainability concerns, or negative internal data. He leveraged competing offers from Facebook, Google, and Airbnb to negotiate a Staff title at Airbnb despite having only 6 years of experience when the job description required 10+. The key is to have an impact story: a compelling narrative of accomplishments that sells you as capable of the higher level.
- Rahul called it out explicitly: "There's also an opportunity to get promoted simply by moving to a company where there's a need for your skill set"
- Lee described it as "promoting yourself when you've done the work and you can convince someone else that you're there and get hired"
- The key requirement: you need to have done the work at the level already, and then convince a new company that you're already operating at that level
- Zach's Netflix project (Asset Inventory) was what Airbnb recognized as staff-level work, even though Netflix didn't have a formal Staff title
- Company hopping is especially relevant when: the company is not growing quickly, is stagnant, or there's insufficient scope for a Staff Engineer role
- Rahul's key insight: "Another core ingredient of getting promoted to a very senior level is there needs to be sufficient business need for that"
- The tradeoff: internal promotion requires working within the system and building relationships; company hopping requires strong interviewing and networking skills
- Zach's experience: at Netflix, everyone was "just Senior Engineer" — but the work was at staff level, and the official title came when he moved to Airbnb
- Steve Huynh recommends job hopping every ~2 years until mid-level or senior, then putting roots down for 3-5+ years to reach staff/principal levels
- Steve's counterpoint: high-level ICs (Principal, Distinguished) typically have at least 3-5 years on the same team/problem — company hopping has diminishing returns at the highest levels
