## Definition

[[Molly Graham]]'s rule of thumb for sustainable headcount growth: the happiest annual company growth rate is around 50%; 100% (doubling) is "manageable"; anything beyond doubling in a year is "signing yourself up for a world of pain."

## Key Information

- Mechanism of the pain: growing headcount faster than ~100%/year outpaces a company's ability to de-duplicate work — two people can be hired onto the same underlying problem by different teams without anyone realizing it, then discover the overlap only after both have started ("I am doing this" / "wait, I thought I was doing that").
- Graham's broader claim: "more people does not actually make you faster... it makes it harder to get work done, it makes it slower" — the counterintuitive implication is that leaders should be *scared* of adding headcount too fast, not treat it as the default fix to any problem.
- Prescription: slow down, hire for quality and demonstrated need rather than "whatever your sales model spits out" or panic-hiring, and ask a lot of questions before opening any role — doing so surfaces leverage ("oh, I didn't need that person/team/function after all").
- Context: Graham says she "had to scream way louder about this 5 years ago" than in the current environment, since the 2021 hiring boom followed by mass layoffs, plus the shift toward scaling with AI tools instead of headcount, has made the broader industry more receptive to the point.
- Connects to a similar point made in this episode by another Lenny's Podcast guest, Matt MacInnis, about deliberately under-resourcing teams so people focus only on the highest-priority work.
