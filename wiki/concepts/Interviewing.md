## Definition

Interviewing is the process of evaluating and being evaluated for a job. Zach Wilson argues it is the single highest-leverage, most important part of a career — more important than on-the-job impact — because it determines your level, compensation, and trajectory, and new companies only see your impact story, not your hours worked or negative internal data.

## Key Information

- Zach's core thesis: "How you interview is the most important, highest-leverage part of your career — more important than the impact you even have on the role"
- In interviews, companies see a different vantage point: they know your accomplishments and impact, but not how many hours you worked, sustainability concerns, or any negative internal feedback
- Zach leveraged competing offers from Google, Facebook, and Airbnb to negotiate a Staff Engineer title at Airbnb when the job description required 10+ years of experience and he had only 6
- He told the Airbnb recruiter directly: "If I get an interview from all three of you and they all come in at senior, I'm not picking Airbnb" — this pushed Airbnb to consider him for Staff
- Having multiple interviews simultaneously is critical: "that's why it's so important to have more than one company that you're interviewing for that you're excited about"
- The interview process has more variance and opportunity for step-change advancement than internal promotion, which is more incremental
- Zach's viral LinkedIn post about this: "apply for jobs that you don't think you're ready for"
- Job hopping interviews require countering the "disloyal job hopper" stigma early by presenting yourself as "extremely ambitious" who "solves hard problems and is on a trajectory to help us solve hard problems"
- Zach interviewed at Google and Facebook for SWE roles (offered Senior) and Airbnb for Data Engineering (offered Staff) — the Staff offer was $100K+ more than the Senior offers
- Steve Huynh (former Amazon Principal Engineer and Bar Raiser trainer) argues that behavioral interviews are the most underappreciated part: they have outsized impact on hiring and leveling decisions
- Steve uses a poker analogy: coding and system design are the "small blind and big blind" (necessary but not sufficient), while the behavioral interview is the "poker part" where the real match is made
- Amazon will reject candidates with strong coding and system design if their behavioral answers are poor — the fundamental question is "do I want to work with this person?"
- Steve recommends "packaging" your experience: communicating who you are, what you're about, and how you operate in a coherent, efficient story
- The way you tell stories and communicate betrays your level: "SD1 stories" from someone applying for a senior role creates incongruity that leads to down-leveling
- The interview prep industry hyper-focuses on coding and system design while neglecting the behavioral side, which Steve argues should be 40% of prep time (not 5%)
- For standing out before the interview, Steve recommends being an outlier in networking: genuine, personalized DMs to people on teams with openings, asking about their day-to-day and how to position yourself
- 90% of people in Steve's Discord said they would engage with and extend a referral to someone who sent a genuine, non-scripted DM

### Brian Attwell on Interviewing
- Attwell: processes that hire strong engineers "explicitly test your brain's ability to reason about on-the-spot tradeoffs," perform job-relevant skills, and assimilate/discuss information quickly
- Good signal: a postmortem exercise (read a 5-page postmortem, debate whether the team should be restructured, noting where one part contradicts another three pages down)
- Bad signal: interviews testing recall, experience, or mindsets that anyone can pass by practicing enough (e.g., gameable LeetCode)
- He is considering adding an IQ screen (likely for PMs first), since IQ is one of the most predictive signals and can't be practiced
- Strong interview processes are rare because they are expensive to build; his company runs ~20 experiments at any time to improve it
