## Definition

Progressive trust with AI is the practice of gradually granting an AI agent more access and permissions over time, mirroring how you would build trust with a human employee. Start with read-only access to low-risk systems, observe behavior, then progressively grant more capabilities.

## Key Information

- Core principle: "You don't onboard your EA by giving the password to your email account"
- Claire Vo's progressive trust ladder for her agents:
  1. Calendar access (read-only, then edit)
  2. Email reading (see what's coming in)
  3. Email drafting (but Claire reviews before sending)
  4. Email sending (autonomous)
  5. Meeting attendance (agent goes to meetings for you)
- The same model applies to family management: "first you get my calendar, then you can read my email, then draft some emails, then send"
- Initial setup: agents get their own email and calendar, and you share/grant access — just like provisioning an employee
- This is both a security practice and a practical one — it helps you discover what the agent is good at and where it makes mistakes
- The physical separation of machines (dedicated Mac Mini for agents) is the hardware-level version of progressive trust

## Why It Matters

- Mitigates security risks: if an agent is compromised or makes a mistake, the blast radius is limited
- Builds user confidence: you see the agent work correctly before giving it more power
- Mirrors real-world management: the same trust-building process used with human employees
- Addresses the fear that prevents many people from installing OpenClaw: "they're afraid of what OpenClaw can do"
