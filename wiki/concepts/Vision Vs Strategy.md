## Definition

A framework articulated by [[Adam Mosseri]] (Head of [[Instagram]]) distinguishing vision (an articulation of a desired future/product state) from strategy (an opinionated, necessarily contestable path toward that vision) — and arguing both are where human judgment will matter most as AI absorbs more of execution.

## Key Information

- Vision = "an articulation of the world or the state of the product you want to get to."
- Strategy = "an opinionated path to achieve that vision." Mosseri's key test for a real strategy: "it has to be controversial — a reasonable person should be able to disagree with it — because otherwise you're probably just trying to compete on raw execution."
- Mosseri predicts human effort will increasingly concentrate in vision and strategy as AI eats more of the execution layer of the product-development life cycle, with AI's role in strategy limited to giving feedback within bounds a human sets (goal, vision, constraints, budget), not originating the strategy itself.
- On why AI is bad at strategy "lazily": strategy requires weighing many hard-to-formalize inputs simultaneously — state of the technology, team personnel and motivations, competitive landscape, regulatory/compliance landscape, and brand identity/reason-to-exist — and a lazy prompt just returns "something pretty predictable that probably the competition would expect you to do."
- To get a genuinely useful AI-assisted strategy, Mosseri says you must steer the AI hard: explicitly identify and feed in all the real constraints, treat it as an iterative back-and-forth conversation, and explicitly ask the model to be critical. He also recommends picking a model with a "vibe" of pushing back rather than one that's a pleaser (see [[Mythos]]).
- Compares the human role in an AI-driven strategy process to management: define what success looks like, decide how prescriptive to be about the path, and give feedback along the way — with the same failure modes as managing people (too prescriptive stifles good ideas; too open-ended wastes time going the wrong direction). Mosseri expects this same autonomy-calibration problem to extend to managing AI agents.
- Connected to the [[Centaur And Reverse Centaur|centaur/reverse-centaur]] framing: the risk of letting AI originate strategy is a "reverse centaur" outcome where the AI effectively directs the human/team rather than the other way around.
