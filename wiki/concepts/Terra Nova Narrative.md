## Definition

[[Sam Lesson]]'s framing (referencing "Teranova," i.e., *Terra Nova*, new/undiscovered land) for a recurring generational pattern: each generation becomes desperate to believe in a new economic "frontier" — the American West, the internet, mobile, crypto, and now AI — and this desperation, more than the underlying economics, drives speculative investment into whichever frontier is currently fashionable.

## Key Information

- Lesson's history: "the US was amazing for so many years because we had the West and if you were going to work hard, you could go west and you'd do great... there was all this opportunity, land of opportunity." His generation's frontier was the internet ("digital Terra Nova"), which "was effectively the same thing as a West all over again."
- Applies the pattern skeptically to subsequent waves: mobile ("if you look at the math on it... not really, it's just more internet") and crypto ("I think it's the closest thing to Terra Nova... but to now, the AI god narratives"), arguing each generation is desperate for their own frontier "the story with good reason... but the story has to be real."
- Central to his Contrarian Corner thesis: seed VCs backing companies branded as "AI companies" will lose enormous money in the coming years, because such businesses are too capital-intensive for seed-stage economics and are fundamentally commoditizing — even as he maintains every startup should use AI as infrastructure ("not using AI in your startup is the equivalent of not using the cloud in 2010").
- Supporting evidence he cites: [[OpenAI]]'s seed investors made only "something like 25 times their money" even at a $500 billion valuation — a middling seed-stage return for the company defining the era, because foundation-model companies are so capital-consumptive.
- Distinguishes "AI companies" (bad seed bets, in his view) from companies that merely use AI as a tool/infrastructure layer (which he actively invests in) — citing examples oriented around managing AI's societal implications (e.g., distinguishing real people from bots, referencing the [[Blade Runner]] Voight-Kampff test) rather than claiming to "win because AI."
- Acknowledges the narrative can still make early investors money regardless of long-run fundamentals (his [[Elon Musk]]/[[SpaceX]] example: storytelling and belief alone can sustain a trillion-dollar valuation), but frames this as "religion," not disciplined seed investing.
