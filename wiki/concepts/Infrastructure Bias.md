## Definition

Infrastructure Bias is the observed tendency for infrastructure and backend teams to have more Staff+ promotion opportunities than product or frontend teams, because infrastructure work naturally creates leveraged impact that is easier to measure and justify for promotion.

## Key Information

- Panelists noted that building tools or platforms that other engineers depend on creates leveraged impact that is easier to measure than product-facing work
- Infrastructure teams have more senior promotion opportunities past L5 because the impact naturally scales across many engineers
- Product teams have fewer spots for L6+ because impact comes from leading large teams of engineers, and there's only one spot for the top tech lead
- Rahul observed: "Most of these really senior engineers I see are more on the infrastructure side or tooling, and it's specifically because of that leveraged impact"
- Lee noted that this bias is partly a legacy issue — the industry has more experience evaluating backend infrastructure work at scale than other domains
- 10 years ago, no one knew what a principal frontend engineer should look like, so getting promoted on the frontend was harder
- Client-side infrastructure (build systems, frameworks, request management) can be a middle ground — still infrastructure but on the frontend side
- The key is having other engineers leverage what you build, which typically means being one layer lower than the user-facing experience
