## Definition

Internal Promotion is the traditional path to Staff Engineer, where an engineer grows within a single company, building the track record, relationships, and scope needed to justify the promotion through the company's formal process.

## Key Information

- Panelists noted that internal promotion requires a track record of multiple halves of staff-level work — not just a single project
- Ryan Peterman and Rahul both achieved internal promotions at Meta (E3 to E6)
- Lee was promoted to L6 as a manager at Amazon after failing multiple times
- Carly was promoted internally at Activision through an internal move triggered by a presentation to the CTO
- Promotion processes vary by company: at Meta, it's by committee; at Amazon, it was skip-level manager decisions
- Key insight: the promotion is "trailing" — you perform at the level first, then get promoted
- Lee's advice: "Write your promo doc right now — not when you think it's ready, write it right now and take it to people and say 'why would you reject this?'"
- The process itself is "beastly" — if you don't understand it, you probably won't get promoted
- Internal promotion depends on business need: there needs to be scope for a Staff Engineer role in the organization
- Rahul's key insight: "If you're asking yourself 'how do I find scope to justify my promotion?' you've already lost"
- The correct approach: identify the most burning problems in the org and solve them

### Rahul's Promotion Journey at Pinterest
- Rahul was rejected for L3→L4 promotion twice at Pinterest
- First rejection was "valid feedback" — he had only been doing Android professionally for 9 months and hadn't shipped much
- Second rejection was "truly demoralizing" because he came in with an attitude of "I should be one of the best engineers"
- The second rejection was due to framing: a 16-month project was interpreted by the promotion committee as lacking urgency, when the real story was different
- Pinterest had an appeal process — Rahul worked with his manager to appeal and won the promotion
- Key lesson: you must be clear about what you think you're saying, because it may not always be interpreted that way
- Get your promotion document reviewed by people who have been around the block and be open to feedback on how things might be interpreted differently
- The same project duration can be framed as impressive (larger scope) or concerning (slow execution) depending on context

### Rahul's Promotion Journey at Meta
- Rahul joined Meta as IC5 (senior) — a promotion from his level at Pinterest through job hopping
- He was intentional about not asking about promotion immediately — spent the first year "just nailing it" to build trust
- Once he had trust and delivered good work, he had deliberate conversations with his manager about IC vs management growth
- His IC6 promotion came from two projects: a debug tool (bottoms-up) and a cross-functional infrastructure migration (top-down)
- The combination of both created a clear promo case
