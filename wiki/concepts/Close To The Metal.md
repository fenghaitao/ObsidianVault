## Definition

[[Mark Pincus]]'s principle that the best product CEOs/founders stay personally involved in pixel-level, minute product details rather than delegating the most important UX/product decisions to their least experienced people.

## Key Information

- Illustrated by [[Steve Jobs]] insisting on picking out conference-room carpeting, and by Pincus's own attempt to micromanage small pixel-level details at [[Zynga]].
- The [[Discord]] founders' realization is cited as a direct application: they found they had been outsourcing the most important product decisions (UX) to their least experienced people every day, and inverted their org into a "first-and-last-mile" structure where founders bless the key UX decisions themselves.
- Related to "be in the room if you can be" as the first principle of management, per Pincus — only delegate once you truly can't be everywhere; all subsequent management strategies exist to approximate this when direct presence isn't possible.
- Cited alongside stories about [[Jeff Bezos]] and Zuck spending dedicated hands-on days per week deeply embedded with a team on the thing that mattered most.
