## Definition

"Tour of duty" is Ilya Grigorik's framing of the IC↔manager seesaw: commit to a problem as an IC doing pathfinding, expand into management when the problem scales past one person, deliver it, then hand off and return to IC to find the next problem.

## Key Information

- The IC↔manager seesaw is "vastly underappreciated" but "surprisingly common" among high-performing ICs and principal+ roles
- As an IC you do the "machete" pathfinding; when the problem needs a "highway," you recruit a team and — naturally — become a TL/manager
- Commitment is to the problem, not the role; IC or manager is chosen by which best serves the mission
- Success as a principal is "Homer Simpson disappearing into the bushes": the team keeps executing at good velocity without depending on you
- A "tour of duty" means committing to own a problem for ~2 years, with a succession plan: solve it, or set it on a self-sustaining trajectory and return to IC
- Management should not be a one-way path or a demotion when returning to IC; organizations should let people "transit between those roles" without stigma
- Management values ICs who understand management: "management always appreciates an IC that understands management because it's much easier to partner with them"
