## Definition

Gatekeepers is the mindset that there are no external authorities who must grant you permission before you can learn, build, or teach. Rahul Pandey's core career insight is "There are no gatekeepers" — you don't need permission from anyone to pursue what interests you. This realization came too late for him, and he wishes he had adopted it much earlier.

## Key Information

### The Core Insight
- "There are no gatekeepers. You don't need permission from anyone when it comes to learning a technology or trying out something new"
- In school, Rahul was methodical and studious with what he was given, but not as creative or exploratory as he could have been
- The most interesting things in your career are the ones you do without being told — building a keyboard, publishing an app, creating tutorials

### The Permission-Seeking Trap
- The default mindset is: "let me get better, let me get smarter, let me wait"
- You're never going to be "ready" — just do it now
- Rahul described his earlier career as being "way more passive" — waiting until he felt "old enough" or "had done enough"
- The shift happened when he realized: "What am I waiting for? Let me just go do it"

### The Liberation
- When Rahul started publishing YouTube tutorials, he felt "so much joy" when people commented that his tutorials were helpful
- Publishing Android apps, running webinars with 800 people — "it felt like someone lifted off the blindfolds and you can do anything"
- You're not going to be ready to start a company, publish an app, or teach people — just start now

### Main Character Energy
- This mindset is about being the "main character" rather than an "NPC" in your own career
- You don't have to be the main character in every domain, but you should be in the parts of your life you care about
- The earlier you adopt this mindset, the better — "even in middle school or high school, it's not too early"
