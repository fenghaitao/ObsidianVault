## Definition

[[Jessica Fain]]'s mental model for how executives actually experience their day: like a strobe light going off, jumping from a finance budget meeting to an executive interview to a people problem to a legal problem to a product review, with no time between to "center" any one person's issue — meaning by the time you meet them, they haven't thought about your topic since your last conversation, even though you've been preparing for weeks.

## Key Information

- Direct practical consequence: never assume an executive shares your accumulated context. The fix is to spend the first 30-60 seconds of any meeting re-establishing why you're there, what happened last time, and what today's goal is — then stop talking.
- Reframes a common complaint ("they just don't get it, they're not in my meetings") — the point isn't that executives are uninformed or uncaring, it's that their attention is a genuinely scarce, constantly-reallocated resource, structurally different from an IC's.
- Complements Fain's advice to apply the same curiosity/empathy PMs use with users to executives themselves — treating the executive's mental state and constraints as something to actively design for, not something to resent.
