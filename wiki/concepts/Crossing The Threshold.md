## Definition

[[Nikhyl Singhal]]'s single most important piece of career advice for the current moment: finding the courage to actively reinvent how you work, despite the exhaustion, ego cost, and identity disruption that reinvention requires.

## Key Information

- Humans are described as poorly wired for this kind of change: we're culturally rewarded for settling into a stable role/relationship/skill set and making as few changes as possible once established — the opposite of what the current pace of AI-driven change demands.
- The moment of actually crossing the threshold, per Singhal, typically arrives via a personal experience of joy with a new tool (building something small and useful for yourself), not through abstract conviction — joy is described as "the biggest antidote to burnout."
- Distinguished from a permanent state of chaos: Singhal frames this as a temporary, unusually demanding transition period (a few years), after which some new equilibrium/routine will emerge, similar to how the internet-era "APM" product-management discipline eventually stabilized after its own disruptive birth at Google.
- Requires, per Singhal, "swallowing your ego" — willingness to take a smaller or less senior-sounding role if it keeps you current, and a long-term (skip-level) view of career rather than optimizing the immediate next title. See [[Skip]].
