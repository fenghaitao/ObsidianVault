## Definition

Objective functions are the goals that AI models are optimized to achieve. Edwin Chen argues that choosing the right objective function is the most critical decision in AI development, encapsulated in the phrase "you are your objective function."

## Key Information

- The most important question in AI development: "What kind of model do you want your model to be?"
- There's a fork in the road with every model behavior: should the model optimize for perfection (iterating endlessly) or for your time and productivity (telling you to stop and move on)?
- Edwin uses the analogy of raising a child: it's not just about test scores (SAT) but "what kind of person do you want them to grow up to be?"
- Defining complex objective functions (like "advancing humanity") is much harder than measuring simple proxies (clicks, likes, benchmark scores)
- The industry is currently optimizing for the wrong objective functions: engagement, leaderboard rankings, and flashy responses rather than truth and accuracy
- AI labs face perverse incentives: "the only way I'm going to get promoted at the end of the year is if I climb this leaderboard, even though I know that climbing it is probably going to make my model worse"
- Edwin's mission at Surge AI: help customers think about their "dream objective functions" and then train models to reach that north star
- "It's very hard and difficult to measure and define whether something is generally advancing humanity. It's very easy to measure all these proxies instead like clicks and likes"
