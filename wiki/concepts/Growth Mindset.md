## Definition

Growth mindset, as described by [[Fiona Fung]], is the disposition to keep learning and to let go of approaches that made you successful in the past when they no longer fit a changed landscape — cited as her primary explanation for why some engineers thrive amid rapid AI-driven change while others become frustrated or fearful.

## Key Information

- Fung's core articulation: "always be learning... what served you to get you to this point may not serve you any longer" — she says this is hard because everyone has succeeded by operating a certain way, and it can feel threatening to be asked to change what made you successful.
- She first consciously recognized this as a distinct concept during her transition from [[Microsoft]] to [[Meta]], before AI tooling was a factor — suggesting the mindset generalizes beyond the current AI moment.
- Practical technique she pairs with growth mindset: when frustration stems from fear, ask "what is within my control?" — reframing a situation as something happening *for* you rather than *to* you, and identifying one actionable step.
- Illustrated with her own biography: as a Hong Kong-born immigrant in Ontario, Canada who didn't initially speak English, she took a minimum-wage bank-teller job (a subject she says she disliked in school) as the one lever within her control to fund her way into an engineering degree, continuing the job for two years through the 2000 dot-com-crash hiring freeze.
- Associated favorite sayings: "what would you do if you're not afraid?" and "do something scary once in a while" — her view is that peak proficiency plateaus unless you deliberately take on something you haven't done before, accepting a temporary dip in performance in exchange for growth.
- Applied directly to AI-era engineering: she frames the current gap between engineers "leaning into AI" and those "resisting" as substantially a growth-mindset gap, with fear (not just skepticism) as an underlying driver of resistance.

### Cross-domain observation (episode 31 — Zevi Arnovitz)

- [[Zevi Arnovitz]] names Carol Dweck's *[[Mindset (book)|Mindset]]*, which coined this term, as one of the books that "completely changed my life" — describing himself as having previously operated with a fixed mindset before deliberately working to cultivate a growth mindset, a shift he connects to his own [[10x Learner Vs 10x Doer|"10x learner" reframe]] from his Failure Corner story.

### Cross-domain observation (episode 28 — Dr. Becky Kennedy)

- [[Dr. Becky Kennedy]] observes that many people who have a "forever learner" growth mindset at work — comfortable saying "it doesn't have to be the way it is," open to trying something new when they learn better information — forget to apply that same mindset at home with their kids, instead treating parenting as something that should just come naturally rather than as a learnable skill set. She frames this as a hopeful reframe: if you already give yourself credit for learning at work, you can extend the same growth-mindset permission to parenting.
