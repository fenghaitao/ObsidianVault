## Definition

Growth Mindset is the belief that abilities can be developed through dedication and hard work. Jia Chen looks for this trait in hackathon teammates, contrasting it with a "limiting mindset" often found in those with impressive credentials.

## Key Information

- Jia found that teammates selected for credentials (PhD, internships, titles) often had a "limiting mindset" about what was possible in 24 hours
- In contrast, teammates selected for skills and passion were more scrappy, put in more effort, and had a growth mindset
- Jia herself demonstrated a growth mindset: starting as a finance major with one Python class, losing her first hackathon, but persisting to win 21
- Her approach to learning: spending nights from 9:00 PM to 5:00 AM learning new APIs, tools, libraries, and microcontrollers
- College dulled her creativity and passion for learning, suggesting that structured environments can suppress growth mindset
- David J. Malan: if a concept isn't clicking, keep trying different approaches (another class, professor, TA, or book) rather than giving up; time is "a perfectly reasonable knob to turn." But if it's still not sinking in after ~100 times and isn't exciting you, it may genuinely not be the field.
