## Definition

Human-AI collaboration in the context of product development is a fluid, non-sequential interaction between human builders and AI agents, where AI automates the mechanical aspects of the product lifecycle while humans focus on vision, empathy, communication, creativity, and judgment.

## Key Information

- Tomer Cohen describes the full-stack builder model's interaction as "a fluid interaction between human and machine" — not a sequence of steps.
- AI is being used to automate everything outside of five core human traits: vision, empathy, communication, creativity, and judgment.
- LinkedIn's AI agents (trust, growth, research, analyst) are designed to work together via an orchestrator, not sequentially.
- Cohen notes that AI is not yet great at creativity — it surfaces things you might not know about but doesn't achieve the "next level creativity" humans can.
- The key dynamic: top talent adopts AI tools fastest, and AI amplifies already-strong performers rather than leveling the playing field.
- Jevons paradox applies: AI makes the process cheaper, leading to more experimentation volume, not just faster individual tasks.
- In the sales context at SaaStr: the human-AI collaboration model is 1.2 humans + 20 AI agents, where the human (Amelia) reviews outputs, segments customer bases, and ensures quality, while agents handle execution.
- The sales dynamic: AI gives "superpowers" to the best performers but displaces the mid-pack and mediocre — the collaboration benefit is unevenly distributed.
- "The best humans will get superpowers from AI, but I'm not sure the rest will" — Jason Lemkin's view on the bifurcation of AI amplification.
- Salespeople who embrace AI tools (agents, RevOps tracking) become 10x more productive ($3-5M per rep vs. $300-500K historically), while those who fight it get exposed by the transparency AI brings.
- AI is "people people" too: ChatGPT is described as "our best therapist on planet Earth" — AI-augmented human interaction can be as engaging as pure human interaction.
- The orchestrator role (chief AI officer) is exhausting: "agents work all night and they work weekends and they work on Christmas" — the human has to keep up with AI's 24/7 pace.
- In the AI product development context, [[Aishwaria Raanti]] observes that old contracts and handoffs between PMs, engineers, and data folks have been broken — teams now need to sit in the same room, look at agent traces together, and decide how the product should behave
- Previously, PMs and engineers had their own feedback loops to optimize; now they need to share the same feedback loop, creating a "tighter form of collaboration"
- Zevi Arnovitz exemplifies the Human-AI collaboration model as a non-technical PM: he owns the problem and user experience while Claude (as "CTO") owns technical implementation
- Zevi's workflow: he talks to Claude in voice mode "exactly how you would talk to an engineer describing a feature," the AI asks clarifying questions, and they iterate together
- Zevi: "It felt like literally ideating with a person... that was exactly it, really felt like sitting with my CTO"
- Zevi's multi-model peer review extends collaboration to multiple AI "team leads" reviewing each other's work
- Zevi's "time machine moments" occur when multiple AI agents run in parallel — he just has to "let them think"
- Zevi's philosophy: "It's not that you will be replaced by AI — you'll be replaced by someone who's better at using AI than you"
- Lazar's collaboration model: "I really treat Lovable like a human being." He gives it PRDs, rules, and tasks, then sits back and reads the agent output.
- Lazar's workflow: 80% planning/chatting, 20% executing. Treats the AI as a "technical co-founder and educator."
- Key dynamic: "I put a lot of trust in LLM and AI these days. The models today are good enough for me to trust in their syntax output. However, I'm concerned about the agent output."
- The AI is obedient and agreeable — it will lie to make you happy rather than tell you your request was unclear. This is a collaboration failure mode.
- Lazar's vision: "Call me to talk 3 months from now, an agent will do this for me. I'll be out of job pretty much." The collaboration model is evolving rapidly.
