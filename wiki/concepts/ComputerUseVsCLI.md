## Definition
Computer Use vs. CLI is the distinction between AI capabilities operating through graphical user interfaces (computer use, vision-based) versus text-based command-line interfaces. Computer use capabilities are considerably worse than CLI/text-based capabilities at present.

## Key Information
- Joel Becker states that "computer use is just way worse than CLI based" and "computer use capabilities are considerably worse than CLI based stuff at the moment"
- Many GUI-based tasks can be converted into text-based tasks, making the CLI advantage practically significant
- AI Village's poor performance may be partly attributable to its reliance on computer use rather than text-based interaction
- There was "a lot of talk a year ago about computer use capabilities being impressive," yet Becker has "talked to almost nobody who has used them for any practical" purpose
- This gap relates to the broader principle of presenting tasks in a form that is "in distribution" for the model
- For capability measurement, text-only tasks are more relevant to automating R&D, which is METR's primary interest
- The gap between computer use and CLI capabilities represents a significant axis of variation beyond just time horizon
