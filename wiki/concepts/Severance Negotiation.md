## Definition

Negotiating severance protections — exit compensation — as part of the initial employment offer, not as an afterthought. Jacob Warwick treats severance as a critical component of total compensation, especially for executives joining risky situations (private equity, turnarounds, competitive poaching).

## Key Information

- Standard severance: about 6 months, plus an additional month for every year served
- The awkwardness: "it's like you're negotiating the terms of your divorce before you get married"
- Key distinction: negotiate on-target earnings (OTE) not base salary for severance. Example: a CRO negotiated 6 months OTE ($350K) but the paperwork said 6 months base ($187.5K). Instead of splitting the difference, Jacob had the client ask "Was that a mistake?" — and it was fixed.
- Example: a CMO joined a PE-backed company and negotiated severance protections for the entire executive team by framing it as "wouldn't it make us look good to proactively give everyone six months?" — she became the hero
- Example: a CPO getting pushed out by PE collaborated with the CEO to set a severance precedent that would also protect the CEO (2-3x leverage)
- Severance is especially important when joining risky situations: "she was getting pulled from a hot company, which is risky cuz she was safe there"
- When companies resist: "Well, given that we've had a problem with talent being poached, you going elsewhere, and we just did a big riff, wouldn't it make us look good to proactively give everyone on the executive leadership team six months to show that they're safe and committed to the company?"
