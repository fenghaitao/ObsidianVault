## Definition
Incremental Delivery is a project management strategy where research and development are broken into short, fixed-duration phases (e.g., six-week sprints), each producing a tangible, potentially productizable deliverable. This approach gives leadership visibility, control, and the ability to stop funding at any phase boundary.

## Key Information
- Used by Northwestern Mutual's GenBI team to secure ongoing investment from risk-averse leadership
- Each phase was a six-week sprint with a concrete deliverable that could be productized independently
- Phases built on each other: research → metadata understanding → semantic search → data pivoting → enterprise readiness → full GenBI agent
- Key benefits:
  - **Early and frequent value**: Business impact realized throughout the process, not just at the end
  - **Transparent progress**: Leadership always knew what was being funded and what it produced
  - **Risk control**: Eliminates sunk cost bias — leadership can pull the plug at any phase boundary
  - **Self-propelling research**: Each phase's outputs fed into the next phase
  - **Competitive awareness**: Produced benchmarks usable for evaluating third-party alternatives like Databricks Genie
- Contrasts with traditional "fund the whole project and see what happens" approaches to research
