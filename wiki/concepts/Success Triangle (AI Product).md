## Definition

The success triangle for AI products consists of three dimensions that companies need to get right: great leaders, good culture, and technical progress. "Every technology problem is a people problem first." This framework was articulated by Aishwaria Raanti based on her work with companies undergoing AI transformation.

## Key Information

- **Three dimensions**: Great leaders, good culture, and technical progress. All three are necessary; none alone is sufficient.
- **1. Great Leaders**:
  - Leaders have built intuitions over 10-15 years — with AI, those intuitions must be relearned
  - Leaders must be vulnerable and comfortable being "the dumbest person in the room"
  - Must get back to being hands-on — not to implement, but to rebuild intuitions
  - Example: [[Rackspace]] CEO Gajen blocks 4-6 AM every morning for "catching up with AI"
  - [[Dan Shipper]] found the #1 predictor of AI success is the CEO chatting with ChatGPT/Claude many times a day
  - AI transformation is "almost always impossible for it to be bottom-up" — you need top-down leadership
- **2. Good Culture**:
  - Many companies have a culture of FOMO and fear — "you will be replaced"
  - Subject matter experts are crucial for building AI products, but often won't collaborate because they fear job loss
  - Build a culture of empowerment: "augment AI into your workflows so you can 10x what you're doing" instead of threatening replacement
  - AI opens up more opportunities — employees can do more things than before
- **3. Technical Progress**:
  - Successful teams are "incredibly obsessed about understanding their workflows very well"
  - AI is never the full solution — you have ML models doing some parts, deterministic code doing other parts
  - Choose the right tool for the right problem, not be obsessed with the technology itself
  - Understand the non-deterministic development lifecycle and iterate quickly
  - Build the flywheel: iterate without ruining customer experience, get enough data to estimate behavior
