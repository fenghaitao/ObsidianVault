## Definition

Credibility in engineering is the earned reputation that makes others trust your ideas, judgment, and technical direction. Ricky described it as a prerequisite for the cross-team influence required at the Staff Engineer level.

## Key Information

### How Ricky Built Credibility
- Ricky "established a brand of being someone who was able to find impactful projects or just find impact in general"
- He built credibility through a track record of successful projects over time
- He described the effect: "people see your name and they're like okay, this project's probably a good idea"
- His batting average on project ideas was "higher than most people" — not every experiment succeeded, but enough did to build trust

### Credibility as Influence Enabler
- Once credibility was established, Ricky could pitch ideas to other people and collaborate without "stepping on anyone's toes"
- People trusted his ideas because they had panned out well in the past
- Cross-pollinating ideas with other teams was easier because of the existing relationship and trust
- Credibility compounds: more successes lead to more trust, which leads to more opportunities for impact

### The Long-Tenure Advantage
- Having been at Google for 7 years in the same org meant everyone knew Ricky and his track record
- This established credibility would be harder to build if hired directly at the senior level — you'd need to build it from scratch
- This is one advantage of staying at the same company: credibility accumulates over time

### Rahul's Perspective on Credibility
- Rahul emphasized that social proof (Stanford, Facebook internship, big tech offers) is a form of instant credibility
- He advises new grads without exceptional opportunities to go to the biggest brand name company they can: "there's so much value in just accruing credibility"
- Even 1-2 years at a big brand creates an "instant step change in your credibility"
- At Meta, he spent his first year building trust and credibility before discussing promotion
- His IC6 promotion projects were enabled by the credibility and relationships he had built — he was the natural person to lead the infrastructure migration because he had been around long enough and knew the context of both teams
- He got the infrastructure migration project because he had "developed some trust" and "a seniority and relationship" with the RTC team
- Credibility compounds: more trust leads to more opportunities, which leads to more impact, which leads to more credibility

### Credibility vs. Batting Average
- Not every project needs to succeed — "honestly some of them didn't" work out
- What matters is that your batting average is higher than most people's
- The key is having enough wins to establish a pattern of good judgment
