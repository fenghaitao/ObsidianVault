## Definition

[[Jacob Warwick]]'s central interview/pitch technique: instead of opening with your own credentials ("tell me about yourself"), ask discovery questions to surface the counterparty's real pain points, then vividly describe the resolved future state — with yourself positioned as the person who got them there — rather than competing on résumé facts.

## Key Information

- Sequence: ask why you're in the room / what's exciting to them; confirm your understanding ("it sounds like..."); ask what they've already tried and what failed; then walk them through a vivid future scene (e.g., "fast forward 6 months, we've solved X — how does your next board meeting feel?") with you standing beside them in that success.
- Rationale: talking about your past puts you "on the back foot," revealing information without gaining any; leading with discovery questions instead extracts the counterparty's pain points while revealing nothing about your own compensation expectations, and makes the eventual pitch feel custom-built rather than generic.
- Illustrated by a Hollywood case: an agency won a sought-after director's signing not by listing past client wins (credibility/logic), but by literally scheduling a lunch with Steven Spielberg before the pitch meeting — making the "vacation" (a produced-film future) real and immediate rather than promised.
- Complements "[[Reciprocity Imbalance]]": making concrete introductions or connections mid-conversation both demonstrates value and creates social obligation.
- Warwick's caution: don't overpromise what you can't deliver — the technique only works if execution follows, and a broken promise is worse than a modest, honest pitch ("under-promise, over-deliver").
