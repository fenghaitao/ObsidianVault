## Definition

[[Adam Mosseri]]'s (Head of [[Instagram]]) stated policy stance that AI-generated content should not be filtered or judged based on the tool used to create it, but that users should be told whether content and/or accounts are AI-generated or human, so they can make their own informed decisions.

## Key Information

- Core principle: "I don't think we should filter out AI content. I think we should let you know if content is AI content or not." Ranking should still separate safe/unsafe and interesting/uninteresting content the normal way — the AI-or-not distinction is informational, not a ranking input by itself.
- Distinguishes content-level labeling (was this specific piece of content AI-generated?) from account-level labeling (is this account/person real?) — both matter but are separate problems. Mosseri suggests it may end up being more practical, long-term, to label camera-captured (non-AI) content explicitly rather than trying to reliably label all AI content, since AI-detection reliability is expected to degrade as generation models improve.
- Confidence framing: Instagram should be honest about how confident it is in any AI/non-AI assessment (e.g., "probably is," "not sure," "definitely is/isn't") rather than presenting detection as certain.
- Distinguishes legitimate AI creators (who are openly AI and simply have a clear aesthetic/creative point of view, e.g. "Plastic Dream Sequence," "If Only AI") from a "new spam vector": fake accounts that hide their AI origin to impersonate a persona/stereotype (Mosseri's example: an "AI monk" account selling bogus supplements) — the latter is the actual policy target, not AI-generated creativity itself.
- Framed as part of a broader thesis that abundant synthetic content will increase (not decrease) demand for human authenticity, creativity, and point of view — so labeling supports user choice rather than gatekeeping AI content out of the ecosystem. Users who want an all-AI feed should be able to have one; users who don't should be able to avoid it.
