## Definition

The Legibility Framework is a 2x2 matrix originated by Evan Tana (partner at SPC) that classifies founders and ideas as either "legible" or "illegible." If both the founder and idea are legible, the idea is probably not novel. The most interesting opportunities are where the idea is illegible (on the frontier, people might not get it yet). Jenny Wen applies this framework to design: designers should spot illegible ideas internally and help make them legible through storytelling and UX.

## Key Information

- Originated by Evan Tana, partner at SPC, shared on Twitter
- 2x2 matrix: founders can be illegible or legible, ideas can be illegible or legible
- If both founder and idea are legible → idea is probably not novel and someone is already doing it
- Most interesting quadrant: idea is illegible (on the frontier, not yet told in a way that makes sense to people)
- Jenny applies this to design: designers should be like VCs internally, spotting illegible prototypes and ideas
- Example: an internal prototype called "Claude Studio" was illegible to Jenny at first, but she noticed energy around it; it eventually yielded the skills framework and influenced Co-work's design
- Part of the designer's role is understanding what's there, diving deeper, and transforming illegible ideas through storytelling or UX
