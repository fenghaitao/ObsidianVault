## Definition

An AI-native startup is a company architected from day one around AI (specifically [[Claude]]) doing much of the execution work, with the founder acting as an orchestrator of AI-driven work rather than an individual contributor performing every task by hand. Anthropic frames this as the emerging default mode for founders in 2026, articulated in its "Founder's Playbook."

## Key Information

- **Founder-as-orchestrator role shift**: the founder's role moves from individual contributor — doing the work directly — to orchestrator, directing AI to do the work, freeing the founder to focus on the work only they (as opposed to AI) can do.
- **Observed outcomes**: founders who have never written a line of code before are shipping production applications, reaching revenue before scaling headcount, and building tools to automate their most tedious workflows.
- **Four-stage lifecycle framework** (per Anthropic's "Founder's Playbook," remapped for 2026-era AI capabilities): the four core startup stages — **Idea**, **MVP**, **Launch**, and **Scale** — each given explicit goals, exit criteria, common failure modes, and AI-powered exercises appropriate to that stage.
- **Intended audience**: founders deciding how to architect their company around AI from day one, and early operators helping them execute on that architecture.
- **Delivery format**: practical exercises, frameworks, and prompts for using Claude at each lifecycle stage.
