## Definition

Coaching vs. advising is a leadership framework for knowing when to guide someone to their own solution (coaching) versus telling them what to do (advising). Most technical leaders overindex on advising, assuming they must have all the answers, but coaching unlocks team brilliance and ownership.

## Key Information

- Most leaders, especially technical leaders, assume they have to have all the answers, but this trains the team to come to them with every hard problem.
- When to advise: urgent issues, the person lacks the skill needed, or you have a specific answer you want to drive.
- When to coach: the person has the right context and can solve their own problem with some guidance.
- Great leaders ask: "Do you want advice or do you want some space to think about it?"
- Coaching is a learnable skill — you don't need to be a professional executive coach. You can shift your energy into curiosity when someone brings a hard problem.
- Leaders only need to create maybe 15% more space in their one-on-ones and meetings for open-ended questions.
- Key coaching skills: Active Listening (three levels) and Powerful Questions (GROW model).
- When someone comes to a conclusion you disagree with, get curious: "Help me understand how you came to that conclusion."
- Coaching creates more buy-in — people are more likely to act on solutions they came up with themselves.
- Example: "Jeff," an AI company founder, shifted from deciding everything to coaching, created squads, and saw teams move faster and feel more empowered.
