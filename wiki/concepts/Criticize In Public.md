## Definition

Management philosophy [[Keith Rabois]] absorbed from a founder he worked with: deliver negative feedback about a person's performance in front of colleagues rather than privately, on the theory that private feedback optimizes for the individual while public feedback optimizes for the whole system.

## Key Information

- Rationale: if negative feedback is only channeled privately, colleagues who already suspect a problem exist have no visibility that it's being addressed — creating background anxiety about an unresolved issue. Making it public signals the issue is known and being handled, and invites other team members to offer to help, turning correction into a collaborative, team-building moment rather than a private shame exercise.
- Rabois pairs this with an explicit rejection of psychological safety as a management value: "high-performance machines don't have psychological safety, they're about winning," citing *The Jordan Rules* / *The Last Dance* as illustrative of what it takes to build a Michael-Jordan-caliber team culture.
- Not absolute in practice: Rabois notes the best coaches mix public and private feedback depending on context, implying judgment about which mode fits a given situation.
- Sits alongside a broader anti-complacency management stance: Rabois believes the CEO's core job is to push harder specifically when a team is winning, since success breeds complacency and top performers get demotivated ("skating") without continued pressure.
