## Definition

The J-Curve vs Stairs model is a career growth metaphor originated by Chamath Palihapitiya and popularized by Molly Graham. It contrasts two career paths: the linear "stairs" of predictable promotions versus the "J-curve" of jumping off cliffs into unfamiliar roles, falling initially, then climbing out far beyond where the stairs could ever get you.

## Key Principles

- The "stairs" path: linear promotions every 2 years (manager → senior manager → director → senior director) — described as "boring"
- The "J-curve" path: jumping off cliffs into unfamiliar roles where you feel like an "absolute idiot" for months, then slowly climbing out to far greater heights
- The J-curve requires embracing fear — Graham describes the experience as "the most fun I've ever had" despite the initial pain
- Graham's personal example: moving from HR at Facebook to the mobile team, where she spent six months feeling like a "total jackass" and got her lowest performance rating ever, then became an expert in mobile over three years
- The Facebook phone project was a "massive, costly failure" for the company but was not a failure for Graham — it taught her she was capable of things she never could have dreamed of
- This model is especially important in fast-growing companies where there are many opportunities to take on things you're "deeply unqualified for"
- Key insight: "If you prove to people that you're actually good, if they believe you are the kind of person they can use to do lots of things, you can get these opportunities"
- The fear you feel is often just "I'm scared I can't do this" — and that kind of fear is usually worth pushing through
