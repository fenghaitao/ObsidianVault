## Definition

Market Leader vs Market Follower is a strategic framework for choosing which company to join or compete against. Philip Su applied this framework to his career decision to join OpenAI, arguing that the market leader has room to experiment while the market follower is constrained by the leader's schedule.

## Key Information

### Philip Su's Framework
- The market leader has room to experiment because it can afford to fail at a few things — with a big enough lead, you can take brave big shots
- The market follower's formula has to be: match all of the leader's checkboxes AND have one unique differentiating thing
- Number twos have their entire schedule and timeline dictated by the leader — they have to be fast follow
- Number threes might be able to do crazy hail marys, but number twos are most constrained
- Philip learned this from working at Facebook, which had a big enough market lead to survive colossal failures
- Philip cited Google+ as the classic example of a market follower: its formula was match all of Facebook's features plus one differentiator (circles), but it still failed

### Application to Career Decisions
- When deciding to join AI, Philip applied only to OpenAI — his plan was "join OpenAI or continue writing my own software"
- His rationale: "I would much rather join the market leader or nobody at all"
- The market leader gets to "blow a lot of time on crazy things" and Philip loves the craziness
