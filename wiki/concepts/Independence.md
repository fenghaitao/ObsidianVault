## Definition

Independence in engineering is the ability to finish projects on your own without requiring handholding or continuous guidance from more senior engineers. It is the primary criterion for promotion from junior (L3) to mid-level (L4) engineer.

## Key Information

### The Hallmark of L3 to L4
- Ricky described the key criterion as: "being able to have Independence and finish projects on your own"
- "You just don't need that much handholding — you can unblock yourself on your own"
- This is the primary signal that separates junior engineers from mid-level engineers

### How to Develop Independence: Ask Questions
- Ricky's primary strategy was "asking a lot of questions"
- He overcame impostor syndrome with the mindset: "I'd rather ask questions and look dumb and learn something than to do nothing, not learn anything, not ask any questions, and get fired"
- He learned from others by asking questions, building the ability to do things on his own
- The difference from dependency: he asked peers for knowledge, not his manager to "figure it out for him"

### The Unblocking Rule of Thumb
- Don't ask too quickly — you won't learn anything if someone just gives you the answer
- Don't block for too long — you're not learning or being productive
- Ricky's rule of thumb: if you spend more than ~4 hours on something with no progress, reach out
- "Nothing's going to change" after that point — you need outside help
- Always give it a good try before asking

### Red Flags
- Asking the same question multiple times is a red flag
- Diffs that constantly get tons of feedback without improvement over months signal a problem
- The ideal trajectory: questions and feedback iterations decrease over time as you learn
- Steve Huynh describes independence at the SDE1 level as: understanding where you fit in the software development lifecycle, taking tasks and seeing them through, gaining trust, and not needing handholding
- Independence does NOT mean being completely self-contained or never asking questions — it means being comfortable surfacing issues and asking for help when stuck
- Hiding that you're blocked because you think you should know everything is actually a performance issue — if a task should take two weeks and you report "on track" until the Thursday before, that's a serious problem
