## Definition

[[Mark Pincus]]'s mentorship model for scaling a founder's product judgment through an organization: put as many people as possible in the room during product decisions (like medical students observing in a teaching hospital), then select someone from the ranks to become a dedicated "tech assistant" who shadows the founder for 6-12 months, absorbing their approach and passion (Pincus calls this "transferring your vampire blood") before being placed in a bigger independent role.

## Key Information

- First step: maximize the number of people present in product management meetings so the founder's passion/approach/ideas spread by osmosis.
- Second step: select a "mini me" — often a "[[Expert Witness Syndrome|frustrated expert witness]]" personality — to follow the founder around, absorb their decision-making, and be tested with real projects.
- Cited real-world example: [[Andy Jassy]] and reportedly everyone on Amazon's C-staff once served as [[Jeff Bezos]]'s "tech assistant" — evidence the model "scales" and produces senior leaders "for free."
- Framed as one of several "non-scalable ideas that actually scale" — deliberately labor-intensive practices (like Chesky's or Bezos's hands-on involvement) that end up multiplying organizational capability.
