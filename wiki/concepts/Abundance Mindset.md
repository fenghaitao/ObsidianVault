## Definition

[[Sam Lesson]]'s term for the mindset underlying good etiquette: treating any given social/business interaction as one of many future opportunities rather than a single, scarce, must-not-fail moment — the mental posture that produces a [[Low Heart Rate]].

## Key Information

- Contrasted directly with a "scarcity mindset": "This isn't your one shot. You'll have other opportunities. You kind of want to show up with the self-confidence and the calm of abundance."
- Explicitly framed as a story you tell yourself even when it might not be true: "even if in your heart of hearts you're like, 'this really is my one shot,' you kind of want to show up with the self-confidence and the calm of abundance... This is part of the story. This is not the entire story."
- Applied specifically to meeting famous or powerful people: the goal is to walk away having been "a pretty nice person" someone might want to talk to again, not to extract maximum value ("email address and phone number") in a single encounter — "let them offer it."
- Also cited as "understand how to give more than you take. Understand how to build a relationship, not collect business cards" — treating people as ongoing relationships rather than one-time transactions.
- Applied to conversation specifically via "leave them wanting more" — ending an interaction while curiosity/interest is still high, rather than exhausting the other person with a full personal monologue, itself an abundance-oriented (not extractive) way to engage. See [[Give To Get]].
- Lesson connects the opposite of this mindset — showing up "like a little Energizer Bunny" out of scarcity anxiety — to burning "mild relationship opportunities" that founders don't realize they're losing, an "unnecessary uphill battle" that's avoidable.
