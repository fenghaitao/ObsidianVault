## Definition
Taste in software is the human ability to distinguish high-quality, well-crafted output from AI-generated slop. It is described as the key moat in AI-assisted development — something that cannot be achieved through high-level prompting alone.

## Key Information
- Peter Steinberger: "Taste is very important" — it's the bottleneck in AI-assisted development alongside "thinking"
- Lowest level of taste: "if it doesn't stink like AI" — writing style, personality, UI that immediately signals AI generation (e.g., purple gradient, colored border on the left)
- "You've seen so much agentic-built UI that you immediately know if it's AI"
- Taste is "a feeling the same as you can identify AI written slop right away" — "even if you can pinpoint this, you will know"
- Higher level: now that so much of software is automatable, there's more time for "the little details" — delightful touches like OpenClaw's roasting messages
- "You'll just not get [these details] if you prompt in a high level"
- swyx's Law of Anti-Slop: "the amount of taste needed to fight slop is orders of magnitude larger than that needed to produce it"
- Taste is one of the key skills Peter recommends engineers develop in the age of AI, alongside system design, saying no, and big picture thinking
- The concept connects to agent personality: Peter iterated on OpenClaw's WhatsApp personality because Claude Code's default "didn't really fit how people would write to you on WhatsApp"
