## Definition

A leadership framework, phrased by [[Dan Gil]] (CPO, Carvana) and used by [[Matt MacInnis]] (CPO, [[Rippling]]) as a guiding principle: if you want a top-1%-percentile outcome, extraordinary effort is necessary but not sufficient — comfort at work is itself a warning sign that something has gone wrong.

## Key Information

- Extraordinary effort does not guarantee an extraordinary outcome, but an extraordinary outcome is essentially never achieved without it.
- MacInnis argues the effort has to feel "really freaking exhausting" — not as a single heroic event, but as a sustained pattern told through "a thousand Jira tickets," not "a thousand grand events."
- The framework only motivates people when there's a real chance of an extraordinary outcome: a company growing 30-40%/year doesn't give leaders the same "air cover" to ask for maximal weekend effort that a company growing extremely fast does.
- Counterintuitively, MacInnis says giving teams idle time/space ("twiddling their thumbs") tends to lower morale, not raise it — people get distracted and start questioning what they're doing.
- Cited alongside a quote from a prior podcast guest (unnamed, sourced from a women's basketball coach): "good teams get tired, and that's when great teams kick the good team's asses."
- Connects to [[Compounding Plus Power Law Plus Entropy]]: MacInnis frames sustained intensity as the only antidote to organizational entropy, and views power-law-distributed outcomes as the reason marginal extra effort near the top produces disproportionate reward.
