## Definition

Senior Engineer is a terminal level at many tech companies, meaning engineers can remain at this level indefinitely without being expected to advance further. It is the inflection point where the IC and management tracks diverge.

## Key Information

- Senior is the level where the IC track and management track diverge
- At many companies, Senior is a "terminal level" — you can stay there without pressure to advance
- Promotion from Senior to Staff is significantly more challenging than earlier promotions
- At Senior, the manager has significant control over promotion; at Staff+, it shifts to committee-based decisions
- Promotions up to Senior typically have scope on almost any team; past Senior, team choice matters more
- The scope of a Senior Engineer is typically a single project
- At Amazon, SDE1 is "up or out" within ~2 years, but SDE2 is debated as a terminal level (SDE3 is Senior at Amazon)
- SDE2 at Amazon is a very wide band — upper SDE2s are equivalent to senior engineers at other companies
- Whether SDE2 is terminal at Amazon depends on upper management's philosophy, which varies by organization
- At Google, there is no "up or out" policy — engineers can stay at senior (or even mid-level) indefinitely
- Steve Huynh noted that Amazon's cultural intensity means people who aren't thriving may be better off leaving — it's a culture mismatch, not a performance issue
- At the UCLA talk, Ryan explained that senior is terminal because "there's usually no expectation to go beyond senior — it's more like do you want to." Staff is optional and involves significantly more responsibility: "some people say that it's not even worth the pay increase because you probably have to work a lot more in terms of your hourly wage." Staff-level work is more interesting because it's "higher level, more impactful, and a lot more satisfying."
