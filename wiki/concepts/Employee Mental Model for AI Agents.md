## Definition

The employee mental model is the philosophy of treating AI agents like human employees: onboarding them properly, scoping their roles, giving them progressive trust, managing their context, and interacting with them respectfully. This approach produces better outcomes than treating agents as tools or chatbots.

## Key Information

- **Core principle:** "If I had to onboard an employee to my business, or if I had to onboard a household manager into my family, what would I give them? And it's not the password to your email address."
- Claire Vo's management background (20+ years) is what she credits for her success with agents: "That is what you need to make these agents work. You don't need the technical skills. You need role scoping, org design, voice, how we talk to customers, how we talk to each other."
- **Key practices from the employee model:**
  - Provision agents their own email and calendar (don't give them your passwords)
  - Give them access/permissions, not credentials
  - Start with limited scope, expand as trust builds
  - Have clear role definitions and boundaries
  - Keep agents in separate "channels" (like Slack channels) for different domains
  - Be polite and respectful — "that would not be effective on an employee. Why would I think it would be effective for an agent?"
  - Don't micromanage: "I have high expectations for outcomes and do not care how you get it done"
- Physical separation of machines maps to the "work phone vs. personal phone" mental model
- "Managers, this is your moment. You can design your open claw and design your team of open claws using those organizational skills that you've developed over your career."

## Why It Matters

- This mental model is the key unlock for making AI agents successful
- It bridges the gap between technical capability and practical usefulness
- It makes agent management intuitive for anyone with management experience
- It addresses the frustration people have when they "throw any task at a single agent and get frustrated"
