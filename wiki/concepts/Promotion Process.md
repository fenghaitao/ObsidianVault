## Definition

The Promotion Process at Google is the formal system for advancing engineers through the leveling ladder. It involves six-month rating cycles, manager support, calibration committees, and demonstrated performance at the next level.

## Key Information

### Google's Rating System
- Google operates on a six-month rating cycle
- Ratings include: "meets expectations" (typical for new hires in their first half), "exceeds expectations," and higher tiers
- Consistent high ratings are a strong signal for promotion readiness, but are not sufficient alone

### Employee-Initiated, Manager-Supported
- Promotions at Google should be initiated by the employee, not the manager
- However, manager support is essential — the manager must agree to put the employee up for promotion
- The process should be an ongoing conversation, not a sudden request

### Tenure and "Performance at the Next Level"
- Ricky was worried about being "docked points" for not showing sustained performance at the next level over an extended period
- His accelerated timeline (1.5 years from L4 to L5, versus the average 2.5–3.5 years) was "really aggressive" and a "pretty big stretch"
- His manager set expectations: "I'm not sure, we'll really have to see, you might be docked, but let's just try to go for it"
- Ricky succeeded on all three promotions on the first attempt, which he attributes partly to luck

### The L5 to L6 Gap
- Going from L5 to L6 is "notoriously hard" — described as "almost like a complete job change"
- Even being a "superstar senior engineer" with tons of impact doesn't guarantee success as a staff engineer
- The process requires demonstrating cross-team influence, challenging the status quo, and leadership beyond just technical delivery

### No "Up or Out" Policy
- Google does not enforce an "up or out" policy — engineers can stay at senior (or even mid-level) indefinitely
- Becoming a staff engineer is explicitly a choice, not an expectation
- At Amazon, the promotion process for SDE3 to Principal (L7) is notoriously difficult because Amazon effectively skips a staff level — it's like jumping two levels
- Amazon's promotion packets are reviewed by a panel; the process changed three times during Steve Huynh's 4-year attempt (2016-2020)
- Steve's key advice for Amazon promotions: don't wait to start — put your packet in early and get actual feedback about gaps rather than waiting for the "big project" to land
- After a rejection, focus on the delta (weaknesses/gaps) rather than doubling down on strengths — put strengths "on the back burner" and prioritize closing gaps
- The promo panel looks at outliers and what changed since the last review — they focus on the delta
- Amazon promotions and performance reviews are decoupled: you can be high-performing at principal-level work but flagged as low-performing at SDE3
- Steve described the process of getting promoted as "becoming an expert in the process of getting promoted" rather than necessarily upleveling himself
