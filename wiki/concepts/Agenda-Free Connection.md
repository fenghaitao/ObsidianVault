## Definition

Agenda-free connection is the mindset of being present with someone without an agenda — even for just 30 seconds. No one wants something of you; they just see you or recognize something you're doing. This is the prerequisite for genuine connection, and it's increasingly hard to do.

## Key Information

- "Being present with someone without an agenda is increasingly hard to do. But that is what connection is about."
- "No one wants something of me. They just kind of see me or they're recognizing something I'm doing or they kind of plop down on the couch next to me."
- If you approach connection with a hidden agenda, it doesn't work and feels "dirty": "You're going to feel it and it's going to feel off."
- The mindset: "Can I actually just kind of drop down into that moment?"
- Concrete examples with kids: sitting next to them with a hand on their back for longer than feels natural; saying "I'm happy to see you" (and that's it); watching your kid play and describing what they're doing instead of peppering with questions
- At work: "Hey, tell me a little about your long weekend" — and not counting down the seconds for them to finish
- The first step is a mindset, not a script: "We feel people's intention, not just their intervention."
- "The same intervention will be felt completely different based on our mindset."
