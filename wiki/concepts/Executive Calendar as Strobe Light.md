## Definition

Jessica Fain's metaphor for understanding how executives experience their day: a strobe light going off — constant context-switching between urgent, unrelated problems. This concept explains why execs often seem unprepared or disconnected in product reviews: they haven't had time to center your problems.

## Key Information

- "I describe an executive's calendar as a strobe light going off. You wake up at 8 a.m., you've already got a huge list of urgent things going on."
- An exec goes from a finance meeting to an interview to a people problem to a legal problem to a product review — all in one morning.
- "The executive coming into that session hasn't thought about you since. They may not have gone to the bathroom today."
- They have not had the time, energy, or wherewithal to center your problems. You must help them get into the right mindset.
- This is why the 30-second meeting opener is so important: "Why are we here? What happened last time? Why is this important? What are the goals?"
- Execs are optimizing for a global maximum, not your local maximum.
