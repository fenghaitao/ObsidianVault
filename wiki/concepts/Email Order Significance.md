## Definition

The order of names in the To and CC fields of an email carries meaning. The first person in the To line is perceived as the primary recipient. The CC line means "you should have a copy of this, but this is not really to you." There's a subtle etiquette to who responds first.

## Key Information

- Sam Lessin: "If you're sending an email and the first person on the email is the assistant and the fourth person on the email is a CEO, you've probably done it wrong."
- The first person in the To line is who you think is most important — who you're really sending this to
- "If I look at an email and I'm the first person, I mildly pay more attention than when I'm the fifth."
- An email sent to many people is almost by definition not that important
- The CC line: "It means, hey, you should have a copy of this. This is not really to you. I'm not expecting an immediate response."
- There's a subtle etiquette to who responds: if you're the fifth CC, you're not expected to be the first response
- Never BCC then reply all
- Never put someone you're talking about in the To line to check the spelling of their name — that's a disaster waiting to happen
