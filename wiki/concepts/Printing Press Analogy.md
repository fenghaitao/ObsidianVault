## Definition

The Printing Press Analogy is Boris Cherny's historical parallel for understanding AI's impact on coding and knowledge work. Just as the printing press democratized literacy from <1% of the population (scribes) to ~70% globally, AI will democratize programming from a small group of engineers to everyone.

## Key Information

- In mid-1400s Europe, literacy was sub-1% of the population — only scribes could read and write
- Scribes were employed by lords and kings who were often not literate themselves
- After Gutenberg's printing press: in 50 years, more printed material was created than in the previous 1,000 years
- Cost of printed material went down ~100x over 50 years
- Literacy went from <1% to ~70% globally over ~200 years (took a while due to education systems, free time, etc.)
- A historical interview with a 1400s scribe showed they were excited: "The thing that I don't like doing is copying between books. The thing that I do like doing is drawing the art in books"
- Boris: "This is sort of how I feel where I don't have to do the tedious work anymore of coding"
- The Renaissance could not have happened without the printing press enabling knowledge to spread
- Boris: "I imagine a world, a few years in the future where everyone is able to program. And what does that unlock? Anyone can just build software anytime"
- "In the 1400s, no one could have predicted this. I think it's the same way"
