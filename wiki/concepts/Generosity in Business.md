## Definition

Generosity in business is the practice of extending the most generous circumstances possible to employees, customers, and partners. Stewart Butterfield views it as both an ethical imperative and a game-theoretic strategy — acts of generosity signal cooperation in the iterated prisoner's dilemma, leading to mutual benefit.

## Key Information

- **Prisoner's dilemma framing**: acts of generosity demonstrate "I am going to cooperate as we iterate in this game." If you cooperate, others will too, and both benefit
- **Customer value mantra**: "In the long run, the measure of our success will be the amount of value that we create for customers." This was chanted at company all-hands
- **Examples of Butterfield's generosity**:
  - Handed $500 cash to an employee before Christmas to help them go home to family
  - Cried real tears when laying people off from Glitch and spent enormous time helping them find new jobs
  - Paid 100% of employees' health insurance
  - Direct listing at IPO with no lockup — best possible situation for employees
  - Employee-friendly acquisition structure with Salesforce
  - Free credits to businesses struggling during COVID
  - Fair billing: stopped charging for inactive seats even though contracts allowed it
  - Slipped release schedules to make features better
  - SLA: 100x money back for downtime, applied automatically and proactively
- **Ethical framing**: it's not just that ethics are good — there are advantages. You attract a better class of employee, have fewer internal problems, and create better outcomes
- **No substitute**: you can put effort into demonstrating you've created value for customers, but there's no substitute for actually having created it
