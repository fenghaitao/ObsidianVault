## Definition

Staff Engineer is a very senior individual contributor (IC) level at large tech companies, typically one level above Senior Engineer. It is characterized by influence and scope across multiple teams and potentially company-wide impact, rather than just individual technical delivery.

## Key Information

- At Google: L6 is Staff (L5 is Senior); at Meta: E6 is Staff (E5 is Senior); at Amazon: there is no "Staff" vocabulary — the level after Senior (SDE3) is Principal
- The defining characteristic of Staff Engineer is having influence and scope across multiple teams, not just within a single team
- It is a very challenging level to perform at — the panel described it as a "terminal level" above which roles become significantly rarer
- The baseline expectation is that you influence others to get large undertakings done — software is inherently collaborative at this level
- Staff engineers are expected to both create and find their own scope, identifying problems that others haven't seen or are afraid to tackle
- Getting promoted to Staff requires a track record of multiple halves of staff-level work, not just a single project
- There needs to be sufficient business need and scope for a Staff Engineer role — some companies or teams simply don't have the scope for it
- Different archetypes exist: Tech Lead, Code Machine, Domain Specialist — each can achieve staff-level impact differently
- Company hopping is a valid path to Staff when internal opportunities are limited. Zach achieved Staff Engineer at Airbnb by age 26 through job hopping, negotiating the title despite the job description requiring 10+ years of experience (he had 6). The key behaviors that shift from Senior to Staff: selling a technical vision, deciding which problems to solve (not just solving them), working through others, and establishing better work boundaries. Zach experienced the consequences of being thrust into a staff-level role without the behaviors at Netflix, leading to burnout — validating why companies resist promoting people with impact alone. At Airbnb, Zach proved that staff-level performance is possible at ~40 hours/week by shifting behaviors rather than working more hours. After Senior Engineer, technical skills become less important and soft skills (vision-selling, problem selection, delegation) become the differentiators.
- At Activision, the equivalent title is "Lead Engineer"

- Ricky described the transition from L5 to L6 as "almost like a complete job change" — even superstar senior engineers with tons of impact aren't guaranteed to be great staff engineers
- At the staff level, Ricky found his schedule dominated by meetings that fragmented his focus time, leading him to do his own work from 5pm until bed
- Ricky's staff-level anchor project came from knowing a space so well that he could challenge the status quo — in one case, experiment results conflicted with user research, and he pushed the boundary to drive significant impact
- Becoming a staff engineer is a choice at Google (no "up or out" policy), and Ricky noted the trade-off: "I really miss taking a nap at like 2:00 p.m. or going to the gym at 3:00 p.m."
- Cross-team and cross-functional influence, including buy-in from high-level leadership, was a defining characteristic of Ricky's staff-level work
- Steve Huynh confirms that Amazon skips the staff level entirely — the gap from SDE3 (Senior) to L7 (Principal) is like jumping two levels, which is why so many senior engineers fail or leave
- Steve's principal-level work at Amazon involved acting as a sponsor, backstop, catalyst, or evangelist for cross-team initiatives spanning multiple organizations
- The principal role at Amazon can sometimes look like a TPM role — Steve was accused of this in a promotion assessment and had to push back
- Many Amazon engineers who couldn't make the SDE3 to Principal jump went to Meta and Google and thrived as Staff, Senior Staff (E8), or Principal
- The "two jobs" problem: engineers trying for principal often stop doing SDE3 responsibilities and focus on principal-level work, then get flagged as low-performing at SDE3 during reviews
- Steve's principal promotion came from taking on scope that sat in the gaps between other principal engineers' assigned areas, requiring an end-to-end solution
