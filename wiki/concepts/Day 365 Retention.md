## Definition

Metric tracking what fraction of users are still active exactly one year after acquisition; per [[Mark Pincus]], [[Zynga]] was reportedly the only consumer company at the time tracking it, and he claims (statistically) that the most valuable companies in the world tend to have the highest Day 365 retention.

## Key Information

- You don't have to wait until day 365 to act on it: early indicators (D1, D30 retention) are correlated with — but not identical to — D365 outcomes. Low D1/D30 reliably predicts low D365. But high D30 can still coincide with near-zero D365 ("most products").
- Pincus argues that if a team doesn't think about why someone would still use their product in a year, users sense that lack of long-term intent too, and it affects whether they invest time, share the product, or give it their information.
- Related to Zynga's proprietary [[Active Social Network]] metric, which functioned as an early leading indicator correlated with long-term retention.
- Contrasted with virality-chasing ("viral-based companies... are sinking speedboats... bailing water out faster" rather than fixing retention, the "hole in the boat").
