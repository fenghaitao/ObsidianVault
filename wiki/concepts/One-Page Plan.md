## Definition

A single-page strategic-alignment framework, adapted by executive coach [[Rachel Lockett]] from [[Alpine Investors]]' "People First Operating Rhythm," used to keep a company's vision, strategy, and goals connected and legible to everyone.

## Key Information

- Structure: vision and values in the first column, strategic intentions and KPIs in the second, annual goals in the third, and quarterly goals in the fourth — so any conversation about prioritization stays explicitly tied back to the company's core strategy and vision, rather than living in disconnected documents/conversations.
- Motivating problem: at most companies, if you ask an employee what their top three priorities are and how they connect to the company vision, they can't easily answer — vision, strategy, goals, and values are typically scattered across different artifacts and communicated with wildly different intensity.
- Alpine Investors reports its portfolio companies that implement this rhythm show higher returns.
- Paired with a recurring cadence, not just a static document: quarterly "balcony" reflection sessions (stepping off "the dance floor" of day-to-day execution to look down at the whole business) asking what worked, what didn't, and — Lockett's favorite prompt — "what's an inconvenient truth?" (surfacing things nobody's been saying because everyone's too busy).
- Framed as structurally similar to [[Rachel Lockett]]'s advice to individuals about protecting space to "tinker, reflect, and create" — leadership teams need the same kind of deliberate, spacious time away from being "in" the business to work "on" it.
