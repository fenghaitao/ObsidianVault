## Definition

Impact is the concrete, measurable outcomes of engineering work — the basis on which software engineers are compensated and rewarded, rather than time spent or years of experience. It is team-specific: on ads teams it's money, on infrastructure teams it's latency or cost, on growth teams it's user metrics.

## Key Information

- Impact is "just another way of saying the concrete measurable outcomes of your work"
- The tech industry is unique because you're not compensated based on time spent or years of experience — you're rewarded for measurable outcomes
- Impact depends on the team: ads teams measure money, infrastructure teams measure latency or database cost, growth teams measure daily active users or sign-up rates
- A one-line code change that makes a 20% improvement in something that matters is far more valuable than "hundreds of thousands of lines doing random things no one cares about"
- Writing 10K lines of code fixing typos has essentially no impact compared to 2x-ing company revenue
- You should "100% learn" what impact means on your specific team from your manager, because that's how you're rewarded

### David Fowler on Impact vs Intelligence
- His senior promotion separated being smart from making impact: "being smart is something else" — promotions are about impact
- The shift to "outcome focused" was the leap from senior to principal: impact through others, not typing harder

### Bobby Holley on Impact vs Title
- "Focus on impact and not the title"; pinning leadership down on "what boxes can I check?" is annoying and doesn't land
- The better conversation is "how do I grow my career and have more impact?" — am I working on the most important thing?
- Mozilla's distinguished-engineer criteria shifted from "owns the most code" to industry-changing impact (protocols, WebAssembly, codecs)
