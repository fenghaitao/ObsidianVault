## Definition

[[Dr. Becky Kennedy]]'s principle that people cooperate once they feel seen as a full person embedded in their own priorities — not as an object to be maneuvered toward what you want — so connection has to precede any request or correction.

## Key Information

- Kennedy compares the same request delivered two ways: "Becky, we have to do our taxes" while she's finally sitting down to read, framed as "you don't listen, you have a listening problem" (no connection) versus "I realize I didn't tell you this earlier, you look like you're just settling in, can we get on top of this together, I know we're on the same team" (connection first) — she says the second version makes her actual compliance "skyrocket."
- Connection works because it "forms a bridge": the other person can walk from their world back into yours once they feel joined in theirs, rather than treated as an obstacle.
- The first step is a mindset, not a script: entering an interaction "without an agenda," even for 30 seconds, is what makes connection read as genuine rather than transactional — Kennedy notes that connecting-in-order-to-get-compliance is felt by the other person as manipulative ("it's going to feel dirty").
- Named tension: Kennedy says efficiency and relationship-building are often in opposition, and that people who are rewarded at work for efficiency (herself included) develop "a little morality" about wanting others to "get to the point" — which actively works against connection. She calls genuinely present, unhurried moments "low stim" and says that feeling is often a sign she's doing it right, not wasting time.
- Small-scale examples: watching and narrating what a kid is doing instead of peppering them with questions; at work, asking "tell me a little about your long weekend" and actually listening rather than counting down to redirect to this week's priorities.
- Explicitly linked by [[Lenny Rachitsky]] to *[[Radical Candor]]*'s "challenge directly but care deeply" framing — connecting before correcting is offered as the "connect" half of that same idea.
