## Definition

North Star Goal is a career-strategy framework of anchoring every role and decision to one long-term destination, so that each move deliberately fills a capability gap on the way there.

## Key Information

- The Snapchat-director guest anchored his entire career to becoming a CTO of an AI company, a goal he held since before graduating with his machine-learning PhD
- He chose roles to fill specific gaps on the way to that goal — having come from research, he acknowledged he understood neither industry operations, infrastructure/backend, frontend, nor product
- At Snapchat he chose Director of Camera (the first page of the app) over the more comfortable Director of Data, learning iOS and Android from scratch for ~2 months, to gain product and consumer-surface experience toward becoming a CTO
- He chose Facebook early on (fast-growing, engineering-first, broad influence) because it fit the north-star criteria
- Later role decisions were anchored to the north star rather than headcount or valuation — "I actually want less people to report to me"; he even anticipates "two people can make a $1B company"
- Weekly time allocation flows from the goal: at the start of each week choose the top three things to achieve and focus only on those, recalibrating toward which themes give the best "bang for the buck" on the north star
- The guest contrasts this with conventional goals like "go as high as I can" or "make as much money as I can," noting there is no right answer but that a north star makes hard decisions easier
