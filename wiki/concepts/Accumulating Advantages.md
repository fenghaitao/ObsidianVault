## Definition

[[Keith Rabois]]'s core due-diligence question for early-stage investing (asked for ~25 years): does this business have a path to advantages that compound over time, making it better and easier to win as it grows — not just an initial edge?

## Key Information

- Network effects are the most commonly cited example, but Rabois stresses they're only one species of accumulating advantage, not the whole category.
- What he looks for at the earliest stages isn't a demonstrated accumulating advantage (too high a bar pre-traction) but whether a founder can conceptually *articulate* where such advantages could emerge and how/when they'd start compounding.
- Framed as necessary because building a durable company requires planning for an 8-20 year horizon, during which foundation-model progress alone could erode any non-durable edge — making this question especially urgent for AI-era startups.
- Related concept from the same episode: [[Software Is Not A Moat]] (Evan Spiegel, episode 12) makes a parallel argument that feature-level software advantages don't compound the way platforms/ecosystems/hardware do.
