## Definition

[[OpenAI]]'s deliberate policy (per [[Sherwin Wu]]) of keeping its API platform neutral and open — every model shipped in a first-party product also ships in the API, competitors aren't blocked — framed as a direct expression of its charter mission to spread AGI's benefits to all of humanity.

## Key Information

- Reasoning per Wu: OpenAI alone cannot realistically reach "all of humanity" directly, so it depends on third-party developers (e.g., someone building a customer-support bot for podcasters) to extend its reach into every use case and market it can't build for directly.
- Practical policy: OpenAI has never held back a capability from the API to advantage a first-party product; codex-optimized models eventually flow into the API as well.
- Wu's advice to founders worried about OpenAI (or any large lab) "squashing" their startup idea: don't over-index on this risk — startups that fail almost always fail because they didn't build something customers loved, not because a big lab entered their space; the addressable opportunity is so large that the standard VC playbook of avoiding competitive overlaps has already broken down industry-wide.
- Connects to OpenAI's free-tier philosophy: Wu argues the gap in AI capability between a paying "pro" user and a free user is comparatively small relative to history (everyone effectively gets a very capable model, unlike, e.g., traditional wealth-gated resources), which the company frames as a meaningful form of the "benefit all of humanity" mission in practice.
