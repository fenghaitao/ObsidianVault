## Definition

AI for journalism is the application of AI tools to help journalists find and tell stories with data. Simon Wilson argues that journalists are uniquely well-equipped to work with AI despite its hallucination problem, because "journalists deal with untrustworthy sources all the time" — they already know how to verify information from unreliable sources.

## Key Information

- Simon Wilson's day job focus: building open-source tools (Datasette) for data journalism
- The apparent contradiction: AI hallucinates and makes things up, which seems like a terrible fit for journalism where "the whole idea is to find the truth"
- The resolution: "journalists deal with untrustworthy sources all the time. Like the art of journalism is you talk to a bunch of people and some of them lie to you and you figure out what's true. So, as long as the journalist treats the AI as yet another unreliable source, they're actually better equipped to work with AI than most other professions"
- Simon is building tools that let journalists: feed in PDFs of police reports, extract key details, build database tables, and run SQL queries
- His goal: "I want somebody in the world to win a Pulitzer Prize where my software was like 3% of what they used"
- This also serves as AI research: "It's also great from an AI research point to have real software that I'm working on that uses this"
