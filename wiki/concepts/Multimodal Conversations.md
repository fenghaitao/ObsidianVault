## Definition
Multimodal Conversations are AI interactions that combine voice and visual UI simultaneously, enabling parallel input/output patterns where users speak their intent and receive rich visual responses (diagrams, text, generated UI), rather than being limited to voice-only or text-only interaction.

## Key Information
- Current voice interfaces are binary: either voice interaction or another mode, not both blended
- Users want to speak their thoughts out (high bandwidth output from their head) and receive information-rich visual responses back
- Voice alone has lower information density per second than reading text or viewing diagrams
- The desired pattern: "single input, my voice" → "parallel output" (maybe some voice, but mostly generated UI, diagrams, text)
- Joe Reeve describes wanting to talk to a "product manager agent" that delegates to coding agents, not talk directly to the coding agent
- The Claude app is cited as doing "interesting stuff" — showing higher-level sections during voice interactions that users can tap on
- A proposed pattern: hold-to-talk for voice input, then see visual cues about what the agent wants to say
- Voice provides "companionship" that lessens loneliness during creative work, even if information density is lower
- The visual cortex is evolutionarily older than voice/text processing — seeing something carries different cognitive weight
- Conciseness in voice sounds rude in a way that concise text doesn't — different modalities have different social dynamics
- The "Her moment" for voice AI hasn't fully arrived; multimodal may be the bridge
