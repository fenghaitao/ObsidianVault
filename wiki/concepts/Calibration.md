## Definition

Calibration is the (often closed-door) meeting where managers compare employees' performance, ratings, and promotion cases across a team or organization, applying the company's leveling and performance rules.

## Key Information

- Laurent learned the "secrets" and "untold rules" of calibration when he became a manager.
- Calibration decisions cover leveling, ratings, and promotions; the untold rules are local to specific teams and orgs and evolve as organizations mature.
- Example rules: transitioning roles less than ~2.5 months before a review means an automatic "meets"; new grads can't be promoted before a minimum tenure because "we never had one that did that."
- Managers represent the company's interest first, before their reports — which is itself an untold rule requiring strong value alignment.
- Laurent's transparency bar: "if you teleport anybody into that room at any point in time and they see what's happening, they would be proud of the work we're doing" — no scheming or objectively unfair behavior.
- To dispute a rule: gather evidence and make your case, but first understand the story behind the rule (some are justified, some are stale precedents); have the conversation one-on-one rather than in the room.
- Intern calibration is "one of the meetings where people cry the most," because every manager is over-attached to their own intern and assumes exceeding expectations/return offer.
