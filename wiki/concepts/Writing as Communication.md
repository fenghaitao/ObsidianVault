## Definition

Writing as Communication is the principle that writing is a core part of the software engineering job — not a peripheral activity. It is the primary mechanism for influencing others, getting feedback, and documenting impact at scale.

## Key Information

- Ryan stated that "writing is the job" — he hated writing in high school but realized in industry that writing is how you influence others
- Every code change has writing around it: design docs before coding (to get feedback), code descriptions, and results write-ups after landing
- Writing is one of the best ways to build visibility after completing good work — you can make a post, write an email, or share results that demonstrate impact
- Writing is also a way to advocate for yourself: hit a milestone, write about it, and put the idea of your success in decision-makers' heads
- The "tree falling in a forest" analogy: if you write great code but nobody knows about it, did it really happen? Writing ensures your work is known

### Stefan Mai's Writing Advice
- Learn to be a critic first: read docs and notice what hits you, what's imprecise, and where the logic breaks down
- Amazon's PR FAQ format: start by making yourself excited about the delivered result ("50% less time here"), not "a single pane of glass"
- If you read your own draft and it bores you, it would bore everyone — that self-check is the first step to good writing
- He credits Amazon's writing culture for building thinking that stuck with him

### David Singleton's Writing Practice
- Writing is his most leveraged communication: a good document gets passed around, read once, and revisited, reaching far more than a live talk
- He improves important drafts by sharing them with reviewers and crediting them (a habit learned at Stripe from watching others)
- Condense the most important message into a short, clear document; you don't need reviewers for every email, but do for what matters

### Bobby Holley's "Writing Things Down"
- Mozilla's internal memo "writing things down" argues writing is both the artifact (for coordination and review) and the process (writing is thinking and refining ideas)
- Prefer short documents: when you run out of things to say, either refine your ideas more or you're done — never add fluff
- Don't use an LLM to produce text under your name; you lose the thinking that happens while writing, which is the growth

### Leslie Lamport on Writing to Think

- "If you think you know something but don't write it down, you only think you know it" — that's where errors come from.
- Writing is conducive to good thinking because "it's very easy to fool yourself"; explaining something forces you to find what you haven't said.
- Developed hierarchical proofs (a proof as a sequence of steps, each with its own proof) that worked for correctness proofs and ordinary math; mathematicians reacted with anger and fear at the thought of writing proofs a machine could check.
- Endorses "write the instruction manual before you write the program" — writing LaTeX's book forced changes to LaTeX itself.
