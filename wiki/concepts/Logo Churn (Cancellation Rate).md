## Definition

Logo churn (customer cancellation rate) is the percentage of customers who cancel in a given period. Jason Cohen argues it is the single most important growth metric because cancellations grow exponentially with company size, setting a hard mathematical ceiling on how large the company can ever become.

## The Math

**Maximum customers = new customers per month ÷ cancellation rate**

Example: A company adding 100 customers/month with 5% monthly churn will never exceed 2,000 customers (100 ÷ 0.05 = 2,000). As you approach this ceiling, growth slows to near zero.

## Why It's the Worst Problem

1. **Cancellations grow automatically with size.** Triple the customer base, and cancellations triple — even if the rate stays the same. Marketing doesn't scale this way; it only grows as fast as you can improve it.

2. **Nothing you can do after they leave.** No saving them, no upselling them, no recovering the acquisition cost.

3. **Negative externalities.** Canceling customers often leave negative reviews or social media complaints, actively hurting future growth.

4. **Emotional signal.** The customer went through the entire gauntlet (discovery, homepage, pricing page, purchase, onboarding) and still chose to leave. Something is fundamentally broken.

## Benchmarks

- **SMB:** 2% monthly churn is good; anything above 3% is "terrible" (Jason Cohen)
- The threshold varies by market segment; SMB naturally has higher churn than enterprise
- The cancellation rate directly determines the company's maximum possible size

## Leaky Bucket Analogy

The classic leaky bucket metaphor is incomplete: the leaks don't just exist, they **automatically increase** in absolute volume as the bucket gets bigger, while the water being added (marketing) only increases through deliberate effort.
