## Definition

Standing etiquette covers when to stand in social situations: stand when people leave the table, stand to shake hands (don't shake hands while sitting), and in formal settings, stand when someone joins the table.

## Key Information

- Sam Lessin: "You should stand when people leave the table. Not ridiculously. Stand to shake hands. Don't be sitting when you're shaking hands."
- Traditional rule: when a woman comes to the table, you stand. Sam's six-year-old has started doing this at home.
- Sam's modern take: "Whenever anyone joins you for a meal, whether it's a man or woman, you stand up" — this might be a better modern approach
- The debate is ongoing in his household: "It's an ongoing somewhat hilarious debate at our dinner table"
- The underlying principle: standing shows awareness and respect
