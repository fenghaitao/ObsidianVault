## Definition
Compute-Capability Proportionality is the thesis that, under standard economic assumptions and absent a software-only singularity, growth in compute investment and growth in AI capability (as measured by time horizon) should be causally proportional. A slowdown in compute growth would therefore imply a proportional slowdown in AI capability growth.

## Key Information
- Presented by Joel Becker as a framework for understanding the relationship between compute scaling and AI progress
- If compute growth halves, time horizon growth should also approximately halve under this model
- This proportionality holds only while "software-only singularity" is not possible — if AI can dramatically improve capabilities relative to fixed compute, the relationship breaks
- Compute constraints include physical limits (power), economic limits (dollars), and manufacturing bottlenecks
- Epoch's analysis suggests compute constraints may not bite through 2030 but could become significant after
- The implication is that AI capability milestones could be significantly delayed if compute growth slows
- Log-linear trends have been "a very highly underrated forecasting tool" that have done "extremely well over now many orders of magnitude"
