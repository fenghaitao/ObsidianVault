## Definition
Living memory is the unique, compounding set of customer interaction signals — edge cases, corrections, emotional intent, and actual behavior at a company's specific scale and context — that forms the true competitive moat in an AI-recursive world. It is distinguished from transactional memory (ERP, CRM, SOPs), which every competitor has a version of.

## Key Information
- Coined by Jess Grogan-Avignon and Jack Wang of Accenture
- In a recursive world where AI codes AI, anything shipped can be cloned the minute it goes viral
- Transactional memory (ERP, CRM, SOPs) gets you to the table — it's a floor, not a fortress
- Living memory is what happens when a customer touches your product: edge cases, corrections, emotional intent, actual behavior
- These signals belong uniquely to you — they cannot be copied
- The day you ship is not the finish line; it's when the race actually begins
- Competitive advantage comes from how quickly you can compound and iterate, turning signals into value
- Every feature should either generate a feedback signal or deliver on what the signal has already taught you
- If a feature does neither, you're building something anyone can copy
- For CEOs: your moat is not in what you hold from yesterday — it's in what you are learning and compounding every day
