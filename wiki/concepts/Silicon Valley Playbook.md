## Definition

The Silicon Valley playbook is the conventional startup approach of raising VC funding, pivoting frequently, blitz-scaling, and chasing growth metrics. Edwin Chen explicitly rejects this approach and advocates for a contrarian model.

## Key Information

- The standard playbook: "get product market fit by pivoting every two weeks, chase growth and engagement with all of these dark patterns, and blitz scale by hiring as fast as possible"
- Edwin's contrarian advice: "Don't pivot. Don't blitz scale. Don't hire that Stanford grad who simply wants to add a hot company to your resume. Just build the one thing only you could build"
- The playbook produces "by the book companies" — "some founder who was doing crypto in 2020 and then pivoted to NFTs in 2022 and now they're an AI company. There's no consistency. There's no mission. They're just chasing valuations"
- "Silicon Valley loves to scorn Wall Street for focusing on money. But honestly, most of Silicon Valley is chasing the same thing"
- The playbook means founders are selected for pitching and hyping ability rather than technology or product expertise
- Edwin hopes AI will disrupt this: "fewer employees means less capital. Less capital means you don't need to raise. So instead of companies started by founders who are great at pitching... you'll get founders who are really great at technology or product"
- "Startups are supposed to be about taking big risks to build something that you really believe in. But if you're constantly pivoting, you're not taking any risks. You're just trying to make a quick buck"
