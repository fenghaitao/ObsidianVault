## Definition

Writing as Leverage is the idea that strong writing ability is a superpower for software engineers, providing outsized influence and reach. Philip Su developed this skill deliberately after initially disrespecting soft skills, and his essays became legendary within Meta.

## Key Information

### Philip Su's Writing Journey
- Philip was initially bad at writing — he came from a culture that respected hard sciences and viewed soft skills as "fluff with no right answer"
- He started re-reading literary classics (Hemingway, The Great Gatsby, Virginia Woolf, David Foster Wallace, Alice Munro, Pilgrim at Tinker Creek) and developed a love of language
- Virginia Woolf's principle: "to write well you need to read well"
- Philip realized writing was a valuable skill "every bit as valuable as a hard skill, like a science skill"
- He rewrites his posts multiple times before publishing — "a lot of writing better is being willing to rewrite multiple times"
- He continues to learn: when he reads others' Substack posts, he notices clever rhetorical techniques and tries to remember them
- He hired a professional editor (Rebecca Ambrose) for his podcast "Peak Salvation" — the only time he used an editor

### Specific Techniques
- Non-chronological narratives can be more compelling than chronological ones when there's a different thematic arc threaded through a person's life
- Philip's essay "As Good As It Gets" (internal to Meta) used creative non-linear storytelling that Ryan L. Peterman found exceptionally effective
- Some ideas are best communicated through fragments rather than full narrative bits — "it somehow gets the idea across"

### Why Writing Matters for Engineers
- Writing provides leverage — it amplifies your reach and influence
- Philip's posts were still being discovered and commented on years later inside Meta

### Marc Brooker

- Writing scales the impact of your expertise in space and time; a decade-old blog post can still be shared and discovered.
- Writing forces a level of mental clarity that speaking and slide decks don't — Brooker calls this a core Amazon cultural belief and notes Leslie Lamport takes it further into formal mathematics.
- Documenting core technical decisions is useful twice over: it records which decisions were carefully chosen versus arbitrary, so later engineers know which to revisit and which were deliberate.
- He writes, sometimes just for himself, to sharpen his own thinking.
