## Definition

The pod model is an organizational structure where small, cross-functional teams (pods) are assembled for a specific mission over a finite period (e.g., a quarter), then reassembled into different configurations. It draws an analogy to Navy SEAL teams.

## Key Information

- LinkedIn is using pods as the organizational manifestation of the full-stack builder model.
- Pods are smaller than traditional teams and focused on the problem/mission rather than functional roles.
- Team members are expected to flex across functions (engineering, design, PM) rather than operating in rigid trios.
- Pods are assembled for about a quarter, then reassembled into different configurations for new missions.
- The analogy is Navy SEALs: cross-trained operators who specialize in the mission, operate in small teams, and can be assembled and reassembled quickly.
- This structure enables organizations to be more nimble, adaptive, and resilient — matching the pace of change to the pace of response.
- The model contrasts with traditional large, functionally-siloed teams that remain static.
