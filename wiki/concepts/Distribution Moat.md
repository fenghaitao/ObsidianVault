## Definition

[[Benedict Evans]]'s argument that as AI chatbot products commoditize and converge on similar quality, existing distribution (not product superiority) becomes the deciding factor in adoption — a bigger moat than the underlying model itself.

## Key Information

- Illustrated by [[Google]] pushing [[Gemini]] via Android/Chromebook integrations and [[Meta]] "spraying" its assistant across every product surface — in both cases winning usage-survey share despite no clear product edge over [[ChatGPT]].
- Evans's framing: for a "normal person," there is no meaningful difference between the major chatbots, so whichever company can put its assistant in front of the most existing users wins by default.
- [[Apple]]'s roughly billion-device install base is cited as a major distribution asset regardless of which underlying foundation model (possibly Gemini) powers a future Apple Intelligence.
- Connects to Evans's broader "capital" question of whether foundation models get durable pricing power up the stack (Windows-style) or become commodity infrastructure (AWS-style) — distribution matters more precisely because the underlying models themselves may not be differentiated. See [[Model Commoditization]].
