## Definition

The "shouty rooster" is a Slack feature that warns users when they use @everyone or @channel, showing an animated rooster with sound waves and a message about how many people across how many time zones will receive a notification. It was designed to shape communication culture and prevent notification abuse.

## Key Information

- **Problem**: @everyone and @channel notifications were being abused — once one person used it, others felt their message was more important and also used it, creating a tragedy of the commons
- **Solution**: an animated rooster pops up with sound waves, saying "Hey, this is going to cause a notification for 147 people in eight different time zones. Are you sure you want to send this message?"
- **Internal motto**: "Don't be a cock" (not used publicly)
- **Effectiveness**: dramatically reduced @everyone/@channel abuse
- **Dual purpose**: shaped behavior AND taught people how the product worked (many didn't know about @everyone/@channel or didn't think about the cost)
- **Implementation**: trivially easy to implement, made a really big difference
- The feature still exists in Slack today
