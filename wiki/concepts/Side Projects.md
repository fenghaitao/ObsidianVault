## Definition

Side Projects are software projects built outside of work or formal education. They are valuable for learning, demonstrating passion, and building practical skills, especially when they solve real problems the builder personally experiences.

## Key Information

- Evan's advice: "observe the problems around you and then build some software to solve it — that's the side project you should be working on"
- Building a "Redis clone or something like that" is "better than nothing but it's not letting you do that full end-to-end path of learning"
- A project that "no one uses" is "essentially the same as a college class project" — you won't get nearly as much out of it
- Ryan's example: he built an occupancy sensor for his IEEE club lab using a Raspberry Pi that tracked officers' devices via MAC addresses, with a Slack bot you could ping — this was "useful" and he talked about it in interviews
- Evan's example: the Cornell hacking club was essentially a massive side project where he spent "all day hacking and writing code" with brilliant friends, building "pretty sophisticated" things
- Side projects should be driven by passion and solving real problems — "you're not going to be as passionate about it or want to be working on it later as you would be something that solves a real problem"
- Side projects are especially valuable for new grads and early-career engineers to demonstrate capability and passion

### Rahul's Side Projects
- Rahul deliberately chose Android at Pinterest so he could build side projects end-to-end on his own
- His whole family was on Android, so he could show them apps he built that thousands of people could use
- He wanted to have his name on an app in the Play Store — that was "really compelling"
- The sandbox approach his mentor taught him (creating mini Android apps for faster iteration) also translated directly to side project ability
- He published Android tutorials on YouTube starting in 2019, which gave him signal to leave Meta and start a company
- He described the joy of getting comments on his tutorials: "this is such a magical, helpful tutorial"
- Published Android apps as side projects while working at Meta
