## Definition

A Landing Team is a group of experienced engineers brought from a company's headquarters to bootstrap a new remote office, carrying the company's culture and engineering practices. Philip Su used this approach to build Meta's London office.

## Key Information

### Philip Su's Landing Team Approach
- Philip brought 12 people from Meta's headquarters to start the London engineering office
- He interviewed ~50 volunteers and selected 12 based on their culture-carrying ability, interviewing not only the candidates but also their peers and managers
- Each person was asked to commit to two years minimum — a verbal commitment with no compensation incentive, but Philip had a serious conversation about it
- Philip asked each landing team member to bring over a project, ensuring they could lead work
- The two-year commitment was critical: it's "very difficult to maintain culture in those early growth periods where the percentage of new people is commensurate with the percentage of old people"
- Many companies make the mistake of sending a landing team for only 3-6 months, then leaving the site on its own
- The result: ~4 people from the original landing team were still in London 15 years later, having become British citizens

### Origin of the Two-Year Commitment
- David Singleton (former Site Director of Google London, later Stripe CTO) advised Philip that Google had experimented with landing teams and concluded the minimum commitment should be two years
- Shrep (Meta CTO) specified the landing team should have enough engineers for two full interview loops (~10-12 people)
