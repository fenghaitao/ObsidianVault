## Definition

A principle for interviews and new roles: don't overpromise on what you can deliver just to get the job. Set realistic expectations, then exceed them. Overpromising and underdelivering will haunt your reputation, while underpromising and overdelivering builds trust and credibility.

## Key Information

- Jacob: "Under promise, over deliver. Always. Under promise, over deliver."
- Don't say "I'm going to fix your onboarding flow" before you've fully understood the problem
- "I wouldn't be so sure that I'm going to fix the onboarding flow. I wouldn't be so sure that you know that's the problem yet."
- The longer you slow down the hiring process, the more you understand about what the company really needs — and the more realistic your promises can be
- "If this guy can't deliver, it's not going to happen. If this gal can't deliver, that's frustrating."
- "I'm not suggesting go so far outside of your comfort zone that you feel like you can't do it. Because that reputation will haunt you."
- The principle applies to the balance between confidence and honesty: be confident in your abilities to achieve something big, but don't overpromise
