## Definition

A polite fiction is a carefully worded message that states what you want — including the implied consequence if refused — while staying warm, non-threatening, and hard to argue with. Ethan Evans calls the underlying skill "being interpersonally warm while being professionally firm."

## Key Information

- Example: "My career is very important to me. I need to understand how important it is to Amazon… if it's not, I need to think about that." — conveys "I might leave" without a direct threat that a manager could label extortion.
- Example: "Once I give my word, I can't go back on that" — how he forced a leader to see there was no option of keeping him in the current role.
- Frame asks as help ("How can you help me?") and avoid seeing the other person as an obstacle; see them as a human trying their best (Leadership and Self-Deception, by the Arbinger Institute).
- Pre-plan a few key phrases before the room; most of these conversations are on the calendar, not spur of the moment.
- Body language matters: the same words delivered warmly versus angrily produce opposite results.
- Both the surface story and the underlying message can be true at the same time.
