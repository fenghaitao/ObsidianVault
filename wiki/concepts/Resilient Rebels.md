## Definition

Resilient rebels are people (kids or adults) who present as strong-willed or defiant, but whose core fear is the loss of control. They are very attuned to people stepping into their domain, and if they sense it, they resist. In the workplace, they can be tricky to collaborate with — but their reactions may be about broader themes of control and independence, not the specific issue at hand.

## Key Information

- "I call these the resilient rebels. They present as strong-willed defiant kids whose core fear is the loss of control."
- "They are very very attuned to people kind of stepping into their domain and if they smell it they resist."
- Workplace parallel: "Well, we have those as adults too. They could be tricky to collaborate with."
- Example: "Why do I ask someone to switch a meeting time and it feels like I took a knife to their heart the way they react?"
- The reaction may not be about the meeting time — it may be about: "Oh, someone got promoted before they did on their team? They just had a change in manager? They just had their vacation denied?"
- Practical approach: "Is there actually what we're talking about and it's manifesting as this difficulty changing meeting time, but actually there's other themes of control and independence at play?"
- Naming your intention upfront helps: "Hey, I want to look over this project together because I want to get ahead of some things and I think that's actually going to help you do the most independent work."
- "When you name the intention very very clearly for someone, they're much more likely to interpret your behavior through the intention you just named."
- Micromanagement parallel: "This is why people in part hate micromanagement — like I want to be in control of something."
