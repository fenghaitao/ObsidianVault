## Definition

[[Dr. Becky Kennedy]]'s name for a temperament pattern — in kids and, by extension, adult coworkers — whose core fear is loss of control, and who present as strong-willed or defiant because they are highly attuned to anyone stepping into their domain of autonomy.

## Key Information

- Kennedy: "these present as like strong-willed, defiant kids... their core fear is the loss of control. They are very, very attuned to people kind of stepping into their domain, and if they smell it, they resist."
- Origin illustration: toddlers control very little in their lives (mainly what goes into and comes out of their body, hence "potty learning" being fraught) — the first ten minutes of a child's day (being woken up, told the weather, told what to wear, told what's for breakfast) already involves near-total loss of autonomy, which Kennedy says would put most adults "in a bad mood the rest of the day" too.
- Workplace translation: a resilient rebel might react to something as small as a meeting-time change "like you took a knife to their heart" — not because the change itself matters, but because it triggers their control/independence theme, sometimes compounded by an unrelated recent loss of control (e.g., being passed over for promotion, a manager change, a denied vacation request).
- Recommended leadership response: explicitly name what someone owns/drives on a project versus where a manager will be involved and why (naming intention up front), since resilient rebels are especially likely to misinterpret unexplained involvement as an attempt to control them; this is closely tied to the broader theme of why people resent micromanagement.
- Contrasted in this episode with [[Deeply Feeling Kids]] (core fear: being "too much"), the other named temperament pattern Kennedy discusses — her second child's temperament (the button-pressing example) is cited as a resilient-rebel illustration, described as "0% people-pleasing."
