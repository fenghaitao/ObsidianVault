## Definition

A contextual assistant is an AI agent that understands what the user is currently doing and offers help at the right moment, in the right context, without requiring the user to explicitly provide context or switch tools.

## Key Information

- Alexander Imbiricos's startup was a screen-sharing pair programming tool, and the idea was to build a "contextual desktop assistant"
- The core insight: "it's really annoying to have to give all your context to an assistant and then to figure out how it can help you"
- If the assistant could "just understand what you were trying to do, then it could maximally accelerate you"
- Codex is conceived as a contextual assistant, starting with coding tasks but expanding to broader contexts
- Atlas (OpenAI's browser) extends this to web-based work, understanding what you're looking at and offering relevant help
- The video game analogy: pressing X near an object does the right thing based on context — the AI should work the same way
- The alternative (push notifications) doesn't scale — thousands of AI notifications per day would be unbearable
- Contextual assistance keeps the user in flow while enabling the agent to take action on many more things
- Users have control boundaries: open things in the AI browser when you want help, use a regular browser when you don't
