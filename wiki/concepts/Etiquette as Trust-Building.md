## Definition

The deeper purpose of etiquette is not about following arbitrary rules — it's about building trust. When you show up with good etiquette, you signal that you understand social norms, can meet people where they're at, and are someone others can work with and trust.

## Key Information

- Sam Lessin: "You want to be able to show up in a way where people like, 'Okay, this is someone I can work with and trust.'"
- In an era of commoditized software and AI fear, trust is the differentiator
- "When you're asking people to trust you with their data, trust you with their business... understand how to meet people where they're at, build trust, mirror kind of expected behaviors — these are all like tools"
- The signaling of effort matters as much as the execution — people notice that you're trying
- Good etiquette should be invisible: "Etiquette is all this invisible stuff that you don't need to spend 80% of your brain on"
- The goal: "I've got this, we're on the same wavelength, my heart rate is low, I'm doing it properly"
