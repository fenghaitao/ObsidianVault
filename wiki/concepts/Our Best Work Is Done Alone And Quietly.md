## Definition

One of [[Applied Intuition]]'s core company values, per [[Qasar Younis]]: every minute spent on public-facing self-promotion (podcasts, posting on X) is a minute not spent on customers and product, which is the only thing that ultimately produces results. Kept the company deliberately under-the-radar for roughly a decade.

## Key Information

- Younis frames the pragmatic case for staying quiet as separate from — and prior to — any psychological explanation: focusing on craft and customers first increases the odds of success, and only after that can a company "go and scale" its public profile.
- He also offers a more personal explanation: neither he nor co-founder [[Peter Ludwig]] get much emotional satisfaction from public-facing work, and as an immigrant who grew up feeling on the outside of mainstream society (Pakistan to Warren, Michigan), he's innately skeptical of visibility-seeking as a goal in itself.
- Modeled explicitly on Berkshire Hathaway rather than a typical "Silicon Valley darling" company.
- Younis only began breaking from this value recently (joining X, per encouragement from [[Marc Andreessen]], [[Naval Ravikant]], and [[Elad Gil]]), reasoning that as the company becomes more influential, he has some responsibility to share his ideas more broadly.
