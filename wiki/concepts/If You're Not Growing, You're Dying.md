## Definition

"If you're not growing, you're dying" is a common business aphorism that Jason Cohen examines as the final question in his Stalled Growth Framework. He argues the phrase may apply more to the person than the company, and that stasis can be a valid and intentional choice.

## The Two Interpretations

### 1. Applies to the Company
- Investors often use this to push founders toward growth at all costs
- Stagnant companies may lose talent, relevance, and competitive position
- But: there are natural sizes for things. Many public companies don't grow, and that's fine.

### 2. Applies to the Person
- If you're not growing (learning, challenged, fulfilled), you may be dying inside
- Founders who retire often "go downhill" because they lose purpose
- The kind of people who build products and start companies are typically not people who want to do rote work for 20 years
- "You're a shark and you got to go"

## When Stasis Is Valid

- **37 Signals / Basecamp:** Bootstrap company that hit a natural limit, now maximizing profit instead of revenue. Founders earn millions in dividends. They don't want a bigger organization or different market.
- **CPA with a few clients:** Life is good. Not everyone needs to grow.
- **Lifestyle businesses:** Growth would mean becoming an organization the founder doesn't want to run.

## When to Act

If growth has genuinely stalled and every other option is exhausted, the question becomes existential:
- Do I need to leave the company?
- Should the company change drastically?
- Is it time to sell?
- Do I need a new chapter of life?
