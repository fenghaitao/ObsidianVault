## Definition

Financial Independence is the point at which someone no longer needs to work for money. Bryan Cantrill argues that treating it as a goal in itself is a mistake: defining it as "you never need to work for money again" still leaves the question "then what?"

## Key Information

- Cantrill observes "you and I are in such a well-compensated domain" that many material goods (a $6 cup of coffee in the Mission) are already attainable — so "financial independence" is often a poorly specified goal.
- His view: don't defer the meaningful thing until after financial independence; if a thing is what actually motivates you, "go off and do the thing that actually motivates you" and find a way to balance it with your career.
- The dot-com bust illustrated this: people who made money in the boom weren't that interested in tech to begin with and sold out; those who stayed were there for the work, not the money.
- He warns that once you achieve the goal ("I can buy any cup of coffee I want"), the question becomes "now what?" — and gives the example of someone buying six houses and realizing "six houses are kind of a pain in the ass."
- Post-achievement emptiness is a general pattern he calls "postpartum in engineering" — the letdown after shipping a long-sought milestone.
