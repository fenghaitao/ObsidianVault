## Definition

Defined (crediting Reid Hoffman's definition, per [[Mark Pincus]]) as blurring the lines between the virtual and the real — not an escapist, separate immersive world. Pincus has pursued a version of this he calls "life at the speed of play" for roughly 20 years, most recently via a project called "Earth."

## Key Information

- Pincus argues we're "getting more and more metaversian" simply as AI advances, without needing a dedicated escapist immersive world — the virtual and real are blurring together anyway.
- Pincus pulled the plug (for the fourth time) on his latest attempt at this vision, a project called "Earth" — a web-browser-based game engine — after four years and $25M, having been "too ambitious" and not achieving product-market fit; he describes becoming more inspired in the two weeks after killing it than in the preceding four years.
- Connected to Pincus's personal "why": building an "internet treasure," a service so embedded in daily life that people can't imagine living without it (a phrase he credits partly to [[Bing Gordon]], who predicts such products will one day be exhibited "in the Smithsonian").
