## Definition

The Challenger Disaster of AI is Simon Wilson's prediction that the AI industry will eventually experience a catastrophic security failure due to prompt injection, analogous to the Space Shuttle Challenger disaster. The prediction is based on the concept of "normalization of deviance" — the industry keeps using AI systems in increasingly unsafe ways without consequences, building institutional overconfidence until a major disaster occurs.

## Key Information

- Simon Wilson's recurring prediction, made every 6 months for the past 3 years
- Based on the Space Shuttle Challenger disaster (1986) and the sociological concept of "normalization of deviance"
- "Lots of people knew that those little O-rings were unreliable, but every single time you get away with launching a space shuttle without the O-rings failing, you institutionally feel more confident in what you're doing"
- "We've been using these systems in increasingly unsafe ways. This is going to catch up with us"
- The prediction hasn't happened yet, which Simon acknowledges: "It's like the black swan turkey chart where it's like the turkey is the most confident it's ever been it will live for a long time until the day they get eaten for Thanksgiving"
- The goal of the prediction: "That will hopefully help us stop trying to figure out how not to do this" — a wake-up call for the industry
- The underlying problem: prompt injection is fundamentally unsolvable ("I cannot imagine what that proof would look like"), and AI companies keep reporting incremental improvements (70% → 85% detection) without reaching 100%
