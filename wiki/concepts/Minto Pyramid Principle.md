## Definition

Presentation framework by Barbara Minto (reportedly the first female consultant at McKinsey, working in the 1950s-60s): structure business communication backwards from how it was developed — lead with the recommendation/conclusion first, then the options explored, then the supporting evidence — rather than walking through the process chronologically (research → analysis → conclusion → recommendation).

## Key Information

- Raised by [[Lenny Rachitsky]] and endorsed (with a caveat) by [[Jessica Fain]] as consistent with her own advice: don't lead with exhaustive proof-of-work, since executives are impatient and structurally short on attention (see [[Strobe Light Calendar Model]]).
- Fain's caveat: individual communication-style preference still matters more than a single universal template — some executives want a doc with quiet reading time, others hate PowerPoint, others want data only, no qualitative research — so use the Minto structure as a default but adapt to the specific person's preferred format.
