## Definition

[[Dr. Becky Kennedy]]'s two-part support formula for helping someone (kid or adult) through a hard task or feeling: validate that the difficulty is real ("I believe you") while also holding a more capable vision of them than they can currently see for themselves ("I believe in you").

## Key Information

- Kennedy's metaphor: someone struggling is "in a hole" without visibility to the way out — as a relational species, what they need is someone they trust to have "one foot in the hole" with them (I believe you) and "one foot out" of the hole (I believe in you). One foot in without one foot out means the supporter has collapsed into the hole with them; one foot out without one foot in feels dismissive.
- Example script (a nervous kid before a hard soccer practice, translatable to a nervous employee before a hard project): "I believe you — this is hard, you've never done something like this before, of course you're nervous, that makes sense... I believe in you — the reason we think you can take this on is because we know you can figure this out. I'm here to help along the way, but I want you to hear that from me."
- Explicitly names this as one of the most common leadership/parenting failure modes: doing the "I believe you" (validation) part but forgetting "I believe in you" — Kennedy says this is a major reason anxiety gets mishandled at any age, because validation alone can read as "you're right to be overwhelmed" rather than "you're capable of getting through this."
- Diagnostic exercise she recommends: ask yourself which half is easier for you personally — do you default to "come on, it's no big deal, you can do it" (only I-believe-in-you) or do you tend to "go into the hole" with someone (only I-believe-you)? Neither is morally better; the value is in noticing your own default so you can consciously supply the missing half.
- Illustrated with a personal anecdote: instead of finishing a hard puzzle for her whining child, Kennedy said "this is a hard puzzle, it makes sense you're frustrated... but I want to tell you why I'm not going to do this for you — there's no better feeling than watching yourself make progress on something you didn't think you could do, and I won't take that feeling away from you."
- Framed as a companion formula to [[Sturdy Leadership]] (which has the same "validate the feeling without being overwhelmed by it" structure) and to [[Resilience Over Happiness]] (the long-term payoff of not shielding someone from difficulty).
