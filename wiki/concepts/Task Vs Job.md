## Definition

[[Benedict Evans]]'s central analytic framework for thinking about AI and jobs: a "job" is a bundle of "tasks," and automating one task inside a job doesn't necessarily remove the reason the job (or the person doing it) exists.

## Key Information

- Some jobs are effectively a single task — e.g. a pre-1950s elevator operator — and those get fully automated away when the task is automated.
- Most jobs are bundles of tasks: automating one (e.g., an AI writing the PowerPoint slides) doesn't remove the underlying reason a McKinsey consultant was hired — the real product is walking the organization and diagnosing the actual problem, not the slide deck itself.
- Explains why AI can visibly automate many individual tasks inside knowledge work without necessarily eliminating the jobs those tasks sit inside — see [[Jevons Paradox]] for the related price-elasticity mechanism (cheaper tasks can expand, not shrink, demand for the surrounding profession).
- One of the three lenses (alongside capital and deployment) structuring Evans's "AI is eating the world" presentation.
- Independently echoed by [[Marc Andreessen]] (episode 29) with his own historical example: executives in 1970 never touched a typewriter or computer themselves — they dictated to secretaries, who typed and mailed letters. When email arrived, secretaries' tasks shifted (receiving/printing emails for the executive) but the job persisted; today executives write their own emails, and secretaries' job persists too, but its tasks shifted again (travel planning, event coordination). Andreessen's summary: "the job persists longer than the individual tasks," and career strategy under AI should focus on swapping/adding tasks and scope rather than assuming the job itself disappears.
