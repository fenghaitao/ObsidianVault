## Definition

A "late-joining co-founder" is an executive who joins a company years after founding but has such transformative impact on the company's success, culture, and direction that they are effectively considered a co-founder. The term was coined by Reed Hoffman and popularized by Jason Cohen.

## Origin

- **Reed Hoffman** coined the term to describe **Jeff Weiner** at LinkedIn
- Weiner joined LinkedIn about four years after founding but was so impactful that Hoffman considered him a co-founder
- The term appears in Hoffman's book and LinkedIn lore

## WP Engine Example

- **Heather Brunner** joined WP Engine as CEO and has been there for 11+ years
- Jason Cohen uses the same term for her: "If I just told you that Heather was a co-founder, you'd say, 'Yeah, no kidding.'"
- Cohen and Brunner co-won Ernst & Young Entrepreneur of the Year 2017 for Central Texas
- Co-winners are rare for this award, underscoring the co-founder-level recognition

## Key Characteristics

- Joins after the company is established (not at founding)
- Has impact comparable to or exceeding what a founding co-founder would have
- Transforms not just the business results but the company culture and direction
- The test: if you told people they were a co-founder, no one would question it
