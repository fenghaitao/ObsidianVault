## Definition
Just-in-Time Specs is Chris Parsons' preferred approach to specification in AI-driven development. Instead of creating comprehensive specifications upfront (which risks waterfall), specifications are created as needed during the development loop, allowing the AI and human to adapt based on what's been learned.

## Key Information
- Chris Parsons' alternative to spec-driven development
- "I like just-in-time specs. I like the idea of building or thinking through what you're trying to build, creating some kind of plan in Claude Code and then executing it."
- Concerns about spec-driven development: tools may "fossilize" approaches that work today but won't work with future models; over-specifying risks recreating waterfall
- "I think it is obvious it's a truism that AI needs more context in order to do well. So, we should try to give it more context. But I think the idea of overdoing that and over-specking a project is one to be careful of"
- Also worries about over-structuring processes based on what works with today's agents, creating legacy processes that persist into 2030
- The loop approach naturally supports just-in-time specs: each iteration can refine understanding based on what was just built
