## Definition

[[Jason Cohen]]'s sequential five-question framework for diagnosing why a product/company's growth has stalled — and, equally, for finding new growth — applied strictly in order, since fixing a downstream issue doesn't help if an upstream one remains unaddressed.

## Key Information

1. **Are customers leaving?** (logo churn) — the worst problem, since it's irreversible and caps the business at a hard ceiling. See [[Logo Churn Ceiling]].
2. **Is pricing (and positioning) correct?** — most early/growing companies have simply guessed and never revisited pricing; price also determines which market segment you're actually selling into. See [[Pricing Selects The Market]].
3. **Are existing customers growing?** (net revenue retention) — since logo churn is a percentage that compounds with company size, only expansion revenue from the remaining customers (upgrades, usage growth) can offset it proportionally. See [[NRR Compounding Fallacy]].
4. **Are acquisition channels saturated?** — you can't "flog marketing" indefinitely; every channel has a practical ceiling and tends to decline over time, not just plateau. See [[Elephant Curve]].
5. **Do you actually need to grow?** — the most existential question: sometimes the honest answer is no (a stable, profitable bootstrapped business is a legitimate outcome), but for founders/builders wired to create, sustained stagnation may itself be the real problem — "if you're not growing, you're dying" reframed as being about the person, not just the company.
