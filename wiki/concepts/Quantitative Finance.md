## Definition

Quantitative finance ("quant") is the application of mathematics and computation to trading and finance, spanning alpha generation, monetization (execution), risk, and data analysis across hedge funds and market makers.

## Key Information

- Unified by a strong math background: stochastic calculus is the "finance 101" backbone, joined by numerics, numerical optimization/interpolation, numerical linear algebra, and increasingly ML and LLM research arms.
- Roles vary hugely by team, sector, hedge fund vs market maker, and front vs back office.
- Compensation is not standardized like tech levels: it is driven by firm/team/strategy performance, exposure to alpha, and seniority — making it more bonus-weighted and variable (and riskier) than tech comp.
- Secrecy is structural: an alpha is valuable because it is secret ("the more people who know about it, the less profitable"). This drives tight-lipped culture, uncorrelated pods, non-competes, and garden leave.
- Career contrast with AI research: skill sets overlap (math + CS), but quant can have better work-life balance, an up-or-out culture, and U-shaped job security (senior quants get expensive).
- Top firms cited by Nimit Sohoni: Renaissance Technologies ("RenTech"), Jane Street, Citadel, Jump Trading, Hudson River, plus elite smaller firms TGS, XTX, and Radix.
