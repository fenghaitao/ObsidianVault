## Definition
Hybrid pricing is a billing model combining a base subscription fee (predictable revenue, committed customer relationship) with a scaling usage fee (protects margins, allows experimentation). It has become the dominant pricing model for AI companies, growing from 6% adoption in 2024 to 41%.

## Key Information
- Combines base fee (subscription) with scaling/usage fee
- Base fee establishes a committed customer relationship and provides predictable revenue
- Usage fee scales with customer consumption, protecting margins from power users
- Allows customers to experiment without fear of unpredictable bills (base fee provides safety)
- Pure subscription risks margin erosion from power users (5-10% of users consuming 80% of compute)
- Pure usage-based makes customers hesitant to experiment deeply with the product
- Hybrid pricing grew 7x from 6% in 2024 to 41%
- 56% of AI company leaders now use hybrid pricing
- Companies using hybrid pricing include Intercom, Lovable, ElevenLabs, OpenAI, and Anthropic
- Even traditional SaaS companies are moving to hybrid pricing when they add LLM/AI features to avoid margin erosion
- Seat-based, SaaS, and subscription-only pricing are all declining as models change
