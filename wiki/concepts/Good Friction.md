## Definition

[[Amol]]'s activation/onboarding philosophy: deliberately-added friction — specifically, questions that help a product understand who a user is and recommend the right features to them — reliably beats a naively frictionless "get them into the product as fast as possible" design, contrary to conventional UX wisdom.

## Key Information

- Tested and confirmed repeatedly across three different companies: [[Mercury]] (breaking long onboarding forms into more, shorter screens reduced cognitive load and improved completion), [[MasterClass]] (a multi-question "what are you here for" quiz before purchase significantly lifted revenue by helping users feel the product/content was tailored to them), and [[Anthropic]] (onboarding questions about user identity/interests, despite looking like unnecessary friction to observers, measurably improve downstream outcomes).
- The distinguishing test: cut friction that doesn't help a user understand *why the product is for them*; add friction (even at the cost of extra steps) when it helps establish that fit, since the resulting user understanding compounds through the rest of the lifecycle (retention, and even look-alike ad targeting for people who drop off).
- Complements Amol's broader activation thesis: activation (the day-zero/day-one experience) is one of the highest-leverage points for long-term retention, and its importance has grown as capability overhead (model capability outpacing users' understanding of what a product can do) worsens.
