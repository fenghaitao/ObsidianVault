## Definition

[[Qasar Younis]]'s framing of the two dominant approaches to vehicle autonomy: the [[Tesla]] approach ("L2++" — few sensors, cheap compute, no HD maps, broadly deployable) versus the [[Waymo]] approach (many sensors, heavy compute, high-fidelity maps, safer but geographically constrained).

## Key Information

- L2++ is cheaper and requires no HD maps, so it can scale everywhere; the Waymo-style (L4) approach works better but only within a geofenced, heavily-mapped area.
- Younis predicts that within 5-7 years both approaches become far more ubiquitous globally (not just the Bay Area or parts of China), with downward pricing pressure already visible in China pushing L2++ toward near-free.
- Draws an analogy to in-car navigation systems: people once paid thousands of dollars for dedicated nav systems, much as some now pay a premium for semi-autonomous driving; but just as CarPlay/Android Auto made navigation free and ubiquitous without a singular "revolution" moment, full autonomy will likely become a default, unremarkable expectation rather than a discrete before/after event.
- Predicts a clear resulting decrease in injuries and deaths as this intelligence layer becomes standard across consumer vehicles, trucking, mining, and defense.
