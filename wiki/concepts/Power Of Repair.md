## Definition

[[Dr. Becky Kennedy]]'s "number one relationship strategy": returning to someone after a moment you're not proud of, taking responsibility for your part, acknowledging the impact, and naming what you'll do differently — rather than pursuing the impossible goal of never making mistakes.

## Key Information

- Motto: "perfect is creepy" — only non-humans can be perfect, and the human condition is defined by trying to do well and messing up repeatedly, not by getting it right the first time.
- Clinical basis: Kennedy recalls learning in grad school that secure attachment is not defined by an adult never messing up, but by the presence of repair after ruptures — a reframe she found hopeful because it's realistically attainable, unlike perfection.
- Parenting script: "Sorry I yelled — I had a stressful day, that wasn't your fault, and I'm working on staying calmer even when I'm upset."
- Workplace script: "Earlier in the meeting I cut you off and used a harsh tone. I did disagree with what you were saying, but that's no excuse for how I talked to you — stuff was going on before the meeting, I'm sorry, I'm going to work on that."
- Effect: repair reestablishes trust and connection, which is what makes people (kids or coworkers) cooperate better afterward and avoid defensive, unproductive "run-on" conversations.
- Complements [[Connecting Before Correcting]] as a relationship-repair mechanism specifically for after a rupture has already happened, rather than before a correction is delivered.
