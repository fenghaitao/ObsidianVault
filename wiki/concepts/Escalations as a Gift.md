## Definition

Escalations as a gift is a philosophy championed by Matt McGinness that customer complaints and escalations are the most valuable input a product leader can receive. They reveal systemic issues and enable process improvement at the root cause level.

## Key Information

- "There's no greater gift to me as a product executive than receiving an escalation from a customer."
- Escalations are literally the job of an executive: find problems and make them better, then iterate on processes so the system that builds the system improves.
- Rippling has a dedicated escalations team whose job is to "pistol whip" other teams into finding real root causes — not surface-level fixes.
- Root cause means: find the software bug that caused the data error, then find the system that created the software that created the data error, and solve all of it.
- Customers often withhold escalations because they don't want to "bother" executives — McGinness emphasizes this is a mistake.
- Withholding feedback is "the most selfish thing you can do" — it optimizes for your own comfort over helping someone improve.
- Escalations enable continuous iteration: specific issues inform general process improvements (e.g., the pickle checklist).
