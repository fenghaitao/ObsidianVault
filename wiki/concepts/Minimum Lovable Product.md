## Definition

Minimum Lovable Product (MLP) is the evolution beyond Minimum Viable Product (MVP). In an era where AI makes software cheap to build, viability is table stakes — the differentiator is emotional connection, delight, and brand personality that makes users love the product.

## Key Information

- Coined/advocated by Elena Verna: "It shouldn't be minimum viable product anymore. Viability is left back in the 2010s. Now it's minimum lovable product."
- The cost of software development has dropped so much with AI that companies can now invest in the emotional feel of software, not just utility
- Lovable's name itself embodies this philosophy: if something isn't lovable, the team won't ship it
- The best way to fix a bug at Lovable is to say "this is not lovable" — everyone jumps on it immediately
- MLP requires designers to be one of the first hires in startups, not just engineers
- Brand work is placed into the product itself through every interaction, not through a separate brand marketing team
- The "lovable" experience creates word-of-mouth because users feel empowered and want to share what they've created
- In the AI era, software needs to feel human — people want to interact with it, not just use it for utility
