## Definition

[[Elena Verna]]'s (Head of Growth, [[Lovable]]) reframing of "minimum viable product" for the AI era: viability/functionality is now table stakes, and the real differentiator is whether a product produces an emotional "wow"/"lovable" reaction, not just utility.

## Key Information

- Verna's framing: "viability [as a bar] was left back in the 2010s" — because AI has collapsed the cost and time of building basic functioning software, simply working is no longer a meaningful differentiator; delight/emotional resonance is.
- Connects to her broader argument that software is moving into "a new era... that needs to feel human," where falling development costs free teams to invest in the emotional feel of a product, not just its utility.
- Illustrated by Lovable's internal quality bar/phrase "this is not lovable," used to flag any bug or rough interaction that falls short of an emotionally resonant standard, triggering immediate fixes regardless of sprint planning.
- The target experience doesn't need to be a full "aha moment," per Verna — for Lovable, it's simply a "wow moment": the very first prompt-generated preview, even though incomplete, needs to make a new user think "I had no idea this was possible, I want to keep building."
- Distinguished from a classic MVP/"minimum viable product," and explicitly connected by [[Lenny Rachitsky]] to product managers' older "painted door"/"smoke test" validation techniques, now made richer and faster by AI (a full-featured, interactive prototype instead of a static landing page).
