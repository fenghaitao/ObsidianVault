## Definition

Dogfooding ("eating your own dog food") is the practice of using the product you are building. Fiona Fung treats it as a core leadership habit that builds empathy, signals urgency, and keeps a manager connected to the work.

## Key Information

- Fiona learned dogfooding in her first job, "using Visual Studio to build Visual Studio," where she realized she'd use the product every day
- It gives empathy for what users go through and a good "pulse" of the product
- She carried it to Facebook Marketplace, keeping a pile of inventory to sell (to her husband's initial annoyance) to gain real insights and inspiration from meeting users
- As a manager who can no longer code daily, dogfooding is her "maker time" — e.g., helping debug hard-to-reproduce issues (like floor-height bugs in Horizon OS) is a small way to contribute to product quality
- When leaders dogfood, it adds urgency to bugs that otherwise get lost — a leader saying "this is broken, I can't use this" focuses the team on what matters
- It builds relationship and rapport with the team — she regularly gets outreach (e.g., at Instagram) along the lines of "it's awesome that you're using our product and care about what we do"
- Tips: integrate dogfooding into what brings you joy (workouts via Supernatural, knitting while watching movies in a VR headset) so it doesn't become a chore; or hold product sessions with the team (her Friday leadership dogfooding sessions with a PM and design partner yielded fast feedback on features about to ship)

### Bill Gates and the NT Server (via David Ronca)

- Ronca credits "12 Secrets to Microsoft Success" with the dogfooding origin: Bill Gates forced the entire company onto the failing NT Server in ~1995-96, exposing its bugs to everyone until the OS "that couldn't ship started getting fixed, moving."
