## Definition

Good Failure is the idea that failure isn't inherently bad — what matters is how you fail, what you learn, and what you bring forward, so that a well-managed failed project can still be a net success.

## Key Information

- David Ronca, citing "12 Secrets to Microsoft Success": "let's quit saying failure is bad... there is such a thing as good failure" — expect some projects to fail in a healthy company; what matters is whether it was a rational bet, how you handled it, and how early you got out.
- Ronca's framing: "failure can be success... it's not that you fail, it's how you fail, what you learned, and what you bring forward."
- Adam Ernst lived this with ComponentScript: he was afraid to kill it until a "meets most" rating prodded him; he then deleted the framework, helped teams migrate, and published a candid public postmortem.
- Ernst's conclusion: "you might get more goodwill from killing it responsibly than just dragging it out and constantly waiting until it's too late" — a clean, no-mess shutdown earns more respect than a slow death.
