## Definition

[[Jacob Warwick]]'s relationship-building tactic: proactively do something valuable for a counterparty (an unprompted introduction, a favor, a piece of useful information) before asking anything of them, creating an unspoken social debt (a reciprocity imbalance) that improves the odds of favorable treatment later.

## Key Information

- Concrete example: while on a call with a client, Warwick will make a live introduction to a relevant contact ("let me make a text for you real quick") — demonstrating tangible value before any deal is closed, and creating multiple touchpoints through which he can later track and influence the relationship.
- Also used defensively/narratively: Warwick asks people what they'll say about him to others (e.g., "are you going to say I was helpful, or an [expletive]?"), pre-framing the answer so the counterparty is socially boxed into repeating the positive version — a technique also applicable to controlling how a hiring committee will describe a candidate after an interview.
- Complements "[[Sell The Vacation]]": both aim to make the interaction feel like a genuine value exchange rather than a one-sided extraction (of information, favor, or approval) from the counterparty.
