## Definition

Onboarding High-Leverage is the principle that small improvements in early user onboarding have outsized, compounding impacts on long-term retention and revenue. It is Jason Cohen's recommended default focus when you don't know where else to reduce churn.

## Why Onboarding Is the Highest-Leverage Point

1. **Most churn happens early.** Almost all companies have significantly more cancellation in the first day, 30 days, or 90 days than in the rest of the customer's life.

2. **Small changes → large effects.** Minor onboarding improvements can have large effects on long-term retention, whereas later-stage improvements rarely move the needle as much.

3. **The YouTube retention analogy.** On a typical YouTube video, ~50% of viewers drop off in the first 30 seconds. Getting that from 50% to 55% stay rate can increase end-of-video retention from 20% to 25% — a 25% improvement in output from a 10% improvement in input.

4. **Early churn is super unprofitable.** You spent all the money to acquire the customer, and they never stayed long enough to pay it back, much less become profitable.

5. **Diminishing returns on later-stage retention.** If you don't know what to do (or even if you do), onboarding is still a good bet because it's where the most leverage is.

## Practical Approach

- Even without data, you can guess which behaviors signal trouble in onboarding
- Form a theory, act on it, and adjust as you get more data
- Look for signals: never uploaded data, too many/too few support calls, disengagement, no login
- Catching people during onboarding (when they're in trouble but haven't canceled) is much better than surveying them after they've left
