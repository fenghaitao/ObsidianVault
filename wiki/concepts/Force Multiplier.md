## Definition

A Force Multiplier is an approach to work that amplifies the productivity and impact of others, rather than focusing solely on individual output. For Staff Engineers, force multiplying through people leadership or technology leverage is a key way to achieve outsized impact.

## Key Information

- Lee discussed force multiplying as an alternative to working long hours — "if you can make people super super effective then the number of hours you work might not have the same correlation to impact"
- Lee achieved Staff-level impact without working more than 40 hours a week by focusing on force multiplying through people leadership
- Force multiplying through technology (infrastructure, tooling, platforms) is another path — scaling efficiency gains across hundreds of engineers
- The concept contrasts with individual delivery — both can achieve impact, but force multiplying can be more efficient in terms of hours worked
- "The people side can show that impact in less hours spent"
- Zach described infrastructure work as "changing how other engineers are doing their job" — a form of force multiplication

### Scott Renfro as a Force Multiplier
- Philip Su described Scott Renfro as a "slam dunk case" of a force multiplier at Meta
- Scott's peer feedback consistently said: "Scott takes time out of his day to make me great"
- He would review PRs with sensitivity, never publicly shaming anyone, always framing feedback constructively
- Having Scott on a team made the entire team much better
- He was capable both personally of guaranteeing a huge scope of work AND being a force multiplier for teams
