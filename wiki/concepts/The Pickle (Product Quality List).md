## Definition

The Pickle ("PQL," Product Quality List) is [[Rippling]]'s internal product-quality checklist, described by [[Matt MacInnis]] as a lightweight standard applied at the end of the "assembly line" before a product ships.

## Key Information

- MacInnis deliberately chose a quirky, memorable name — "pickle," from the acronym PQL — because he believes cultural change in a large organization (Rippling's R&D org has ~1,300 people) requires creating a distinctive "vessel for meaning" that becomes part of daily vocabulary, rather than a generic term.
- His test for whether someone genuinely understands a concept (used when people pitch ideas using buzzwords like "strategy"): ask them to explain it without using the word itself; if they can't, they haven't thought it through — the same logic behind giving "pickle" its own name rather than calling it something generic.
- Lightweight and comprehensive but not universal — not every line applies to every product — and is explicitly designed to be iterated on as the team learns.
- Concrete example of iteration: after Rippling's CEO, [[Parker Conrad]], installed a new internal feedback app and was dumped onto a blank screen due to a forgotten feature flag, MacInnis added a new pickle line item: a product should ship with no more than one feature flag governing the entire product — an intentionally extreme, possibly unachievable standard.
- MacInnis is vocally hostile to feature flags in general, comparing them to shims a contractor forgets under a wall that eventually cause a door to fail — "the bane of my existence."
- Framed as a concrete instantiation of the [[Alpha Beta Hiring And Process Framework]]: a deliberately lightweight process meant to suppress beta (unpredictability/quality failures) with minimal alpha (creative latitude) suppression.
