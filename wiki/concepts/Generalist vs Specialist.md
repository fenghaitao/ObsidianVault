## Definition

Generalist vs Specialist is a career strategy decision about whether to develop broad skills across many domains or deep expertise in a narrow domain. Philip Su, a self-identified generalist, argues that generalists are more valuable at small companies while specialists become more valuable at large companies.

## Key Information

### Philip Su's Perspective
- Philip Su identifies as a generalist, not a specialist — he's a "very generic coder" who can do a wide variety of things
- At small companies, generalists are more valuable due to quantization effects: you can't hire a part-time 3-hour-a-week comms person, so you tap a generalist to handle it
- At large companies, each fraction of work warrants one or more full-time employees, making the generalist's remaining 85% of skills "wasted energy"
- When Facebook grew from 500 to thousands of engineers, Philip felt his unique value as a generalist diminished — specialists became more useful
- Most small companies value generalists more than specialists, though every small company can use at least one specialist

### When to Specialize
- If you have "unnaturally unique talent" (like a chess prodigy at age 8), you should specialize
- Most people should avoid early binding to a specialty for three reasons: (1) how do you know you're a specialist and not a generalist without trying? (2) risk of "idiot savant" over-specialization (like the mathematician who couldn't cut his own grapefruit); (3) the specialty might become irrelevant (the "COBOL person hoping Y2K happens again")
- In the age of AI, the risk of a specialty becoming obsolete is heightened

### The Diamond Shape
- At very senior levels (E8+), skills converge again in a "diamond shape" — both ICs and managers at those levels need vision, communication, and strategic thinking
- However, some archetypes (like deep SQL query optimization specialists) may never develop the breadth needed for management

### Boris Cherny's Perspective
- Boris prioritizes generalists — on the Claude Code team "everyone does everything" (PMs, data scientists, and researchers all code), echoing early Facebook's reward of generalists who do UX, design, and product work ([[summary-20251215 - Boris Cherny (Creator of Claude Code) On What Grew His Career And Building at Anthropic]])
