## Definition

[[Dr. Becky Kennedy]]'s concept of "sturdiness" as the essence of good leadership (in parenting and at work): the ability to see someone else's emotional experience as real for them, while not being overwhelmed by it — holding on to your own judgment and decision regardless.

## Key Information

- Illustrated with a turbulent-flight/pilot metaphor with three possible pilot announcements during severe turbulence:
  1. **Dismissive** ("Stop screaming, you're distracting me and making this awful for everyone") — makes passengers feel less safe, because it reveals the leader can be destabilized by their distress.
  2. **Overcorrected/abdicating** ("I'm opening the cockpit door — does anyone want to fly the plane?") — terrifying, because the leader hands the decision to whoever is loudest/most anxious rather than holding their own judgment. Kennedy explicitly flags this as an overcorrection she's seen in parenting (validating feelings to the point that feelings dictate the decision) and at work (treating a leadership call as a consensus vote when it isn't one).
  3. **Sturdy** ("I hear you, this is scary, it doesn't scare me, I know what I'm doing, I'll see you when we land") — validates the fear as real without being destabilized by it; produces calm because the passenger's worry is "contained" by a leader who isn't overwhelmed.
- Two distinct components: (1) seeing someone else's experience as real for them, and (2) holding on to your own experience/judgment so you're not overwhelmed by theirs. Both are required together.
- Applied to parenting: holding a boundary (e.g., ending TV time) while acknowledging the child's upset ("I get that you're upset, I don't love ending it either, we'll get through it") rather than either dismissing the upset or reversing the decision.
- Applied to work: announcing a difficult decision (e.g., a policy change) by naming that it's hard, validating that the reaction makes sense, and still holding the decision with conviction, rather than either shutting down complaints or opening it to a vote that was never meant to be one.
- Related to being someone others can "locate" — Kennedy says she is drawn to (and values) people with a clear, expressible point of view, even when she disagrees with them, over people who simply mirror what she says; a sturdy leader with a clear POV that they'll share plainly ("I believe this is the best decision, I believe we're a strong enough team to work toward it") is experienced as "holding," even by people who disagree with the specific call.
- Kennedy connects sturdiness to [[Boundaries Vs Requests]] — she frames boundary-setting and validating-without-being-taken-over as "the same two jobs" of any sturdy leader (parent or manager).
