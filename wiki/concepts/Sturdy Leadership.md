## Definition

Sturdy leadership is the combination of two jobs: (1) setting boundaries and (2) validating someone's experience without being overwhelmed by it. A sturdy leader can see someone else's emotional experience as real for them while still holding onto their own experience and making decisions. The pilot metaphor captures this: "I hear everyone screaming back there. That makes sense. You're right, it's very bumpy. And I know what I'm doing."

## Key Information

- Two distinct aspects of sturdy leadership: (1) "I can see someone else's experience as real for them" and (2) "I can still hold on to my experience and so I'm not overwhelmed by someone else"
- Three types of pilot announcements (metaphor for leadership during turbulence):
  - Type 1 (dismissive): "Stop screaming. You're making this flight awful for everyone." — Makes people feel less safe
  - Type 2 (overcorrected): "I'm opening the cockpit door. Does anyone want to fly the plane?" — Terrifying; leader is abdicating
  - Type 3 (sturdy): "I hear everyone screaming. That makes sense. You're right, it's very bumpy. And I know what I'm doing." — Contains worry while validating experience
- At work: "Look, I'm going to share something that I know some people are going to have a reaction to. Here's the new policy. I get it, this is a big change and you're right, this does make things hard. And the way you're feeling makes sense. And here's some version of why we're doing it. And I know we're going to get through it."
- With kids: "I'm turning the TV off. That's my decision. I get that you're upset. I don't really like to end TV time either. I also know we'll get through it. Do your thing and then we'll transition."
- "Validating kids' feelings is an incomplete parenting approach. That is half of our job. But it doesn't work if we're not doing the other half, which is setting boundaries."
- Dr. Becky uses the word "sturdy" because it conjures an image/feeling: "If you picture someone in your life who's the sturdy person I know, I bet you can kind of locate them."
- People want a leader they can "locate" — someone with a clear POV: "I personally love people I can locate even if I really disagree with them more than being around people who seem to mirror what I'm saying."
- At our worst moments, we speak our fears, not our wishes
- The teenager therapy story: "Can you believe they let me make that decision?" — Sometimes the most loving act is to make a decision for someone who can't make good ones
