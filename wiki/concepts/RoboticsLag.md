## Definition
Robotics Lag is the observation that robotics capabilities significantly trail LLM capabilities, with approximately 2 orders of magnitude less compute investment, despite similar rates of improvement. This gap has major implications for AI's ability to automate physical-world tasks like chip production.

## Key Information
- The rate of compute going into robotics models is about the same as LLMs, but the absolute levels differ by ~2 orders of magnitude
- Joel Becker is "kind of curious about if that gap closed" — more capable robots seem "very on the table as something that could be the case very soon"
- The gap could be closed by scaling data, scaling parameters with the same data, or both
- Manufacturing iteration is fundamentally slower than software iteration — "an order of magnitude worse"
- Chip fabrication specifically: costs billions per fab, each generation requires massive capital, and human expertise remains critical
- Self-driving cars are an example of physical-world AI that got "really close" quickly but the last bit of reliability took much longer
- Becker is skeptical that full chip production automation will happen soon but acknowledges the "GPT-2 moment for robotics" possibility
- The lag is one reason software-only singularity skeptics believe hardware bottlenecks will constrain AI progress
