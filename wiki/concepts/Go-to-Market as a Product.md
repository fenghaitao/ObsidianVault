## Definition

The thesis that as software commoditizes and technical differentiation narrows, the experience of being sold to becomes a key differentiator. Companies should design their customer buying journey — from first awareness through long-term retention — with the same intentionality and UX rigor as a product.

## Key Information

- Jeanne DeWitt Grosser developed this thesis ~10+ years ago, observing that cloud computing was enabling rapid commoditization.
- Core insight: "We buy a lot of things because of how we feel about them." The buying experience will increasingly differentiate a company and drive buying decisions when products are only different at the margin.
- The goal is to create a customer buying journey that feels like "very unique experiences" — not transactional, flat, or boring.
- Map the full journey from awareness → qualification → discovery → evaluation → purchase → onboarding → expansion → retention, making each touchpoint human, personalized, and valuable.
- Example from Stripe: Instead of a traditional discovery call (question-based quizzing), the first session was a whiteboarding session where the customer drew their payment architecture. This created value for the customer (they often hadn't mapped their own architecture) and built a collaborative relationship.
- Core principle: "Add value at any touchpoint regardless of whether or not that customer bought." Even lost customers may return 3-4 years later in a new buying cycle.
- Example from Vercel: When reaching out, share performance insights (Core Web Vitals, CrUX benchmarking) immediately — giving value even if the prospect doesn't engage.
- Another principle: Provide "blueprints" or "well-architected guides" (like AWS) — customers want best-practice guidance, not generic docs, especially for larger deployments.
- This approach requires thinking like a product manager at every stage and making the journey feel like an experience rather than a transaction.
