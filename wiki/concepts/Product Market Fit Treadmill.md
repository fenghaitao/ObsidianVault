## Definition

[[Elena Verna]]'s (Head of Growth, [[Lovable]]) framing for why AI companies can no longer treat product-market fit as a one-time milestone reached before shifting into pure scaling — instead, PMF must be continuously recaptured, roughly every three months.

## Key Information

- Historically, product-market fit evolved on a multi-year timescale: a company would spend years scaling an initial PMF before facing a "second horizon"/"third horizon" question about the next stage of growth.
- Two forces compress that cycle to roughly 3 months for AI-native companies: (1) underlying LLM capability keeps making step-function jumps with each new model release, raising the ceiling of what's buildable — and companies must build *ahead* of the model (betting on a capability before it exists) so they're ready the moment it lands; (2) consumer expectations shift on the same cadence, since users' sense of "what AI should be able to do" is anchored to whatever the newest frontier model has shown them, unlike historical technology-adoption timelines where consumer perception lagged capability by years.
- Practical consequence: a company can be a $200M-ARR business growing 10% month-over-month and still be at real risk of losing product-market fit within the next three months — illustrated by [[Lenny Rachitsky]]/Verna's discussion of [[OpenAI]] reportedly losing meaningful ChatGPT market share within a single week after [[Gemini]] 3's launch, despite ChatGPT's dominant incumbent position.
- Implication for org design: the team that originally finds product-market fit is traditionally different from the team that scales a company, but AI companies now need both capabilities simultaneously and continuously, on an ongoing basis, rather than sequentially.
- Related to the "capability → value → scale" framework Verna credits to [[John Cutler]]: while overall software categories eventually mature past the capability-discovery stage, vibe coding is still very much within it, which is part of why the PMF treadmill hasn't yet slowed down.
