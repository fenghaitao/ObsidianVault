## Definition

Nuclear risk is Martin Hellman's later concern that nuclear weapons — and possibly AI coupled to them — create an existential threat, motivating his "rethinking national security" work.

## Key Information

- Hellman estimates a child born today has worse-than-even odds of living out a natural life from nuclear weapons alone, even setting aside climate change and AI.
- He advocates HR 3564 (~20+ co-sponsors), which keeps a "launch on warning" exception (making it passable but weaker) while restricting the president's sole launch authority; most people do not realize the president can launch alone, potentially while impaired.
- On AI, he notes the split between Geoffrey Hinton and Yoshua Bengio (AI as a genuine existential threat) and Ed Feigenbaum and Raj Reddy (calling that "ridiculous"); he worries AI is likely to be built into nuclear command and control, where decision time is already only minutes.
- Framing: humanity may die eventually regardless (the sun will burn out), but dying "now" would be infant mortality; the goal is to save humanity from an infant death via a gradual "process of change."
