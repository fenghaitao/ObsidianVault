## Definition

[[Mark Pincus]]'s management principle: give each team member a clear "hill to take," full operating control and degrees of freedom to pursue it however they want (within an agreed plan/budget), rather than managing their day-to-day execution — effectively making each of them a real "CEO" of their area.

## Key Information

- Pincus frames all of management as answering one question: "how do we get people to do the right thing when we're not in the room?" Making people real CEOs of their hill removes the need for him to manage them directly — they stop coming back with questions.
- Best suited to people with a strong "[[Expert Witness Syndrome|expert-witness]]" personality — a bit of a know-it-all with pent-up demand to prove they're right.
- Echoed in a broader Silicon Valley shift Pincus cites approvingly: getting rid of middle management, with Brian Armstrong's view that everyone should be an individual contributor and manage themselves — "the best CEO is like the best player at the position," not someone spending time on management for its own sake.
