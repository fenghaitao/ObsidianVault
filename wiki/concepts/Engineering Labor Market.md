## Definition

The engineering labor market is the supply/demand dynamic for software engineers. John Myles White argues it has flipped from undersupplied (employers sacrificed to retain talent) to oversupplied (employers make fewer sacrifices), making being an employee harsher.

## Key Information

- Historically Silicon Valley assumed it was "incredibly easy to lose your good employees," so companies sacrificed to retain them.
- Now the "general perception is the supply of engineers is way oversupplied," especially with AI concerns; the exception is a small set of AI researchers in frontier labs.
- Consequence: employers think "I don't actually have to make some of these sacrifices to acquire and retain talent," affecting compensation, perks, employee voice, and reorg calculus (attrition is less of a downside).
- White says this is a general Silicon Valley property, not unique to Meta.
