## Definition

Luck refers to the role of chance and serendipity in career outcomes. Both Evan King and Ryan Peterman acknowledged that luck played a significant role in their rapid career growth, alongside skill and effort.

## Key Information

- Evan's first "tick of luck": the night before his Meta interview, he skimmed the exact LeetCode hard problem that appeared in his interview — without that, he "certainly wouldn't have passed, certainly wouldn't have gone onsite, wouldn't work at Meta"
- Evan noted that luck was a "reoccurring theme throughout" his career journey
- Ryan acknowledged that "so much of this stuff is luck and opportunity" and that agency is what allows you to capitalize on it
- The right manager is a form of luck — Evan's manager was exceptional at creating opportunities and advocating behind the scenes
- Being on the right team at the right time (e.g., when the Christchurch shooting happened, when the team was growing) involved luck
- Both Evan and Ryan described themselves as "very lucky" in their trajectories
- The key insight is that luck creates opportunities, but agency, skill, and effort are what allow you to capitalize on them

### Rahul's Perspective on Luck
- Rahul believes "a lot of it was luck" — he was "super lucky" with the opportunities and people he came across
- He described his career as "I have failed my way into success" — being rejected from opportunities that turned out to be bad for him
- Examples: jobs he was rejected from were at companies that "completely exploded a year later"; VC firms that strung Taro along and didn't invest would have created unhealthy pressure
- He reframes rejection as potential luck in disguise
- Luck can be "manufactured" through genuine curiosity, keeping in touch with people, and sharing what you're working on
- Posting on social media is a deliberate way to create luck — you don't know who will see your post, but people will think of you when opportunities arise
- This is a "repeatable algorithm or repeatable process that's not dependent on luck"
