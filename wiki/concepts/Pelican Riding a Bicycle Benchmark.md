## Definition

The pelican riding a bicycle benchmark is an informal AI benchmark created by Simon Wilson to make fun of numeric AI benchmarks. It tests text models (not image models) by asking them to generate an SVG of a pelican riding a bicycle. Surprisingly, there appears to be a strong correlation between pelican-drawing quality and overall model capability.

## Key Information

- Created by Simon Wilson about 1.5 years before the podcast as a joke mocking numeric benchmarks
- "All of those always frustrated me because they don't really tell you anything interesting. If this one got 74 and this one got 72, does that actually mean that one of them's better?"
- Tests text models, not image models — they output SVG code (which can include comments like "added a fish for whimsy")
- "There appears to be a very strong correlation between how good their drawing of a pelican riding a bicycle is and how good they are at everything else. And nobody can explain to me why that is"
- It has become a meme: AI labs are aware of it and compete on it
- Gemini 3.1's release video featured an animated pelican riding a bicycle
- OpenAI released GPT-5.4 with five thinking levels; Simon created a 15-pelican grid and GPT-5.4 at X-high drew the best one
- Simon has backup benchmarks (ocelot on a moped) to detect if labs specifically train on pelicans — but Gemini 3.1 preempted this by doing all the animal/transport combinations
- "All I want from life is a really good picture of a pelican riding a bicycle. And if I can trick every AI lab in the world into cheating on benchmarks to get it, then that just achieves my goal"
- Simon lives near the world's second-largest mega roost of California brown pelicans
- "This space is inherently funny. It is ridiculous. The fact that we have these incredibly expensive, power-hungry, supposedly the most advanced computers of all time, and if you ask them to draw a pelican on a bicycle, it looks like a 5-year-old drew it. That's really funny"
