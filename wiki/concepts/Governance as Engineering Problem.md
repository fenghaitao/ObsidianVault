## Definition
Governance as an engineering problem is the reframing of enterprise governance — security reviews, compliance checks, deployment approvals, change freezes — as the CTO's top engineering priority rather than an immutable organizational constraint. The prescription is to convert every human governance process into adaptable, executable code.

## Key Information
- Articulated by Jess Grogan-Avignon and Jack Wang of Accenture as the third pillar of their prescription
- Governance speed should be the CTO's top engineering problem and the ultimate technical debt to rectify
- The real technical debt is not legacy application code — it's years of underinvestment in engineering automation, CI/CD, and deployment infrastructure
- Every single human process needs to become adaptable, executable code — not another meeting, not a sign-off chain, code
- AI can help build this automation faster and cheaper than traditional approaches
- These aren't new capabilities (CI/CD, automated testing, deployment pipelines) — but deploying them requires a fundamental mindset shift
- Current state: coding agents turn everyone into builders, causing supply of deployable code to explode, but approval and deployment infrastructure hasn't changed
- GitHub stats illustrate the mismatch: 1 billion commits in 2025, on track for 14 billion in 2026
- The pathway requires treating governance as infrastructure that can be engineered, not organizational reality that must be navigated
- Connects to enterprise scaffolding: governance processes are the primary component of the scaffold that slows down AI value capture
