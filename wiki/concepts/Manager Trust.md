## Definition

Manager Trust is the relationship dynamic between an engineer and their manager where the manager has confidence in the engineer's ability to deliver and makes decisions (hiring, promotion, scope assignment) based on proven track record rather than formal credentials. It is one of the most powerful accelerators or blockers of career growth.

## Key Information

- Zach's manager Jender at Facebook trusted him enough to let him tackle full-stack problems outside his data engineering role, which led to his first promotion
- When Jender left Facebook, Zach had three different managers in one year, and "no one really had the context" — he had to repeatedly prove himself, which disrupted his promotion to E5
- "Having a good manager who trusts you takes time and you have to build that up with them" — good managers trust implicitly and only remove trust when given a reason; other managers require you to prove it first
- Manager trust was the differentiator when Zach transitioned to Netflix: Jender hired him as a Senior Engineer despite limited software engineering experience because he had already seen Zach deliver
- At Facebook, Zach was denied a transition from Data Engineer to Software Engineer without a down-level because his manager didn't trust his software engineering skills, despite Zach writing 90% of the JavaScript on his team
- Zach contrasts his experience with a friend named Ryan who had a supportive manager at Facebook and successfully transitioned roles — "your life is the life I would have had if I didn't have a bad manager"
- At Netflix, managers have significant power over hiring and firing ("freedom and responsibility"), making manager trust even more critical
- The lesson: "find those people who you really click with because you never know, you might be working with them again in the future"
