## Definition

[[Qasar Younis]]'s advice, drawn from his time as COO of [[Y Combinator]] watching thousands of companies, for founders who are roughly two years in without traction: if the market isn't giving you increasingly specific, informative signal, consider a hard reset rather than continuing to iterate on the surface.

## Key Information

- Uses the analogy of a wobbly table: if you keep adjusting the table (the product) and a marble keeps rolling off, the actual problem may be the foundation — often the co-founding team, but it could also be the market itself or the founders' personal capacity/willingness to commit at that phase of life.
- Frames a founder's first company as inherently "a zero" — the value is in building the muscle of being a founder, not in expecting success out of the gate; Younis notes his own third company (Applied Intuition) being his most successful is not a coincidence, and that some funds deliberately focus on multi-time founders for this reason.
- Warns against putting so much pressure on a first attempt to be great that a founder misses the actual lesson available (learning the craft), comparing it to expecting a first hand-built woodworking table to be flawless.
- Notes the best ideas often emerge from low-stakes tinkering/fun rather than from consciously trying to build "the next Google" — but acknowledges this advice is hard to internalize for a founder already deep in a stressful grind.
