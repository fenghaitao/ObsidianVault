## Definition

Social Proof is the credibility and validation that comes from prestigious institutions, companies, and accomplishments on your resume. Rahul Pandey credits having Stanford and a Facebook internship on his resume as the "insurance policy" that enabled him to take the risky startup path early in his career.

## Key Information

### Rahul's Social Proof Stack
- Stanford University (undergrad and master's)
- Internship at Facebook (Meta) in 2013
- Offers from all five major tech companies: Google, Facebook, Microsoft, Twitter, and Square
- This combination gave him confidence that he could always go back to big tech if the startup failed

### The Insurance Policy Effect
- Rahul explicitly said: "if I had never gotten that big tech offer, I think I might have really thought twice"
- Having Stanford and the offers meant he could "be a lot more risk-on" early in his career
- The standard path was still available as a backup plan

### Social Proof as a New Grad Strategy
- Rahul's advice to new grads without exceptional opportunities: go to the biggest brand name company you can
- "Go work at Amazon. Go work at Google if you can get in. That will set you up well at a baseline for whatever else you want to do later"
- There's "so much value in just accruing credibility" — even just 1-2 years at a big brand creates an "instant step change in your credibility"
- The standard path is standard for a reason

### Social Proof and Startup Founders
- Having social proof from big tech makes it easier to raise funding and attract talent for a startup
- Y Combinator provides social proof for first-time founders who don't have an existing network
