## Definition

Horizontal Impact is the concept of enabling your team and organization to move faster by improving the development process, tooling, and how work gets done — as opposed to what work gets done. Coined by Zach Wilson as one of two directions of impact for staff engineers.

## Key Information

- Zach described creating impact in two directions: Vertical Impact (direct business impact) and Horizontal Impact (enabling team velocity)
- Horizontal impact asks: "What about the development process sucks and how can we make the development process better?"
- It's about thinking about "not just what we are building but how we are building it"
- Examples: Zach's Groovy script that cut deploy times by removing unused dependencies, saving 1-7 minutes per deploy
- The key insight: once you solve a "how" problem, scaling it is more about adoption than technical excellence
- If you can get 100 engineers using a tool that saves them time, the aggregate impact is enormous
- Combining horizontal and vertical impact is what makes people say "wow, this person is really changing things"
- Rahul's debug-ability tool that became a platform for other engineers to contribute to is another example of horizontal impact
