## Definition

[[Kiriti Badam]]'s closing thesis: in a fast-moving, playbook-free field like AI product development, durable competitive advantage ("moat") comes not from being first to market or from a flashy feature, but from having gone through the accumulated, largely undocumented pain of iterating toward what actually works for a given problem.

## Key Information

- Badam's framing: "successful companies right now building in any new area... are successful not because they're first to the market or... they have this fancy feature... They went through the pain of understanding what are the set of non-negotiable things and trade them off with... what are the model capabilities that I can use to solve that problem."
- Explicitly tied to persistence as an individual trait: because information/tutorials are now abundantly available (an "Iron Man" style ability to learn anything overnight), the differentiator shifts from access to knowledge toward the willingness to sit through repeated failed iterations ("let's try this, and if this doesn't work, let's try this") until something works.
- Badam's summary metaphor for the resulting organizational knowledge: "turning coal into diamond" — the accumulated, hard-won trade-off knowledge across an organization (which could take the form of a proprietary eval set, a particular product architecture, or simply lived experience) is what becomes genuinely difficult for competitors to copy, unlike a feature or a model choice.
- Directly reinforces the episode's broader critique of "one-click agent" vendors: since there's no textbook or established path for AI product development yet ("no straightforward way, no known threaded path"), any credible shortcut around this iterative pain should be treated skeptically — see the "one-click agents" marketing critique under [[Continuous Calibration Continuous Development]].
- Connects to [[Problem First Approach]] and the broader "success triangle" (see [[AI Product Success Triangle]]): the pain being described is specifically the pain of understanding workflows, trade-offs, and non-negotiables — not pain for its own sake.
