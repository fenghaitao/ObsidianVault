## Definition

Jacob Warwick's assertion that the two most important traits in negotiation are curiosity and empathy — not frameworks, not aggression, not academic knowledge. When you hit a block in negotiation, it's because you haven't asked the right questions. Curiosity reveals information; empathy reveals motivation.

## Key Information

- Jacob: "When we try to focus so much on frameworks and academic, we stop listening. And that's one of the most important parts is curiosity and empathy are the two things you need to have the most."
- "If you're reaching a block, why does that block exist? If you don't know the answer, you haven't asked the right questions."
- Negotiation "really comes down to information and timing. That's what creates power."
- Curiosity in practice: asking discovery questions to understand what the company truly needs, what problems they have, what they've tried, what success looks like
- Empathy in practice: understanding the motivations of the person across the table, reading body language, recognizing when someone is stressed or frustrated
- Jacob's personal origin: "I felt a little powerless growing up... I started to understand psychology a little bit differently because the same fear I'd have as a child, I could see in the boardroom. I could see the same fight or flight mechanisms."
- "All I ever wanted to do because I felt that from a little kid was help that person feel safe."
- This approach makes negotiation frameworks (BATNA, ZOPA, etc.) secondary — they're useful but shouldn't replace listening
