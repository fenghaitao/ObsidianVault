## Definition

Surprise Factor is Laurent's metric for whether a manager has aligned expectations before a performance review — measured by whether manager and report independently write down the same expected rating and reveal them simultaneously.

## Key Information

- Before delivering a review, Laurent asked each report to write their expected rating (and whether they expected promotion), while he wrote his own; both were revealed at the same time.
- A match is a pass; a mismatch is a fail.
- Goal: minimize surprise at review time — stressed or uncertain people cannot do their best work.
- Negative surprises are bad, but positive surprises are also bad: promoting someone who doesn't think they're performing well means the manager should have coached them earlier to take more risk.
- Laurent drove the metric toward 100% and got close, though never exactly.
- It ties to a broader lesson: career progression should be an ongoing conversation, not a passive end-of-year summary.
