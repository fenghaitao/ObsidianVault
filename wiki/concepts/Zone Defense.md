## Definition

"Zone defense" is [[Andrew Ambrosino]]'s sports analogy (developed with a colleague, Alexander) for how product people should organize themselves now that implementation is cheap and curation/[[Taste]] is the bottleneck: rather than clustering on the same idea, product people should spread out to cover distinct areas of the problem space.

## Key Information

- Motivation: when implementation is cheap, many people across a company independently generate similar prototype ideas simultaneously (Ambrosino's "90 different uncoordinated teams" example). Two product people working too closely on the same area is often "not a good signal" — it suggests redundant coverage rather than efficient use of scarce curatorial/taste capacity.
- Practice: product people at [[OpenAI]]'s [[Codex]] org identify who's best at what, deliberately "create some space" between each other so the team collectively has full coverage of the problem space, then fill in gaps as needed — described as a "force-directed" activity.
- Explicitly framed as a replacement for older, top-down, year-long roadmap planning, which Ambrosino says "is not going to work" given the pace and chaos of parallel AI-driven exploration.
- Complements the org's approach to hiring: they look for "engineers who are product minded" so that code doesn't need heavy separate review purely for product coherence — spreading product judgment more broadly across the org rather than concentrating it in a single review gate.
- Related to but distinct from [[Role Collapse]]: zone defense is about how a team allocates coverage/attention across a large possibility space, while role collapse is about the blurring of any one person's job function.
