## Definition
Bounded autonomy is the productive middle ground between free will (fully autonomous agents) and determinism (fully scripted workflows) in agent design. It acknowledges that agents operate within constraints — context windows, model limitations, tool availability — and that these boundaries, rather than being obstacles, create the conditions for effective and creative agent behavior.

## Key Information
- Introduced by Angus J. McLean (AI Director at Oliver) as a framework for thinking about agent design.
- Positioned as "conventional wisdom for unconventional times" — based on practical industry experience designing agents.
- The talk could have been alternatively titled: "Between Automation and Customization," "Between Oversight and Agency," or "Between Possibilities and Constraints."
- Agents need enough autonomy to be useful but enough structure to be reliable — neither fully free nor fully predetermined.
- Context windows act as soft constraints that bound autonomy: the agent can only act on what fits within its context.
- Constraints are not just limitations — they create the conditions for creativity and effective problem-solving.
- The concept parallels Adam Smith's pin factory: capitalism naturally breaks tasks into small, repeatable chunks, and structured workflows are often more effective than unstructured agent freedom.
- Practical implication: don't automate a job unless you can do it yourself — understanding the bounds of the task is prerequisite to automating it.
