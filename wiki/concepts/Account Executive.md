## Definition

An Account Executive (AE) is a sales role responsible for closing deals — taking a qualified prospect from interest through evaluation to a purchasing decision. AEs are "closers" who run the full sales process after an SDR has generated the pipeline.

## Key Information

- AEs take prospects from "I'm interested" → "I have a legitimate problem" → "I could make a decision" → "Your product is the best for me and I'm willing to pay."
- AEs work up the complexity ladder across segments:
  - SMB/Startup: Transactional, single decision maker.
  - Mid-Market/Commercial: Economic buyer (finance) + technical buyer (engineering).
  - Enterprise: Procurement, committees, ~10 people weighing in, migration risk, complex coordination.
- Sales is a skill — "just like writing code is a skill or building a financial model is a skill." It involves discovery (asking the right questions), objection handling, demonstrating value, and managing a process.
- Jeanne DeWitt Grosser's litmus test for AEs: "If I put you in front of 10 engineers at our company, it should take them 10 minutes to figure out you aren't a product manager." — AEs need deep product knowledge and credibility.
- Hiring strategy: mix traditional salespeople (sales as a skill) with consultants/bankers (analytical, quantitative) for a richer learning environment.
- Founders are often incredible salespeople (convincing VCs to invest) but their sale is an "evangelist sale" — not always replicable.
- Founder advice: wait until ~$1M ARR with repeatable ICP before hiring first salesperson; then hand over the reins but stay connected to customers for R&D.
- Jason Lemkin's SaaStr: replaced 5-6 AEs with 1.2 humans (one full-time AE + Amelia at 20% time), with AI agents handling all top-of-funnel work.
- The AE role is more resilient than SDR/BDR: Lemkin predicts ~70% of AE jobs will be safe by end of next year, declining to 40-50% over time.
- "If there's not a lot to negotiate in price and the agent knows the product better than a human" — AI can close deals too.
- For high-value enterprise deals (e.g., 50 target accounts on a whiteboard with $500K sponsorships), human AEs are irreplaceable — the bespoke, high-touch approach still wins.
- "Being a people person is not enough anymore" — the "people person" defense is obsolete when AI can be people people too (ChatGPT described as "our best therapist on planet Earth").
- The sales profession will bifurcate: elite AEs doing high-value enterprise deals thrive, while transactional AEs face displacement.
- AI-native companies (OpenAI, Vercel, Replit) can't hire enough enterprise AEs — net demand for salespeople may actually increase despite per-rep efficiency gains.
