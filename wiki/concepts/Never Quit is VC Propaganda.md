## Definition

"Never quit" is VC propaganda is the argument by Matt McGinness that the Silicon Valley mantra of never giving up serves venture capitalists' incentives, not entrepreneurs'. VCs benefit from founders grinding indefinitely because there's always the rare pivot success story, but the vast majority of founders should quit earlier and start fresh.

## Key Information

- VCs cannot take their investment back — they are incentivized to keep founders trying against all odds.
- The rare pivot success stories (Slack from gaming, Notion's persistence) are used as justification, but they are "exceedingly rare."
- McGinness's own experience: he should have quit Inkling after the second or third pivot (around year 4) instead of persisting for 9 years.
- "The Silicon Valley try until you die mindset is not pro-entrepreneur. It's pro-venture capitalist."
- Advice: "You should fucking quit. You should reset the clock. You should reset the cap table."
- Most VCs, when you quit, are supportive and want to invest in your next company. Good seed investors assume every investment goes to zero.
- "Ask people for relevant experience, not advice. If you ask for advice, they will always give it. If you ask for relevant experience, they rarely have any to offer."
- On timing: companies that hit it big hit big pretty quick. If you're at year 4-5 without "screaming rip-roaring growth," it's extraordinarily difficult to pivot to success.
