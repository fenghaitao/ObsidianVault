## Definition

[[Jason Lemkin]]'s term for the internal role required to run a fleet of AI go-to-market agents day-to-day — exemplified by [[Amelia]], SaaStr's "Chief AI Officer" — distinct from the vendor-side [[Forward Deployed Engineer]] role. Lemkin is skeptical that "GTM engineer" (a title he sees people posting about hiring for on LinkedIn) is yet a real, hireable, pre-formed profession.

## Key Information

- Core duties: segmenting the contact/customer base across agents so they don't conflict or duplicate outreach, reviewing agent output for quality/accuracy (10-15 hours/week at SaaStr's scale), and continuously retraining agents as they drift or make mistakes.
- Lemkin's hiring guidance: today (as of this recording) this role is ~95% best filled by promoting someone internal — a "nerdy" generalist who already loves data and has personally experimented with tools like [[Replit]] or [[Vercel]] — rather than hiring an outside "chief agentic GTM officer." He explicitly says odds of finding this person via a traditional sales-career path are "approach zero"; more likely candidates come from product, marketing, or RevOps backgrounds.
- Not a passive job: "they operate autonomously but not without constant oversight and iteration... if you just buy one of these products and disappear, you will have zero ROI."
- Distinguished from the vendor-side Forward Deployed Engineer: the FDE's job is to get a specific agent live and working for a customer; the Chief Orchestration Officer's job is the customer-side, ongoing management of an entire portfolio of agents across functions (support, outbound, inbound, RevOps).
- Lemkin predicts durable demand for this skill set: "if that is you, you're going to be super employable next year... you're going to have so many job offers, you're not going to know what to do."
- Connects to Lemkin's broader "$250,000 SDR" prediction: he envisions future senior GTM staff being paid at that level specifically because they're managing ~10 agents rather than 10 people — i.e., pay scales with orchestration responsibility, not headcount managed.
