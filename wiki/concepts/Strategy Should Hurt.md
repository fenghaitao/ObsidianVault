## Definition

"Strategy Should Hurt" is a prioritization principle originated by Claire Hughes Johnson and adopted by Molly Graham as rule #4 of her Six Rules for Goal Setting. It means that if you're not making trade-offs that are painful, you are not prioritizing heavily enough.

## Key Principles

- Originated by Claire Hughes Johnson in her book "Scaling People"
- "If you're not making trade-offs that are painful, you are not actually doing strategy"
- Graham's original formulation was "set non-goals" — make it as clear what you're not going to do as what you are going to do
- "Strategy should hurt" is a much better way to explain this to people
- Founders often can't cut things off the list — they "just have to have the 10 goals"
- But "six of these goals are not going to get done. So either you pick which four it is or other people are going to pick for you."
- People will show up every day and do something — if you're not clear about priorities, they'll prioritize for you
- "If your goal setting process is not painful, then you're not prioritizing heavily enough"
