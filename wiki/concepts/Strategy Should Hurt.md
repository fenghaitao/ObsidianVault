## Definition

A phrase coined by [[Claire Hughes Johnson]] (author of *Scaling People*) and adopted by [[Molly Graham]] as rule #4 of her "[[Six Rules For Goals And Alignment]]": if a company's strategy or goal-setting process isn't painful — if it doesn't require cutting things you'd like to do — it isn't actually prioritizing.

## Key Information

- Graham says she used to describe the same idea as "set non-goals" (make it as clear what you're *not* doing as what you are), but found Claire Hughes Johnson's "strategy should hurt" phrasing a much more effective way to communicate it.
- The mechanism: the nature of work is that people show up every day and do *something*. If leadership isn't explicit and painful about trade-offs, employees end up silently prioritizing on the company's behalf by choosing whatever they personally find interesting or comfortable.
- Common founder failure mode Graham cites: founders who can't cut a 10-goal list down. Her response: "cool, six of these goals are not going to get done — either you pick which four it is, or other people are going to pick for you."
- Applied test: if your goal-setting process doesn't hurt (doesn't force you to give something up), you're not prioritizing heavily enough.
