## Definition

Scope Creation is the process by which senior engineers identify, define, and own large-scale problems that are not yet recognized or prioritized by the organization. It is a core expectation of Staff Engineers, who must find their own work rather than having it assigned.

## Key Information

- Lee emphasized that scope is "discovered, not invented" — like calculus, it's found by identifying problems that already exist but haven't been surfaced
- The wrong approach: asking "How do I find scope to justify my promotion?" — this is starting from the solution, not the problem
- The right approach: asking "What are the most burning problems in this org?" and "What is the top-of-mind concern for my manager, director, and other engineers?"
- Lee described it as "archaeology or discovery in the code" — finding the gaps, the rickety old systems, the things no one wants to work on
- Rahul compared it to building a startup: you don't start with a solution, you start with the problem
- Ryan Peterman's approach: find the opportunity, convince people it's a good idea, get buy-in, then lead the execution
- Sometimes the most valuable scope is deprioritizing things — like Zach realizing at Airbnb that certification work was a diminishing return
- A key signal: put your hand up when no one else will — those are often the highest-impact opportunities
- The best scope often comes from asking a principal or senior staff engineer: "What would you fix if you had more time?"
