## Definition

[[Nikhyl Singhal]]'s central dichotomy for the current product-management job market: "builders" (people who love hands-on making, prototyping, shipping) are thriving in the AI era, while "information movers" (whose core skill was relaying/translating information up and down an org hierarchy) are being obsoleted — roughly half of existing PMs fall into each camp, per Singhal.

## Key Information

- Historically, most of a senior product person's day was "moving information from one to another" — framing things for a boss so that boss could reframe them for their boss — a role Singhal calls "responsibility without authority," historically a major source of workplace stress.
- AI (especially coding agents) is removing much of the tedious information-moving work (status reports, ticket-writing, backlog grooming), while dramatically lowering the cost of building, which is why builders are experiencing what Singhal calls a "renaissance": more comp, more offers, more paths to founder/CEO/C-level roles.
- Information movers, by contrast, face a much harder path — some may need to leave tech, pivot to a different industry, or use AI tools in a non-tech context, since the specific skill they built a career on (moving information, not building) is shrinking in value.
- Reframes what "PM leverage" should mean going forward, per [[Amol]] and Singhal: not personally shipping code alongside engineers (which Singhal calls "a cheap knockoff of an engineer"), but building the internal tools/systems that track and evaluate the now much-higher volume and pace of product changes — replacing tickets/backlogs/status-reports with automation, freeing time for judgment.
- Distinguishes "pixel generators" from "taste makers" within design, a parallel split Singhal believes explains why design-role hiring is comparatively flat despite the AI boom: many companies conflate design with production capacity rather than the judgment/taste dimension that AI can't replace.
