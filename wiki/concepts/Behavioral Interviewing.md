## Definition

Behavioral Interviewing is the portion of the interview process focused on past experiences, how a candidate operates, and cultural fit. Steve Huynh argues it is the most underappreciated part of big tech interviews, having outsized impact on both the hire/no-hire decision and the level at which a candidate is brought in.

## Key Information

- Steve uses a poker analogy: coding and system design are the "small blind and big blind" (necessary but not sufficient), while behavioral is the "poker part" where the real match is made
- Amazon will reject candidates with strong coding and system design if behavioral answers are poor
- The fundamental question being answered: "Do I want to work with this person?"
- The interview prep industry dramatically under-indexes on behavioral — Steve recommends spending 40% of prep time on behavioral, not 5%
- "Packaging" your experience is key: communicating who you are, what you're about, and how you operate in a coherent, efficient story
- The way you tell stories and communicate betrays your level — "SD1 stories" from someone applying for senior creates incongruity that leads to down-leveling
- Even simple questions like "Tell me about yourself" should be treated as an opportunity to communicate your level and passions
- Behavioral interviews have outsized impact on leveling — the coding and system design are pass/fail, but behavioral determines level
