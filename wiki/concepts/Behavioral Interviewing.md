## Definition

Behavioral Interviewing is the portion of the interview process focused on past experiences, how a candidate operates, and cultural fit. Steve Huynh argues it is the most underappreciated part of big tech interviews, having outsized impact on both the hire/no-hire decision and the level at which a candidate is brought in.

## Key Information

- Steve uses a poker analogy: coding and system design are the "small blind and big blind" (necessary but not sufficient), while behavioral is the "poker part" where the real match is made
- Amazon will reject candidates with strong coding and system design if behavioral answers are poor
- The fundamental question being answered: "Do I want to work with this person?"
- The interview prep industry dramatically under-indexes on behavioral — Steve recommends spending 40% of prep time on behavioral, not 5%
- "Packaging" your experience is key: communicating who you are, what you're about, and how you operate in a coherent, efficient story
- The way you tell stories and communicate betrays your level — "SD1 stories" from someone applying for senior creates incongruity that leads to down-leveling
- Even simple questions like "Tell me about yourself" should be treated as an opportunity to communicate your level and passions
- Behavioral interviews have outsized impact on leveling — the coding and system design are pass/fail, but behavioral determines level

### Austin McDonald's Methodology
- The story-selection priority order is scope → relevance → recency → uniqueness: scope beats relevance because the goal is to exit the interview having told the highest-scope stories that best represent you, even if that means pivoting a "conflict with a PM" question into a bigger conflict story.
- Meta's five behavioral signal areas: driving results, embracing ambiguity, resolving conflicts, growing continuously, and communicating effectively; broader signal areas (his set of eight) cover ownership, ambiguity, conflict, and growth.
- In the interview, run "decode, select, deliver": decode which signal the question targets, select the matching story, and deliver it with an arc.
- He prefers CALR (context, actions, results, learnings) over STAR — STAR has no place for the reflection/learnings that signal above-staff judgment.
- Match the interviewer's energy (casual banter vs efficient list-driven) and manage the clock: a trunk question ("tell me about an ambiguous project") allows a long story, while leaf questions get short answers.
- Common senior mistakes: low-scope stories, too much irrelevant context, opening yourself to uncharitable interpretations ("the technical debt was your fault"), skipping the non-technical (mentoring, organizing, managing up), and unpracticed storytelling.
- Treat every touchpoint as evaluative: the recruiter call, the hiring-manager chat, and even the final "do you have questions" — asking an insightful "what makes this role successful?" question reinforces engagement ([[summary-20260316 - Meta Hiring Lead： Avoid Downleveling, Liars, Passing OpenAI And Anthropic Interviews]]).
