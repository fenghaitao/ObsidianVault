## Definition

Technical consulting is the practice of being inserted into a client organization to rapidly build or transform systems on a project basis, via either a "get in, build, get out" engagement or a longer retainer model.

## Key Information

- Two models: "get in, build, and get out" versus the retainer model, where the agency builds, maintains, and enhances the software over years and accumulates institutional knowledge.
- Fly-by-night projects (sold purely to keep consultants billable) leave clients with a redundant, incompatible patchwork of platforms that a digital transformation must then "stitch together."
- Consultants are parachuted into varying client cultures — rigid hierarchies, open/cooperative, or internal-startup models — and must map the client's political topology to survive.
- Career progression within consulting: junior dev → senior → tech lead → architect → enterprise architect → technical director, after which the work shifts to selling vision to the C-suite.
- Technical consulting draws less resistance than management consulting, which is closely associated with layoff recommendations.
