## Definition

Founder idiosyncrasies is the concept that every successful company succeeds on the foundation of the specific idiosyncrasies of its founder(s). The specific traits that make a founder unusual are what drive the company's success, and these traits are not replicable by other founders.

## Key Information

- Articulated by Matt McGinness as an investing insight: great investors spot the "element of spikiness and greatness" in a founder.
- Example: Rippling succeeds for almost the polar opposite reasons that Notion succeeds. Both succeed on founder idiosyncrasies.
- Notion succeeded because of Ivan Zhao's "absolute insistence on craftsmanship" — this is not generalizable advice.
- McGinness on replicating Notion: "You can't replicate it because you're not Ivan. You can't replicate it because it's not 2010."
- The takeaway from Notion is NOT "persist" or "don't give up." The takeaway is that every company succeeds on founder idiosyncrasies.
- Narrative violations (Jeff Lewis concept): every company with outsized success violates common wisdom in some important way.
- The "checklist" approach to investing (suppressing beta) misses the alpha that comes from founder idiosyncrasies.
