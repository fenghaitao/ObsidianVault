## Definition

Curiosity is the drive to understand how things work, not just that they work. It is the primary driver of deep technical learning and is essential for building a sustainable foundation of engineering knowledge.

## Key Information

- Evan identified a lack of curiosity as his biggest career regret: "I took shortcuts... once it works, you might move on, alternatively you get there and then you're inquisitive about why it actually worked"
- Meta's impact-driven culture had a downside: it encouraged "ship it and run" and didn't reward curiosity about how things worked
- Evan's realization: "I used [Meta's caching layer] every day as an abstraction... but didn't care to or know significantly about how it actually worked"
- Since leaving Meta, Evan now reads engineering blogs and is "inquisitive about underlying libraries or platforms that we're using, how they work, why they work, how they were built"
- Ryan agreed: "curiosity is probably the biggest tool for actually driving technical learning"
- Evan wished he had been more curious at Meta: "Facebook publishes all of these different blogs... I read all these blogs now and I wish I had done some of that while I was actually at Meta"
- The shift from "ship it and move on" to "understand why it works" represents a maturation in engineering mindset
- Curiosity is what allows you to build "a much more sustainable foundation" of knowledge
