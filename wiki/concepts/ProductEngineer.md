## Definition
A Product Engineer is an evolving software engineering role that combines traditional engineering skills with product management, customer understanding, and UX judgment. As AI agents handle more implementation details, Tuomas Artman predicts that all engineers will need to become product engineers — mini-PMs who talk to customers, understand their needs, and build the right things rather than just piping data around.

## Key Information
- Tuomas Artman predicts that in four years, if AI growth continues, "everybody will become a product engineer"
- Engineers won't be needed to pipe data from one place to another — AI will handle that
- Engineers will still be needed to know what customers want, what good features look like, and what good UX feels like
- Product engineers are "mini-PMs who talk with customers, engage at that layer, and then implement functionality that customers want"
- Linear already hires for this: most of their engineers are product engineers focused on the product, building features and engaging with customers
- Linear's hiring process (full-week paid trial) screens for product thinking by having candidates build a greenfield project from start to finish
- Gergely Orosz noted the historical evolution: programmers used to need one language, then multiple languages, then QA, then DevOps — now PM and customer support skills are being added
- Tuomas recommends two ways to grow product sense: (1) get closer to customers by building things for real users, (2) read Apple's Human Interface Guidelines for UX knowledge
- The role shift is driven by AI commoditizing pure implementation work, making product judgment the remaining differentiator
