## Definition
The AI addiction trap is the psychological hook of AI coding tools that makes engineers keep prompting despite diminishing returns. It has two components: the slot-machine dynamic (the next prompt might be the one that works) and the illusion of productivity (producing lots of output tricks you into thinking you're being efficient when you're actually skipping critical thinking).

## Key Information
- Identified by Cristina Poncela Cubeiro as one of two core problems in AI-assisted development (alongside the engineering challenge)
- **Slot-machine dynamic**: You never know if the next prompt will add a working feature or be the "last drop of slop that brings your product crashing down" — this unpredictability is addictive
- **Illusion of productivity**: High output velocity tricks engineers into feeling productive while they skip the critical step of stopping to think about whether they're taking the best approach
- The baseline expectation shifts: what started as a fun productivity boost becomes mandatory pressure — everyone must use AI to keep up
- When you're in the flow, it's very difficult for yourself to stop, and impossible for your agent to stop — it reads files it shouldn't, makes changes it shouldn't
- The only solution is to consciously slow down and insert friction at critical moments
