## Definition

Taking 100% responsibility is a concept from "The 15 Commitments of Conscious Leaders" that means rejecting the roles of victim, hero, or blamer in conflict. Instead, you ask: "What is my part in creating this dynamic?" and "How am I complicit in creating the conditions I claim I don't want?" (Jerry Colonna). It's the prerequisite for any effective conflict resolution.

## Key Information

- From "The 15 Commitments of Conscious Leaders" (one of Rachel Lockett's most recommended books)
- When in challenging interpersonal conflict, leaders often fall into victimhood ("this is happening to me"), blame ("it's their fault"), or hero ("I'll just do it myself").
- Instead, take responsibility for your part: "What is my piece in making this dynamic happen? And how can I address it?"
- Jerry Colonna's question: "How are you complicit in creating the conditions you claim you don't want?"
- You are always somehow complicit in creating the issue you're complaining about
- Entering any conflict conversation with humility and curiosity about the other person's experience is critical — no model (NVC or otherwise) can fix a person who's coming in rigid and full of blame
- Use this self-awareness to put down your own defenses and acknowledge your contribution before engaging
