## Definition

Co-founder relationship management is the practice of intentionally building and maintaining a healthy co-founder dynamic, which is likened to a marriage. 65% of startups fail because of co-founder conflict. Key practices include self-awareness, role clarity, making conscious commitments (vows), and creating regular space for reflection and connection.

## Key Information

- **65% of startups fail because of co-founder conflict.**
- The co-founder relationship is like a marriage — it's a private, tender, high-stakes relationship with few supports.
- **Step 1 — Self-Awareness**: What do I bring into this dynamic? How am I being experienced? Understand the dance you're in. Classic pattern: CEO (visionary, blind optimism, selling) vs. CTO (skeptic, facts, depth, self-sufficiency).
- **Step 2 — Conscious Commitments (Vows)**: Make explicit commitments to each other. How are we going to be together? How will we make decisions? How will we deal with conflict? Consider co-founder "vows" and recommitments/renewals.
- **Step 3 — Regular Space for Relationship**: Just like couples need date night, co-founders need time to connect. Weekly check-ins, monthly check-ins, annual in-person sessions. Rachel Lockett and First Round published a document with questions for each cadence.
- **Dance floor vs. balcony**: Instead of being on the dance floor (hustling), you need time on the balcony to look down at what's happening: "How are we doing? Is this still working for both of us?"
- When relationships are already strained: Name the current state, use 360 feedback, be open and vulnerable, recognize how lonely it is to lead without each other's support.
- "End it or mend it" — a decision framework one co-founder used. Sometimes the outcome is clarity and a loving exit, which is still success.
- Outside support (coach, HR leader, GC with people skills) can be useful as a facilitator when dynamics are challenging.
- The Enneagram can be used as a common language for understanding co-founder dynamics.
