## Definition

Managing Up is the practice of proactively communicating with and influencing your manager to align expectations, secure support, and create conditions for your own success. Ricky described it as essential for an accelerated promotion timeline.

## Key Information

### Ricky's Approach
- Ricky explicitly endorsed managing up: "I fully support managing your manager. I think it's something that you have to do if you want an accelerated promotion timeline."
- Promotions should be an ongoing conversation between you and your manager, not a one-time request
- He regularly asked his manager: "Do you feel like these projects that I'm currently doing are for the next level?"

### The One-Year Roadmap Strategy
- A year before his L5 promotion, Ricky created a one-year roadmap of projects he planned to do and brought it to his manager
- He asked: "Do you feel like this is enough for the next level?"
- This set expectations early and gave his manager visibility into his trajectory
- The roadmap was built bottom-up — he identified his own projects, then got manager buy-in rather than waiting for top-down direction

### Saying No to Non-Promotable Work
- When someone else assigns work that isn't at the next level, Ricky recommended escalating to your manager immediately
- When the manager themselves assigns such work, push back: "Is this something I have to do? I have my own projects here that I feel like would have more impact and also help me grow more."
- A good manager's job is to find projects that reports actually want to do — forced work leads to poor quality
