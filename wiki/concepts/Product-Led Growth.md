## Definition

Product-Led Growth (PLG) is a go-to-market strategy where the product itself serves as the primary driver of customer acquisition, activation, and expansion — typically through self-serve signup, freemium, or free trial models. It remains relevant but has a natural ceiling.

## Key Information

- PLG makes sense for most companies at the outset unless they are explicitly building for enterprise from day one (e.g., Sierra targeting Global 2000 with eight-figure deals).
- PLG is a major driver of growth at both Vercel and Stripe.
- The common mistake: waiting too long to add a sales-assisted motion. PLG has a ceiling — "people are generally not going to give you a million dollars via self-served flow."
- Outbound takes time to become a predictable engine, so companies that delay adding sales hit growth walls.
- Jeanne DeWitt Grosser: "I don't know that I can think of a hundred billion dollar company that's PLG only." Even LastPass, a long-time PLG company, eventually added sales.
- Vercel's pricing unbundling (August change) drove PLG growth by making enterprise features available self-serve online for startups — removing the need for a human intermediary.
- At some point, to sustain growth rates, deal sizes must get bigger, requiring a sales-assisted or sales-led motion.
- Elena Verna describes Lovable's approach as "product-led growth to the max, supercharged": giving the product away for free to drive word-of-mouth, treating LLM costs as marketing spend rather than margin drain.
- In AI-native PLG, the product itself is the primary marketing channel — giving credits to hackathons, events, and ambassadors who will do the distribution for you.
- This supercharged PLG model works because it avoids competing on AdWords or paid channels; the cost per eyeball through product giveaways is lower than traditional paid marketing.
- **Jason Cohen's caveat:** "PLG is nice, but you still need marketing to bring the people in in the first place. PLG just means there's not a salesperson unless you're expanding or some other segment." PLG doesn't solve the fundamental problem that marketing grows linearly while cancellations grow exponentially with company size.
