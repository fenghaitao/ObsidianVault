## Definition

[[Keith Rabois]]'s contrarian investing heuristic, drawn from the idea (referenced via Ed Catmull's Pixar writing) that every great creative idea starts as an "ugly baby" no one wants to nurture: if fellow VCs would laugh at an investment, that's a positive signal, not a negative one, since it suggests real alpha (an idea the market hasn't correctly priced) rather than consensus competition.

## Key Information

- Rabois explicitly runs a mental check on seed/Series A investments: "I want half my friends who are VCs to laugh at me... if so, this is potentially a great investment because it's an ugly baby."
- Connects to a broader pattern Rabois and Lenny discuss: early employees at generation-defining companies (OpenAI, Palantir, Stripe) often report that outsiders explicitly mocked the idea before it worked.
- Complements [[Undiscovered Talent]] and [[Barrels And Ammunition]] as part of Rabois's broader thesis that real competitive edges come from correctly valuing things (people, ideas) that conventional evaluation processes dismiss or misprice.
