## Definition

The crypto wars are a series of recurring conflicts between government surveillance agencies and cryptographers over the public availability of strong encryption.

## Key Information

- First crypto war (late 1970s–early 1980s): over the freedom to publish cryptographic papers in international journals and the DES key size; the NSA threatened Martin Hellman with jail, and his life seemed at risk.
- Second crypto war (1990s): centered on the Clipper Chip and key escrow — allowing strong encryption while the government holds master keys.
- Third crypto war (~10–15 years ago): the San Bernardino/Apple iPhone case, a repeat of key escrow under a new name; Ron Rivest co-authored "Keys Under Doormats" about the dangers of mandatory backdoors.
- Hellman's core argument: you cannot give strong encryption only to "the good guys" and not the bad guys; any backdoor makes everyone insecure. He tells the car analogy — had cars been invented classified, the government would have fought their civilian release.
