## Definition

"Role collapse" refers to the blurring of traditional functional boundaries between product management, design, and engineering as AI makes implementation cheap across every discipline — a pattern [[Andrew Ambrosino]] says he has observed more strongly within [[OpenAI]]'s [[Codex]] org than elsewhere in the company or economy.

## Key Information

- Ambrosino's formulation: people are no longer defined by a fixed role/title but by "the average of where they're working" — e.g., a designer on his team may write plenty of code and do plenty of PM-style work, but if you plot their time, the "average" still reads as design.
- On the Codex team specifically: designers "speak engineer" and write code; product managers write code; one engineer/designer (Alexander) holds a master's degree in computer science. Ambrosino, by contrast, does not have a CS degree despite doing highly technical work.
- Ambrosino attributes this partly to Codex being a technical product built for engineers, requiring closer collaboration and shared vocabulary across disciplines than typical.
- He is explicitly cautious about the more extreme version of this idea — some companies "getting rid of the product role" entirely, expecting everyone to just be a "builder." He calls this "a terrible idea": it risks discarding real, hard-won best practices and treating engineering-adjacent skills as if they require no expertise (e.g., "you can use Excel, but you cannot work on the finance team").
- He distinguishes between the *boundary/gatekeeping* aspect of roles (e.g., "this isn't your lane," or conflating a role with mastery of a specific tool) — which he welcomes eroding — and the *discipline/skill* aspect of roles (real expertise and practices built up over time) — which he thinks should not disappear.
- Notes that management itself is not disappearing either; rather, "everyone's kind of both" IC and manager now — ICs manage agents/work rather than typing code character by character, and managers of teams do the same thing at a different granularity.
- Connects to OpenAI's long-standing use of the job title "[[Member Of Technical Staff]]" (rather than role-specific titles) as an earlier, research-culture-driven precedent for this kind of role fluidity.
- Related but distinct from [[Zone Defense]]: role collapse describes the blurring of what any individual does; zone defense describes how a team of increasingly fluid, high-taste people should organize their coverage of a large problem space.
- Per [[Lazar]] (episode 27, professional vibe coding at [[Lovable]]): "these venn diagrams of engineer, designer, PM used to be very separate. Now they're converging" — framed alongside his prediction that everyone becomes some flavor of "rapid engineer," "forward deployed engineer," "AI assistant engineer," or "vibe coder," with the specific title mattering less than the underlying skill of directing AI with good (or bad) judgment. See [[Professional Vibe Coder]] and [[Forward Deployed Engineer]].
