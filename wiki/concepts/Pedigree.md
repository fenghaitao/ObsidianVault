## Definition

Pedigree in the tech industry refers to the name-brand value of having worked at prestigious companies (especially FAANG) at senior levels. It serves as a signal to future employers and investors, setting a floor on perceived competence through pattern matching.

## Key Information

- "Pedigree matters. I don't care how much people talk about 'tech is merit-based' — pedigree and name brand matter a ton"
- Humans fundamentally do pattern matching — "you worked at Meta or Google and you're a staff engineer at Google, most other staff engineers at Google are pretty good, so from pattern matching I assume you're pretty good"
- This can be used for positive purposes — setting a floor on your next job or startup credibility
- "I am an ex-Meta staff engineer" carries weight whether you call it ego or not
- The panelist said it should matter to anyone who "really thinks about career progression and job hopping"
- Pedigree can also be used negatively (pattern matching on race or gender), which the panelist acknowledged as "obviously bad"
- The concept connects to down-leveling — titles at prestigious companies carry more weight than inflated titles at smaller companies
