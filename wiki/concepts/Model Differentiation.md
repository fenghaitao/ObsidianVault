## Definition

Model differentiation is the prediction that AI models will increasingly diverge in personality and behavior based on the values and objective functions chosen by their creators, rather than converging toward commoditization.

## Key Information

- Edwin initially thought AI models would become commoditized, all behaving similarly with slight intelligence differences
- Over the past year, he realized that "the values that the companies have will shape the models"
- Example: Claude will iterate through 30 versions of an email to make it perfect; a different model might say "your email is great, just send it and move on"
- This is analogous to how Google, Facebook, and Apple would each build fundamentally different search engines based on their principles and values
- Grok is an early example of model differentiation — it already has a distinctly different personality
- The differentiation comes from the objective function each lab chooses to optimize for: engagement, truth, productivity, creativity, etc.
- "In the same way that there's a fork in the road between how you could choose how your model behaves for this question, it's like for every other question that models have, the kind of behavior that you want will fundamentally affect it"
