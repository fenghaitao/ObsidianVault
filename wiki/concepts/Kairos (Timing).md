## Definition

Kairos is the ancient Greek rhetorical principle of opportune timing — saying the right thing at the right moment. Jacob Warwick references it as critical to negotiation: when you communicate is as important as what you communicate. This connects to his principles of slowing down, choosing the right time of day, and controlling the environment.

## Key Information

- From Aristotle's rhetoric (alongside ethos, pathos, logos)
- The principle of seizing the opportune moment
- Jacob: "Timing is another one that we don't often talk about. Kairos is important."
- Applications in negotiation:
  - Don't respond to offers immediately — take 2-3 days ([[Haste Equals Risk]])
  - Choose the time of day when you're at your best ([[Home Field Advantage in Negotiation]])
  - Don't negotiate over email where you can't control when the message is read ([[Don't Negotiate Over Email]])
  - The "rubber band effect" of saying no to inconvenient meeting times
- Information + timing = power in negotiation (Jacob's core formula)
- "Too hasty equals risk" — the inverse of kairos
