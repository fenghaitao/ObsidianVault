## Definition

First-party data as an AI moat is the strategic insight that in the AI era, companies that own their own comprehensive data (the "mine") will be able to build powerful AI products, while point solutions that must integrate with third-party data are at a severe disadvantage.

## Key Information

- Articulated by Matt McGinness: "If you're selling the shovels like OpenAI and Google with Gemini, you can make money. And if you own the mine like Rippling with the data, you can make money."
- Companies in the middle (building AI software that uses others' shovels and others' data) are in a difficult economic position — neither the shovel provider nor the data owner will let them build a big business on their backs.
- Point solutions don't have enough data in the age of AI to be useful. AI needs context across many data sources to do joins, correlations, and "magic."
- Integration with third-party systems means "drinking data through a straw" — even the best integrations are limited.
- Example: The biggest HCM company integrates with the biggest payroll company through flat files via SFTP — this is fundamentally incompatible with AI.
- This will kill off 80-90% of standalone AI businesses according to McGinness.
- Rippling's strategy: own all customer business data in one place with a semantic layer, enabling AI to work across all functions.
- This is part of the broader bundling vs. unbundling cycle: we are in a period of bundling.
