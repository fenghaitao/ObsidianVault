## Definition

[[Jeetu Patel]]'s heuristic for telling a durable "mega trend" (worth going all-in on) from a passing "hype cycle" (not worth vanity investment): can most ordinary people understand what the trend could ultimately do for them, or does it require a PhD to explain?

## Key Information

- Rule: "when there's a mega trend, don't fight it"; conversely, don't succumb to the temptation to chase a hype cycle for appearances.
- Test in practice: AI passes — you can go to ChatGPT, ask a question, get a useful answer, no advanced explanation needed. Web3 failed the test for Patel — he says he could never make heads or tails of a clear, simple use case for it despite widespread hype.
- Underlying logic: a genuine mega trend by definition affects a large population; if an idea is too complicated for most people to grasp its value, it's unlikely to have that scale of impact.
- Feeds directly into "timing," the top-ranked factor in [[Six Ingredients For Winning Companies]]: Patel uses this heuristic to decide whether to double/triple/quadruple down on something right now, or wait, since some initiatives are legitimately mega trends that just haven't hit the right timing yet (may take years to manifest).
- Related caution: don't over-index on past experience when judging a fast-moving mega trend like AI — assume the state of the art 6 months from now will differ meaningfully from today, and prepare for that future state rather than anchoring on current capability.
