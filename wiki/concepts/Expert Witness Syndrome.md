## Definition

[[Mark Pincus]]'s term for the position of individual contributors early in their careers: closest to the primary data and probably closest to the right answer, yet furthest from the authority to make the decision — "called to the stand," giving testimony, then excused while others decide and they must live with the consequences.

## Key Information

- Pincus: many people become founders specifically because they're "sick of being in that position" — tired of disagreeing (correctly, in their view) yet having to "commit" to clean up someone else's mistaken decision.
- The best hires to turn into "CEOs" of their own area (see [[Make Everyone A CEO]]) are described as "frustrated expert witnesses" — a bit of a know-it-all, convinced they have the right answers, with pent-up motivation to finally prove it.
- Connects to "[[Close To The Metal]]": once a person becomes a founder/CEO, Pincus argues they must resist losing the closeness to ground-truth data/decisions that defined their expert-witness years, rather than delegating away the most important product judgment calls.
