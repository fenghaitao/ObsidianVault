## Definition

Question Asking is the habit of aggressively seeking information, clarification, and help at work. It is described as the single most important differentiator between high and low performers, especially early in a career.

## Key Information

- Ricky's one piece of advice: "Ask tons of questions. Keep asking questions when you're at work"
- He told an intern to ask questions, and she later said she wished he had told her to ask questions — he had, but she didn't internalize how much he meant it
- The fear: "If I ask too many questions, they're going to think I'm stupid" — but the reality is the opposite
- The mindset shift: "I'd rather ask questions and look dumb and learn something — and maybe I'll get fired because people think I'm dumb — than to do nothing, not learn anything, and get fired"
- "I'm just going to try to learn as much as I can here and if I get fired, I get fired, but at least I learned"
- Ryan added that the biggest differentiator between those who succeed quickly and those who don't is "how quickly they're able to start asking questions, learning, being curious, unblocking themselves"
- High performers have "the audacity to ask questions, propose improvements" — even when they're sometimes wrong, the logic is visible
- Low performers are quieter, try to figure everything out alone, make no progress, and don't get return offers
- The advice is especially important for juniors and interns
