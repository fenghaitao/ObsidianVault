## Definition

A co-op program (exemplified by the University of Waterloo) alternates academic terms with work terms each semester, giving students repeated, structured opportunities to try different roles, industries, and environments before committing.

## Key Information

- Waterloo's co-op alternates study-work-study-work every semester, giving "at least six shots on goal" at trying things
- Removes the stigma of short tenure: "that's my co-op, that's the whole point"
- Lets students discover early that an industry, city, or role "didn't click" (Wall Street, medicine, consulting) without resume damage
- Provides hands-on "pragmatic engineering" (actually building and shipping) rather than only academic theory
- Grigorik: an "open secret" and "lever for success" more universities should copy
