## Definition

A critical investment distinction drawn by Sam Lessin: there's a difference between a great business that uses AI to supercharge or improve itself (good investment) and a company that brands itself as an "AI company" (likely a terrible seed investment). AI is a powerful tool for existing businesses, not a great startup opportunity on its own.

## Key Information

- Sam Lessin's contrarian take: "The seed venture capitalists who invest in companies that are branded as AI companies are going to lose an impossibly large amount of money."
- "Not using AI in your startup is the equivalent of not using the cloud in 2010 or not using the internet in 2000. It would be insane."
- "But there's a difference between a great business that you're using AI to supercharge or make better... That's not an AI business. This is a business."
- AI companies are too capital-intensive for seed-stage returns: "Even at a $500 billion market cap, the seed investors have made something like 25 times their money — that's basically a middling at best seed investment."
- "These things are so capital consumptive... seed investing does not work in highly capital-intensive businesses."
- They're fundamentally commoditizing — "it's very unclear what the lock-in or value is on any of these things."
- Sam invests in the cultural implications of AI: companies like Sublime Security that address the "Voight-Kampff test" problem (are you real or a bot?)
- "There's a lot of opportunity... businesses that will be disrupted in interesting ways. They're not AI businesses. They're businesses that will be disrupted."
- Example: June Date is a dating app that uses AI (ChatGPT history analysis) but is not an "AI company"
