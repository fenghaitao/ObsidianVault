## Definition

A career strategy articulated by Marc Andreessen where professionals combine expertise across multiple domains (e.g., product management, engineering, and design) rather than specializing in just one. The additive effect of being good at two things is more than double; being good at three is more than triple — creating a "super relevant specialist" in the combination of domains.

## Key Information

- Articulated by Marc Andreessen on Lenny's Podcast in the context of AI collapsing role boundaries
- The multiplicative (not additive) effect of combining skills: 2 domains = more than 2x value, 3 domains = more than 3x value
- AI enables this by making it possible for any professional to perform tasks in adjacent domains (e.g., a PM can now code, a designer can now manage products)
- Contrasts with traditional specialization: instead of being the best at one thing, be very good at a unique combination of things
- Directly connected to the "Mexican standoff" between PM, engineer, and designer roles
