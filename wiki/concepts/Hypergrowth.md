## Definition
Hypergrowth is a phase of extreme scaling where a company ships features at all costs to win a winner-takes-all market. Tuomas Artman and Gergely Orosz both experienced this at Uber, and they draw parallels to the current AI landscape where the ease of shipping with AI agents creates similar dynamics — everyone can ship fast, making quality the key differentiator.

## Key Information
- Tuomas Artman described Uber's hypergrowth as "I never want to go through again" — fighting fires, keeping infrastructure running, scaling as quickly as possible, trying everything to win
- At Uber, hypergrowth meant shipping features at all costs with revenue as the golden metric
- Quality was sacrificed because it didn't affect revenue — until Lyft launched a competing product at the same price point
- The parallel to AI: when everyone has the capability to ship tons of functionality, you're always in competition with someone who can match your feature set
- In hypergrowth, quality is the first thing sacrificed because it's hard to measure and its degradation is gradual
- The early Uber had engineers who cared about quality (e.g., pixel-perfect PR reviews), but this culture eroded as the team grew and incentives shifted
- The lesson for the AI era: don't let the ease of shipping with AI agents recreate hypergrowth dynamics where quality is sacrificed for speed
