## Definition

"Bending the Reed vs Breaking the Reed" is Philip Su's metaphor for understanding the limits of what you can push in life. Some things bend under pressure (and can recover), while others break permanently. Philip nearly broke his relationship with his now-wife through extreme work hours.

## Key Information

- Philip's personal story: when his fiancee visited him in Seattle during his sleeping-bag era at Microsoft, he would see her only from 11 PM to midnight each evening for a late Denny's dinner
- "Anybody I tell that story to, you could bet nine out of ten times that's the story that ends with 'that's how I got the engagement ring back'"
- He was "clearly bending something to an extreme that most things would have broken"
- He is "very fortunate and lucky that she is my wife now"
- The metaphor applies to career vs. life trade-offs: "be sure you're comfortable with other things breaking, because that is what it will take to get there if that's truly what you want"
- His advice to his younger self: in the big arc, getting to E7 at 30 vs 38 doesn't matter — but keeping a healthy relationship with spouse and kids does
