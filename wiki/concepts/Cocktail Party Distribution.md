## Definition

[[Mark Pincus]]'s metaphor for consumer/social distribution: a "great cocktail party" is a place people are excited (not just obligated) to be, because they get real value there — historically, "leads" (connections, dates, jobs, listings). Reinventing social/consumer distribution means finding or building the next such gathering place.

## Key Information

- Lineage of "cocktail parties": [[Napster]] (the lead was a music file) → [[Facebook]]/[[LinkedIn]] (the lead was better lead generation/productivity for one's time, replacing noisy Google/Craigslist searches).
- Diagnostic: "we know it when we see a great cocktail party... you're like, 'Oh, I'm so glad I'm here.'" Contrasted with current social media, which Pincus says has "lost the adrenaline" — evidenced by NPS swinging from +35 to -35 when people quit Facebook/Instagram, and people being proud to say they've left.
- Pincus's Easter-egg claim: "today we're all hanging out on our Claude, on our GPT, but there's no cocktail party" — i.e., AI chat is a quiet, lonely gathering with no social layer yet, and his challenge to founders is to "figure out how to make it rowdy."
- Connects to the "[[Social Membrane]]" idea: AI agents brokering relationships/trust/attention between people could be the mechanism that builds the next cocktail party.
- Zynga's own strategy was explicitly to "go to the cocktail party" (Facebook, where everyone already was) and "drop games in the middle" to give people a new dimension to socialize.
