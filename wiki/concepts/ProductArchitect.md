## Definition

The product architect is the emerging role for builders in the AI era: someone who takes a raw idea, shapes it into a clear spec, and directs AI agents to build the right thing. The skill is not coding — it's thinking clearly about what to build and why, bringing strategy, creative direction, and judgment about user needs.

## Key Information

- The hardest parts of traditional software development (syntax, debugging, getting stuck) are solved by AI.
- What remains: deciding what to build, defining scope, anticipating edge cases, making product decisions.
- The product architect thinks like a product owner with an AI development team at their disposal.
- Business owners and operators with a systems mindset are already well-positioned — they understand processes and customers.
- The role involves: shaping specs, making scope decisions (in/out), defining data models, breaking work into milestones, reviewing output, giving feedback.
- No certification or gatekeeping — the skill is learnable.
- Brian Casel's [[PRDCreator]] skill bridges the experience gap by asking the questions an experienced product designer would ask.
