## Definition

"Building in public" is the practice of openly sharing your work, failures, and knowledge (via YouTube, LinkedIn, courses, etc.) as a career strategy — cited by [[Lazar]] as literally how his [[Professional Vibe Coder]] job came to exist, before any company had formally hired him for it.

## Key Information

- Lazar's framing: "it became a job by building in public... you don't need a company to hire you. You can hire yourself as a professional vibe coder first." He was already vibe coding, teaching it via a YouTube channel, and running a paid course before [[Elena Verna]] hired him at [[Lovable]] — he says he simply "changed the vehicle" once hired, since he was already doing the job.
- Concrete tactics Lazar cites: sharing all failures and knowledge without holding back "secrets," posting long-form reflections on LinkedIn (his preferred platform, since he says his cadence doesn't fit X/Twitter), running a YouTube channel documenting projects and lessons, and participating in hackathons to connect with other builders.
- Also cited as a hiring signal in the other direction: Lazar notes some Lovable job candidates got hired by sending a Lovable-built app instead of a resume — a form of "building in public" aimed directly at a specific decision-maker.
- Connected to a broader point about distribution/attention as a scarce resource: in a world where almost anyone can build something, getting in front of eyeballs (of both users and potential employers) becomes a key differentiator alongside the building itself.
- Per [[Elena Verna]] (episode 38): building in public — coupled with employee and founder social presence — is one of Lovable's biggest company-level growth strategies, not just an individual career tactic. Lovable ships and publicly narrates changes multiple times per day, which Verna says functions simultaneously as a resurrection strategy, a re-engagement strategy (users check social to see what shipped instead of reading a newsletter), and a way to make users feel heard, since the gap between voicing feedback and seeing it shipped is very short. She contrasts this with larger companies, where narrative control across many employees is harder to maintain. Cites [[Anton Osika]] (Lovable's CEO), [[Grant Lee]] (Gamma's CEO), and [[Gaurav Misra]] (Mirage/Captions' CEO, "ship things you can talk about") as comparable founder-led examples.
