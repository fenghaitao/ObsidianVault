## Definition

Desire for certainty is a psychological tendency that can "spiral people out of control" — especially engineers and product teams facing ambiguous projects. The tactic for managing it: instead of needing certainty about the outcome, create certainty about the next check-in point. Say "I don't know if this will work. We'll check in on X date."

## Key Information

- "As human beings, many of us have a huge desire for certainty. Now more than ever, that is not possible"
- Engineers especially often have a very high desire for certainty
- The tactic: "I don't know the answer right now. I don't know if this is going to be successful. I'm going to check in with you on X date"
- At least there's certainty about the next check-in point
- This is a powerful tool for de-escalating fears and risks around a given initiative
- Complements "Shrinking the Change" — making the next step small and concrete
- Works for convincing executives to fund experiments: "We're going to test this. Here's how we're going to know it's working. We'll come back to you on X date"
