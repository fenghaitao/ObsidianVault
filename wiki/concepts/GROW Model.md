## Definition

The GROW model, as taught by executive coach [[Rachel Lockett]], is a four-category framework of coaching questions used to help a direct report solve their own problem rather than being given an answer: Goal, Reality, Options, Way forward.

## Key Information

- **Goal**: what does success look like? What's the outcome you want?
- **Reality**: where are you stuck today? What are your current challenges? What have you tried?
- **Options**: what are the various paths you could take? What choices do you have in front of you?
- **Way forward**: what are you going to do next? A concrete next step.
- Used non-linearly — a leader doesn't have to march through the four categories in order; if someone is already clear on their goal, skip straight to reality or options based on where they're actually stuck.
- Paired with "[[Active Listening Three Levels]]" as the two core skills of leader-coaching (as distinct from professional/executive coaching): active listening surfaces what's really going on, and GROW-model questions turn that into forward motion.
- Positioned as an alternative to reflexively advising: leaders default to giving answers because they've been rewarded for having them, but this trains a team to keep bringing every hard problem back up the chain instead of building their own judgment. Advising remains appropriate when there's genuine urgency or a real skills gap.
- Demonstrated live on [[Lenny Rachitsky]] in the episode: Lockett coached him through feeling overwhelmed by "endless work," using GROW questions to help him articulate that he wanted roughly 25% more free time while maintaining output quality, and to identify concrete near-term steps (skipping a newsletter issue, revisiting his policies on saying yes to requests).
