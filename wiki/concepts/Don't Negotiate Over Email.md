## Definition

A critical tactical rule from Jacob Warwick: never negotiate compensation over email. Email strips away tone, body language, and the ability to read and adjust to the other party's emotional state. What you intend as collaborative can be received as confrontational if the recipient is in a bad mood or rushed.

## Key Information

- Jacob: "Often we'll hide behind the easiest communication channel possible. I might email you my demands. The problem with that is I can't control tone."
- Danger scenarios:
  - CEO reads your email in the airport security line, pissed off → "That bastard wants more money"
  - Someone reads it on their phone while picking up kids from school
  - Someone reads it in traffic, stressed
- Always prefer video call or in-person over email
- Video calls let you: share tone, read body language, correct in real time, see if they're visibly frustrated and offer to reschedule
- If the recipient looks frustrated: "Would you like to reschedule to a time that feels more comfortable for you?"
- When you must use email, keep it extremely short and neutral — "Open to chat" is enough
- "The more confident you get, the shorter your communication gets"
