## Definition

A tactical principle: control the environment, timing, and setting of your negotiation conversations to maximize your comfort and performance. Going to a company's office puts you out of your element. Walking side by side creates collaboration. Choosing the time of day when you're at your best gives you an edge.

## Key Information

- Jacob: "More deals are closed on the golf course than they are in a boardroom."
- Environmental control:
  - Don't go to their office if possible — you're out of your element
  - Walk side by side instead of sitting across a table (creates collaboration, not confrontation)
  - Take a walk, go for coffee, have lunch — neutral or your territory
- Timing control:
  - Know when you're strongest: "I'm strongest between the hours of 10:00 a.m. and 1:00 p.m. I want a meeting in that time."
  - Don't accept a 6:00 a.m. call just because the CEO wants it
  - Saying "I'm unavailable until that time becomes available" communicates scarcity
- The "rubber band effect": saying no to the CEO makes you more attractive
- OpenAI's interview tactic: walking outside the office through downtown San Francisco instead of interviewing in the chaotic office
- "This is all behavioral psychology"
- Even for ICs: "Hey, I've got a couple of questions. Are you open to take a walk and have a conversation about it?"
