## Definition

[[Eric Ries]]'s proposed antidote to [[Financial Gravity]] in *[[Incorruptible (book)|Incorruptible]]*: a company resists decay by combining "ethos" (a clear, written purpose) with "integrity" (structural governance mechanisms that enforce it), not by relying on good intentions alone.

## Key Information

- Ethos alone is insufficient — [[Google]]'s "don't be evil" was a genuine ethos with no enforcement mechanism, and it eroded to nothing under quarterly-reporting-grade financial rigor with no comparable structural counterpart.
- Integrity is supplied via concrete mechanisms: [[Mission Guardian]] roles, [[Mission Protective Provisions]], [[Perpetual Purpose Trust]]s or industrial-foundation ownership ([[Spiritual Holding Company]]), and cultural reinforcement like the [[Culture Bank]].
- Positive case studies combining both: [[Novo Nordisk]] and [[Zeiss]] (industrial foundations), [[Patagonia]] (perpetual purpose trust with a "purpose protector" who can sue trustees), [[Anthropic]] (Long-Term Benefit Trust plus Public Benefit Corporation status), [[Cloudflare]] (bottom-up mission plus the [[Figure It Out Principle]]).
