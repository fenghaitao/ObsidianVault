## Definition

Organizational-design framework from Safi Bahcall's book *Loonshots*, applied by [[Evan Spiegel]] at [[Snapchat]]: successful companies need both a large, hierarchical, operationally-rigorous organization (needed to reliably serve customers at scale) and a small, flat, low-hierarchy innovation team — with leadership responsible for keeping healthy dialogue between the two rather than letting them become mutually contemptuous.

## Key Information

- Large hierarchical organizations tend to make people risk-averse and focused on promotion within the hierarchy, which suppresses willingness to try new things — a dynamic that actively works against innovation at scale.
- Flat, non-hierarchical teams (Snap's design team oscillates between 9-12 people) allow rapid idea generation and failure without status risk, since "if you want to have a good idea, you have to have lots of ideas."
- The common failure mode: the innovative team dismisses the large org as "bureaucratic and slow," while the large org dismisses the innovative team as unfocused on the actual business — Bahcall's finding, per Spiegel, is that successful companies' leaders actively cultivate mutual respect and dialogue between the two rather than letting this tension fester.
- At Snap, this plays out as ongoing dialogue between engineers focused on reliability/serving ~1 billion users and the small design team constantly generating new ideas — Spiegel considers this dialogue the actual source of the company's innovation output.
- Traces back to Spiegel's own founding relationship with [[Bobby Murphy]] — one design-oriented, one engineering-oriented, in constant collaborative dialogue — as the template later scaled into the whole organization.
