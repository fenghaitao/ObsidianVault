## Definition

Deliberate understaffing, per [[Matt MacInnis]] (CPO, [[Rippling]]), is the practice of always staffing every project below its ideal level on purpose, because the ideal level is never knowable in advance and the two possible errors are not symmetric.

## Key Information

- Framed as a special case of a more general management heuristic: because you can never know the exactly correct value for a resourcing decision (headcount, budget, deadline), you must pick a default and then decide whether to habitually over- or under-shoot it.
- Overstaffing is "poison": it causes politics, and — critically — causes people to work on lower-priority backlog items (the 6th-through-20th items on a stack-ranked list) before it's even confirmed the top 5 are handled correctly. MacInnis calls this "crust."
- Understaffing is the lesser evil, but the wisdom lies in not tipping into starvation — knowing the difference between healthy understaffing and actually crippling a team.
- At [[Rippling]], "everyone is constantly asking for more resources," and new resources do arrive when appropriate — but the default posture is always deliberate scarcity.
- Echoed independently by [[Molly Graham]]'s "[[Fifty Percent Growth Rule]]" in a separate episode, which [[Lenny Rachitsky]] explicitly connects to this framework.
