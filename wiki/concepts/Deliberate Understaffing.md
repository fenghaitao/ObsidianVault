## Definition

Deliberate understaffing is a leadership principle articulated by Matt McGinness at Rippling: always understaff projects rather than overstaff them, because overstaffing leads to politics, waste, and working on low-priority items. The wisdom is in knowing when understaffing has gone too far.

## Key Information

- Core principle: you cannot know the exact right staffing level, so you must choose between over- and under-staffing. Understaffing is always the better choice.
- Overstaffing creates: politics, people working on items further down the priority list than necessary, wasteful work, "crust" (unnecessary code/features), and slower execution.
- The goal is for every team to feel "deliberately understaffed" — constantly asking for more resources but not quite getting them.
- Metaphor: teams should be "dehydrated," always wanting more water. When they're too dehydrated, add resources.
- This framework complements the "extraordinary results require extraordinary efforts" principle — understaffing forces prioritization and intensity.
- McGinness emphasizes that this is a deliberate strategy, not an accident of resource constraints.
