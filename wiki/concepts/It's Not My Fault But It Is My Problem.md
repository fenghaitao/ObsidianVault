## Definition

A mantra coined by Annie Pearl (Jessica Fain's first PM manager at Box) that captures the product leader's responsibility: even when a bad outcome isn't directly your fault, it's still your problem to solve. This is the mindset required for influencing executives — you can't blame them for not seeing your vision.

## Key Information

- **Origin:** Annie Pearl, Jessica Fain's first PM manager at Box, former CPO at Calendly and Glassdoor, now at Microsoft.
- "It's not my fault, but it is my problem."
- Lenny's corollary: "It's your fault if the leaders didn't buy into your ideas."
- You can't just say "they don't see it" or "they agreed to do something else" — that's a failure of your ability to influence.
- This doesn't mean being a "yes man." "You get paid to have an opinion. You get paid to be a domain expert."
- "If you don't respect that person, if you don't believe they know something you don't, you need to quit."
- This is the vantage point a product leader must have to put themselves in the shoes of an executive.
