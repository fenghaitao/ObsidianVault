## Definition

Trailing Promotions refer to the practice at most big tech companies where promotions lag behind demonstrated performance — you must consistently perform at the next level before being promoted to it, rather than being promoted in anticipation of future performance.

## Key Information

- "Promotions are trailing" — you perform at the level first, then get promoted
- This is a common practice at most big tech companies
- Some engineers misinterpret this as "I shouldn't ask about promotion because I haven't been doing it long enough"
- The panel's advice: even though promotions are trailing, you should still have the conversation early to understand what's missing
- Without the conversation, you may think you're "knocking it out of the park" while missing critical elements
- The work needed for a high rating at your current level may be different from the work needed to set up a promotion
- A manager may not know you want promotion if you don't express it — not everyone wants to advance

- Marius Schultz calls them "lagging promotions": ratings look back at the impact you delivered, while promotions check whether you already exhibit the next level's behaviors — so a top rating can coexist with a blocked promo, as it did for him ([[summary-20251208 - Instagram Senior Staff Eng (IC7)： What Held Him Back, Redefining Expectations, Promo Stories]])
