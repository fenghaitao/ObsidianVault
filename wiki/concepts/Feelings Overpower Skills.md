## Definition

[[Dr. Becky Kennedy]]'s core explanation for bad behavior at any age: "kids are born with all the feelings and none of the skills to manage feelings" — behavior is a manifestation of a gap between the feeling someone is having and the skill they have available to manage it, not a moral failing or a deliberate choice.

## Key Information

- Kennedy: "bad behavior at any age can basically be reduced to feelings that overpower skills... behavior is a problem, but behavior isn't the core problem. It's a manifestation of the problem." The actual problem is the missing skill to manage what's happening internally; if the person had and could access that skill, the behavior would change as a downstream effect.
- Analogy: nobody would punish a child into learning to swim by sending them to their room until they "learn how to swim" — it's obviously absurd, yet the same logic (punishing/shaming someone for lacking a skill) is routinely applied to emotional-regulation skills, which she argues are an even more important life skill.
- Punishment/shame don't teach the missing skill and actively widen the feelings-skills gap; the fix is to (a) set a boundary around the out-of-control behavior itself (see [[Boundaries Vs Requests]]) and (b) separately, explicitly teach the missing skill — which changes behavior both short- and long-term, unlike punishment alone.
- Contrasted with the reward/punishment/sticker-chart model Kennedy was originally trained in, which she says worked cleanly on paper (behaviorist logic her "very logical left brain" liked) but didn't address "how are we raising a human," and was in direct tension with what she saw actually help adults change in her private practice.
- This lens reframes several behaviors as attempts to communicate an unmet need rather than defiance: a kid hitting because they lack skills to manage anger/jealousy about a sibling's toy; a kid "not listening" when they lack the skill to transition from a fun activity to a boring one.
- Extended to workplace "difficult adults": Kennedy frames the episode's opening "adults are babies in disguise" framing (credited to [[Ryas Doshi]]) through this lens — not that adults are literally childish, but that unmet needs and missing regulation skills produce the same category of ineffective behavior at any age.
- Underlies [[Behavior Vs Identity]] (don't confuse the manifestation with the person) and is the diagnostic starting point for [[Most Generous Interpretation]] (asking what skill gap or unmet need could be producing this behavior).
