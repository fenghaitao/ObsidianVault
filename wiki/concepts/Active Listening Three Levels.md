## Definition

[[Rachel Lockett]]'s three-level model of listening, used to explain what distinguishes genuinely effective leader coaching from ordinary conversation.

## Key Information

- **Level 1 — internal**: you're nominally listening but actually absorbed in your own inner dialogue (e.g., thinking about how the other person's problem affects you). Lockett says most people move through their day rushed and stuck at this level — echoing the *Fight Club* line "most people aren't listening, they're just waiting for their turn to talk."
- **Level 2 — focused**: you can accurately repeat back what the other person said; you're tracking their literal words. Typical of a decent one-on-one where you're jointly problem-solving.
- **Level 3 — global**: you hear beneath the words — tone of voice, body language, the surrounding context — and can reflect back more insight about what's happening than the speaker was consciously aware of. Great leaders operate here when coaching, influencing, selling, or pitching a vision.
- Demonstrated live on [[Lenny Rachitsky]]: Lockett asked him what it's like to be a father, then reflected back not just his words but his body language (initial discomfort/squirming) and the mixed emotions underneath (deep joy alongside genuine exhaustion) — which Rachitsky confirmed felt more meaningful than simple word-repetition.
- Practical framing for managers: this doesn't require scheduling long dedicated coaching sessions — even brief moments of full, undivided attention within existing conversations (rather than a formulaic "mirror the feelings" trick) build trust and surface what's actually going on for a team member.
