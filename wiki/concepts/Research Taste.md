## Definition
Research taste is the intuition for which path to take through the stochastic dependency graph of a research project — the ability to a priori estimate how likely an approach is to work and what it will cost.
## Key Information
- Vlad uses the DAG analogy: software engineering projects have a more-or-less deterministic DAG with monotone progress, while research DAGs are stochastic (some nodes may not pan out) and can even be a hidden MDP.
- In a hidden MDP you must factor in success rate and time investment for each idea, and estimate those rates before doing the work — which is what people colloquially call research taste.
- It is a mindset shift from deterministic planning and is typically built up through training like a PhD.
- Research taste is distinct from classical SWE "write out all paths then take the shortest"; that strategy fails when edges become unreliable.
