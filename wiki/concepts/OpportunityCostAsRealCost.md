## Definition
Opportunity cost as the real cost is the economic insight that when AI makes software production cheap, the dominant cost shifts from implementation effort to the opportunity cost of choosing what to build. You can't build everything, and whatever you pick comes at the cost of everything else you could have built instead.

## Key Information
- Articulated by Maggie Appleton of GitHub Next
- When production is cheap, opportunity cost becomes the real cost
- You can't build everything — whatever you pick comes at the cost of everything else
- This makes alignment (agreeing on what to build) the critical bottleneck
- The hard question is no longer "how to build it" but "should we build it?"
- Agreeing on what to build requires the whole team: "Are we making the right thing? Are we spending our energy in the right place? How do we have the most impact?"
- This is why single-player agent tools are insufficient — they optimize for individual output without addressing the team-level decision of what's worth building
- Related to the idea that agents allow teams to reclaim implementation time and redirect it toward rigorous critical thinking, exploration, research, and deeper problem-solving
- Doing fewer things better requires strong alignment on what those fewer things should be
