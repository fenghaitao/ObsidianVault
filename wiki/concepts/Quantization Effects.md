## Definition

Quantization Effects describe the phenomenon where small companies cannot hire fractional specialists for every role, making generalists more valuable. When a company is large enough, each function warrants full-time specialists, reducing the generalist's relative value.

## Key Information

- Philip Su's explanation: "If your company is large enough, you can hire a PR comms person whose full-time job 40 hours a week is to handle comms. But before you're that large, it's very difficult to hire a part-time three-hour-a-week comms person. So you tap some product manager on your team that's pretty good with communication and you ask them to handle it for a while."
- Generalists are "more purposeful in cases of fractionalization" — you can fractionalize a generalist across many different roles
- Eventually a company gets so large that each fraction of work warrants one or more full-time employees, at which point having the generalist is "a lot of wasted energy because only 15% of their skill set pertains to the actual work you're doing"
- This is why generalists are more valuable at small companies/startups and specialists at large companies
