## Definition

Jenny Wen identifies three designer archetypes that are especially valuable in the AI era: (1) the strong generalist — block-shaped, 80th percentile good at multiple core skills; (2) the deep specialist — T-shaped with exceptional depth in one area, top 10% in the industry; (3) the craft new grad — early career, wise beyond their years, humble, eager to learn, with a blank slate unburdened by old processes.

## Key Information

- **Strong generalist (block-shaped)**: Not just "kind of good at a lot of things" — 80th percentile good at multiple core skills. Rare and hard to hire for. Valuable because the design role is stretching and spanning (becoming more PM-shaped and engineering-shaped). If you already have strong skills in multiple buckets, it's easy to flex and expand your role.
- **Deep specialist (T-shaped with deep stem)**: Top ~10% in the industry in one area. Examples: a designer who is 50% software engineer, or someone exceptionally good at visual design or icon design. In a world where "anybody can make anything," deep specialization helps differentiate.
- **Craft new grad**: Early career, "wise and experienced beyond their years," humble, very eager to learn. Blank slate — doesn't have baked-in processes and rituals. Most companies are overlooking this archetype by only hiring senior talent. Given how much roles are changing, a quick learner without old habits is super valuable.
