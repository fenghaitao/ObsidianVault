## Definition

A tactical rule: always negotiate compensation with the person who controls the P&L and has the most skin in the game, not through intermediaries like recruiters. Negotiating through recruiters is playing a game of telephone — your carefully crafted message gets reduced to the worst possible interpretation.

## Key Information

- Jacob: "You have to go to the one who has skin in the game, who controls the P&L, who controls EBITDA. What's their motivation? That's who you want to talk to."
- The problem with recruiters: "I explain it to the recruiter. I get the tone right. The recruiter goes to the CEO and says, 'That bastard wants more money.' And that's all they communicate."
- Recruiters are intermediaries — they don't have the authority to move comp, and they distort your message
- When you must go through the recruiter, do it respectfully — but find a way to have the actual conversation with the hiring manager
- Jacob's recommended approach: text or email the hiring manager directly: "Hey, we're almost across the line. It sounds like we had a sticking point. Are you open to have a conversation?"
- "I don't need to have a big laundry list of I'm going to do for you. Like that will not work."
- Keep communication short: "Open to chat" can be enough
