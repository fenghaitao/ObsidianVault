## Definition

Leveling Rubric is the framework used to place a candidate (and to interpret levels) in big-tech hiring. Austin McDonald describes levels as primarily a measure of how much ambiguity someone can handle, cascading from tasks up to industry-wide responsibility.

## Key Information

- Levels cascade by ambiguity: a new grad does tasks; a mid-level engineer does features (many tasks); a senior engineer does projects (many features, often through others); a staff engineer owns goals (multiple projects); principal owns an organization (many goals); distinguished owns an industry or business.
- Meta's staff loop concretely requires two system-design interviews, so staff candidates are expected to demonstrate breadth of impact and organizational depth across an entire team or multiple teams.
- Initial leveling at the recruiter stage is set mostly by years of experience and previous title; because Meta levels aren't public, recruiters rely heavily on years of experience.
- Communicating level in a behavioral interview means quantifying technical depth, organizational depth, and business value: time invested, risk, number of people consulted, and how hard it was to back out of a decision.
- Specialists get leveled by business context: pinning the insight that made predecessors' efforts unsuccessful (not just "I'm better") is a key signal of above-staff judgment.
- Different companies encode this differently (e.g., Meta's five signal areas); Austin's broader set of eight "signal areas" is a superset covering ownership, ambiguity, conflict resolution, and growth.
