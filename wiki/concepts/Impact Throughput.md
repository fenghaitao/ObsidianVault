## Definition

Impact Throughput is a framework for prioritization where you optimize for the ratio of impact to effort. The best projects are those with the highest impact achieved through the simplest means. This is closely tied to Meta's culture of rewarding impact over difficulty.

## Key Information

- Ryan described the framework as a fraction: "the numerator is how much impact is there, but the denominator is how much effort and time and complexity is this thing going to be"
- A one-line change with massive impact is preferred over a "crazy year-long project that's super complex and adds all this maintenance"
- Meta's culture "excelled" at rewarding impact over difficulty — "if you're the guy who made the one-line change and it had huge impact, you're going to be rewarded proportional to the difficulty... which is zero"
- This creates a culture of "go find the impact, it doesn't have to be the hard crazy thing, it doesn't have to be a lot of work"
- Ryan noted that at other companies, you might be "rewarded proportional to the difficulty," but at Meta, impact is what matters
- The concept generalizes across all career levels: "as an engineer, focusing on actually what matters and what's impactful is the absolute number one thing that's going to grow your career"
