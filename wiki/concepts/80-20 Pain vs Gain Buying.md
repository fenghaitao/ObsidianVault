## Definition

The statistic that approximately 80% of customers buy to avoid pain or reduce risk, while only 20% buy to increase upside. This has profound implications for how startups should position and sell their products, especially when selling to enterprises.

## Key Information

- Jeanne DeWitt Grosser shared this stat (acknowledging it's "round numbers" so not perfectly precise) as a key insight for startup founders.
- Startups tend to sell the "art of the possible" — the visionary future — which resonates with other founders but not with most buyers.
- Enterprise buyers are primarily motivated by: avoiding risk of missing revenue targets, avoiding being outdone by competitors, avoiding brand damage, avoiding career risk from making a bad purchasing decision.
- The "visionary" sale (what you'll enable in the future) is often a sale that resonates with another founder, not with the broader market.
- Related insight from April Dunford (prior podcast guest): adopting a major product like Stripe is a "massive career bet" — if it goes wrong, your career is hurt, your manager is mad, the company is set back.
- Jenna Ael (prior podcast guest): instead of focusing on "here's the pain and problem we're solving," focus on "here's how you will be better than your competitors" — the competitive gap/alpha.
- Setting up "concern that I might not be well positioned" often drives more buying behavior than painting a vision of future upside.
- Many startups find this pivot hard because it "feels off-brand," but it drives more buying behavior.
