## Definition

When team members feel comfortable roasting each other (and their manager), it's a strong signal of high psychological safety. Jenny Wen doesn't force roasting culture but sees it as a natural outcome of teams that genuinely get along. The key is balancing this comfort with high standards — like a "tough parent" who supports but challenges.

## Key Information

- Jenny doesn't force roasting — she sees it as a sign that a team has high psychological safety
- "When you think about your friends, you're always willing to push the boundaries a little bit and roast them" — same dynamic should exist in healthy teams
- Jenny's team would make fun of phrases she said at crits (e.g., "Okay, what are next steps?")
- Shows team members aren't afraid of the manager and trust they won't get fired for saying something
- Must be balanced with high standards — the "tough parent" model: "I'm always going to be there, I'm not going to fire you on a whim, but I want the best for you and have high standards"
- Connects to radical candor: caring deeply and challenging directly
- The tension between psychological safety and high standards actually works well together — once you have safety, applying high standards becomes easier
