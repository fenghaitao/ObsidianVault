## Definition

Boot Camp is the onboarding program at Meta (Facebook) for new engineers, lasting several weeks. New hires go through training, complete small tasks, and interview with different teams before selecting which team to join permanently. It is designed to be a low-pressure, educational experience that instills company culture.

## Key Information

- Zach described Facebook Boot Camp as feeling "like an extension of college" — you attend classes, learn, and are not held accountable for any deadlines
- The experience is designed to instill Facebook's core values: "move fast and break things," "this is your company," "be bold"
- Zach said his first month at Facebook "didn't even feel like I was working a job — it felt like I was in Disneyland"
- During Boot Camp, Zach interviewed with three teams: Ads, Growth, and Community Support
- He chose Growth because they told him "you'll have a lot of fun," while Ads promised impact and Community Support promised purpose
- The team selection process during Boot Camp is critical because it determines your initial manager and project scope
- Evan King went through Boot Camp at Meta's Seattle office with two-week stints in Menlo Park, and the rest out of Seattle
- Evan initially explored cyber security teams during Boot Camp (aligned with his hacking club background) but found the culture "less welcoming" and "more stoic"
- He ultimately chose the Content Integrity org's terrorism team, which was a brand new team being moved from the London office to Seattle — he was the first engineer on the team
- The terrorism team had no full-time manager yet, just one manager splitting time across teams, and one engineer half-tech-leading it
- Ryan Peterman also went through Boot Camp and chose an Instagram infrastructure team, but the manager had no headcount — Ryan had to beg and convince him to find headcount, demonstrating that the best opportunities are ones you have to fight for
- Meta has since replaced Boot Camp with Team Match, which Evan considers "a shame" because Boot Camp gave new engineers more control and the ability to try different teams
- Boot Camp allowed engineers to "try a bunch of things, determine which one you preferred, and then join that team" — it felt like the engineer was in control

### Pinterest's Base Camp
- Pinterest had a program called "Base Camp" — a play on Facebook's Boot Camp
- It was a bridged version: 2-3 weeks where new hires learned about different teams at Pinterest and decided where to work
- Rahul went through Base Camp and used it to deliberately choose Android mobile development, pivoting from his recommendation systems background
- This program allowed him to talk to multiple teams and make an informed decision about where he could best grow
