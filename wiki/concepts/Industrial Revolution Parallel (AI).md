# Industrial Revolution Parallel (AI)

The analogy, used by [[Qasar Younis]], that AI will bring net positive change to humanity just as the Industrial Revolution did — despite real downsides that must be mitigated.

## Core Idea

"Net suffering in humanity overall should go down significantly. I'm a fundamental optimist in that view that technology will bring that positivity."

## Key Points
- If you were sitting in the late 1800s, you'd see child labor, monopolies, wars — but also unprecedented access to healthcare, goods, heating/cooling
- 80% of German towns in WWII didn't have electricity — we assume technology is equally distributed, but it's not
- AI will bring abundance to many more people at near-free cost: coaching, mobility, healthcare, basic services
- Self-driving cars as an example: free mobility transforms lives in developing countries (Rwanda example — 2 hours from nearest hospital)
- 30,000+ people die annually in US car accidents — in 20-30 years, we'll look back at human driving like we now look at child labor
- "There's a lot of literature [from the Industrial Revolution]... you can read both the people who are impacted and people who are benefiting. By and large, it's a very positive experience"
- Putting brakes on frontier technology hurts the most marginalized people the most — the American economy vs. Europe example
- The solution isn't "pump the brakes on AI" — it's actively steering technology toward good
