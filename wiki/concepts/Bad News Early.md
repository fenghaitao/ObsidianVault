## Definition

"Bad News Early" is the principle that surfacing problems proactively is always better than hiding them. As articulated by Rahul Pandey and quoted by Steve Huynh: "Bad news that's delivered early is just news; bad news that's delivered late is terrible news."

## Key Information

- Quoted by Steve Huynh, attributed to Rahul Pandey of Taro (who may have gotten it from someone at Meta)
- If you ask about expectations early in your tenure or early in the cycle, and there's a problem, you have a fighting chance to address it
- If you wait until the month before performance review, you're in a bad spot
- Applies to performance: explicitly asking "Am I meeting expectations?" early gives you time to course-correct
- Applies to project management: hiding that you're blocked because you think you should know everything is a performance issue, not just a delay
- Example: if a task should take two weeks and you report "on track" until the Thursday before, that's a serious problem
- Only ~25% of engineers have explicitly asked their manager about expectations
