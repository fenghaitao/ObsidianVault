## Definition

Conceptual integrity is the design quality in which every part of a system fits together as if designed with every other part in mind.

## Key Information

- Roberto Ierusalimschy credits this term for "everything fits together, everything is designed with everything else in mind."
- It is much easier to achieve with a small group where everyone knows what everyone else is thinking than with a committee.
- On committees: everyone fights to put their own favorite mechanism into a language, and far fewer people fight to keep things out, so committees keep adding features — the old joke is "a camel is a horse designed by a committee."
- His default rule is "when in doubt, don't add it": it is easier to add a feature later than to remove one you regret, so keep the door open.
- Lua benefited from the privilege of not having to satisfy clients under deadline pressure; they could wait a year before committing to a feature.
- Boundaries on simplicity exist: "the simplest possible, but not simpler" — lambda calculus and Turing machines are simple yet unusable for real programs.
