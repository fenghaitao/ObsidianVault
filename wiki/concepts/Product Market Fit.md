## Definition

Product Market Fit (PMF) is the degree to which a product satisfies strong market demand. In the context of the Cos acquisition, Pinterest's pitch was that while Cos had interesting IP and academic ideas but no clear pathway to revenue, combining it with Pinterest's existing PMF could create a "1+1=3" outcome.

## Key Information

### The Cos Acquisition Pitch
- Cos had interesting IP and academic ideas but no clear pathway to generating revenue or building a product
- Pinterest already had PMF with 100 million users
- The acquisition thesis: pairing Cos's technology with Pinterest's PMF could create something where "1+1=3"
- This was an "opportunistic bet" rather than a traditional acquisition

### PMF and Career Decisions
- When choosing a team to join, Rahul advises considering how important that team is to the company
- At Pinterest, Android was strategically important because international growth was a major initiative, and Android is dominant outside the US
- Being "adjacent to or operating in an area of high growth" means you'll "inevitably see some of that growth too"
- A rising tide lifts all boats — teams working on the company's most important initiatives get more resources, smart people, and money

### PMF in the Startup Context
- Cos lacked PMF — it was spun out of academic research without a clear product or revenue model
- This is why the acquisition was closer to a failure: the company never validated whether there was real market demand
- The lesson: academic IP without PMF is a bet, not a business
