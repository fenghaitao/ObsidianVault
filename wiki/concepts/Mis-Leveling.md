## Definition

Mis-Leveling occurs when a company hires an engineer at a level below their actual capability, typically due to the interview process not capturing the full scope of their experience. Companies that recognize mis-leveling will often promote the engineer quickly to correct the error.

## Key Information

- Zach was hired at Facebook as an IC3 (entry level) despite having two years of experience building big data pipelines at startups
- He noticed that Stanford new grads with zero years of experience were hired at the same level and same pay, which he felt was unfair
- This recognition drove Zach to "bust his ass" to get promoted, which he achieved in only 5 months (half of one performance cycle)
- Zach's rapid IC3-to-IC4 promotion happened because Facebook recognized he was mis-leveled at hire and didn't make him go through all the standard promotion steps
- Mis-leveling is treated as an opportunity for companies to correct: if you join and immediately perform at a higher level, they will promote you quickly because they view it as a hiring miss
- Zach contrasts this with his experience in Utah where promotions were based on tenure ("been here three years, okay you're a senior engineer now"), not on impact
- The concept demonstrates why interviewing is such high leverage — getting hired at the right level from the start avoids the catch-up period

- Ryan Olson felt he was hired underleveled at IC4 at Facebook/Instagram and told his director he should be promoted IC4 → IC6 in one cycle; he seeded the idea so later promotions wouldn't be deferred for "he just got promoted last half"
