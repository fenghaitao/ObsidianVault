## Definition

Leadership in the engineering context refers to the ability to guide teams, set direction, influence without authority, and grow people around you. It is a set of behaviors that is distinct from management and can be practiced at any IC level.

## Key Information

- Evan King demonstrated leadership early by founding the Cornell hacking club: setting up flyers, recruiting founding officers, scaling to 25 core members and 200 lecture attendees
- Ryan noted that the behaviors required to start a club (alignment, communication, marketing, being a leader) are "a lot of what more senior engineers need to do"
- Evan's manager gave him a leadership opportunity by making him tech lead of the graphic violence team as a junior engineer — "give them more than they can chew, make them feel empowered, and then support them"
- Evan read "The Making of a Manager" when he became tech lead, treating leadership as a skill to be learned
- His leadership was characterized by being "on everyone's team and on everyone's side" — he was genuinely invested in his teammates' growth
- Leadership at higher IC levels requires influence without authority, especially at Meta where levels are not public
- The transition from IC to management (TLM) is a different form of leadership that involves direct people responsibility, performance reviews, and dealing with "people problems"
