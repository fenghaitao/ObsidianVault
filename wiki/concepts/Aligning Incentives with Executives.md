## Definition

The practice of understanding what an executive is measured on — their goals, OKRs, board pressures, and definition of success — and connecting your pitch to that success. Instead of asking "What's top of mind?" (which has become a trope), ask spicier, more specific questions about what's driving them.

## Key Information

- "What are their goals? What are they trying to do? How are they measured? Connect the thing you're pitching them with that success."
- Instead of "What's top of mind?" ask: "What is the board pushing you on?" "What are you seeing as the key inputs to your success?" "What's the most urgent priority you're scared about messing up?"
- "Execs want to be successful, too. They want to be good at their jobs."
- In the best case, your team-level incentives align with the exec's incentives. If not, you're in misalignment from the start.
- This isn't just for pitches — it should inform your regular roadmap. "How did the OKRs get to be the way they are? How did the positioning statement get to be that way?"
- Make sure your metrics and proposals ladder up clearly to what the executive is measured on, and that the executive agrees to that connection.
- "What if you know something that they don't?" — use curiosity questions to extract their worldview and then bring your domain expertise to bear.
