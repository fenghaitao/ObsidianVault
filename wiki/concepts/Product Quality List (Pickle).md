## Definition

The Product Quality List (PQL), affectionately called the "pickle" at Rippling, is a lightweight, continuously iterated checklist of standards that every product must meet before shipping. It is a process designed to lower beta (volatility) with minimal impact on alpha (creativity).

## Key Information

- PQL = Product Quality List, pronounced "pickle" (consonant-based acronym)
- Purpose: lower beta in the product development system with minimal suppression of alpha
- Deliberately named "pickle" as an idiosyncratic, memorable term to create a vessel for cultural meaning
- Not every line item applies to every product, but the checklist is comprehensive and iterated continuously
- Example: after a feature flag caused a blank screen for Parker Conrad, a line item was added: "You are allowed to have one feature flag that governs your entire product at ship"
- Part of the "factory inspection" process: at the end of the assembly line, McGinness reviews every product against the pickle
- Every major flow through a product must be recorded via Loom; McGinness personally reviews all of them
- Feedback is always given in public channels so all PMs and EMs can learn from each other's reviews
- The name "pickle" has spawned a culture: pickle emojis, dancing pickle in Slack, etc.
