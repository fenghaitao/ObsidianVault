## Definition

Deferred Impact is the idea that work can keep paying off — in recognition, credit, and promotions — long after it was originally shipped. David Fowler observed it most clearly at the highest levels of Microsoft's ladder.

## Key Information

- Fowler says the bigger promotions felt like "a conglomeration of history: past success adding up plus success"
- Microsoft officially measures impact for the year, but band-crossing and top-level promotions draw on a longer body of work
- His promotion from principal to partner explicitly cited .NET Core's impact — about seven years after the fact
- At the highest levels you "can't just become a DE because you had one fun year": NuGet, SignalR, and .NET Core all accumulated into the case
- This is the flip side of job hopping early: sustained, compounding impact is what later-level decisions are built on
