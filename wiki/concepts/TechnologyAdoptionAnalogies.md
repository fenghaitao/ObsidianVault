## Definition
Technology Adoption Analogies are historical parallels drawn to explain patterns in how transformative technologies are initially adopted — often poorly, by grafting them onto existing paradigms — before a genuine redesign unlocks their full potential. The primary analogy is the 1882 electricity revolution, where factory owners simply swapped steam engines for electric motors without reimagining factory layouts.

## Key Information
- **Electricity (1882)**: The world's first commercial power plant opened. Factory owners replaced steam engines with electric motors but kept inefficient layouts dictated by steam-era transmission lines. The real revolution came only when factories were redesigned from the ground up around electricity's unique capabilities (distributed power, specialized tools, flexible layouts)
- **Mobile-first (2010s)**: Companies initially just shrunk desktop websites onto phones. True innovation (Snapchat, Uber) emerged only when apps were redesigned around mobile's unique capabilities: always-on, camera, GPS
- **AI / "Magic star icon phase" (2020s)**: Companies are adding AI star icon buttons to existing products rather than redesigning products with LLMs at their core. The "Snapchats and Ubers of AI" have yet to emerge
- **Common pattern**: New technology is initially grafted onto existing paradigms by replacing the old tool without changing the system. Breakthrough value comes from redesigning the system around the new technology's unique affordances
- **Implication for AI builders**: Don't just replace existing features with AI equivalents — reimagine the product, workflow, or experience from an AI-first perspective
