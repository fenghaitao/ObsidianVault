## Definition
AI as translation is the framing that AI's fundamental capability is translating between representation spaces — converting data from one form to another through common internal representations. This extends from the original "Attention Is All You Need" paper (English to French translation) to modern capabilities: text to images, images to audio, audio to video, and beyond.

## Key Information
- Introduced by Angus J. McLean as a unifying lens for understanding AI capabilities.
- Rooted in the Transformer architecture's origin: "Attention Is All You Need" was initially demonstrated on English-to-French translation.
- Modern extensions: text→images, images→audio, audio→video, structured→unstructured, unstructured→structured.
- Knowledge production itself can be seen as summarization — compacting experience into transferable knowledge.
- Different types of data can be converted into common internal representations and then transformed into something else.
- A key implication: structure is not an inherent property of data, but a property of the representation chosen by the observer.
- If you can manipulate something through a representation space, the structure of that data is not fixed — it's a choice.
- Practical example: the same content can exist as a slide, a diagram, written text, or a voiceover — each is a translation into a different representation.
- This framing connects to MCP (Model Context Protocol) and model handoffs for long-running agents: structured data on one side, unstructured on the other, coexisting.
- The concept implies that agent design should consider multiple representation structures and the ability to translate between them.
