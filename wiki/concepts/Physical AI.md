## Definition

[[Caitlin Kalinowski]]'s central thesis: once AI's ability to do purely digital/knowledge work saturates, the next AI boom is in the physical world — robotics, manufacturing, industrialization, and eventually space. Independently echoed by [[Qasar Younis]], who argues the real AI impact over the next 5-10 years is in farming, mining, construction, and trucking.

## Key Information

- Humanoid robots ([[Humanoid Robots]]) are the most visible manifestation, but Kalinowski frames physical AI more broadly as encompassing supply-chain re-industrialization (magnets, [[Actuators]], silicon, RAM) and defense/drone technology, not just consumer robots.
- Gated mainly by supply chain and manufacturing scale-up, not by AI model capability — a recurring theme distinguishing physical AI's bottlenecks from purely digital AI's.
- Requires AI models that understand physical properties (friction, weight, contact) for real engineering work — current LLMs/video models can't yet do real [[CAD]]; companies like [[World Labs]] (world models) are cited as a candidate but unproven direction.
- Connects to her advocacy for U.S. re-industrialization and independent supply chains for economic and military-security reasons, and to [[Palmer Luckey]]'s parallel shift from consumer VR into defense hardware.
- Per [[Qasar Younis]] (episode 22): argues the earliest, highest-impact physical AI use cases will be ones with the most "bang for buck" — adding intelligence to giant machines whose core engineering (tractors, mining rigs, trucks) has already been solved over decades, rather than building humanoid form factors from scratch, even though humans are emotionally drawn to the humanoid concept "because we're monkeys."
- Younis frames the underlying driver as demographic/labor necessity rather than novelty: farmers average 58 years old, and industries like long-haul trucking and mining struggle to attract workers at all — autonomy is "coming just in time" rather than displacing wanted jobs. His company, [[Applied Intuition]], supplies the autonomy software layer across these verticals.
- Younis's barometer for how early-stage this remains societally: at an airport gate, the number of people actively using an AI coding agent is close to zero, versus the near-universal physical-world impact he expects from farming/mining/construction/trucking autonomy.
