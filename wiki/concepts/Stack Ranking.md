## Definition

Stack Ranking is a performance management approach where employees are ranked against each other and a fixed percentage are managed out each year. At Amazon, this has historically targeted ~5-6% of the organization, shifting from a soft recommendation to a harder mandate.

## Key Information

- Amazon historically targeted ~5-6% managed out per year (~1 in 20 people)
- The guidance has shifted from a soft recommendation to a harder mandate in recent years
- Without backfills, repeated annual cuts start cannibalizing solid performers — the 6% in year 2 is from the remaining 94%, and so on
- Talent review meetings are multi-day locked-room sessions where people are discussed in terms of measurables and feedback
- The process is not a linear algorithm — conversations focus on the edges of the distribution due to the sheer number of people
- Steve's perspective: people on the left side of the distribution who aren't thriving in Amazon's culture may actually benefit from leaving
- For people near the median, the repeated cuts become concerning — they'd be fine in any other year
- Middle management is particularly vulnerable since 6% of 4-8 managers doesn't make statistical sense
- The "low performer" label is problematic — Steve argues it's more about culture mismatch than actual performance
