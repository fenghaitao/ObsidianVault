## Definition

Tony Fadell's reframing of "micromanaging" as a necessary, targeted leadership practice — not controlling all operations, but tightly directing the small set of decisions and details that truly determine product quality.

## Key Information

- Fadell distinguishes micromanaging *decisions* from micromanaging *operations*: "when I mean micromanaging, it means micromanage the decision, not necessarily operations of doing it."
- Early in his career he micromanaged everything ("I drove everybody nuts... it became everybody's got to do it the way I would do it") before learning to identify only the few things that really matter — usually for the customer, or for manufacturing/cost, or long-term vision — and delegate the rest.
- Framed as "sweat the details": some details need hands-on, orchestrator-level attention (e.g., coordinating hardware, software, and filtering layers simultaneously for the iPhone keyboard) because too many interdependent variables exist for anyone else to hold the whole picture; leaders sometimes have to "ask why a lot" to cut through excuses.
- Connected to getting the right *data* to support an opinion-based ("informed gut") decision — Fadell frames rigorous data-gathering (like the iPhone keyboard typing-speed/error-rate tests) as itself a form of good micromanagement in service of a bigger judgment call.
- Also invoked for crisis response or systemic issues that span multiple teams/layers, where someone has to act as "orchestrator" to align many moving parts at once.
