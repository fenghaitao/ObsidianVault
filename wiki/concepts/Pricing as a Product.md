## Definition

The approach of treating pricing strategy with the same rigor and iterative mindset as product development — understanding where customers drive value, where costs are incurred, and aligning the pricing model accordingly rather than defaulting to industry norms.

## Key Information

- "You got to think about pricing like a product." — Jeanne DeWitt Grosser.
- Key questions: Do you really understand where customers are going to drive value? Do you understand where you incur costs? Are you aligning those things?
- Common mistakes:
  - Grossly underpricing because of fear of charging for value provided.
  - Defaulting to a premium strategy without it being an actual strategy.
- Stripe Billing example: launched with a premium pricing strategy (because "that's what you do"), then killed the free trial with zero downside — integration work created natural retention.
- Vercel example: Consumption-based model initially bundled into SaaS-like pricing. As functionality grew, this stopped working. Unbundled in August into enterprise and pro SKUs, making enterprise features available self-serve for startups, driving PLG growth.
- Getting pricing right is "a key to both a great customer experience and optimal revenue outcomes."
- **Jason Cohen's perspective:** Pricing is not just the number on the page. Structure and positioning are equally important. The Double Down example shows the same product can command 8x the price by repositioning from "cut costs in half" to "double your leads" — selling what the CEO values (growth) rather than what saves money.
- Patrick Campbell's quote: "Your prices are way too low because you just guessed and you haven't changed them."
- Jen Abel's insight: enterprise sweet spot for SaaS contracts is $75K–$150K.
