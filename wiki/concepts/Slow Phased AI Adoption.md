## Definition
Slow Phased AI Adoption is a change management strategy for introducing AI coding agents into an engineering organization. It advocates starting with the best engineers, experimenting in non-critical systems, and scaling gradually — in contrast to giving everyone AI tools at once in a hackathon-style rollout.

## Key Information
- **Origin**: Presented by [[MikeSpitz]] based on the [[PFF]] case study
- **Phase 1 — Personal Experimentation**: One person experiments with AI agents on proof-of-concept features in non-critical systems (2 months at PFF, November–December 2025)
- **Phase 2 — Expand to Top Engineers**: Select the engineers with the best system knowledge — the ones everyone already goes to for help — and give them AI tools
- **Phase 3 — Non-Critical Systems**: Build features where bugs don't matter much; validate the approach
- **Phase 4 — Critical Systems**: Only after proving the model on non-critical work, move to high-traffic systems (PFF moved to their 100M page view system)
- **Phase 5 — Gradual Org-Wide Scaling**: Rely on engineers to signal whether to accelerate or slow down; don't onboard everyone at once
- **Key Principle**: "Not everyone can drive a sports car" — some engineers will struggle with agent-driven development, and that's okay; be honest about team capabilities
- **Anti-Pattern**: Giving everyone a coding assistant in a hackathon and declaring adoption complete
- **Small Company Advantage**: Easier to phase in with 20 engineers than 1,000 or 10,000
- **Urgency**: Don't be too conservative — competitors are moving at full speed, and the compounding effect means delays multiply
