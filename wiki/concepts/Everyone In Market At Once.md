## Definition

[[Jason Lemkin]]'s description of 2026's go-to-market demand condition: whereas historically only about 3-5% of prospects in a given category were actively "in market" to buy in any given year, AI-category adoption has pushed that figure above 50% in many categories — a phenomenon he says the market hasn't seen since 2020 and attributes to top-down organizational pressure ("we need to adopt AI, we need to be more productive").

## Key Information

- Historical baseline: "the traditional metric was in most categories, 3 to 5% of prospects would be in market a year" — hence classic high-volume, low-conversion outbound (cold emails/calls hoping a tiny fraction convert).
- Current state: "in many categories, we're north of 50% in market" — not just a few law firms evaluating [[Harvey]], but nearly every law firm; not a few companies exploring AI video, but nearly every company.
- Root cause per Lemkin: not organic bottom-up interest alone, but pervasive executive-level pressure to visibly adopt AI and demonstrate productivity gains — pushing "everyone" into the market simultaneously rather than the usual staggered adoption curve.
- Consequence: this creates a temporary bifurcated GTM environment — hyper-growth companies (see [[Plays Vs Playbooks]]) can't service all the resulting inbound demand, while legacy SaaS companies push AI GTM automation to compete for efficiency/margin instead.
- Lemkin flags a coming headwind: enterprises are getting "exhausted" from adopting so many new agentic tools in a single year ("I had to do five last year, I just cannot bring one more app into my enterprise"), meaning vendors have a closing window (roughly the next 12 months, per Lemkin) to land deals before adoption fatigue sets in.
- Lemkin frames this surge explicitly as a bubble-adjacent phenomenon that will partially revert — "not everybody can be in market every year, it exhausts an enterprise" — while still expecting overall demand and hiring in AI-adjacent GTM roles to keep growing for the next 3-4 years.
