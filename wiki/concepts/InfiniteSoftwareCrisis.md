## Definition
The current era of the recurring software crisis, characterized by AI code generation that produces code faster than humans can understand it. Unlike previous crises where complexity accumulated slowly enough to manage, AI makes the "easy" path frictionless, leading to infinite-scale accumulation of complexity.

## Key Information
- Coined/used by Jake Nations to describe the AI-era software crisis
- Key mechanism: AI generates thousands of lines of code in seconds, but understanding it can take hours, days, or never
- AI treats all code patterns equally, preserving technical debt and accidental complexity as if they were essential
- The conversational AI interface (e.g., "add OAuth," "fix this error") leads to dead code from abandoned approaches, fragments of multiple solutions, and overwritten architectural patterns
- Each interaction chooses easy over simple, and complexity compounds until it is too late
- A dangerous side effect: the instinct to recognize growing complexity atrophies when developers don't understand their own systems
- The solution is not better prompts or models, but maintaining human understanding through structured processes like the three-phase approach
- The core question: "Will we still understand our own systems when AI is writing most of our code?"
