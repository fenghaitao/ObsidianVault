## Definition

[[Stewart Butterfield]]'s application of Parkinson's Law (from a 1955 essay in *The Economist*: "work expands to fill the time available for its completion") to organizational headcount and scope: teams and org charts grow to match available time/resources and career incentives, largely independent of actual workload.

## Key Information

- Original essay's core example (Parkinson, writing about the mid-20th-century British Royal Navy): a chart showing the number of capital ships declining over time while the number of administrators kept rising in lockstep — a pattern Butterfield says shows up identically today in the ratio of university administrators to students/faculty, or corporate headcount to actual output.
- Why it happens, per Butterfield: not because people are evil or stupid, but because it's rational self-interest — headcount, scope, and number of direct reports are tightly tied to career trajectory, compensation, and internal status in most organizations, so nearly everyone who manages anything wants to hire more people under them.
- Illustrative example from Slack: 27-year-old product managers who, immediately upon joining, wanted to hire a report to do the actual product management work "so I can do strategy."
- Butterfield's corollary ("Butterfield's Law," his own tentative coinage): "everything is simple if you have no idea what you're talking about" — for anything involving a large organization or many humans, if a problem looks simple, you probably don't understand it; no CFO or head of engineering ever proposes shrinking headcount through attrition, because the org-growth incentive is close to overpowering.
- Leadership implication: since this pressure is structural, not a matter of individual bad faith, it has to be actively and continuously managed by leadership (clarity on priorities, willingness to say no to new hiring asks) rather than treated as an occasional problem to fix. See [[Hyperrealistic Work-Like Activities]] for the downstream effect of headcount outpacing genuinely valuable work.
