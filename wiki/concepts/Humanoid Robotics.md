## Definition
Humanoid robotics is the field of building and deploying general-purpose robots with human-like form factors.
## Key Information
- Sergey Levine estimates meaningful deployment is in "single-digit years rather than double-digit years": structured tasks (near factories, trained humans) sooner — "now or next year" — and unstructured home robots "a few more years out."
- Structured vs. unstructured is a tradeoff: structured domains can start earlier but with a smaller improvement slope; unstructured domains (homes) start later with more diversity and a bigger slope but a higher safety bar.
- Named competitors include Figure and Tesla, with many others; impressive Chinese labs are visible on social media.
- The hard problem is generalization, not the physical hardware or any single impressive demo; acrobatic demos are often rehearsed and do not indicate reliable at-home performance.
- The test for claims is whether the robot does mundane tasks reliably with unseen objects in unseen environments.
- A concrete milestone is a robot that improves with autonomous experience in a novel setting and reaches practically relevant robustness without capping out (e.g., at 50%).
