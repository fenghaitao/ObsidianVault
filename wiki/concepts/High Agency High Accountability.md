## Definition

High agency, high accountability is a paired team value described by [[Fiona Fung]] as core to how [[Anthropic]]'s [[Claude Code]] and [[Claude Cowork]] teams operate: everyone is expected to take initiative on problems (high agency), balanced by clear ownership of the outcome and the underlying hypothesis being tested (high accountability).

## Key Information

- Framed as "two sides of the same coin": agency without accountability risks activity without results ("everyone's off doing stuff, but okay, what have you actually busted up? What have you done?"); accountability without agency risks bottlenecking on permission.
- Operationalized as: given a problem, everyone on the team has ideas for how to address it (agency), but each person/initiative is expected to articulate what hypothesis it's testing and be answerable for the result (accountability).
- Connected by Fung to a broader observation (attributed in the conversation to economist Tyler Cowen and echoed by Lenny Rachitsky) that people thriving in the AI-tooling era are distinguished by "initiative" or "agency" — proactively identifying and acting on opportunities rather than waiting to be assigned work.
- Practically enables Anthropic's "routines" feature (see [[Claude Cowork]]) and other autonomous-agent workflows: Fung notes that as verification quality improves, teams can extend more autonomy to agents ("if the verification is really good, go for it").
- Paired with the related hiring philosophy of favoring creative builders with product sense and deep systems experts (see [[Trust But Verify]]) — both profiles are expected to exercise this kind of agency within their own domain.
