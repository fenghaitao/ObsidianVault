## Definition

Human evaluation of AI is the practice of using expert human annotators to deeply assess AI model performance, as opposed to relying on automated benchmarks or casual user voting. Edwin Chen argues this is the only reliable way to measure true model progress.

## Key Information

- Surge AI's approach: human annotators who are experts at the top of their fields have deep conversations with models across diverse topics (physics, teaching, coding)
- Unlike casual users who "skim responses for 2 seconds and pick whatever looks flashiest," Surge AI's annotators "are not just skimming the responses, they're actually working through the responses deeply themselves"
- Annotators evaluate code, double-check physics equations, and assess models on accuracy, instruction following, and many other dimensions
- Casual users on platforms like LM Arena and ChatGPT response comparisons are "just vibing and picking whatever response looks flashiest" — not doing real evaluation
- Human evaluation covers both aspects: (1) measuring model progress to pick the best checkpoint for release, and (2) generating training data by evaluating whether the model did a good job and rewarding it
- This approach will be necessary until AGI is reached — "by definition, if we haven't reached AGI yet, then there's more for the models to learn from [humans]"
