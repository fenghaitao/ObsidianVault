## Definition
The J-Curve Familiarity Effect is the hypothesis that AI tool productivity follows a J-shaped curve: developers initially slow down when adopting AI tools, then after a period of familiarization (3-6 months), they begin to experience genuine speedups.

## Key Information
- Meta's developer experience research found this J-curve when giving developers AI agents: initial slowdown followed by speedup after 3-6 months
- Joel Becker is "totally on board" with J-curve explanations being real in many economically relevant cases beyond software engineering
- In METR's RCT, Becker argues the J-curve is not explaining much of the observed slowdown because:
  - The effect size difference between AI-allowed and AI-disallowed is "very large"
  - Watching screen recordings convinced him developers were using Cursor competently
  - Plots showed no clear relationship between Cursor familiarity and productivity (though sample sizes were too small for strong conclusions)
- One participant with 140+ logged Cursor hours was conservatively coded as 30-50 hours, illustrating the difficulty of measuring familiarity
- Becker acknowledges J-curve explanations are "still on the table" but considers the evidence weak
- The distinction between learning the tool vs. learning what AI can and cannot do is important — developers improve at scoping problems appropriately over time
