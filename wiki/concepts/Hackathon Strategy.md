## Definition

Hackathon Strategy is a systematic approach to winning hackathons, encompassing pre-event research, team selection, execution, and pitching. Jia Chen developed this through winning 21 hackathons with a 70% win rate.

## Key Information

- **Pre-event research:** Before writing any code, research sponsor tools, understand how they integrate with different ideas, and visualize the development plan to assess feasibility within 24 hours
- **Early integration:** Connect frontend and backend within the first few hours, even in the simplest way possible, then iterate on that connected foundation — do not save integration for the end
- **Scope management:** Aim for a solid, simple, straightforward finished product rather than something absurd or complex; a finished product beats an ambitious unfinished one
- **Grand prize strategy:** For grand prizes, aim high with technical complexity but have thought through all steps in advance so the execution is flawless
- **Pitching:** Most judges are non-technical — state the idea simply, then focus on the demo and judge interaction; avoid explaining the tech stack in detail
- **Social component:** Read people for team fit, maintain energy for the full 24 hours, and present to judges in a way they can follow
- **Diminishing returns:** Jia recommended 3-5 hackathons as optimal; beyond that, the marginal benefit decreases and it is more valuable to build a product with long-term users
