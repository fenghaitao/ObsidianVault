## Definition

A talent-evaluation question used at [[Stripe]] (cited by executive coach [[Rachel Lockett]], who worked there as an HR leader): "would you enthusiastically rehire this person for the same role?"

## Key Information

- Deliberately binary and physically/emotionally immediate — Lockett says people have an honest gut-level reaction to the question, similar to the "full-body yes" concept, that cuts through drawn-out ambivalence about an underperforming or misaligned team member.
- A "no" answer doesn't mean immediate termination — it means *something* has to change: a real conversation, a performance plan, a role change, or, if none of those work, an exit. It's a forcing function for clarity, not a mandate to fire someone.
- Particularly useful when a leadership team's roles have outgrown the person in them as a company scales (e.g., an early CFO who was right for a much smaller company but should now become a controller under a new hire) — the same question applies to role-fit questions, not just performance problems.
- Lenny Rachitsky notes Netflix CTO [[Elizabeth Stone]] cites a similar practice in her own separate podcast appearance.
