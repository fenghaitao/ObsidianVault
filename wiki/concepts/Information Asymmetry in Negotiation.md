## Definition

In negotiation, information asymmetry refers to the imbalance of knowledge between the company and the candidate. Companies negotiate thousands of times a day and know what everyone makes, what budgets are, and how to change them. Candidates negotiate 4-5 times in their career. This asymmetry is a core source of the company's leverage — and understanding it should give candidates confidence to negotiate.

## Key Information

- Companies negotiate thousands of times a day; you negotiate 4-5 times in your career
- Companies know: what people make, what others make, what they'll accept, their budgets, how to change their budgets
- Jacob uses the game of Werewolf as an analogy: even when werewolves are outnumbered 5:1, they win 60-70% of the time because they have more information
- "These companies have significant leverage over you. They know what people make, they know what others make, they know what they'll accept."
- "This should also give you confidence to negotiate in the first place. You are at such a disadvantage."
- The information you share early (with recruiters, on LinkedIn) becomes part of your value chain — recruiters take notes and may anchor you to old numbers years later
