## Definition

Jeetu Patel's framework for building great companies, consisting of six elements stack-ranked in descending order of importance: Timing, Market, Team, Product, Brand, and Distribution. All six are required to win, but they are prioritized in this order.

## Key Information

- Articulated by Jeetu Patel
- **1. Timing**: The most important element, and the one you control the least. Many companies built amazing products at the wrong time and didn't win. When timing is wrong, don't scrap the idea — put it on ice (like Steve Jobs did with the iPad).
- **2. Market**: You need a large enough market, prosecuted a chunk at a time. "If you have a great market and mediocre team, the market pulls you up. If you have a shitty market and a great team, the market drags you down. The market always wins."
- **3. Team**: A well-rounded team where people complement each other — the things you suck at, someone else is really good at. Both must accept that of each other.
- **4. Product**: The soul of a company — where people seek value. "It's unethical to have a mediocre product sold in the market."
- **5. Brand**: Once you lose your brand and trust, "people don't come back to you that much." A mentor told Jeetu: "Don't ever go to a company who's lost their brand mojo because it's very hard to resurrect."
- **6. Distribution**: "Just because you build it, they will not come." You need a scaled mechanism for getting the offering to many people.
- Mnemonic: "Timing trumps market, market trumps team, team trumps product, product trumps brand, brand trumps distribution. You don't have all six, you don't win."
- Jeetu uses this as an operational template when evaluating new business units or products
