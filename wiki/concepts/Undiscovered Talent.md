## Definition

[[Keith Rabois]]'s recruiting philosophy, learned from [[Peter Thiel]] at [[PayPal]]: build a company around people the market hasn't correctly evaluated yet, rather than competing (and losing, on price) for already-proven "everybody wants them" hires.

## Key Information

- Startups operate under an effective "salary cap" — often a fraction of what large incumbents can pay — so out-bidding for already-recognized talent isn't a viable strategy; the edge has to come from correctly pricing people others misjudge.
- Rabois's diagnostic: figure out specifically why a person would get misprocessed by a conventional, homogeneous hiring pipeline (at a Meta, Google, or similar), then hire them before the market corrects.
- One major driver of this mispricing is age/data sparsity: the more career "data points" exist on a candidate (analogous to a credit/FICO score), the more a large-company hiring pipeline will pattern-match and homogenize their evaluation — younger candidates with less data are harder for that pipeline to process accurately, creating an opportunity.
- Complements attracting undiscovered "[[Barrels And Ammunition|barrels]]" once found: convincing them their specific skill overlaps with the company's single biggest current blocker (e.g., a superb marketer joining a company whose central bottleneck is marketing) is more persuasive than generic mission-selling.
- Rabois notes this same evaluative skill — spotting undervalued potential before consensus recognizes it — is essentially identical to his job as an early-stage investor.
