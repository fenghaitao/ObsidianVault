## Definition
A self-service platform enables developers (and their AI agents) to provision resources, deploy applications, and perform operational tasks independently, without requiring manual intervention from other teams or individuals. No process should require talking to a specific person or waiting for a specific person to do something.

## Key Information
- Juan Herreros Elorza identifies self-service as the first and most critical requirement for AI-agent-ready platforms
- AI agents cannot walk to another floor, send a message and wait, or negotiate with infrastructure teams — any human-dependent process is a hard blocker
- Self-service must be both technically possible and practically intuitive: if it requires fetching building blocks from five different places and assembling them manually, it is not truly self-service
- The goal is to "remove people from the process and make it easy to the greatest extent possible"
- Benefits: eliminates bottlenecks, reduces wait times, lowers support request volume, and enables both human and AI agent productivity
- Counterpoint: if self-service is implemented poorly (confusing flows), support requests may actually increase rather than decrease
- Related to the broader platform engineering principle of treating the platform as a product with developer experience as a first-class concern
