## Definition
Genchi Genbutsu (現地現物) is a Toyota Production System principle meaning "real things, real places" — the practice of going to see the actual situation firsthand rather than relying on reports or abstractions. For LLM applications, this means looking at real user interaction data rather than relying on aggregate metrics or proxy signals.

## Key Information
- Originated in the Toyota Production System as a core management principle
- At Toyota, it meant sending executives to factory floors to observe directly
- For LLM applications, the equivalent is looking at real user interaction data — the actual inputs, outputs, and traces from production
- "There's nothing more valuable than that" — real data is the most important source of information about how an LLM application delivers value
- Complements the continuous improvement cycle: you cannot improve what you do not observe directly
- Contrasted with getting "overly excited about tools" — direct observation of data is more important than tool infrastructure
