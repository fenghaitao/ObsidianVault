## Definition

Anchoring is the psychological tendency for the first number mentioned in a negotiation to set the frame for all subsequent discussion. The party who anchors first sets the range, and the natural tendency is to split the difference. Jacob Warwick advises: if you must anchor, anchor high.

## Key Information

- The first number mentioned becomes the reference point for the entire negotiation
- Natural tendency: counterparties want to split the difference between positions
- Jacob cites studies from the 1970s: "those who anchored egregiously high won 75% more than those who just try to be reasonable"
- If you anchor too low, you create a ceiling that limits your upside
- If you share a number too early (before understanding the full scope of the role), you may anchor yourself to a number that doesn't reflect the actual work — this is [[Scope Creep in Hiring]]
- Jacob's advice: don't share your number first. If you must, anchor significantly higher than what you'd accept
- "If you ask for 50 more, you might get 15. But if you ask for 150 more, you might get 70."
