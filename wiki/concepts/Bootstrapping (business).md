## Definition

Bootstrapping is the practice of building a company without external venture capital funding, relying on revenue from customers to fuel growth. Surge AI is a prime example, reaching $1B+ in revenue while completely bootstrapped.

## Key Information

- Surge AI hit over $1B in revenue in under 4 years with under 100 people, completely bootstrapped with no VC funding, and profitable from day one
- Edwin believes bootstrapping allowed Surge to attract customers who truly understood data quality rather than those attracted by PR and hype
- "The only way we were going to succeed was by building a 10 times better product and getting word of mouth from researchers"
- Bootstrapping meant Surge avoided the "Silicon Valley industrial complex" — VCs tweeting about you, TechCrunch headlines, and PR-driven valuations
- Edwin argues that less capital means founders don't need to be great at pitching and hyping — they can focus on technology and product
- Fewer employees means less capital needed, which means less fundraising pressure — a virtuous cycle
- "Instead of companies started by founders who are great at pitching and great at hyping, you'll get founders who are really great at technology or product"
- **Jason Cohen's perspective:** Bootstrapped companies like 37 Signals can hit a natural size limit and choose to maximize profit instead of revenue. Founders earn millions in dividends and don't need to grow further. This is a valid, intentional choice — not a failure.
- Cohen has built both bootstrapped and VC-funded unicorns, giving him perspective on both models
