## Definition

[[Boris Cherny]]'s deliberate practice of assigning fewer people to a project than it seemingly needs, forcing the team to lean on Claude to move fast — paired with giving engineers unrestricted token budgets rather than optimizing cost early.

## Key Information

- Cherny's framing: when a project is intentionally under-staffed (e.g., a single engineer on a [[Claude Cowork]] project who wants to ship quickly), people are "forced to Claudify" — to automate work with Claude rather than doing it by hand — out of intrinsic motivation to ship, not top-down mandate.
- Companion advice to CTOs: don't optimize or cost-cut token spend at the start; give engineers as many tokens as possible so they can freely try ideas that would otherwise seem too expensive to attempt. Only optimize (e.g., swapping to a cheaper model) once an idea has proven itself and needs to scale.
- Practical cost framing: token spend for an individual experimenting engineer is typically small relative to salary/other business costs — the "big bill" concern usually only becomes real once a successful idea scales up significantly, which is also the appropriate time to start optimizing.
- Was true from Claude Code's origin: in the earliest days it was just Boris alone, so speed was the team's only competitive advantage — a principle the team has kept even as it's grown.
