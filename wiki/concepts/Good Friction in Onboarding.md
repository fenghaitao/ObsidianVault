## Definition

Good Friction in Onboarding is the counterintuitive growth principle that adding the right steps to an onboarding flow — steps that help users understand why the product is for them — leads to higher conversion and better long-term retention. The key insight is that friction is not inherently bad; the question is whether it adds to the user's comprehension of the product's value.

## Key Information

- Core insight: "Cut friction when it doesn't add to the experience of helping a user understand why the product is for them. But if you can help users understand why the product is for them and it's going to add friction, don't shy away from it."
- The most common mistake: "Just get them into the product, cut all the steps, solve time to value" — this doesn't work most times
- **Anthropic**: onboarding asks users who they are and their interest areas, then recommends different products and features. People say "you have so much friction" but the data shows it performs well
- **MasterClass**: purchase flow includes a quiz about interests and what you're here for — thoroughly tested, significant revenue driver
- **Calm**: landing page, purchase flow, and login flow all include a quiz — not a coincidence
- **Mercury**: breaking out form inputs from one screen to two screens reduced cognitive load and performed well
- The "for them" is key: helping users feel the product is for them by understanding their interests
- Benefits flow through the lifecycle: helps activation, then lifecycle marketing, then look-alike targeting for ads
- "That initial piece of how you understand who the user is — it's a juice that just keeps on giving"
- Related to the broader concept of [[Friction vs. Comprehension]] — the real challenge is often comprehension, not friction
