## Definition

Influence is the ability to increase the odds that your good ideas survive. It is distinct from politics — politics is manipulating outcomes and people for your own gain, while influence is about building momentum behind great ideas so that great products get built. Jessica Fain calls it "the single most important skill for product leaders that AI can't replace."

## Key Information

- **Influence vs. Politics:** Politics = manipulating outcomes for your own gain. Influence = increasing the odds that your good ideas survive.
- Without influence and buy-in from key stakeholders and executives, you can't build great products.
- "One of the ways that I phrase this is: how do you get the best out of your exec? How do you help them be their best selves?"
- As AI commoditizes execution, influence becomes the 10x skill — the leverage shifts from doing the work to deciding what work survives and getting others to buy in.
- Product managers who made careers on being the most type-A, Gantt-chart-master, best note-taker will be displaced by AI. The skill that remains is influence.
- "It's your fault if the leaders didn't buy into your ideas." — you can't blame the exec.
- Influence is not one personality type. "You could be shy and introverted or super data-oriented and technical. All types of personalities can in their own authentic way communicate with the people around them to build trust to build influence."
- Jessica's early career feedback was to "show up more like a man" — she learned that authenticity is her superpower.
