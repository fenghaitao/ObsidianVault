## Definition

Divine discontent is a concept attributed to Jeff Bezos describing how customer standards continually rise. Once people are familiar with a piece of software, their standards go up, and the quality bar is always moving — requiring continued investment even in features that were once "good enough."

## Key Information

- **Attributed to**: Jeff Bezos (Amazon)
- **Effect on utility curves**: the S-curve line actually moves because as customers become familiar with software, their standards rise
- This creates a competition: as you improve your search capability, login experience, checkout experience, etc., everyone else improves too — so the bar continually rises
- **Implication**: most things in products get implemented once and then rarely improved, even though the quality bar has gone up across the board
- **Examples of neglected areas**: account creation, sign-up, forgot password — essential parts of the app that very infrequently get iteration and improvement
- Even though a bad time zone picker won't cause someone to switch from Gmail, the emotional connection (or lack thereof) matters when people advocate for products at new companies
