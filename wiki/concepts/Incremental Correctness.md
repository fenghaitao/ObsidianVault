## Definition

Incremental correctness is a core value [[Max Schoening]] (Head of Product, [[Notion]]) says he holds personally and tries to instill at Notion: get really, really good at *iterating* toward quality, rather than trying to isolate yourself until a product is fully finished ("[[Obviously Good]]") before shipping anything.

## Key Information

- Schoening names it directly as "one of my core values": "iterate, get really, really good at iterating."
- Positioned as the corrective to a specific failure mode he sees in teams chasing an "obviously good" bar: retreating into isolation ("we're going to be in this cave") until the product feels finished, instead of shipping and refining in public.
- Works in tandem with [[Shots On Goal]] (Notion's push to increase the number of experiments/attempts, since exploration is now cheap): shots on goal generates the raw material; incremental correctness is the discipline of converging that raw material toward quality over repeated cycles rather than in one long isolated push.
- Illustrated by Schoening's own admitted tension at Notion: the company has organically grown six overlapping "automation primitives," and incremental correctness requires periodically doing the harder work of consolidating them into one clean "naked robotic core" — sometimes at the cost of delaying the next new feature.
- Echoes Schoening's broader "first 10%/last 10%" framing: AI has made early exploration free, but he argues the final 10% (making something reliable and good) is still 90% of the actual work — incremental correctness names the discipline of grinding through that last mile.
