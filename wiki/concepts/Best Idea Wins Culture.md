## Definition

[[Qasar Younis]]'s description of [[Applied Intuition]]'s deliberately emotion-minimized decision-making culture: employees are actively encouraged to speak up and disagree regardless of seniority or past track record, and the idea itself — not who proposed it — is meant to determine the outcome.

## Key Information

- Younis argues emotions are "generally not helpful in making rational decisions" and are often just a proxy for ownership/ego rather than sound judgment; a useful heuristic is whether multiple different people would reach the same decision given the same facts.
- Cites a Gandhi line ("truth is what stands the test of time") and notes this resembles the "truth-seeking" ethos that has become something of a meme in the Bay Area.
- Founders resist this culture instinctively because popular narratives equate strong leadership with a single unwavering vision — Younis explicitly rejects that model.
- Once the best idea is identified through open debate, the company still has to move decisively — see [[Move Fast Move Safe]] — holding "be open" and "decide fast" in tension.
- Younis stresses that being right, not merely having conviction or ambition, is what actually matters: "you have to be right," measured by whether the company is a sustainable, standalone business.
