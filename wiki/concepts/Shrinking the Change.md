## Definition

A concept from the book _Switch_ about change management: if something seems scary and overwhelming, make it smaller — an experiment, a one-week proof of concept. This builds momentum and trust with executives who are hesitant to commit to a large, uncertain investment.

## Key Information

- **Origin:** The book _Switch_ by Chip and Dan Heath, which Jessica Fain calls "one of the only business books I've ever liked."
- "If something seems scary and overwhelming, how do you make it so much smaller so that it's an experiment? It's a one-week proof of concept."
- "Shrinking the change is a huge way to build momentum and trust."
- Applies to any product that takes longer than a month: what are your milestones? What are the chunks at which you're showing outcomes and fit?
- **Practical application:** "We're going to test this. Here's how we're going to know it's working. We'll come back to you on X date."
- This addresses the **desire for certainty**: instead of needing certainty about the outcome, create certainty about the next check-in point.
- "I don't know the answer right now. I don't know if this is going to be successful. I'm going to check in with you on X date."
- This is easier than ever with modern prototyping tools.
