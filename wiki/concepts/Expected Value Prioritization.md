## Definition

Expected-Value Prioritization is Brian Attwell's method for choosing work: maximize expected value created per hour by computing (importance × probability of success with vs. without you) ÷ time.

## Key Information

- Three inputs: (A) how much expected value the initiative has over a fixed window; (B) the probability of success with and without you; (C) how much time it takes you
- Formula: A × B ÷ C = expected value creation per hour; always maximize it
- Key nuance: the most important initiative is often NOT the most valuable use of your time — the top priority is usually already figured out and well-staffed (you'd be "a cog"), whereas a valuable-but-understaffed lower priority lets you "be a hero"
- To build the skill: ask why leaders care about a metric (e.g., "improve recall 10%" → reduce churn → dollars), and read the company's expected-value spreadsheet column if one exists
