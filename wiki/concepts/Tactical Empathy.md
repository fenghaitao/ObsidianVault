## Definition

A concept from Chris Voss's negotiation framework: understanding the other party's emotions, motivations, and perspective in order to create leverage. Jacob Warwick notes that this can sound "more Machiavellian than anything I've ever said," but the core idea is that understanding what drives someone enables more effective collaboration.

## Key Information

- Attributed to Chris Voss (former FBI hostage negotiator)
- Jacob Warwick: "Understanding those motivations and understanding how to play on that can be important."
- The technique involves:
  - Labeling emotions: "It sounds like you're frustrated with the current process."
  - Understanding motivations: "What's important to you about this hire?"
  - Mirroring: repeating the last few words someone said to encourage them to elaborate
- Jacob applies tactical empathy through the [[Positive Reputation Technique]] and [[If You Were in My Shoes]] question
- "If you were in my shoes, what would you do?" — puts the other party in your position and forces empathy
- "If you were in my shoes, like go backwards 5 years in your career, that's where I'm at right now. If you were in my shoes, what would you ask for?"
- The concept is distinct from genuine empathy — it's strategic empathy used to achieve negotiation goals
