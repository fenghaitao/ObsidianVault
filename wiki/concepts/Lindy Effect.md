## Definition

The Lindy Effect is a concept stating that the future life expectancy of a non-perishable thing (idea, technology, institution) is proportional to its current age. If something has survived for X years, it will likely survive at least X more years.

## Key Information

- Applied by Lenny Rachitsky: after writing his newsletter every week for 9 months, he realized he could probably do it for at least 9 more months, giving him confidence to add a paywall
- The concept originates from the observation that Broadway shows lasting 100 days tend to last another 100 days
- Also referenced by Lenny about his podcast: "According to the Lindy, you have 7 more years" (since the newsletter has been going 7 years)
- Useful mental model for evaluating whether to commit to a creative or business endeavor
