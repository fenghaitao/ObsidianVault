## Definition

[[Peter Thiel]]'s 2x2 framework (optimism/pessimism × determinate/indeterminate), applied by [[Marc Andreessen]] to explain Silicon Valley and his own firm's venture strategy: a "determinate optimist" has a specific plan for how the future gets better (Thiel's example: Elon Musk — electric cars, then solar, then Mars); an "indeterminate optimist" believes the future will be better without being able to specify exactly how or why.

## Key Information

- Thiel historically criticized indeterminate optimism as bordering on wishful/delusional thinking, favoring determinate optimists as the people who actually build the future.
- Andreessen partly disagrees: he argues indeterminate optimism, properly understood, isn't vague wishful thinking but a specific structural bet — that having many determinate optimists (founders) running many different concrete plans, at scale, is itself a reliable strategy, even though no single funder/observer can know in advance which specific bets will pay off.
- Applied to Andreessen Horowitz's own strategy: founders must be determinate optimists (a specific plan, full commitment, "you have to make the bed you lay in"), while the firm operates as an indeterminate optimist — placing many bets across as many strong determinate founders as possible, since the value of the system comes from having thousands of concrete attempts rather than picking the one correct answer in advance.
- Andreessen's rebuttal to the "founders take all the risk, VCs have it easy" critique: founders get to actually run their companies and, in the long run, get remembered by history (Henry Ford, not Ford's seed investor) — the "indeterminate" role in the background is real but secondary.
- Connects to his broader stance on AI moats and industry-structure predictions ([[AI IQ Scaling]], general product-strategy uncertainty): confident, specific predictions about winners tend to be wrong early in a major technology shift, so the right individual posture (as an investor) is to run many experiments rather than commit to one determinate industry-structure bet.
