## Definition

Luck vs Ability is the reflective framework for understanding how much of career success is attributable to fortunate circumstances versus personal effort and skill. Ricky estimated his career outcomes were roughly 50% luck and 50% ability.

## Key Information

### The Two Parts of Luck
- Ricky described luck as having two components: (1) the chance of an opportunity actually coming along, and (2) being prepared to seize it when it does
- "Sometimes you just have to wait for that and it might take longer, it might not take as long"
- The second part is within your control: "making sure that you're doing your best and doing the right thing so that when an opportunity does come along, you can actually take advantage and seize the opportunity"

### Ricky's Lucky Circumstances
- He was matched to a team in a product area (ads) that he ended up really liking, despite not picking it himself
- His team was "relatively stable" — no major reorgs or restructuring that could have hindered career progress
- He had supportive managers throughout, even through some manager shifts
- His manager shifts were still within the same broader team, maintaining continuity

### The Counterfactual
- "An engineer that's better than you" could be on a terrible team with reorgs every 6 months and no consistent projects — in that case, they couldn't replicate the same trajectory
- Ricky was "pretty confident" he would eventually get to senior regardless of team, but timing could have been much longer
- If he had to switch teams, the ramp-up time in a new code stack would have added delays

### The 50/50 Split
- Ricky attributes roughly half his success to luck and half to his own abilities
- He was "very conscious about the next level and what I had to do to get there"
- He "made sure to put in the effort" — preparation met opportunity
