## Definition

A launch strategy where a product is labeled a "research preview" to set expectations that it's early, has flaws, and will improve. The key is committing to the promise: you must continuously iterate and respond to feedback. Trust degrades when you ship early and never improve. Used by Anthropic for Claude Co-work.

## Key Information

- Anthropic labels early products as "research previews" to signal: "it's early, it's going to have flaws"
- Claude Co-work is a key example — labeled a research preview at launch
- The philosophy: "This is the worst it's ever going to be, but we're going to put it out there because we believe the benefits outweigh the cons"
- The product might not yet be the easiest to work with or highest quality, but the core value proposition is strong enough
- Critical follow-through: you must iterate, take feedback, and show continuous improvement
- "The way you really lose trust around quality is if you release it early and then nothing ever happens"
- This approach actually makes design more fun: you put something out, learn immediately, and know what to do next
