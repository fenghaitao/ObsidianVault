## Definition

Jevons Paradox is the economic principle that as a resource becomes more efficient to use, consumption of that resource increases rather than decreases. Applied to AI, as coding becomes cheaper and faster, the total volume of coding increases rather than decreasing the need for engineers.

## Key Information

- Referenced by Lenny in the context of AI and job displacement
- The paradox: as AI makes coding cheaper, demand for coding goes up, potentially leading to more hiring, not less
- Boris's experience at Anthropic: despite 200% productivity increase per engineer, "we probably 4x the engineering team"
- Boris: "As the thing scales up... the cost becomes pretty big. That's the point at which you want to optimize it"
- The paradox suggests AI won't necessarily eliminate engineering jobs, but will transform them
- Connected to the printing press analogy: making writing cheaper led to dramatically more writing, not less
