## Definition

Classical economic paradox — invoked by [[Benedict Evans]] (misheard as "jeans paradox" in the transcript) — describing how making something more efficient/cheaper can increase, rather than decrease, total consumption of it, and by extension the surrounding demand for labor.

## Key Information

- Evans's example: accountant headcount has risen through the 20th and 21st centuries despite the introduction of adding machines, spreadsheets, ERP systems, and cloud accounting software — each of which made individual accounting tasks dramatically cheaper.
- Used to explain why cheaper individual tasks (via AI automation) don't necessarily shrink a profession: cheaper tasks can expand the total addressable scope of work rather than eliminate the workers doing it.
- Works alongside [[Task Vs Job]] as Evans's core rebuttal to the assumption that automating visible tasks straightforwardly destroys jobs.
