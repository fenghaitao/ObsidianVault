## Definition

[[Eric Ries]]'s central antagonist concept in *[[Incorruptible (book)|Incorruptible]]*: the internal force that drags successful organizations toward mediocrity and extraction once they succeed, not competition from outside.

## Key Information

- The book's core thesis is that this force is preventable through the combination of ethos (a clear, written purpose) and integrity (structural governance mechanisms) — see [[Ethos And Integrity]].
- Illustrated by case studies of incremental, individually-justifiable erosion: [[Groupon]]'s creep from "one email a day" to eight, [[Google]]'s quiet abandonment of "don't be evil."
- Counter-illustrated by durable exceptions that resisted it structurally: [[Novo Nordisk]] and [[Zeiss]] (industrial foundations), [[Patagonia]] (perpetual purpose trust), [[Anthropic]] (Long-Term Benefit Trust), [[Cloudflare]] and [[Costco]] (culture-driven resistance).
- Academic research cited by Ries: companies protected by durable governance structures (industrial foundations, perpetual purpose trusts) are six times more likely to survive to year 50 and show superior return on invested capital versus conventional [[Shareholder Primacy]] companies.
