## Definition

Influence vs. Politics is a distinction drawn by Jessica Fain: politics is manipulating outcomes and people for your own gain, while influence is about increasing the odds that your good ideas survive. When people frame influence as "icky" or "political," they're missing the point — it's about learning, empathy, and building great products together.

## Key Information

- **Politics** = manipulating outcomes and people for your own gain
- **Influence** = increasing the odds that your good ideas survive
- "When people frame this as politics, they're completely missing the point"
- Influence is about learning, not politicking — treat stakeholder conversations as discovery interviews
- "If we do that in a genuine and low-ego way," we end up in a much better place
- Noah Weiss's notebook of "things I learned from Stuart" exemplifies genuine learning, not politicking — he kept it for his own growth, not to show off
- "If you don't respect that person, if you don't believe they know something you don't, you need to quit. You need to go work somewhere else"
- If you respect the executive, "take the feedback, take the insight, ask questions, and don't just treat it like a rubber stamp"
