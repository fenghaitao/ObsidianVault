## Definition

Human-shaped robots discussed by [[Caitlin Kalinowski]] as the most visible manifestation of [[Physical AI]] — currently advanced prototypes, not yet ready for at-scale (hundreds of thousands to millions of units) deployment.

## Key Information

- Safety is a primary design constraint: mass and impact compliance matter as much as capability. [[1X Neo]] is cited favorably for "pulling mass inwards" to reduce impact energy — "softer robots is safer."
- [[1X Neo]], [[Optimus]], and [[Figure]] are named as the companies "very ahead" in the category.
- At-scale deployment is gated mainly by supply chain (magnets, [[Actuators]], silicon, RAM), not by AI capability, per Kalinowski.
- Designing a robot that feels non-threatening (soft appearance, telegraphing intent, acknowledging people entering a room) is a distinct discipline she learned from HRI researcher [[Leila Takayama]], comparable to how [[Pixar]] and [[Disney]] approach character animation.
- Per [[Qasar Younis]] (episode 22): argues humans are emotionally drawn to the humanoid form specifically "because we're monkeys," but that the more pragmatic, faster-arriving path is adding intelligence to already-engineered, non-humanoid heavy machinery (tractors, mining rigs, trucks) rather than building humanoids from scratch. See [[Physical AI]] and [[Nunchuck Robot Fallacy]] for his framing of public fear around humanoid robot videos.
