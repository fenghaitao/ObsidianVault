## Definition

[[Evan Spiegel]]'s core lesson from 15 years running [[Snapchat]]: software features are trivially cloned by competitors (despite patents), so durable competitive advantage has to come from something harder to copy — ecosystems/platforms and vertically-integrated hardware — not from the code itself.

## Key Information

- Snap learned this the hard way early on: despite having patents, competitors easily copied Snapchat inventions like [[Stories]], swipe-based navigation, and camera-as-primary-interface.
- Spiegel argues everyone is "discovering today with AI" what Snap learned 15 years ago — implementation/features are becoming even cheaper and easier to replicate, echoing [[Vibe Coding]]'s critique of pure-software differentiation.
- Snap's response: invest in things hard to copy — the creator/developer ecosystem (millions of community-built AR lenses), and vertically-integrated hardware ([[Spectacles]]/Specs) — rather than relying on network effects or feature velocity alone.
- Distribution is framed as the emerging moat of the AI era: AI is climbing "up the funnel" (autocomplete → code → review/testing → ideas/strategy), but can't hand a company an audience — so whoever controls distribution wins as products commoditize. Parallels [[Benedict Evans]]'s independent argument via [[Distribution Moat]].
