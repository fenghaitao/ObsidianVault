## Definition

[[Cat Wu]]'s framing of [[Anthropic]]'s key structural advantage: the company's central mission (safe AGI for all of humanity) sits explicitly above any individual product line, so teams are willing to sacrifice their own goals and KRs in service of the company's.

## Key Information

- Concrete effect: when two priorities compete, Anthropic settles the question by asking which is more important for the company's mission — this makes cross-org trade-offs faster and gets broad buy-in once decided, rather than triggering prolonged internal politics.
- Wu's illustrative extreme case: "if Claude Code failed but Anthropic succeeded, I would be extremely happy" — the individual product line's success is explicitly subordinate to the company's mission.
- Distinguished by Wu from mere "focus": focus is about prioritization; mission is specifically about willingness to accept a personal/team loss for an organizational win.
- Cited by Wu as explaining why Anthropic doesn't chase every adjacent opportunity (e.g., a social feed product) the way some competitors do — see [[Anthropic]]'s broader case study alongside [[Financial Gravity]] and [[Ethos And Integrity]] from Eric Ries's episode.
