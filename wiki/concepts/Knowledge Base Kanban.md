## Definition
A Knowledge Base Kanban is a structured board of documentation gaps produced by the Context Gap Scanner, organized by priority (critical, high, medium). It transforms the abstract problem of "we need better documentation" into concrete, actionable tickets — analogous to how Jira tracks software work items.

## Key Information
- **Output of Context Gap Scanner**: After running past work items against the knowledge base, the scanner produces a consolidated Kanban board showing what's clean, stale, incomplete, or entirely missing.
- **Priority Classification**: Critical (appears repeatedly across multiple incidents — fix first), High (significant gap affecting multiple tasks), Medium (isolated gaps).
- **Contrast with Miro Boards**: Traditional approach is "endless Miro boards" of documentation gaps that never get resolved. The Knowledge Base Kanban provides structured, prioritized, actionable items.
- **Fixing Process**: Just like Jira tickets, documentation gaps on the Kanban board are worked through systematically. As gaps are filled, agent confidence scores improve.
- **Living Document**: The Kanban board evolves as new work items are processed and new gaps are discovered. It's not a one-time audit but an ongoing knowledge management tool.
- **Enterprise Reality**: Raj predicts that within a year, enterprises will realize the importance of knowledge base quality and Kanban boards for documentation gaps "will definitely come into reality very soon."
