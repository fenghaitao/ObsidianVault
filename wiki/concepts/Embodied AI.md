## Definition
Embodied AI (called "physical AI" in this transcript) is AI that acts in and learns from the physical world through robotic bodies.
## Key Information
- Sergey Levine frames it as potentially "even bigger" than the LLM wave: physical AI and humanoid robots.
- Argues robotics historically is not a learning-friendly ecosystem (unlike vision/NLP) because data is not freely shared and physical-safety concerns are higher.
- Expects growing activity — e.g., OpenAI (transcribed "openthropic") investing in robotics — to shift the field toward learned models (not hand-designed controllers) and toward data sharing, since no single company can build a true foundation model "in a single vertical."
- Emphasizes grounding: a model trained on lots of real embodied data can then absorb other sources (simulation, video, human demonstration) more effectively.
- Warns against narrow vertical thinking: even for warehouse automation, collecting a breadth of task data yields more generalizable skills.
