## Definition

[[Marc Andreessen]]'s central macro thesis: AI is arriving "miraculously" well-timed to offset two converging structural problems — 50 years of surprisingly low productivity/technological growth, and accelerating global demographic collapse (below-replacement birth rates, including in China).

## Key Information

- Contrary to the felt sense of rapid technological change, US/Western productivity growth (the economist's measure of technology's actual economic impact) has run at roughly half the 1940-1970 rate and a third of the 1870-1940 rate over the last 50 years — meaning the real economy has changed far less than people assume.
- Simultaneously, birth rates are falling below replacement level in most developed countries and increasingly in China, implying real depopulation over the coming century.
- Without AI, this combination (low productivity growth plus shrinking population) would mean a shrinking economy, declining opportunity, and no new jobs or fields — a genuinely dystopian scenario Andreessen says nobody is talking about.
- With AI, the argument flips: AI/robots substitute for the workers that won't exist, arriving "precisely when we actually need them" — meaning remaining human workers become scarce and valuable ("at a premium, not a discount") rather than displaced.
- Feeds into Andreessen's broader argument that AI-driven productivity growth, even if it "only" triples today's rate, would still just return the economy to the job-turnover rate of 1870-1930 — a period widely remembered as one of abundant opportunity, not mass unemployment.
- Provides the economic backbone for why Andreessen is not worried about mass AI-driven job loss (see [[Task Vs Job]]) and frames his overall optimism as good news "either way" — whether AI's impact ends up incremental or dramatically fast (the latter triggering a deflationary abundance scenario where falling prices for AI-affected goods/services function as an effective universal wealth increase).
