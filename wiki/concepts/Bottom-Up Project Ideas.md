## Definition

Bottom-Up Project Ideas refers to the strategy of identifying and proposing your own projects rather than waiting for top-down assignments from a manager or tech lead. Ricky identified this as the primary driver of his accelerated promotion trajectory at Google.

## Key Information

### Ricky's Bottom-Up Approach
- Ricky's projects were "bottom up rather than top down" — he found his own work rather than waiting for manager assignments
- He felt his manager "was not too worried about the projects I was doing and just trusted that I'd find my own projects to push on"
- The manager still provided feedback and guidance, but the ideas originated from Ricky

### Finding High-Impact Projects
- First, figure out what "impact" means in your org — for Ricky in ads, it was revenue; for others it might be daily active users or product interactions
- He ran experiments to identify gaps: "here's a gap where I can probably have a lot of impact and drive a lot of Revenue"
- "Numbers don't lie" — if your idea makes the most money, no one can argue against it
- Having metrics-aligned projects with clear milestones makes impact undeniable

### The One-Year Roadmap
- Ricky built a one-year roadmap of projects he planned to do, then showed it to his manager for buy-in
- He asked: "Do you feel like this is enough for the next level?"
- This created alignment a full year before his promotion
- He looked at what other people on his team had done to get promoted to senior and tried to find projects of similar scope and complexity

### Why Bottom-Up Matters
- "The best projects that you can find are the ones you find yourself"
- Your manager might not always give you a project at the next level
- Bottom-up projects naturally push your boundaries and put you outside your comfort zone
- Self-found projects are more likely to be ones you actually want to work on
