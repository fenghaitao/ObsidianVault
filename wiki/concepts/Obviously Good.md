## Definition

"Obviously good" is an internal quality bar at [[Notion]], described by [[Max Schoening]] (Head of Product) as a literal sticker/motto used inside the company: ship things that are unambiguously, immediately good rather than incrementally better — "you know it when you see it."

## Key Information

- Schoening: "we have a obviously good stickers... let's just only make obviously good stuff... I'm like, ah, you know it when you see it. Like, I don't think anyone argued when they saw the first iPhone that it's obviously good. I don't think anyone argued that when Chat GPT first came out that it's obviously good."
- Named as the bar Notion tries to hold shipped work to, alongside two other internal principles that manage the tension between shipping fast and shipping well: see [[Shots On Goal]] (increase experimentation) and [[Incremental Correctness]] (get good at iterating rather than waiting for perfection).
- Schoening flags the common failure mode of taking "obviously good" too literally: retreating into isolation/"a cave" until something feels finished, rather than iterating in public — he frames incremental correctness as the corrective to this.
- Connected to Schoening's broader "tiny core" theory of great products (see [[Tiny Core]]): an obviously good product is typically one built around a single exceptional capability, not one padded with many marginal features.
- Notion's internal conference rooms are named after famous well-designed objects (the first typewriter, the Macintosh, a Porsche 911, etc.) — Schoening cites this as a deliberate environmental nudge toward the "obviously good" bar, since sitting in such a room makes shortcomings in current work more visible ("nothing I'm doing amounts to this... I got to do better").
