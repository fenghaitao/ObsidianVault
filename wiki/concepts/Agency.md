## Definition

Agency, as framed by [[Max Schoening]] (Head of Product, [[Notion]]), is the disposition to believe the world around you is malleable and act on that belief — to just make things rather than waiting for permission or citing a "skill issue" as a reason something can't be done. He argues agency, not raw technical skill or job title, is the decisive trait separating people who thrive in the AI era from people who stall, because AI has made the skills themselves widely accessible while agency remains unevenly distributed.

## Key Information

- Core claim: "Before it was very easy to always say, well, I will never be able to do this because [insert skill issue]. We're realizing that even if you have the skills at your fingertips... the thing that matters is agency. I don't think agency is very evenly distributed in the world."
- Schoening equates high agency with the "founder mindset" — people who understand their environment is malleable will do great; people who cling to fixed definitions of their role ("what does it mean to be a PM," "what's my job as an engineer") will find the transition much harder.
- Practical advice for cultivating agency: **just start making things.** Tinkering (his example: even cooking a home meal is "a form of tinkering") puts you on a "treadmill" of creation, where you learn incrementally that hard things ("build that chair," "learn how computers work") are more approachable than they seem — "it just really awakens you to the idea that you can just change things."
- Explicitly contrasts this with a common misreading of agency as "circumventing your terrible boss to get what you want" — Schoening says that's the wrong frame; agency starts with making things, and influence/change follows once people notice what you've built.
- Origin quote he returns to repeatedly: the Steve Jobs line, "one day you wake up and you realize the world is made up by people no smarter than you" — cited as his personal reason for going into software, and as advice for listeners (go outside and look at how everything man-made around you was built by people no smarter than you).
- Notion examples of high agency, offered as concrete illustrations:
  - [[Brian Leven]] — blurs engineering/design roles and unofficially acts as a top recruiter, driven by "what does the org need" rather than "what's my job."
  - [[Eric Lou]] — proactively reskilled from writing long strategy docs/PRDs to prototyping directly in Figma and then in code, explicitly to make himself hireable at an earlier startup stage.
- Framed as "the thing that matters" now that "even an AGI-adjacent model" can supply most of the missing skill — i.e., agency becomes more decisive, not less, as the skill floor drops for everyone.
- Connects to Schoening's closing career advice: don't let "last train out" anxiety (fear of missing some final window before AI takes over) distract you from work you actually care about; if you tie yourself to agency instead of certainty about how the future unfolds, "you're probably going to be fine."
