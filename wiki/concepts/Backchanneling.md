## Definition

Backchanneling is driving your message one-on-one outside public forums — either positively (giving people a private chance to raise concerns and feel heard) or manipulatively (trading support out of sight).

## Key Information

- Positive backchanneling works partly through information exchange but mostly through emotion: you make the other person feel consulted, important, and heard, which builds trust.
- An executive deciding whom to fund also weighs "who can I count on when the chips are down?" — a leader who consults one-on-one wins ties or even beats a slightly better proposal.
- The alternative is treating the other person as an object ("I make the right call, I get the right result") rather than a human with pressures.
- Negative backchanneling (backroom deals / "kickbacks") is risky: "other people would see that you are a snake"; some will do it anyway and justify it with a self-narrative.
- Against slippery operators, bring allies/witnesses so it becomes easier for them to go along than to fight.
