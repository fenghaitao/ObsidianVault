## Definition

Top-Down vs Bottom-Up AI Adoption describes the two modes of AI deployment in organizations. Sherwin Wu argues that successful AI deployments require both top-down executive buy-in AND bottom-up grassroots employee excitement. Companies that only have top-down mandates (without bottom-up adoption) typically end up with negative ROI on AI.

## Key Information

- Sherwin Wu observes that many AI deployments have negative ROI, partly because of poor adoption patterns
- The anti-pattern: top-down mandate from the CEO/exec team ("we are going AI-first, everyone will be judged on AI usage") without grassroots support
- Why this fails: employees are told to use AI but don't understand the technology, have no peers to learn from, and the tools feel forced onto them
- "AI is being forced onto them" — sentiment from outside tech
- Silicon Valley bias: we forget we live in a bubble. Most people are not software engineers, not AI-pilled, and not following every model release
- The successful pattern: top-down buy-in (tools purchased, executive support) + bottom-up excitement (employees who are eager to learn, evangelize, and share best practices)
- At OpenAI, Codex adoption really took off when individual employees could apply it to their own work, not just from a company mandate
- Every job is different (engineering vs finance vs operations vs sales), so bottom-up last-mile customization is essential
- Recommendation: staff a full-time internal "AI tiger team" to explore capabilities, apply to workflows, do knowledge sharing, and create excitement
