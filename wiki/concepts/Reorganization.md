## Definition

A reorganization (reorg) is a reshuffling of org structure — triggered by a business change or a personnel move — onto which leaders load additional retention, promotion, and "manage out" motives.

## Key Information

- Triggers: a change in the business (new market, acquisition, new project) or a cascade (somebody quit or was moved).
- "There's always something else going on": once the business case is set, leaders pile extra motives onto the "ship leaving the dock" — retention, setting up promotions, and giving low performers less so they move on.
- Managing someone out via reorg means giving them the "garbage can"/window seat they won't want, so they leave on their own (the Japanese "window seat" signals exile to irrelevance).
- Retention is central: "who do I absolutely need to keep, and who is it if they leave I'll be fine or even happy."
- Because "too many reorgs cost you people," leaders wait as long as possible and then cram everything in at once.
- Defense/delay tactic: "we know reorgs cause a 6-month hit in productivity" — use it to push the conversation off.
