## Definition

"Good Inside" is Dr. Becky Kennedy's core philosophy that all people are fundamentally good regardless of their behavior. The framework requires separating behavior from identity: someone can be a good person who is doing something problematic. This separation is what enables effective behavior change.

## Key Information

- The philosophy requires separating behavior and identity: "One hand is identity, the other hand is behavior. And literally just separating them forces you to distinguish who someone is good inside from their behavior."
- The quickest way to have an unproductive conversation is to lose sight of the fact that someone's good inside
- When we collapse behavior and identity, the person becomes defensive because they feel their identity is being attacked — not just their behavior
- "If I'm talking about someone at work who's always late, here's a good inside infused conversation: 'I want to say we're on the same team. I know you're a good person. You probably don't need me to tell you that we need to start meetings on time. We both know that. It's also been happening consistently, which lets me know something is going on.'"
- The same approach works in sports coaching: "You missed all the layups today. You're a good player. Something's going on. Let's get in the gym tomorrow."
- Core insight: "Most difficult times we have personally or interpersonally come because we've collapsed behavior and identity."
- The philosophy applies to work: "This is a good person who is late" rather than "that person is lazy"
- Dr. Becky was initially trained in reward/punishment models but found them at odds with what actually helped adults change
