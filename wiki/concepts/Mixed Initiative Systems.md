## Definition

Mixed initiative systems are UX designs where AI and humans collaborate fluidly, with the AI surfacing help contextually while the human retains control. The AI can suggest actions, but the human can override, adjust, or ignore without disrupting the AI's assistance.

## Key Information

- Alexander Imbiricos cites Tesla's self-driving as a master class in mixed initiative: the car drives itself, but you can accelerate, turn a knob to change speed, or steer slightly without turning off self-driving
- The goal is to make the human feel "maximally empowered" and "maximally in control" while getting a lot of help
- This is contrasted with push notifications — getting thousands of AI notifications per day would be annoying; instead, the AI should surface help contextually when you're already looking at the relevant thing
- The Atlas browser is designed as a mixed initiative system: AI surfaces contextual actions at the moment they're helpful, like pressing X near an object in a video game
- IDE auto-completion is cited as one of the most successful AI products today because when it's right you're accelerated, and when it's wrong it's not that annoying
- The concept is central to OpenAI's product philosophy under Nick Turley's mantra "are we maximally accelerated?"
