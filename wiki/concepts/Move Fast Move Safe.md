## Definition

[[Applied Intuition]]'s "speed" value, per [[Qasar Younis]]: managers are formally assessed, compensated, and promoted specifically on the behavior of decisiveness under this value — holding openness to being wrong in tension with the need to act once a decision is made.

## Key Information

- Younis frames good decision culture as walking "a very thin line" between two conflicting imperatives: be humble enough to listen when someone says "we're 5 degrees off course," but also make decisions quickly once enough information is in and commit to them fully.
- Contrasts a company with too much unchecked momentum (illustrated by Google failing to compete with early Facebook — "how does a gorilla learn to fly? by not being a gorilla") against a company too indecisive/conflict-averse to ever correct course.
- Presented as the practical, tactical counterpart to [[Best Idea Wins Culture]]: openness during debate, decisiveness after.
