## Definition

Luck Surface Area is the concept that you can increase the probability of fortunate outcomes by being curious, maintaining relationships, and putting yourself and your work out there. Rahul Pandey believes much of his career success was luck, but that luck can be "manufactured" through deliberate actions that expand your exposure to opportunities.

## Key Information

### How Rahul Manufactures Luck
- Keeping in touch with people: being friendly, asking what they're up to, offering to help with projects
- This has career value because "you're able to get exposure to projects, people, ideas that you wouldn't know otherwise"
- His debug tool at Meta came from talking to people and understanding their workflow
- His job hops came from being in the know about what respected people were doing

### Social Media as Luck Surface Area Expansion
- Posting content on social media is a way to "create luck"
- You put out content, people see it and resonate with it, and they'll think of you for opportunities
- It's a deliberate activity: "we probably spend many hours per week or per month thinking about what that will look like and how we can help people"

### The Repeatable Process
- This is a "repeatable algorithm or repeatable process that's not dependent on luck"
- The key inputs are: genuine curiosity about people, keeping in touch, sharing what you're working on
- Even internal visibility at your company — writing, putting things out, reaching out to people — increases your luck surface area

### Luck and Failure
- Rahul described himself as having "failed my way into success" — being rejected from opportunities that would have been bad for him
- VC firms that strung Taro along and didn't invest would have created unhealthy pressure
- Jobs he was rejected from would have been at companies that "exploded" a year later
- This reframes rejection as potential luck in disguise

### Thariq Shihipar's Example
- His Anthropic job illustrates the concept: a ~1-month Good Fire interpretability visualization, shared publicly (his first big Twitter post, ~500 likes), drew DMs and led to an intro into a role at Anthropic.
- Advice: pick an interesting project, work hard, ship it and write about it — and repeat; Twitter is optional ("maybe it's Reddit or Hacker News"), but sharing work publicly is what matters.
- Real, novel work is the prerequisite: "there's not a shortcut to that… but everyone is always looking for interesting work and wants to support you."
