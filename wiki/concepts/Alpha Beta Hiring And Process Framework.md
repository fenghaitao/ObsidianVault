## Definition

A framework used by [[Matt MacInnis]] (CPO, [[Rippling]]), borrowed from financial-markets terminology, for deciding how much process to apply to a team, product area, or hire.

## Key Information

- **Alpha** = outperformance relative to a benchmark; in MacInnis's usage, a person or process that produces outsized upside, typically alongside higher volatility. Wanted in 0-to-1 product work or any exploratory problem where you're searching for an angle that beats the default solution.
- **Beta** = volatility/unpredictability, generally undesirable in a financial asset; in MacInnis's usage, a person or process that produces reliable, low-variance, predictable output. Wanted in mature, must-not-fail systems — his example is Rippling's payroll product, where the company "badly wants" zero unpredictability.
- **Process exists solely to lower beta** — its fundamental cost is that it also suppresses alpha. The leadership skill is applying "just a touch" of process where low beta is wanted, and a "very clear rigid process" where beta must be crushed, while deliberately avoiding process in places that need alpha (creativity, risk-taking, latitude).
- Applied to hiring: MacInnis uses it alongside [[SPOTAK Hiring Framework]] to decide not just whether a candidate is good, but whether they're the right *kind* of good for a given role — e.g., a valuable, high-upside "high alpha" candidate may be the wrong fit for a low-beta payroll-stability role.
- Example given of an extreme, deliberately-tolerated high-alpha hire: "Dennis Rodman" types — difficult but very high-value, with "room on every team for one."
- Rippling's product-quality checklist, "[[The Pickle (Product Quality List)]]," is presented as a concrete instantiation of a lightweight, iterative low-beta-inducing process designed to minimize alpha suppression.
