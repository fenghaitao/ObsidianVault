# Listening to Naysayers

A leadership principle practiced at [[Applied Intuition]]: creating a culture where the best idea wins, regardless of who proposes it, and where everyone must speak up.

## Core Idea

"We're trying to find the best idea and then let's go full bore at that best idea. Why do companies fail when they have great talent? It's because maybe the best ideas are not being surfaced."

## Key Points
- The ideal culture: put in lots of competing ideas, shake them out, no emotion attached — the best idea wins
- Companies fail because momentum in one direction overwhelms signals that the market is changing
- Google vs. Facebook example: Google had the best engineers, $1B/month cash flow, 15-20x Facebook's headcount, but couldn't win social because "a gorilla can't learn to fly by not being a gorilla"
- Even small companies can fail this way: a founder's view is just 10° off from the correct path, and the whole company is led astray
- "Everyone has to speak up because that one person with their one experience... that one idea they have in their head, they have to feel comfortable sharing it"
- Applied Intuition specifically says "Speak up" — not "Disagree with everything"
- The culture is "so scared of failing and so scared of losing that I will humble myself and listen to other people"
- Once the debate is done, execute decisively — "Move fast, move safe"
