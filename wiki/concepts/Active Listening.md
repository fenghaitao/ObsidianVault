## Definition

Active listening is a three-level framework for tuning into what someone is communicating. Level 1 is internal listening (distracted, thinking about implications for oneself), Level 2 is focused listening (can repeat back words), and Level 3 is global listening (hearing beneath the words — emotions, body language, tone, context). Great leaders use Level 3 listening when coaching, influencing, selling, and pitching vision.

## Key Information

- **Level 1 — Internal Listening**: Completely distracted with your own inner dialogue, thinking about the implications of what's being said on you. Most people go through their world rushed and in Level 1.
- **Level 2 — Focused Listening**: You can repeat back what the person is saying. You're focused on their words. This is typical in a good one-on-one.
- **Level 3 — Global Listening**: You hear beneath the words — what they're communicating beyond just what they're saying. You notice body language, tone of voice, context, and can reflect back insight the person may not be aware of.
- Quote from Fight Club: "Most people aren't listening. They're just waiting for their turn to talk." This is rampant in tech.
- Active listening doesn't require hour-long coaching sessions — even in the time you're already spending, giving full attention to notice feelings under the words goes a long way.
- There's power in synthesizing what you hear verbally (focus listening) and then mirroring back the emotions you notice (global listening).
- You can phrase it as a guess: "Is that right?" — giving the person space to correct you.
