## Definition

[[Kiriti Badam]]'s (and [[Aishwarya Naresh Reganti]]'s) term for deliberately anchoring AI product work on the underlying business problem rather than on the complexity or novelty of the AI solution — the discipline of asking "what is the problem I'm solving?" before "what agent/model/tool should I use?"

## Key Information

- Badam names the "slippery slope" it guards against directly: "in all this advancement of the AI... one easy slippery slope is to keep thinking about complexities of the solution and forget the problem that you're trying to solve."
- Starting small with low agency (see [[Agency Control Trade-off]]) is presented as a forcing function for problem-first thinking: constrained scope makes it obvious what problem is actually being addressed, whereas jumping straight to a fully autonomous, all-tools-and-context agent obscures the underlying problem behind solution complexity.
- Reganti's own closing advice echoes it directly: "be obsessed with your customers, be obsessed with the problem... AI is just a tool"; she claims 80% of effective "AI engineers/AI PMs" spend their time deeply understanding workflows and data rather than building the fanciest models — "look at your data" is presented as a perennial, still-underrated discipline even for engineers new to AI.
- Their Maven course on building enterprise AI products is explicitly framed as teaching "unlearning mindsets and following a problem-first approach instead of a tool-first or hype-first approach" — see [[Maven]].
- Connects to the "[[AI Product Success Triangle]]" technical pillar: successful technical teams are "obsessed about understanding their workflows very well," choosing the right tool (ML model, deterministic code, or LLM) per sub-task rather than reaching for an agent by default.
- Reganti separately calls jumping across tools/chasing the newest AI tool "overrated" relative to obsessing over the business problem, arguing that as implementation gets cheaper, design and judgment become the scarcer, more valuable skill (echoing [[Taste]]).
