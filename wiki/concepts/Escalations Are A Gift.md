## Definition

A stance held by [[Matt MacInnis]] (CPO, [[Rippling]]): withholding feedback or an escalation to avoid inconveniencing someone is selfish, not considerate, and escalations should always be treated as gifts rather than burdens.

## Key Information

- Core claim: "the most selfish thing you can do is withhold feedback from someone" — when you notice something that would help someone improve and don't say it because it would make you (not them) uncomfortable, you're optimizing for your own comfort, which MacInnis calls fundamentally selfish.
- Applies this to customers as much as to internal teams: he observes that customers, and even founders of companies he's personally invested in, are reluctant to escalate problems to him directly because they don't want to bother him — which he says is backwards, since surfacing and fixing problems is literally his job as a product executive.
- Rippling runs a dedicated "escalations team" whose specific skill is pushing past superficial fixes ("we fixed the data error and closed the ticket") to trace issues to true root causes — finding not just the bug, but the system that produced the bug, and the system that produced that system.
- MacInnis personally reviews product feedback in public channels (rather than DMs) so that other product/engineering managers can see how issues get resolved across teams, deliberately modeling the behavior he wants to spread.
- Connects to his broader argument (see [[Compounding Plus Power Law Plus Entropy]]) that feedback withholding is one more form of local-comfort optimization that leaders must actively counteract.
