## Definition

"Tilting your umbrella" is a metaphor for small acts of courtesy and empathy in product design that create emotional connections and competitive advantage. It originated from an observation Stewart Butterfield and Brandon Velestuk made while walking in Vancouver.

## Key Information

- **Origin**: walking in Vancouver's Yaletown in the rain, Butterfield and Velestuk noticed how few people would tilt their umbrellas out of the way on narrow sidewalks. Only about 1/3 of people did it
- **Three explanations for why people don't tilt**:
  1. They exercise power through domination (tiny percentage)
  2. They see the problem but don't think they can do anything about it
  3. They simply don't notice — oblivious to their impact on others
- **Reinterpretation of Bezos**: "Your margin is my opportunity" → "Tilting your umbrella is our opportunity"
- **Applied to Slack**: the little conveniences and courtesies caused people to form emotional connections with the product
- **Growth impact**: a lot of Slack's growth came from cross-pollination — someone left a startup that used Slack, joined one that didn't, and advocated passionately for it because of those emotional connections
- Became part of Slack's new hire welcome and internal culture
