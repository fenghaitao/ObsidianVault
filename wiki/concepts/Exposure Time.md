## Definition

"Exposure time" (or "exposure hours") is the practice — described by [[Lazar]] and attributed by him to an earlier [[Lenny's Podcast]] guest — of deliberately spending time exposed to great work (designs, products, onboarding flows, people) as the primary way to build [[Taste]] and judgment, as distinct from spending that time building or coding.

## Key Information

- Lazar's framing: clarity and taste are developed largely through "exposure" — deliberately exposing yourself to content, people, and relationships that help you level up in a domain — rather than through study or specification alone.
- Concrete practice: following excellent designers on X/social media, seeking out tools/products where great design is produced, and following their creators directly (Lazar cites following a designer who posts 40-50 minute videos of himself designing, specifically to watch how a world-class designer talks to and prompts AI tools).
- Lazar's own account of developing taste: he didn't know terms like "Bauhaus" or "glass morphism" before deliberately exposing himself to them — he even vibe-coded a small reference app cataloguing different UI/design styles and prompts to replicate them, to teach himself.
- Framed as complementary to, not a replacement for, building: Lazar's explicit advice is "set aside more time on learning than building" — but learning itself happens partly by building and testing many projects, not only by passive consumption.
- Directly tied to reading AI agent output rather than code: Lazar treats the agent's natural-language reasoning/output as one of the most valuable things to be "exposed to," since (per his framing, echoing a quote he can't fully attribute) "the ceiling on the AI isn't the model intelligence, it's what the model sees before it acts."
