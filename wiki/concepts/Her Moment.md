## Definition
The "Her moment" is the voice AI industry's shorthand for achieving truly natural, human-like voice interaction — as depicted in the 2013 film "Her" where the protagonist develops a relationship with an AI voice assistant named Samantha. Neil Zeghidour calls it "the most overused, most annoying analogy in the field" while acknowledging it remains extremely relevant as a benchmark for what voice AI should aspire to.

## Key Information
- Originates from Spike Jonze's 2013 film "Her" starring Joaquin Phoenix and Scarlett Johansson (as Samantha)
- In the film, the AI: understands paralinguistic cues (tone, discomfort), performs complex tasks (sorting thousands of emails instantly), and engages in natural full-duplex conversation
- The term has been overused on Twitter and in demos — many claimed "Her moments" are far from the real thing
- What's still missing: true full-duplex conversation, paralinguistic understanding, instant tool execution, always-on availability, natural turn-taking
- Neil Zeghidour argues we know how to make natural-sounding models (Moshi proved it), but they lack intelligence, reliability, and tool-calling
- The path forward requires solving both the interaction quality (full duplex, back-channeling) and the intelligence gap (tool calling, reliability, personalization)
- Cost and privacy are additional barriers — the "Her" vision implies always-on, always-available AI
- Neil's conclusion: voice is not a commodity, and the last mile to the Her moment is the most difficult
