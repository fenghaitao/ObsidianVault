## Definition

Counterfactuals are the answer to "what if I had done otherwise?" queries that take the observed outcome into account — the third and highest level of Judea Pearl's Ladder of Causation, which he calls "explanation."

## Key Information

- Example: "I am 80 years old, alive and alert, and smoke five packs a day — what if I had not smoked?"
- They differ from interventions because you already have information about the outcome, which reveals things about the subject's metabolism and anatomy you did not know before.
- Answering them requires different, stronger assumptions and techniques than the intervention level.
- Pearl describes them as "creative retrospection" and notes finite-sample uncertainty complicates even the first level.
