## Definition
"Software fundamentals matter more than ever" is the core thesis of Matt Pocock's talk at aiDotEngineer. It argues that classic software engineering principles — from books like A Philosophy of Software Design, The Pragmatic Programmer, and Domain-Driven Design — are more critical in the age of AI coding than they were before, because AI amplifies both good and bad codebases.

## Key Information
- Articulated by Matt Pocock at aiDotEngineer conference
- The thesis: AI does really well in good codebases but poorly in bad ones, so codebase quality matters more than ever
- Good codebases are easy to change; bad codebases are hard to change (John Ousterhout's definition of complexity)
- AI amplifies the effects of codebase quality: it can be incredibly productive in well-structured code but produces slop in poorly structured code
- The specs-to-code movement is dangerous because it divests from system design
- Five practical tips derived from classic software engineering books:
  1. Reach shared design concepts with AI (Grill Me skill)
  2. Establish a ubiquitous language with AI
  3. Use TDD to force small deliberate steps
  4. Structure codebases with deep modules
  5. Design interfaces, delegate implementations to AI
- The engineer's role shifts from tactical programmer to strategic designer
- AI is a great tactical programmer (sergeant on the ground); humans must be the strategic thinkers above
- This requires software fundamental skills that have been used for 20+ years
