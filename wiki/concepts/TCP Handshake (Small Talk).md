## Definition

A metaphor for small talk: like the TCP/IP handshake in networking, small talk is the protocol that establishes a connection between two people before they can communicate meaningfully. It's the "modem crash" from the dial-up era — the noise made while two systems find the right wavelength.

## Key Information

- Lenny references someone on Twitter describing small talk as "the TCP/IP act handshake"
- Sam Lessin: "I always describe it as the modem crash from when we were kids on a 144 [baud modem]. That's what small talk is. It's a modem crash. We're trying to hit the wavelengths."
- Small talk may feel transparent ("the weather is nice," "how was your weekend"), but it's still useful
- It signals: "I am here for business but I'm a normal person, and I'm willing to have a normal conversation"
- The purpose: get both parties ready to "really communicate"
- Sam recommends starting meetings with a little small talk before flipping into business
