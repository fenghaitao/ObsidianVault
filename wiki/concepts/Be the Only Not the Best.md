## Definition

"Be the only, not the best" is Ilya Grigorik's career strategy: instead of optimizing to be the top specialist in a single domain, build a portfolio of 80–90% competence across many skills so that the unique intersection becomes your differentiator and source of resilience.

## Key Information

- Grigorik's central advice: "A better way, a more resilient way to build your career is to optimize for being the only person as opposed to the best person"
- Being "the best" is fragile: the field can change, or your company may not need that specific skill
- Being "the only" is "literally probability math": the product of many skill-likelihoods is rare, so a wide toolkit makes you unique and versatile
- You can reach ~80% competence in a domain in days-to-weeks (and ~90% in a year), especially with LLMs accelerating context acquisition
- In practice this means acting as a "translator" between engineering, marketing, PR, design, and business — not being the most knowledgeable person, but knowing how to find and leverage the most knowledgeable people
- Grigorik's example: he was "a mediocre designer, a mediocre salesperson, okay at support," but the combination ("sling together a website, know Photoshop enough, host it, secure it in two days") is what others can't replicate
