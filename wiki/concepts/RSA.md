## Definition

RSA is the first public key cryptosystem, introduced by Ron Rivest, Adi Shamir, and Leonard Adleman, using modular arithmetic modulo a composite number N.

## Key Information

- Martin Hellman and Stephen Pohlig (transcribed in the episode as just "Steve") had a closely related earlier system modulo a prime P; RSA generalized the idea to modulo a composite N.
- In RSA the public and secret keys invert one another regardless of the order applied, enabling both encryption and digital signatures.
- Patent war: Hellman and Diffie's Stanford patent was written by a law-school intern (not a patent attorney) and relied on claims that did not cover RSA; MIT/Stanford's fight was won by RSA Data Security, which sold for $250 million while Hellman made virtually nothing. Hellman notes that only patent claims matter, and a properly written claim could have covered RSA.
- Around 2000 Hellman reconciled with RSA Data Security president Jim Bidzos ("friends are better than enemies") and suspects Ron Rivest may have nominated Diffie and Hellman for the Turing Award.
