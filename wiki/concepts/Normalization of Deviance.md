## Definition

Normalization of deviance is a sociological concept that emerged from the investigation of the Space Shuttle Challenger disaster (1986). It describes how organizations gradually accept increasingly risky behavior as normal when each instance of risky behavior doesn't result in catastrophe. Simon Wilson applies this concept to the AI industry's approach to prompt injection and AI safety.

## Key Information

- Originated from a research paper published after the 1986 Challenger disaster
- "Lots of people knew that those little O-rings were unreliable, but every single time you get away with launching a space shuttle without the O-rings failing, you institutionally feel more confident in what you're doing"
- Applied to AI: "We've been working increasingly unreliably with these systems... and we've been using these systems in increasingly unsafe ways, and so far there hasn't been a headline-grabbing story of a prompt injection... which means that we keep on taking risks"
- The mechanism: each successful (but risky) use of AI agents builds institutional confidence, leading to more risk-taking
- Simon Wilson: "We have this normalization of deviance in the field of AI around how we're using these tools"
