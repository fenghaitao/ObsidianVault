## Definition

"Feelings overpowering skills" is Dr. Becky Kennedy's framework for understanding bad behavior: bad behavior at any age can basically be reduced to feelings that overpower skills. Behavior is not the core problem — it's a manifestation of the problem. The actual problem is that someone doesn't have the skill they need to manage something happening internally.

## Key Information

- "Kids are born with all the feelings and none of the skills to manage feelings."
- "Bad behavior at any age can basically be reduced to feelings that overpower skills."
- "Behavior is a problem, but behavior isn't the core problem. It's a manifestation of the problem. The actual problem is someone doesn't have the skill they need to manage something happening internally."
- "If they had that skill and could access it, the behavior would change. But that would be the outcome, not the place of actual intervention."
- When a kid hits: they're out of control. Maybe tired, angry that a sibling has a toy, jealous — and don't have skills to manage those emotions
- The coaching lens: "Nobody learns new skills by being sent to their room. Nobody learns new skills by adding shame. All that does is increase the gap between feelings and skills."
- Swimming analogy: "If you paid for swim lessons and the person said 'I'm just not going to put up with this. Send them to their room and tell them to come back when they learn how to swim' — it's absurd."
- "An even more important life skill [than swimming] is learning how to manage your emotions."
- The approach: set boundaries around out-of-control behavior, then teach the missing skills
- This approach changes behavior both short-term AND long-term, unlike punishment
- "Kids who hit, no matter what they say, they really don't like feeling out of control."
- A four-year-old throwing isn't making a calculated decision: "I don't think most of us are in a decision-making place when we escalate."
