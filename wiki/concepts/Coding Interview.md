## Definition

The Coding Interview is the technical portion of big tech interviews where candidates solve algorithmic and data structure problems, typically on a whiteboard or in a shared coding environment. Steve Huynh describes it as "necessary but not sufficient" — the "small blind" of the interview process.

## Key Information

- Steve uses a poker analogy: coding is the "small blind" of getting into a company — necessary but not sufficient
- The interview prep industry hyper-focuses on coding, with platforms like LeetCode providing automated test case evaluation
- When Steve was preparing for his SDE transition in the mid-2000s, no such platforms existed — he manually collected interview questions and generated his own test cases
- The competition in the job market exists at the pre-qualification/resume stage, not at the coding interview level — by the time you reach the interview, it's just about clearing the bar
- Steve's advice: go deep rather than broad — instead of surface-level tutorials in many languages, understand one language deeply (JVM internals, garbage collection, etc.)
