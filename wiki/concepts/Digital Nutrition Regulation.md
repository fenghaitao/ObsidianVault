## Definition

Tony Fadell's analogy calling for platforms and product designers to regulate/label digital consumption the way physical food is regulated and labeled, to counter addictive design in phones, social media, and AI chatbots.

## Key Information

- Core analogy: "we have lots of junk food and we have an obese nation... because of all the junk food"; digital products create the same over-consumption dynamic ("digital food") but "doesn't have the nutrition labels, doesn't have the warnings, doesn't have the regulation" that physical food does.
- Fadell frames the iPhone itself as neutral ("iPhone's just a refrigerator... you can put in junk food or you can put in good food") — the unintended consequence was social media's exploitation of the platform, not Apple's original intent.
- Calls on platform companies (Google, Apple) to do more around digital-consumption tools and information to help people (and families) make better decisions, similar to age restrictions on other regulated goods.
- Extends the concern to AI companionship products: warns against "turning personal connection into a product with AI chatbots" in ways that "devolve social interaction," arguing this trades long-term human/societal wellbeing for short-term engagement gain.
- Frames this as a leadership/ethics responsibility for product designers specifically — citing Steve Jobs' refusal to add adult content to the iTunes Store as an example of a leader drawing a clear ethical line despite commercial pressure.
- Explicit caveat: Fadell says he isn't arguing for paternalistic "nanny" regulation or imposing a particular moral code, just for balance, tools, and information that current platforms mostly lack.
