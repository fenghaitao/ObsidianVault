## Definition

The product development lifecycle is the end-to-end process of taking a product idea from insight through research, specification, design, coding, launch, and iteration. At scale, this process has become bloated with sub-steps, reviews, and specialized roles.

## Key Information

- Tomer Cohen describes the basic lifecycle as: research the problem, spec it out, design it, code it, launch it, iterate.
- At large companies like LinkedIn, each step expanded into many sub-steps: research alone involves 10-15 sources of information (data pulls, customer interviews, feedback tickets, social media).
- Reviews multiplied: design reviews, privacy reviews, security reviews, and many more — each with a valid reason to exist but collectively creating massive overhead.
- The result: small features require multiple teams, multiple codebases, and multiple sprints just to launch, not including iteration where success actually happens.
- The full-stack builder model aims to collapse this lifecycle back to its essence, automating everything except vision, empathy, communication, creativity, and judgment.
- Cohen splits the lifecycle into two phases: "idea to design" and "code to launch" — the latter has received more AI investment, while the former is where LinkedIn's current agent work is focused.
