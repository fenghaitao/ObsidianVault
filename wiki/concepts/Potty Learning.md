## Definition

Potty learning (as opposed to potty training) reframes the process as skill acquisition rather than behavior training. Just as you teach a child the alphabet, you teach them the steps of using the toilet. This framing parallels workplace skill development: people don't need to be "trained" — they need to be taught skills they're missing.

## Key Information

- "Potty learning, not potty training... You're not training them to do a thing. You're just they don't know how to do it. So, you just teach them like you're teaching them the alphabet."
- Dr. Becky has a free potty learning course at goodinside.com (Google "good inside potty")
- "Potty stuff is like one of the early lessons around body autonomy and skills and the quickest way we kind of mess it up as parents is kind of trying to take over the process."
- When kids are young (around 2), they control two things: what goes into their body and what comes out. This is why potty training can become heated — it's one of the few domains they control
- "When kids even smell: my parent is stepping into one of the only two domains that are mine, holy moly, will they do nutty things to push you back."
- Workplace parallel: "What is it like for someone to have not that many areas of life that they're in control of?"
- At work: when someone reacts strongly to a small change (like switching a meeting time), it might be about themes of control and independence, not the meeting time itself
- "What's the general lesson? A human lesson is what is it like for someone to have not that many areas of life that they're in control of?"
