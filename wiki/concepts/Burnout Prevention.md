## Definition

Burnout prevention centers on the idea that people have more energy when operating from their natural gifts and strengths. By identifying what energizes vs. depletes you, you can design your career and life so that roughly 80% of your time is spent in your zone of genius, dramatically increasing your energy and impact.

## Key Information

- When people are in their gifts and strengths most of the time, they have more energy. We all have more energy when operating from things we naturally are good at and innately love doing.
- Target: 80% of your time in your gifts (aspiration). You'll always have ~20% of things you don't love doing.
- **Two-week energy audit**: Every night, reflect on the 5 things that gave you the most energy and the 5 things that depleted your energy the most. After two weeks, look for patterns.
- Alternative methods: Ask 5-10 people in your life "When I walk in the room, what shows up? What are my strengths? What are my gifts?"; review your calendar and note themes of what excites vs. what you dread.
- It's no one else's job to help you live in your gifts. Your manager's job is to help you perform in the job you were hired to do. It's your job to navigate your career.
- Start small: Stop going to optional things that exhaust you. Leave space between depleting commitments. Read 30 minutes before bed. These small changes plug the "slow leak in your gas tank."
- Founders and technical leaders often start solving a problem they're obsessed with, then end up managing a board and team — a completely different job that doesn't play to their strengths.
- Examples: Rahul Vora (Superhuman) hired a president for operations; Dharmesh Shah (HubSpot) has zero direct reports as CTO; Rachel Lockett herself moved from PM to coaching.
- The key is to match your gifts with the world's needs — over the arc of your career, not just in your current role.
- Tell your manager and people around you what your gifts are so they can help you apply them to business needs.
