## Definition

Cancellation Diagnosis is Jason Cohen's approach to understanding why customers cancel, centered on three principles: (1) ask "What made you cancel?" not "Why did you cancel?", (2) never accept the first answer (especially "too expensive"), and (3) catch customers before they cancel, not after.

## Key Principles

### 1. The Question Phrasing Matters

- **"Why did you cancel?"** → 10% usable responses (Groove case study)
- **"What made you cancel?"** → 20% usable responses
- The shift encourages specific, actionable answers instead of generic ones (budget, project ended)
- Multiple-choice surveys are complete noise — randomize the list and all options are picked equally (Jason's own experience at Smart Bear)

### 2. Never Accept the First Answer

- **"Too expensive"** is almost never the real reason. The customer already saw the pricing page and bought — they decided it wasn't too expensive. Something else happened (didn't fulfill the promise, missing integration, poor experience).
- **"Project ended"** may be true, but: would the project have ended if the product was more successful? Did you pick the wrong market segment where projects naturally end more often?
- Think of it like cause of death: "stopped breathing" is the proximate cause, but the real question is why they stopped breathing (undiagnosed diabetes, car accident, etc.)

### 3. Root Cause Analysis is a Fallacy

Complex systems don't have one root cause. Instead of five whys looking for a single answer, look for an array of contributing factors that could each be addressed. The goal is a set of actionable improvements, not one magical root cause.

### 4. Catch Them Before They Cancel

- Look for signals of trouble: never uploaded data, too many support calls, too few support calls, no login for a while, not engaged
- Even without data, you can guess, form a theory, act on it, and adjust as you get more data
- Once they've canceled, they've mentally disinvested and won't give good feedback

## Using AI for Analysis

- LLMs are good at summarization, themes, topics (averaging tasks)
- LLMs are bad at picking out interesting, non-average details that trigger actionable insights
- Best approach: use AI for themes, then ask it to extract every specific detail under each theme with customer attribution — then have a human read the details
