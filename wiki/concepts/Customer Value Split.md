## Definition

Customer Value Split is Jason Cohen's framing for how to think about NRR and pricing: first create more value for the customer, then figure out how to split that additional value with them. This keeps the customer's interests front and center while still capturing revenue for the business.

## Core Principles

1. **Create value first, then split.** Don't start with "how can we charge more?" Start with "how can we deliver more value?" Once you've earned the right to take a piece, decide how to split.

2. **The customer should feel they're getting far more value than the price increase.** If the price doubles, they should feel like they're getting 5x the value. This should be the feeling whether or not they can measure it precisely.

3. **Measure what the customer values, not just your internal metrics.** Usage metrics are easy to measure but may not reflect whether the customer perceives value. Ideally, find a metric that directly measures customer value. If not possible, use proxy metrics or qualitative measures.

4. **Not all important things are numbers.** Differentiation in the market, customer satisfaction, and perceived value may not be quantifiable but are critically important.

## The NRR Connection

NRR works when customers willingly pay more because they're getting more value. If customers feel the price is growing faster than the value, they start looking for alternatives — everyone has experienced this with products they once loved.

## The Litmus Test

When anyone proposes a pricing change, ask: "Is this actually good for the customer?" If the answer is no, the equation is incomplete — it needs to be better for both the company AND the customer.
