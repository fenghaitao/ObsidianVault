## Definition

The "I believe you" + "I believe in you" framework is a two-part communication approach for supporting someone through difficulty. "I believe you" is validation — one foot in the hole with them. "I believe in you" is seeing a more capable version of them than they can see in the moment — one foot out of the hole. Both are necessary; neither alone is sufficient.

## Key Information

- Visual metaphor: someone struggling is in a hole. They can't see the way out. They need someone with one foot in the hole (validation) and one foot out (seeing their capability).
- "I believe you" examples: "I believe you. You really don't want to go to soccer practice. You're no longer starting on the team. The first soccer practice after you lose your starting spot sucks. Totally sucks. I believe you."
- "I believe in you" examples: "Look, you're going to go to practice today. It's not going to be fun. You're going to feel embarrassed. And you're going to come home having survived. I'm going to be there for a hug. I'm going to let you know I'm proud of you."
- At work: "I believe you. This is a hard project. You're totally right. You've never done something like this before. It makes sense you're nervous. And the reason we think you can take on this project is because we know you can figure this out."
- "I'd be nervous if you weren't nervous" — normalizing the feeling
- "It makes sense" — a powerful phrase for validation
- One of the ways we've misunderstood anxiety is doing the "I believe you" part but forgetting the "I believe in you" part
- Reflect on which end of the spectrum you're naturally on: "Is the I believe you part easier for me or is the I believe in you part easier for me?" — Knowing where you are gives insight into where you can grow
- Lenny used this framework with his wife and it was "so good and so effective"
- "Doing those two things at once is like it's magic"
