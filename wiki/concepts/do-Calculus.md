## Definition

The do-calculus is Judea Pearl's symbolic calculus for reasoning about interventions — the `do` operator that "forces" a variable — replacing the symmetric, reversible algebra that cannot express causal direction.

## Key Information

- Motivated by algebra's limitation: equations with a symmetric equality sign can be inverted (F = ma implies m = F/a), which a "stupid robot" might misread as allowing it to change the weather by fiddling with a barometer.
- Borrows from computer science the non-reversible assignment operation to give causality its needed directionality.
- Distinguishes intervention ("forcing" a variable, e.g., making someone smoke five packs a day) from passive observation used by ordinary statistics.
- Answering intervention-level queries requires experiments or causal assumptions, which the do-calculus manipulates symbolically.
