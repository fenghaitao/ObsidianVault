## Definition

Statistics is a field that "fundamentally sells people the idea that they can use statistical methods in real life" while actually doing pure mathematics about how those methods behave in an idealized world. John Myles White argues that statistical rigor is often missing in A/B test reviews.

## Key Information

- White's framing: statistics is "super crazy" because you reason in mathematics but make claims about usefulness in practice, so opinions about honesty/rigor vary widely.
- Book recommendations: Larry Wasserman ("hyper intense honesty," read anything by him) and "Introduction to Agnostic Statistics" (author transcribed as "Peter Eranell"/"Peter Arena"; less math-heavy, good for social scientists and practitioners).
- In A/B test review meetings, many people see a "green bar" and ask for approval without digging into why; their understanding is superficial.
- The "almost significant" pathology: a team blocked Deltoid 3 because a holdout flipped from a barely-significant win to not significant; White saw treating "barely significant" as a win as dangerous culture.
- Goal pathology: a team planned to ship code on July 2nd and delete it the next day "because we made a goal" — White argues goals should not be written so shipping something you intend to delete counts as success.
