## Definition

The Waterline Model is a diagnostic framework by Molly Graham for diagnosing organizational and performance problems. It teaches leaders to start at the top of the "waterline" — goals, roles, and clarity of expectations — before assuming the person is the problem. Graham estimates 80% of problems in companies are structural, not personal.

## Key Principles

- When you have a problem with a team member, many people jump to "the people are the problem" — but most often the issue is the situation, not the person
- Start at the top: does the person actually know what you expect of them? Are the goals clear? Is the role clear? Do they know what success looks like?
- If goals aren't clear, that's a structural issue — "How can someone show up and decide what they're going to do with their day all day if the goals aren't clear?"
- "Just literally right there you got like probably 80% of problems inside of companies" — goals, roles, and clarity of expectations
- Graham recommends a two-way dialogue approach: "Here's what I'm seeing. Tell me what's going on for you. What do you think your job is?"
- Often the person's picture is different from your picture — "you described an elephant and they spat out a tiger"
- Re-clarifying roles and expectations, redescribing the elephant over and over, is one of the hardest parts of leadership — "you feel like a broken record"
- "Turns out no one heard you the first 43 [times] and you have to redescribe it in order for people to hear you"
