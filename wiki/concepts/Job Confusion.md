## Definition

Job confusion is when a parent (or leader) asks their child (or subordinate) to do their job for them because they don't want to deal with the emotional fallout. It results in frustration for everyone. The parent/leader should set the boundary rather than making a request and then getting frustrated when the request isn't honored.

## Key Information

- "Over and over in parenting, maybe at work too, we're asking our kid to do our job for us because we just don't want to deal with the emotional fallout."
- Example: "Don't press the buttons. If you press the buttons, no dessert tonight." — This is job confusion: the parent is asking the child to enforce the boundary, then threatening punishment when they don't
- The honest truth: "At night I'm going to give my kid sorbet anyway and then make up something how sorbet isn't dessert because it has fruit. All because I just didn't set a boundary in the first place."
- Job confusion "makes everyone frustrated"
- The solution is to set a boundary (what YOU will do) rather than making a request (what you're asking THEM to do)
- Connects to boundaries: "I'm going to stand between you and the buttons" (boundary) vs. "Don't press the buttons" (request, leading to job confusion)
