## Definition

[[Dr. Becky Kennedy]]'s term for the failure mode where a parent or leader implicitly asks the other person to do their job for them — i.e., to spare them the discomfort of holding a boundary themselves — rather than doing the emotional labor of setting and holding it.

## Key Information

- Kennedy's example: a parent says "don't press the elevator buttons or no dessert tonight" instead of setting a true boundary (physically standing between the child and the buttons). The threatened consequence is rarely enforced (she admits she gives her kid dessert anyway and rationalizes it), which means the parent was actually relying on the *child's* compliance to avoid an uncomfortable moment — asking the child to do the parent's job of holding the line.
- Frames this as a common source of frustration in parenting ("maybe at work too"): "we're asking our kid to do our job for us because we just don't want to deal with the emotional fallout" — leading to inconsistent limits, undermined authority, and a parent/leader who feels like their boundaries "aren't respected" when in fact no real boundary was set (see [[Boundaries Vs Requests]]).
- The fix is the same as for [[Boundaries Vs Requests]]: state and enact what *you* will do, independent of the other person's cooperation, rather than issuing a request with an unenforced threat attached.
