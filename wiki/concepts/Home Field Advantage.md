## Definition

[[Jacob Warwick]]'s negotiation tactic: deliberately choose the meeting environment and time that puts you at your best, since more deals close outside a formal boardroom setting (a walk, a coffee, a lunch, a golf outing) than inside one, and a comfortable, non-adversarial setting produces better outcomes for both sides.

## Key Information

- Concrete example: OpenAI executives reportedly took interview conversations outside the office and walked around downtown San Francisco with candidates rather than meeting in a chaotic startup office — physically removing the "us across a table" adversarial framing in favor of a side-by-side, collaborative posture.
- Also covers controlling meeting *time*, not just place: Warwick advises declining meetings scheduled during your personally weakest hours (e.g. very early morning) and instead proposing times when you perform best — itself a small, safe way to signal you won't be endlessly pushed around.
- More broadly available to senior/executive candidates (who can more easily suggest "let's get a drink" or "let's take a walk") than to more junior candidates who may have less latitude to reject a standard office interview, though Warwick still encourages junior candidates to try.
- Complements "[[Sell The Vacation]]" by removing friction/adversarial framing so the counterparty's guard is lower during the discovery/pitch process.
