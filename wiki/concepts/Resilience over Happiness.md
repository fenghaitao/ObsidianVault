## Definition

"Resilience over happiness" is Dr. Becky Kennedy's guiding parenting philosophy: optimizing for long-term capability over short-term comfort. "Optimizing for happiness in childhood is the quickest way to build anxiety and fragility in adulthood." The goal is to raise people who can say "this is hard and I can do hard things" rather than "this is hard, so someone must be at fault and it should be easier."

## Key Information

- "Optimizing for happiness in childhood is the quickest way to build anxiety and fragility in adulthood. Hard stop."
- Children's early years are about learning: "What range of experiences am I able to cope with?"
- Happiness in adulthood actually comes from the ability to manage the widest range of difficult situations — that's what resilience is
- "Happiness actually comes in adulthood from our ability to manage the widest range of difficult situations."
- When we jump in to fix discomfort for kids ("Oh, that's not true. But you're so good at soccer"), we teach them that discomfort is unmanageable
- "Our kids can only learn to tolerate the feelings we tolerate in them."
- "Nothing's scarier to a kid than when they're upset — if I can't tolerate their upset feelings, that makes them doubly upset because it's like, 'Oh, I guess this is unmanageable.'"
- At work: build a culture where people can say "this is hard and I can do hard things" — not "this is hard, so someone must be at fault"
- Kim Scott example: avoiding giving feedback for 9 months to avoid upsetting someone → having to fire them → they ask "Why didn't you tell me?"
- The earlier you optimize for comfort/happiness in a relationship, the more fragile the building (relationship) is going to be
- "I will not take that feeling away from you" — Dr. Becky tells her son about finishing a puzzle: "There is no better feeling in the world than watching yourself work on something and make progress on something and maybe even complete something that you originally didn't think you could do."
- "When we're thinking about a resilient work culture, we want people who can say this is hard and I can do hard things."
