## Definition

Chatter-driven development is a proposed software development paradigm coined by Alexander Imbiricos where work emerges organically from team communication (Slack, social media, meetings) rather than formal specifications. The AI agent monitors conversations, identifies work to be done, and proactively ships code.

## Key Information

- Coined on-the-spot by Alexander Imbiricos as an alternative to spec-driven development
- Inspired by how many teams already work: "they often don't necessarily have specs, but the team is just really self-driven and so stuff just gets done"
- The AI agent would monitor communication channels, identify what needs to be built or fixed, and take action
- Example: "here's the customer service channel, tell me what's interesting to know, but if it's a small bug, just fix it"
- The human doesn't need to write a spec unless they want to — they can choose their preferred level of abstraction
- This aligns with the vision of proactive agents that anticipate needs rather than waiting for prompts
- John G Donji (CTO of Block) described similar behavior with their Goose agent: it watches screens, listens to meetings, and proactively ships PRs
