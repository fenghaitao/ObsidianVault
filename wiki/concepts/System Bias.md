## Definition
System bias is James Cowling's term for an organization's tendency to keep investing in the system it already built rather than the problem it needs to solve — the team defends its creation instead of the mission.
## Key Information
- Examples Cowling gave: spending 6 months for a 10% performance gain that wasn't needed; forcing tooling on clients who don't need it; riding an outdated system "to the grave like the captain going down on the Titanic."
- The fix is to orient a team around the problem it solves, not the system it built — which is why he renamed the Magic Pocket team to the Storage team after shipping.
- If the Storage team concluded S3 was better, its job would be to advocate moving back — an exciting new thing to own, not a threat to identity.
- A diagnostic question to ask repeatedly: "If we could spend these resources on any project at the company right now, would this still be the best use of time?"
- Inertia is strong; talented people can spend years on projects they privately don't believe in, so leaders should gently surface "does this make sense?"
