## Definition

A mental model articulated by Matt McGinness that likens product-market fit to drug discovery: the market either has binding receptors for your product or it doesn't. The outcome is predetermined; you cannot market your way to product-market fit.

## Key Information

- The market is immutable — no amount of tweeting, LinkedIn posting, or advertising will change whether the market wants your product.
- Your startup is an experiment to discover whether binding receptors exist in the market. Fate has already decided the outcome.
- "Just as nobody at Genentech thinks they can market their way to better performance inside your body, you cannot market your way to product-market fit."
- If you ship and find lack of success, do not try to market your way through it — the binding receptors likely don't exist.
- This mindset is liberating: you just have to find the right drug (product), not try to convince the body (market) to develop receptors.
- Implication: launches don't matter much. "Big fat pull-the-slingshot-back launches amount to the teeniest thimble of water in the ocean of noise."
- Product-market fit is binary and obvious: "You absolutely know it when you see it. Therefore, if you don't absolutely know it, you don't have it."
- McGinness experienced this at Inkling: they thought they had PMF multiple times, but in hindsight (after experiencing true PMF at Rippling), it was clear they never did.
