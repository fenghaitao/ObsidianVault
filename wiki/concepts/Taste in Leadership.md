# Taste in Leadership

The idea, articulated by [[Qasar Younis]], that many Silicon Valley CEOs lack taste because they have had narrow life experiences. Broad exposure develops better judgment.

## Core Idea

"If your life experience is very narrow, you could still be good... but there's something about understanding humans and understanding life and then being able to discern with some judgment what is good and what's not good."

## Key Points
- Taste applies both in the artistic sense AND in practical business judgment (HR policies, product decisions, etc.)
- Many founders grow up in Cupertino, go to Berkeley, start a company straight out of school, and do nothing else for 20 years — never even being an employee
- Working in large organizations (100K+ employees) teaches you how bad it is to be an employee — essential knowledge for creating good policies and culture as a leader
- Qasar: "A bunch of founders who just never had the fortune of being at the bottom of the totem pole"
- Broader life experiences — backpacking, traveling, diverse reading — develop taste
- "There's some founders that would be good at lots and lots of things, not just being a founder of an AI company in the Bay Area"
- No peer-reviewed research proves this, but Qasar strongly believes it
- This connects to the value of reading old books, diverse inputs, and diverse experiences — like feeding an LLM diverse training data
