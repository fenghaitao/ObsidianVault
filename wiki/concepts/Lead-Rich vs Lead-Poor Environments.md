## Definition

A framework for understanding the bifurcated B2B market: lead-rich environments (AI-native companies with 50%+ of prospects in-market at once) vs. lead-poor environments (legacy B2B with evaporating demand). Both have incentives to adopt AI for GTM, but for opposite reasons — lead-rich companies can't service all their demand, while lead-poor companies need ruthless efficiency.

## Key Information

- AI-native companies are "lead-rich": 50%+ of prospects are in-market at once (vs. traditional 3-5%), creating unprecedented demand that even the fastest-growing companies can't fully service
- Example: Bolt closed a seven-figure deal "stolen from Lovable because no one called them back at Lovable" — even the hottest companies leave money on the table
- Legacy B2B is "lead-poor": demand has evaporated, growth has decelerated, and nothing seems to work — they need AI for ruthless efficiency
- Lead-rich companies need AI to service the massive inbound they can't touch manually
- Lead-poor companies need AI to do more with less and maintain productivity
- In lead-rich environments, reps naturally ignore leads that aren't worth their time (low commission, busy with bigger deals) — AI can fill these gaps
- SaaStr's Agentforce reactivation campaign: 70% response rate on leads human salespeople deemed "not worth their time"
- Even at scale, humans can only reach out to ~2,000 people from a database of 400K+ — AI can touch them all
- This bifurcation is a new phenomenon: pre-AI, all B2B companies grew similarly; now it's "wildly bifurcated"
