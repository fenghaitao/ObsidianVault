## Definition

Technical Learning refers to the acquisition of engineering knowledge and skills. Evan King identified his startup experience as the period of fastest technical learning, and regretted not being more inquisitive during his big tech years.

## Key Information

- Evan's fastest technical learning came during his startup experience: "everything pales in comparison to the startup journey from skill growth"
- The startup learning was "uncomfortable, stressful, emotional at times" — but this is how growth should feel
- Evan's biggest regret: not being "more inquisitive about how things actually worked" at Meta — he would "experiment, see numbers go up, and if they went up, success" without understanding the underlying technical cause
- In the last year (at Hello Interview), he has made the transition to deeper understanding: "now that it's not about career growth, that 30% can go to actually learning and understanding"
- He now reads engineering blogs from other companies, is inquisitive about libraries and platforms, and focuses on "how they work, why they work, how they were built"
- Meta's culture encouraged "ship it and run" which discouraged deep technical learning
- The ideal approach: "once it works, you're inquisitive about why it actually worked — this is a much more sustainable foundation"
- Curiosity is "probably the biggest tool for actually driving technical learning"
