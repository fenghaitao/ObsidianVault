## Definition

Aristotle's three modes of persuasion: ethos (credibility/authority), pathos (emotion), and logos (logic). Jacob Warwick observes that too many executives lean on credibility and logic while neglecting emotion, which is where many deals are actually won or lost.

## Key Information

- From Aristotle's rhetoric
- Ethos: credibility, authority, character — "I've done this before, I'm trustworthy"
- Pathos: emotional appeal — "where a lot of deals get unfair"
- Logos: logical argument, data, reasoning — "the numbers show this makes sense"
- Jacob: "Too many executives lean on credibility and logic because we're trying to do that. We don't show emotion. We don't show the authentic side of ourselves in corporate."
- "The emotional side is where a lot of deals get unfair. So if we understand how to tap into that..."
- Jacob also references kairos (timing) as a fourth rhetorical principle
- Application: the [[Selling the Vacation]] technique is primarily pathos-based — it creates an emotional vision of the future, not a logical argument
- Application: the [[Positive Reputation Technique]] is pathos-based — it leverages social emotions
