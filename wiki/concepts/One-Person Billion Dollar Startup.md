## Definition

The "one-person billion dollar startup" is the vision that AI leverage could enable a single individual to build a company worth a billion dollars. Credited to Sam Altman, the concept implies that AI tools give individuals so much agency and leverage that they can handle everything a business needs without a large team.

## Key Information

- Credited to Sam Altman, CEO of OpenAI
- Implies extreme individual leverage through AI tools — one person can handle all aspects of a business
- Sherwin Wu focuses on the second and third order effects rather than the literal one-person unicorn
- Second-order effect: if one person can build a billion-dollar company, it's way easier for anyone to build startups in general — a massive startup/SMB boom
- Third-order effect: a golden age of B2B SaaS — hundreds of small startups building bespoke software for each other, enabling the one-person unicorns
- Fourth-order effects: the VC ecosystem may change — venture-scale returns may shrink as the number of $10-50M companies explodes
- Lenny Rachitsky is skeptical: support costs alone make a true one-person billion-dollar company very difficult, even with AI
- Sherwin's counter: the one-person unicorn might outsource to other one-person startups that build specialized support software
- The key insight: as the cost of building software collapses, the number of viable businesses explodes — there could be tens of thousands of $10M companies, each life-changing for their founders
- The concept is not necessarily about a literal one-person company, but about the dramatic increase in individual leverage
