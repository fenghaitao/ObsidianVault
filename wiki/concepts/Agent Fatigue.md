## Definition

Agent Fatigue describes the operational exhaustion that comes from managing too many AI agents. Because agents work 24/7 (nights, weekends, holidays), the human orchestrator's workload compounds — constantly reviewing outputs, segmenting bases, and preventing agent conflicts. SaaStr hit this ceiling at 20 agents and may not be able to add a 21st.

## Key Information

- SaaStr may be at capacity: "I don't know when we're going to do the 21st. We may be full."
- Amelia, the chief AI officer, spends 10-15 hours per week reviewing agent outputs — and it's "exhausting"
- "Agents work all night and they work weekends and they work on Christmas. It's a big issue."
- Being the orchestrator/chief AI person is "not a good job for lazy people because the agents never sleep"
- Running multiple agents requires careful base segmentation to prevent agents from conflicting with each other
- There are no "master agents that can manage agents that can manage agents" yet — human oversight is still required
- This creates a headwind for AI GTM adoption: companies may get exhausted from deploying too many agents and close the window for new vendors
- "The window may close where people say, 'Listen, that's the coolest agent I've ever seen. I'm exhausted from the last five. I just can't literally cannot bring one more app into my enterprise.'"
- This is a business process change problem — a classic barrier to enterprise software adoption
