## Definition

Teaching is the craft of helping learners grasp material. David J. Malan's approach centers on "memorable moments" — theatrical, physical demonstrations that make abstract ideas latch onto memory — and on the belief that "dryness has no place" in education.

## Key Information

- Memorable moments: tearing a phone book to demonstrate binary search; having students sort themselves on stage for bubble/selection/insertion sort; physical doors or lockers with hidden numbers.
- The purpose is not just engagement but memory: alumni years later still remember the phone-book demo.
- Energy and excitement come "from a place of insecurity" — no one wants to be the person on stage in front of a bored audience.
- Trade-off: the theatricality costs density (10 minutes on linear search), criticized by students with prior background; the balance was discussed in a CS-education conference paper.
- "If it's just someone reciting words... do we really need to be all in this room together?" — dryness belongs in a book or email, not a live class.
- Metaphors are the instinct for teaching a hard concept: binary is "the same methodology" as decimal places; pointers are "just an address."
- Pointers in C are the most consistently difficult concept, "true for me 30 years ago."
- Time is "a perfectly reasonable knob to turn" — take 24 or 52 weeks if 12 is too few.
