## Definition

Project Taste is the ability to pick problems that have large business impact and actually land, rather than merely interesting or hard ones. Carey Nachenberg built his entire career on it.

## Key Information

- Carey looks for "big business impact" and "gaps" — things the company needs where no one else is stepping up
- Impact beats technical difficulty: what moves the company's needle matters more than cleverness
- He credits project taste (picking well, project after project that shipped) for his rise to Fellow
- At Google X he found many brilliant engineers lacked the same project/research taste, and such people stall regardless of IQ
- Adjacent habit: focus on the company's outcomes (metrics that matter), not your own outcomes, or people won't care about your work

- Nimit Sohoni (research framing): "90% of the battle in research is finding the right problems" — taste means choosing an interesting, meaningful, appropriately scoped problem, convincing others it matters, then executing; build it by reading broadly and starting with small sub-problems before tackling bigger ideas
