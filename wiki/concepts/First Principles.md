## Definition

First Principles is a reasoning style that starts from the actual dynamics of a problem rather than from "how did we solve this last time." David Singleton says Stripe's leadership team taught him its value.

## Key Information

- At Google, Singleton's reflex was pattern reuse: "how did we do it last time? We'll just use that again"
- Stripe's constant refrain was to ask "from first principles, what is actually going on here?" and deeply understand the dynamics before deciding
- A second first-principles move was to ask people at multiple external companies how they handled similar problems, then triangulate rather than copying one answer
- He cautions it is not for every decision — most of the time you check yourself and it's the same pattern (~80%), but the ~20% that are subtly different justify the step back
- Use it in the most consequential places; over-applying it grinds everything to a halt
- David J. Malan uses "first principles" in CS50: keeping C forces students to build hash tables and tries themselves so they can "reason through first principles" about what could go wrong, understand how data is stored, and diagnose problems from the bottom up.
- Malan (July 2026): building data structures in C lets you "diagnose problems by reasoning through first principles what could possibly go wrong" because you understand how data is stored and which algorithms run on it — a stated goal of CS50.
- He ties first-principles understanding to the "engineer" vs "coder" distinction: engineers know the underlying building blocks and can create the next solution, rather than only outputting what AI could now produce.
