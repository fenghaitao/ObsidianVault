## Definition

Part of a designer's role at a frontier AI lab is to act like a VC internally — scanning for illegible ideas and prototypes that have energy around them but don't yet make sense, then diving deeper to understand what's there and transforming them through storytelling, UX, or form factor. This is applying the Legibility Framework to internal design work.

## Key Information

- Jenny Wen applies Evan Tana's Legibility Framework to the designer's role internally
- Designers should scan internal Slack, prototypes, and projects for illegible ideas (ideas that have energy but don't yet make sense)
- People who gravitate toward early ideas can't always articulate why — it's up to the designer to dive deeper and understand
- Example: "Claude Studio" internal prototype — looked dense and confusing to Jenny at first, but researchers and builders were excited about it; it ultimately yielded the skills framework and influenced Co-work's design
- Jenny's process: "I'm trying to see what are the ideas that are really... there's some energy there around but might not make sense yet, that are worth me thinking about more in my work"
- Ties to the research finding that people who join massively successful companies early often look for ideas "so crazy that everyone's laughing"
