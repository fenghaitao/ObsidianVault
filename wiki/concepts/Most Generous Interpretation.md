## Definition

"MGI" — [[Dr. Becky Kennedy]]'s actionable framework for practicing "assume good intent": before intervening on someone's behavior (a kid's or a coworker's), actively construct the most generous plausible explanation for why they're doing it, rather than defaulting to the least generous one.

## Key Information

- Origin: Kennedy noticed that after a hard day with her kids, she would start describing them to herself in ways where she "stopped liking" them (e.g., jumping on the couch after being told to stop reads as "sociopath" in the least generous interpretation). She developed MGI as a nightly practice to counter this — asking herself what the most generous explanation could be (e.g., a strong-willed kid doubling down because he feels his control is threatened).
- Kennedy frames it as a response to her own "allergy to ideas without action" — MGI is the concrete tool for enacting the more abstract "people are good inside" / [[Behavior Vs Identity]] principle.
- Two direct effects she reports: (1) she "liked her kid again" — reframing restores warmth toward the person, not just changes the words used; (2) it produces a different, better-targeted intervention than the least-generous read would.
- Workplace example: someone belaboring a point in a meeting after others have moved on. Least generous interpretation: "they're being a baby" / vain. Most generous interpretation: maybe they didn't feel heard the first time, so they escalate — worth naming ("something happens in meetings where I wonder if you don't always feel heard... then the room gets annoyed, which makes us tune you out more, which makes you belabor your point more — we're in a bad cycle, can we work on this together?").
- Kennedy notes MGI and judgment are mutually exclusive: "you inherently cannot be judgmental when you're curious, and when you're judgmental about something, you're inherently not curious about it." She also notes other people use different phrasing for a similar idea (e.g., "assume positive intent") and that whichever phrase resonates is the one to use.
- Component belief underlying MGI: "this person would want to work through this with me" — i.e., assuming shared goals, not adversarial ones.
