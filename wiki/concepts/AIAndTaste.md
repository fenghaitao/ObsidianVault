## Definition
AI and Taste refers to the fundamental limitation of AI agents: they have no taste. They cannot feel time, experience user frustration, know what a good animation feels like, or exercise the human judgment needed to create software that feels natural and well-designed. Taste is described as the "last bastion" that AI may or may not ever conquer.

## Key Information
- Tuomas Artman: AI agents "have no taste — they simply don't. They are not human beings."
- AI doesn't have a concept of time: it knows one second is better than two seconds, but doesn't know whether two seconds feels slow to a user
- AI can't experience frustration from slow interactions, so it can't judge performance from a user's perspective
- AI doesn't know what a good animation feels like: Emil (Linear design engineer) demonstrated that agents built technically correct animations but they felt unnatural — wrong easing, wrong timing
- The human touch made the same animations feel natural and well-designed
- Taste is described as "the last bastion" — something AI may or may not ever be able to do
- This limitation is why AI tools like Claude Code can produce code with visible quality issues: they optimize for progress, not for the feel of the final product
- The implication: as AI handles more implementation, human taste and judgment become the key differentiator in software quality
