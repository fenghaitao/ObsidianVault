## Definition
Hypothesis-driven delivery is an approach to building agentic AI systems that replaces traditional feature-scoping and milestone-based project management with small loops of build-evaluate-iterate, driven by statistical confidence rather than activity completion. It recognizes that agentic systems are non-deterministic with emergent behavior that cannot be fully specified upfront.

## Key Information
- Articulated by Jack Wang of Accenture as the only delivery approach that works for agentic systems
- Models are non-deterministic; agent behavior is emergent — you cannot scope it like a traditional feature build
- You cannot milestone agentic delivery like a fixed program
- The core goal: building statistical confidence through small loops of build, evaluate, iterate — fast evidence
- Delivery teams need different composition: people comfortable with ambiguity who can articulate what they have learned, not just what they have delivered
- Critical skill: translating statistical numbers into stakeholder confidence
- Organizations must hire for, train for, and value these different skill sets
- Contrasts with traditional enterprise delivery: endless utopian design up front, constant conversations about guaranteed performance, status updates for decisions that never get made
- The IT/data science crowd is not the problem — the organization needs to learn the language of agentic delivery
- The current energy drain in enterprise agentic delivery comes from bridging the gap between how agentic systems actually work and what stakeholders expect
- Prescription: shape projects around hypotheses rather than requirements or specific features
