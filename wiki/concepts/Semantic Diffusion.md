## Definition

Semantic diffusion is a term coined by Martin Fowler in the 2000s describing the phenomenon where someone creates a term, everyone starts using it with their own definitions, and the original meaning gets diluted or lost. It was cited by Aishwaria Raanti to describe what is happening to the word "evals" in the AI industry.

## Key Information

- **Coined by**: [[Martin Fowler]] in the 2000s
- **Definition**: Someone comes up with a term → everybody starts butchering it with their own definitions → the actual definition is lost
- **AI example**: The term "evals" — data labeling companies say "our experts write evals" (meaning error analysis), PMs are told to "write evals as the new PRDs" (meaning product specs), some say "evals are everything" (meaning the entire feedback loop), and some confuse it with benchmarks (LM Arena, Artificial Analysis)
- If you sit practitioners together, they all agree on the importance of building an actionable feedback loop — but they use "evals" to mean different parts of that loop
- Illustrates a broader communication challenge in fast-moving fields like AI
