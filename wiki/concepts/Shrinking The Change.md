## Definition

Change-management technique from the book *[[Switch]]* (Chip and Dan Heath), cited by [[Jessica Fain]] as a key trust-building tool: when a proposed change feels scary or overwhelming to a stakeholder, make it dramatically smaller — a one-week proof of concept, a small experiment — so the ask becomes low-risk rather than a large upfront commitment.

## Key Information

- Fain's application: instead of asking an executive to commit six months of investment to an unproven idea, break the work into milestones/chunks that each demonstrate outcomes and fit, building momentum and trust incrementally toward the larger goal.
- Explicitly connected to identifying what specific *type* of risk a stakeholder fears (wasted time, missed time-to-market, doubt that customers will respond) and directly addressing that fear with a scoped, time-boxed test — including a stated check-in date, which Fain notes reduces anxiety even when the outcome itself remains uncertain.
- Positioned as a general trust-building tool: cumulative delivered results from small, successful experiments earn the credibility needed to later pitch bigger, more novel ideas.
