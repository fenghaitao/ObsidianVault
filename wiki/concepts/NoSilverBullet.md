## Definition
"No Silver Bullet — Essence and Accident in Software Engineering" is a 1986 paper by Fred Brooks arguing that no single innovation will yield an order-of-magnitude improvement in software productivity. The hard part is not the mechanics of coding but understanding the problem and designing the solution.

## Key Information
- Published in 1986 by Fred Brooks
- Central thesis: the hard part of software is not syntax, typing, or boilerplate — it is understanding the actual problem and designing the solution
- No tool can eliminate that fundamental difficulty
- Every tool and technique created up to this point makes the mechanics easier, but the core challenge remains just as hard
- Introduced the essential vs. accidental complexity distinction
- Jake Nations uses this framework to argue that AI, despite its power, is not a silver bullet — it only accelerates the mechanical parts
- The paper's thesis remains relevant in the AI era: better prompts, better models, and better specs are not silver bullets either
