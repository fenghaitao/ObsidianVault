## Definition

Scheduling etiquette is the set of norms around booking meetings, with the core principle being: the person who is asking for something should do the work. This means respecting power hierarchies, giving real options, and treating gatekeepers (EAs, PAs) with deep respect.

## Key Information

- **Core principle**: "You're asking for something, you do the work." If you're the less senior or less busy person, let the other person tell you when they're free and make it work.
- **Calendly critique**: The default should not be Calendly. It implies a flat hierarchy where all meetings are treated equally. Sam Lessin went viral criticizing it.
- **Real options**: If you use a scheduling tool, it must have actual real options — one of the first three times should work.
- **Rescheduling**: When you ask to reschedule, you need to be even more accommodating — within reason, make it work for the other person.
- **Time zones**: Check carefully. Don't propose 4 AM for someone on the other coast.
- **Gatekeepers**: The number one way to look classless is to not respect EAs, PAs, and anyone helping the other person. "There should be this deep well of respect for anyone who's helping you." Say thank you, follow up with them, make eye contact, ask where to put things.
- **"When are you free?"** is context-dependent: "It totally depends on who's asking. If Barack Obama wants to meet at 4 AM, I'm going to make it work."
