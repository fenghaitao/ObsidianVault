## Definition

Judgment (also called taste-making) is the ability to make high-quality decisions in complex, ambiguous situations. Tomer Cohen identifies it as the single most important trait for a builder in the AI era.

## Key Information

- Cohen ranks judgment as the most important of the five irreplaceable human builder traits (vision, empathy, communication, creativity, judgment).
- Defined as making high-quality decisions in what is complex and ambiguous.
- AI is not yet capable of this level of judgment — it can surface information but cannot make nuanced trade-off decisions in ambiguous contexts.
- This is the trait that AI cannot automate and where builders should focus their time and energy.
- The full-stack builder model is designed to automate everything else so builders can concentrate on judgment.
- Lazar's perspective: "I'm optimizing 100% of my time today on good judgment, clarity, quality, taste, good copy, good fonts."
- "We won't be rewarded in the world of AI for faster raw output. We will be rewarded for better judgment."
- Judgment is developed through exposure time: deliberately consuming great work to develop your sense of what's good
- "Good product managers are not compensated for writing good PRDs. They're compensated for good judgment. Somebody else can do the writing."
- The 80/20 rule: spend 80% of time planning and developing judgment, 20% executing
