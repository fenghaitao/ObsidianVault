## Definition

Most Generous Interpretation (MGI) is a practical tool for applying the "Good Inside" philosophy. It asks: "What's the most generous interpretation of why someone is behaving this way?" Instead of defaulting to the least generous interpretation (which comes naturally), MGI shifts the story you tell yourself — and that story shapes the leader or parent you become.

## Key Information

- Dr. Becky created MGI because she has "an allergy to ideas that don't have action" — it's a concrete tool for applying the "people are good inside" philosophy
- The story you tell yourself at night becomes the parent/leader you are the next morning: "The story you tell yourself as a [parent/leader] at night kind of becomes the [parent/leader] you are the next morning."
- Natural default is the least generous interpretation (LGI): "I think my kid's a sociopath" (from a 2-year-old not listening)
- MGI example: Kid jumping harder on couch when told to stop → MGI: "This is a kid who's really oriented around wanting to feel in control. And when that feels threatened, he doubles down."
- At work: Someone belaboring their point in a meeting → LGI: "they're being a baby" → MGI: "Did they not feel heard the first time?"
- Using MGI: "I liked my kid again. And I think we don't talk about that enough as parents — that's the thing that keeps us up at night. It's not their behavior, it's how we slowly stop liking them based on how we're describing them."
- MGI leads to different interventions: If someone doesn't feel heard, address that directly rather than just complaining about them
- Alternative framing: "assume positive intent" — whatever language resonates
- MGI also assumes: "This person would want to work through this with me"
- Connects to [[Curiosity over Judgment]]: "You inherently cannot be judgmental when you're curious"
