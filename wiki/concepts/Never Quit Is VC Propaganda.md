## Definition

A contrarian argument by [[Matt MacInnis]] (CPO, [[Rippling]]; former founder/CEO of [[Inkling]]) that Silicon Valley's "never quit" ethos serves venture capitalists' incentives, not founders'.

## Key Information

- MacInnis's core claim: a VC who has already invested has no mechanism to claw the money back, so their only rational incentive is to encourage the founder to keep trying against long odds, hoping for the rare pivot that works (he cites [[Slack]]'s pivot from a gaming company as a famous, extremely rare example). This makes "never quit" advice structurally self-serving for investors, not necessarily good advice for founders.
- Distinguishes the *people* (VCs as individuals, whom he calls "beautiful and wonderful") from the *incentive structure*, which he says preys most easily on inexperienced (his example: 25-year-old) founders who don't understand it, while veteran founders and VCs "get it."
- His rule of thumb, offered explicitly as "relevant experience" rather than generic advice (see his own aside: "ask people for relevant experience, not advice"): if a startup hasn't clearly, obviously found product-market fit by roughly year four or five, it is extraordinarily unlikely to arrive later, and quitting/resetting the cap table is often the right call, not a failure.
- Applies this directly to his own nine-year run at Inkling, saying in hindsight the company could have been called off around its second or third pivot (roughly year four), while stressing he doesn't regret the experience.
- Explicitly contrasts this with [[Notion]]'s roughly four-plus-year path (including a period the founders spent in Japan reworking their approach) to product-market fit, which he frames as a rare, unrepeatable "narrative violation" (see [[Jeff Lewis]]) driven by the founders' specific idiosyncrasies — not a playbook other founders should copy.
- Notes that good seed investors already assume every investment may go to zero, and that founders who quit are rarely resented by good VCs, who instead want to back the founder's next attempt.
