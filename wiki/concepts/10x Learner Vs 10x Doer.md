## Definition

[[Zevi Arnovitz]]'s reframe, from his Failure Corner story at [[Wix]], that a junior person's job is to be the best learner in the room, not to impress with independent output — since senior colleagues expect growth, not immediate mastery.

## Key Information

- Origin story: as a new PM straight out of Wix's student program, placed on a team of four highly experienced PMs, Zevi tried to work alone and "blow their socks off" at his first product review — and failed badly (wrong format, missed questions). He expected humiliation; instead the team's reaction was simply "come back in two weeks and we'll keep getting at this."
- The realization: "they had zero expectation of me being a 10x PM, but the expectation of me was being a 10x learner. And the second I understood that, my whole mindset shifted."
- Applied technique: he mapped each of the four senior PMs to their single strongest skill (product sense, methodology/frameworks, systems thinking) and deliberately used each one as a targeted mentor for that specific area, rather than treating them as generic senior colleagues.
- Secondary effect Zevi highlights: this also changed how his colleagues experienced his later success — because he'd openly leaned on them as mentors, his eventual strong product reviews read to them as *their* success too ("it was our mentee kind of making us all proud"), not a threat from someone trying to outshine them.
- General advice extended to junior PMs broadly: don't hide unfinished work or avoid asking for help to preserve an image of competence — visible learning, not invisible effort, is what's actually being evaluated early in a career.
