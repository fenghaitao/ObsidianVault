## Definition

The Diamond Shape is a pattern where IC and management skills diverge in the middle of a career but converge again at very senior levels (E8+). Philip Su observed that at high levels, both ICs and managers need vision, communication, strategic thinking, and the ability to lead teams.

## Key Information

- Early in career (1-2 years), lateral moves between tracks are easy — you're a junior in either role
- In the middle (E5-ish range), the transition is very difficult — going from E5 SWE to E5 PM is hard because the skills are very different
- At senior levels (E8+), the skills once again converge — "once you're senior enough, the skills once again overlap"
- What makes a great E8 IC: vision for where the product needs to go technically, understanding of infrastructure weak points, a plan for staged migration, and the ability to coach others compellingly
- "Are you good at communicating with others? Do you have vision? Do you understand the business strategy and how it pertains to the technology you're building? Those things once again become shared"
- The diamond shape doesn't apply to all archetypes: deep specialists (like SQL query optimization) may never develop the breadth needed for the convergence
- The tech lead generalist archetype has the most overlap with director-level management skills
