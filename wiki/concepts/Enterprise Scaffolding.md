## Definition
Enterprise scaffolding is the accumulated layers of governance, process, control, security reviews, deployment approvals, stakeholder meetings, and organizational structures that large enterprises have built to operate safely at scale. Originally designed for human-paced operations, this scaffolding increasingly becomes the primary drag on capturing AI value at machine speed.

## Key Information
- Coined/articulated by Jess Grogan-Avignon and Jack Wang of Accenture
- Built over years for control, process, repeatability, and governance — and it worked well for human-paced growth
- At machine speed, the scaffolding itself becomes the bottleneck: the very thing that made enterprises successful now holds them back
- Real example: an agentic solution took 2 weeks to build but 12 months to reach production because infrastructure, security, AI gateway, data governance, and application teams all needed to align
- Analogized as: Google Search results being reviewed by three teams with legal sign-off before appearing, with 2-week delays during quarter-end change freezes
- The real technical debt is not legacy application code but years of underinvestment in engineering automation, CI/CD, and deployment infrastructure
- Coding agents are turning everyone into builders (PMs, designers, domain experts), causing supply of deployable code to explode — but approval and deployment infrastructure hasn't changed
- GitHub stats: 1 billion commits in 2025, on track for 14 billion in 2026 — approval infrastructure can't keep up
- Solution: every human process must become adaptable, executable code — not another meeting, not a sign-off chain, code
- The pathway requires a fundamental mindset shift for many organizations
