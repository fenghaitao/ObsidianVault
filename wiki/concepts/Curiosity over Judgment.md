## Definition

Curiosity and judgment are mutually exclusive states: you inherently cannot be judgmental when you're curious, and when you're judgmental about something, you're inherently not curious about it. Approaching with curiosity — rather than judgment — unlocks productive conversations and behavior change.

## Key Information

- "You inherently cannot be judgmental when you're curious, and when you're judgmental about something, you're inherently not curious about it."
- This is the mindset behind MGI: get curious about why someone is behaving a certain way rather than judging them
- "Assume good inside or assume MGI. Other people say assume positive intent. Whatever language hits your heart, I think is the language."
- Also assume: "This person would want to work through this with me."
- "The kids who hit, no matter what they say, they really don't like feeling out of control. A kid who's lying to your face, they don't want to lie to your face. Like, they don't want to behave this way either."
- This doesn't make the behavior okay, but it "makes us soften a tiny bit and realize we're on the same team"
- Curiosity leads to different interventions: instead of "stop complaining about the person," have "one conversation through an MGI lens and things start moving forward in a more productive way"
