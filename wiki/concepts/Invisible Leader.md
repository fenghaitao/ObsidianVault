## Definition

Concept coined circa 1920 by management theorist [[Mary Parker Follett]], presented by [[Eric Ries]] as the intellectual origin of *[[Incorruptible (book)|Incorruptible]]*'s closing argument: a company's true leader is not its formal head but the shared sense of common purpose that head is skilled at instilling in the people who work there.

## Key Information

- Illustrated by Follett's own example: Mr. Rowntree, owner of the Rowntree Chocolate Factory, is not really the factory's leader — its true, invisible leader is the shared purpose he cultivates among employees.
- Related Follett ideas Ries cites as strikingly contemporary: "we need to focus on power with, not power over"; "the superior and the subordinate together obey the law of the situation"; the hallmark of a leader is whether they can create more leaders.
- Follett was largely erased from management history for most of the 20th century and only rediscovered and republished in the 1990s; [[Peter Drucker]] later called her "the prophet of management."
