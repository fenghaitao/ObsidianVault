## Definition

The principle that a leader must be the "custodian of the message" — personally owning and telling the company's story rather than delegating it to others. The storytelling is not a marketing exercise after building the product; the story is why you build the product. Articulated by Jeetu Patel based on advice from Cisco board member West Bush.

## Key Information

- Articulated by Jeetu Patel, based on advice from board member West Bush
- "Whatever you do, don't think about your story of the company as a marketing exercise. Think about it as the most intrinsic foundational exercise of the company and always be the custodian of the message. Don't delegate that to someone else."
- If you delegate storytelling and cascade it through layers, "every one of them will add a flavor with well-intentioned and then by the time it gets to the end, people won't know what it is"
- The story massively simplified Cisco's business because the leader understanding it well enough to convey it is the prerequisite for 20,000 sellers and customers understanding it
- "The story is why you build the product to make the story come real" — the story comes first, then the product provides evidence and proof
- Jeetu compared communication to a "direct Ethernet Cat 5 connection" — no packet loss, message gets to the intended audience
- As companies get large, they can lose touch with the front lines — "everyone gets really good with the math of the business, but they don't always preserve the soul of the business"
