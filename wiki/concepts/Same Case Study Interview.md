## Definition

The same case study interview is a hiring practice where every product candidate at every seniority level is given the same extraordinarily difficult case study. The goal is not to see who solves it, but to see how far they get and how they handle new information.

## Key Information

- Used at Rippling for all product roles from entry-level PM to VP
- The case study is intentionally extraordinarily difficult — requires thinking about many dimensions simultaneously, data propagation issues, and technical depth
- Everyone comes away feeling like they failed, but the rubric evaluates how far they got
- Key evaluation dimensions: how many corners they saw around, whether they were defensive when given new information, whether they interrupted to ask more questions
- The recruiting team "cavetas a bit" about this approach, feeling it eliminates people too aggressively
- McGinness: "hand them a drill bit, give them the concrete wall and see if they can get a millimeter or an inch into the concrete"
- Wisdom: "They're never going to get all the way through the wall. It doesn't matter. You're going to learn a lot."
