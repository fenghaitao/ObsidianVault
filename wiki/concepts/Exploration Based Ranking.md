## Definition

A recommendation-systems distinction described by [[Adam Mosseri]] (Head of [[Instagram]]) between exploitation-based ranking (showing content already known to perform well, based on existing engagement data) and exploration-based ranking (deliberately testing content a user might like even though the system doesn't yet have strong evidence for it).

## Key Information

- Exploitation-based ranking is easier to move engagement metrics with, because it shows people content that's already known to be broadly popular (Mosseri's example: showing more Bieber to someone who already likes Bieber).
- Exploration-based ranking is harder but more valuable for niche/small creators, because it actively tests whether a user might like something outside their known preferences (Mosseri's example: showing a Bieber fan some Afrobeats/"Afrounk" content to see if it lands) — giving under-discovered creators a chance to reach an audience that didn't know it was interested in them yet.
- [[Instagram]] credits [[TikTok]]/ByteDance with pioneering strong exploration-based ranking and breaking small talent; Instagram has invested heavily over the last couple of years in originality, content breakout rate, and cultural recency in its own ranking, explicitly inspired by TikTok. Mosseri believes Instagram is now ahead of TikTok in some areas of this and still behind in others, with a path to being best-in-class.
- Related to Instagram's broader rejection of pure chronological/recency-based feeds: recency is described as "an important input into relevance, but not the only one" — chronological-by-default feeds get overwhelmed by high-frequency professional/publisher content and underweight the rarer-but-more-meaningful post (Mosseri's example: a sister's engagement announcement buried under a friend's sandwich photo).
- The "[[Your Algorithm|Your Algorithm]]" feature is a related but distinct mechanism: it makes exploitation-side personalization (what the algorithm already infers about a user) legible and user-adjustable, rather than changing the exploration/exploitation ranking trade-off itself.
