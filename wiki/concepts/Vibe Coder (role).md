## Definition

Vibe Coder is an emerging full-time job role for non-technical people who use AI tools (like Lovable, Bolt, Replit) to prototype and ship products. It represents vibe coding as a skill being added to job descriptions for designers, product managers, and marketers.

## Key Information

- Elena Verna: "This is a new job role that is actually popping up here and there."
- Vibe coding is becoming a skill added to job descriptions for designers, PMs, and marketers
- "Finally, Excel can move over — we have a new skill to add that is super empowering."
- The first full-time vibe coder at Lovable is Lazar, a former Chief of Staff (non-technical, self-taught)
- The role accelerates velocity: "once you get into vibe coding and see its value, leaning into somebody like that accelerates your velocity because it is like an engineer on your team"
- Vibe coders can be non-technical if they're really good at using AI tools
- They teach the team what's possible by pushing the tools to the limit
- They handle the scaling of vibe coding output: creating templates, prototypes, internal tools
- Elena herself vibe codes and would put it as a skill on her resume
- Zevi Arnovitz is a prime exemplar of the Vibe Coder role: a non-technical PM at Meta who builds real products using AI tools
- Tal Rav described Zevi as "the most hands-on vibe coding PM I know"
- Zevi's engineers at Meta ask him to teach them his AI workflow, demonstrating the value of the role
- Zevi distinguishes between "vibe coding" (no planning) and "really building serious apps" (structured workflow with planning, review, and documentation)
- Zevi's brother, a non-technical entrepreneur, is also a vibe coder — he built a full CRM and automation system replacing Zapier and AirTable
- Lazar's path: became a professional vibe coder by building in public on LinkedIn and YouTube; "You don't need a company to hire you. You can hire yourself as a professional vibe coder first."
- S&P 500 companies are putting "Lovable skills" in job descriptions as recommended qualifications
- Some companies hired vibe coders before Lovable did; three full-time vibe coders at one company migrating old codebases
- The role covers all departments: growth, GTM, enterprise, community — "every department needs a Lazar now or yesterday"
- Lazar's advice: "If you haven't learned to code yet, don't — optimize for judgment, clarity, and taste instead"
- The role is evolving: "I'll refer to myself as a rapid engineer in a year from now because vibe coding is just coding in 12 months from now"
