## Definition

Amazon's Performance Improvement Plan (PIP) is a formal process for managing underperforming employees. It has historically targeted ~5-6% of the organization per year, shifting from a soft recommendation to a harder mandate in recent years.

## Key Information

- Historically ~5-6% managed out per year was a soft recommendation, not a hard mandate
- In recent years, the guidance has become much more of a mandate, coupled with no backfills
- Without backfills, repeated annual cuts start cannibalizing solid performers who would have been fine in any other year
- The process is not a linear algorithm — talent review meetings are multi-day locked-room sessions focused on the edges of the distribution
- SDE1 is "up or out" within ~2 years; SDE2 is debated as a terminal level
- Steve's perspective: for people on the left side of the distribution who aren't thriving in Amazon's culture, leaving can be a "blessing in disguise"
- Steve recommends proactively asking your manager about expectations and whether you're meeting them, rather than guessing
- "Can you do a pre-PIP?" — proactively putting together a plan with your manager to get to a safe spot (though Steve joked not to use those exact words)

### Ethan Evans' Perspective
- Calls most PIPs "dishonest and psychologically unrealistic," aimed at documenting a decision rather than genuinely improving behavior
- His advice: once you're on the list, start job hunting rather than assuming you can survive it
- A manager can spin the same facts two ways, and HR will not rescue you — "don't bring a knife to a gunfight"

### Stefan Mai's Perspective
- PIPs are first a legal paper trail, but people genuinely can exit them — the process must stay credible to hold up legally
- Amazon HR mainly wanted boxes checked ("a pretty savage culture"); Meta HR historically exhausted every avenue before firing
- Most managers prefer the person recovers rather than firing, so your manager may legitimately be rooting for you
