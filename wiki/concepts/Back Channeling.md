## Definition
Back channeling refers to the short verbal acknowledgments ("mhm", "uh huh", "yeah", "right") that listeners make during conversation to show they are actively listening and following along, without intending to take the speaking turn. It is a fundamental aspect of human conversation that varies across cultures and languages.

## Key Information
- Common back-channeling sounds: "mhm", "uh huh", "yeah", "right", "I see"
- Function: signals active listening and engagement without interrupting the speaker's flow
- Cultural variation: in Japanese, back-channeling is a sign of politeness and can constitute up to 20% of conversation time with overlapping speech
- Half-duplex voice AI models treat back-channeling as interruptions, causing the model to stop speaking
- Full-duplex models (like Moshi) can handle back-channeling naturally without breaking
- Back-channeling is one of the key conversational phenomena that make the difference between robotic and human-like voice interaction
- Essential for achieving the "Her moment" in voice AI
