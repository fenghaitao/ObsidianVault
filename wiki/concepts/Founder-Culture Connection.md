## Definition

The Founder-Culture Connection is Molly Graham's insight that 80% of a company's culture is defined by the personality, strengths, and weaknesses of the founder. Culture is not what you say on the wall — it is what you do, how you hire, how you fire, and who you don't hire.

## Key Principles

- "80% of the culture of a company is literally defined by the personality of the founder"
- Different founders create radically different cultures: Google was a "two PhD students paradise" (idea-driven, campus-like), Facebook was a "19-year-old hacker's dorm room" (shipping above all else)
- Culture is what you do, not what you say — "It's how you hire. It's how you fire. It's who you don't hire. It's all of those decisions."
- "The job of building a company around a founder is to build a company that would make a decision the way the founder would when they're not in the room"
- Your job as an operator is not to shape culture — it's to help articulate the culture the founder is creating
- When you ship a set of values that don't match who the founder is, it creates "cultural dissonance" — people see the gap between what's said and what's done
- Graham has not done a values exercise since running culture at Facebook — she focuses on process, systems, and how decisions are made
- "You don't need a consulting firm to tell you [the culture] — just go do a personality diagnosis on your founder"
