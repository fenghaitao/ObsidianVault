## Definition

[[Evan Spiegel]]'s deliberate philosophy at [[Snapchat]]: design approval is an intentional gate that everything must pass before shipping, even though this slows down the shipping process — because it's what produces a cohesive customer experience.

## Key Information

- Contrasted with apps built by teams responsible for separate pages/parts of the experience with no cohesive design through-line — Spiegel says you can visibly tell when an app lacks this kind of gate.
- Snap's design team reviews new work weekly (Spiegel personally spends a couple of hours each week reviewing hundreds of new design ideas); there is no gate to bring work to this review — any idea, however unpolished, can be shared.
- Complements [[Loonshots]]: the small, flat design team is simultaneously a rapid-idea-generation engine and a quality-control bottleneck, both by design.
- Extends to AI-generated work: rather than requiring designers to ship code, Snap built AI guardrails (automated code review, ~10,000 auto-detected bugs, agent-assisted debugging from user bug reports) so that more people can safely contribute code without breaking the product at near-billion-user scale.
