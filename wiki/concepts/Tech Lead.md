## Definition

Tech Lead is a set of leadership behaviors that can occur across multiple engineering levels, not a distinct level itself. It involves leading technical direction, coordinating work across engineers, and ensuring project execution — behaviors that are expected at Staff Engineer and beyond.

## Key Information

- Ryan Peterman clarified: "I view Tech Lead as a set of behaviors that can happen across levels"
- There are Senior Engineers who are tech leads leading smaller projects, and Staff Engineers who perform tech lead behaviors on larger initiatives
- "Tech leadership is just a set of behaviors that is orthogonal to the level that you're at"
- Lee noted that being a tech lead might put you in a position to demonstrate leadership behaviors, but "it doesn't mean it's a stepping stone necessarily"
- "You doing those behaviors and being a strong tech lead does not mean you're on your way to staff necessarily"
- The key behaviors of a tech lead: influencing others, growing people around you, taking on the largest problems in the org
- Companies have different leveling systems that define tech lead expectations — you should consult your specific company's documentation
- Evan King was made tech lead of the graphic violence team at Meta as a junior engineer (IC3/IC4) — his manager proactively created the opportunity
- Evan didn't know what "tech lead" meant when his manager offered it; it's not a formal title at Meta, doesn't show up anywhere, and is "largely made up in optics"
- As tech lead of the graphic violence team, Evan led three engineers (himself, a junior engineer, and a mid-level PhD research scientist), set up weekly team meetings, helped management with roadmaps, and held weekly one-on-ones with both teammates
- This was the "transformative moment" in Evan's career where he grew and expressed leadership skills
- Evan's tech lead role had significant people management aspects: he was involved in performance reviews, attended calibrations (as an IC6), and read "The Making of a Manager" to prepare
- Meta's levels are not public, so Evan had to earn credibility every day through being right rather than relying on his title — this kept him hungry and prevented complacency
- Ryan Peterman similarly grew into a tech lead role naturally — he was the first backend engineer on a work stream, and as the project grew in importance, he became the go-to person and de facto lead
- Ryan described the experience of "leading meetings but not feeling like a leader" — he was doing it because he knew what had to be done and buckled down
- The ability to influence without authority is critical for tech leads, especially at Meta where levels are not public and engineers don't report to the tech lead
- James Cowling: a tech lead has little authority; leadership is an oversight→accountability slider, and the empowering move is explaining the why rather than telling people what to do.
