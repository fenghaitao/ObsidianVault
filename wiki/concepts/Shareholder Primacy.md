## Definition

The standard corporate-law doctrine that a board's fiduciary duty is to maximize shareholder value — cited by [[Eric Ries]] in *[[Incorruptible (book)|Incorruptible]]* as a key driver of [[Financial Gravity]] when a company has no [[Mission Protective Provisions]] structurally overriding it.

## Key Information

- Illustrated by Ries's recurring thought experiment: imagine the company you consider most evil (his hypothetical is [[Philip Morris]]) offering to buy your company for $1 more per share than it's worth — most people instinctively say no, yet under standard shareholder-primacy charters, the board would have a fiduciary duty to say yes.
- In reality, Philip Morris acquired [[Vectura]] (a UK inhaler-therapeutics company) for roughly 165 pence per share (~£1.1 billion total); within about three years Philip Morris took a $900 million write-down and disposed of Vectura for parts.
- Cited by Ries as one of the "standard" governance modes that major AI labs on a Vatican AI-governance panel (Google, OpenAI, Anthropic, Cohere, Palantir) explicitly do not use.
