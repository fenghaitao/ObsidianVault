## Definition

[[Qasar Younis]]'s personal reading philosophy: prioritize old, well-regarded books over new ones, since time filters out noise, and deliberately seek out subjects you know nothing about rather than reinforcing existing knowledge.

## Key Information

- Rationale: across a lifetime you'll realistically only read 50-100 books, so each one should be high-signal; new books haven't yet been filtered by time the way old ones have.
- His personal test for a good book: does it change your existing framing on life? Cites *The Emperor of All Maladies* (cancer) as a recent example, alongside older touchstones like *Guns, Germs, and Steel*, *Collapse*, *SPQR*, *The Autobiography of Malcolm X*, Sam Walton's *Made in America*, and Colin Powell's *My American Journey*.
- Cites [[Charlie Munger]]'s line that he's never met a very successful person who doesn't read constantly, as validation of the broader practice.
- Draws an explicit LLM analogy: consuming diverse, high-quality inputs (not just today's stack of narrow professional knowledge) makes a founder's understanding of the world "more rich and nuanced," the same way diverse training data improves a model.
- Connects this reading practice to founder taste/judgment more broadly — Younis argues many Silicon Valley CEOs lack real taste specifically because their life experience is narrow (never worked as a rank-and-file employee at a large bureaucratic organization, never read broadly outside their industry).
