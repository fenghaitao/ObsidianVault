## Definition

Storytelling is the ability to craft and communicate a compelling narrative about your career, decisions, and experiences. Rahul Pandey emphasized that storytelling is "incredibly powerful" — the narrative you create about what you did, why you did it, and why it's interesting has immense career value.

## Key Information

### Storytelling as a Career Asset
- Rahul got "a lot of mileage" out of his story of being an enterprising 22-year-old engineer who took a bet on a startup
- Being able to tell that story and explain what happened and what he learned has "a ton of value"
- The story you tell yourself and the story you tell other people shapes how opportunities come to you
- One reason Rahul advises people to pick unique opportunities is that they give you "exposure to more unique opportunity, unique people, unique stories" — which broadens your perspective and gives you storytelling ability

### Storytelling in Promotion
- Rahul's second promotion rejection at Pinterest was caused by a storytelling failure: the promotion committee interpreted a 16-month project duration as lacking urgency
- The framing of the story matters — the same project could be seen as impressive (large scope) or concerning (slow execution) depending on how it's told
- You must be clear about what you think you're saying, because it may not always be interpreted that way
- Get your promotion document reviewed by people who have been around the block to understand how your story might be interpreted differently

### Storytelling vs. Networking
- The best way to develop relationships is not by asking for mentorship, but by sharing what you've worked on
- Showcasing your unique insights or projects creates gravity — people gravitate toward you because they want your opinion too
- This is a two-way street: if it always feels like you're the one asking and it's not reciprocated, you might need to make yourself more interesting

### Stefan Mai's Storytelling Advice
- Storytelling is a bedrock skill for managers; improve it by noticing and critiquing the stories you already tell yourself
- Paint your team's wins clearly every few months so people feel proud and like they're winning — morale follows the story
- A winning narrative ("we were two people with terrible on-call; we invested and turned it around") makes downstream problems vanish
- Teams without a winning story develop zero-sum bickering and low morale

### David Singleton on All-Hands Storytelling
- Prepare: for a 15-20 minute audience segment, spend 30+ minutes thinking about what's most useful — an unprepared "top of mind" falls flat
- Take a step back and give the context behind a question (modeled on Google's Alan Eustace), building an arc: why it matters → the thing → remind them what you told them
- Use "dynamic range": if everything is always awesome (or always grim), people stop believing you
- A senior leader is always on stage in the hallway; use those micro-moments to remind people of what and why
