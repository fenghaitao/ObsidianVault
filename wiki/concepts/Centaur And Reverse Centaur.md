## Definition

A metaphor (credited by [[Lenny Rachitsky]] to writer [[Cory Doctorow]]) for two opposite modes of human/AI collaboration: a "centaur" (human body, horse body below — human in charge of the tool) versus a "reverse centaur" (horse head, human body — the AI/machine in charge, with the human just doing its bidding).

## Key Information

- Discussed in the [[Adam Mosseri]] interview as a lens for the risk of over-delegating product strategy to AI: if an AI effectively dictates the strategy and a human just executes it, that is the "reverse centaur" outcome nobody wants.
- Lenny Rachitsky ties the "reverse centaur" state to real-world analogs like Uber drivers and DoorDash couriers, whose work is directed moment-to-moment by an algorithm — framed as a cautionary example of what happens when the human stops being "in charge."
- [[Adam Mosseri]] responds to this framing by noting there are cases where outsourcing a workflow to AI is a clear "win-win" and cases where "the risk outweighs the benefits" — i.e., the availability of an AI shortcut doesn't mean it should always be taken.
- Directly informs [[Vision Vs Strategy]]: Mosseri's insistence that strategy come from a human working within AI-assisted feedback (not the reverse) is a practical application of staying in "centaur," not "reverse-centaur," mode.
