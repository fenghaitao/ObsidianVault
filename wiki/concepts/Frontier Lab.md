## Definition
A frontier lab is a company working at the cutting edge of AI model development — examples include Google DeepMind, OpenAI, and Anthropic — with overlapping but distinct research/applied and software/research demands.
## Key Information
- LLMs connect research and product in ways classical machine learning did not, so frontier-lab work spans many roles across a spectrum.
- Key in-demand skills: kernel development and low-level engineering, programming-language abstractions (for kernels), reinforcement learning (RLHF onward), and the distributed-systems × optimization overlap for multi-GPU training.
- Vlad delays drawing a sharp applied-vs-research line: even product integration (e.g., making Gemini good for search) requires hard research; everyone must stay fluid across the spectrum.
- Skill demands are similar across labs — differences are more about business strategy, specialties, and customers — and OpenAI/Anthropic people agreed with his advice.
- Strongest hiring signal for external candidates: tangible open-source contributions (vLLM, SGLang, TensorRT, NVIDIA Dynamo).
- Core differentiator among researchers: "research taste" plus mathematical maturity for navigating the stochastic graph of research.
