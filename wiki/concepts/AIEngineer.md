## Definition
AI Engineer is a role popularized by swyx that sits at the intersection of software engineering and AI, focused on building applications with LLMs through chains, agents, tooling, and infrastructure. The 2024 AI Engineer Summit keynote critically examined this role, noting it is often overscoped but underspecified, and that evals and data literacy are essential but frequently missing skills.

## Key Information
- Term popularized by swyx, who organized the AI Engineer Summit series
- Positioned on a spectrum of AI skills, sitting to the right of an "API dividing line" — focused on chains, agents, tooling, and infra rather than model training
- Key critique from the keynote: evals and data literacy are auspiciously missing from the typical AI engineer skillset, but without them you cannot progress beyond 0→1
- Hamel Hussein and Jason Lou found that evals and data literacy can be developed with 4-6 weeks of deliberate practice
- Vague job titles create inflated expectations — when anything goes wrong with AI, people look to the AI engineer to fix it, even if they lack the specific skills needed
- Historical parallel: the "data scientist" role a decade ago was similarly a unicorn role that the industry had to decompose into specialized roles (decision scientist, ML engineer, data engineer)
- GitHub Copilot cited as an example of deliberate, specific job titles that name exact skills needed at a particular maturity stage
- Recommended hiring sequence: application/software engineering first (0→1), then platform/data engineering (capture data), then ML engineering (only once data exists)
- Do not hire an ML engineer without data-generating products
