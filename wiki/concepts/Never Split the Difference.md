## Definition

A negotiation principle popularized by Chris Voss: avoid lazy compromise where both parties simply split the difference between their positions. The middle ground is often not the optimal outcome — it represents a failure to find the true ceiling or floor of what's possible.

## Key Information

- Popularized by Chris Voss in his book "Never Split the Difference"
- Jacob Warwick: "Just because somebody says, you know, you started 100, you say 200, they come in at 150, that's lazy"
- Example: A CRO negotiated 6 months of on-target earnings as severance ($350K), but the paperwork said 6 months base salary ($187.5K). Instead of splitting the difference, Jacob had the client simply ask "Was that a mistake?" — and the company fixed it
- The CRO almost lost $90K by instinctually splitting the difference
- Another example: Hollywood attorneys asked for $1.3M on a $700K offer. The studio offered $1M. They settled at $1M — but the ceiling wasn't found. If they'd asked for $1.5M, would the deal have landed at $1.1M?
