## Definition

The Alpha/Beta framework, adapted from finance by Matt McGinness, classifies people and processes by their return profile: Alpha represents outperformance (high upside, high volatility), while Beta represents stability (low volatility, predictable output). Effective leaders deploy each where appropriate.

## Key Information

- From finance: Alpha = outperformance relative to the index; Beta = volatility (undesirable in assets, but useful conceptually)
- High-alpha people: Dennis Rodman archetype — difficult, volatile, but capable of outsized returns. "Room on every team for one."
- Low-beta people: reliable, consistent, predictable. Valuable in their own right.
- Processes exist for the sole purpose of lowering beta — decreasing volatility in system output.
- The downside of process: it suppresses alpha. Leaders must be judicious about where to apply process.
- 0-to-1 product development needs high alpha; mature products (like payroll) need low beta.
- Example: Rippling's payroll product needs low beta (no unpredictability); new product exploration needs high alpha.
- Application to hiring: evaluate whether a candidate is high-alpha or low-beta, and whether that fits the role's needs.
