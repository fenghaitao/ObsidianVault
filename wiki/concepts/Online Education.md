## Definition

Online education is the distribution of teaching over the internet (streaming, podcasting, MOOCs, open courseware). David J. Malan argues it is underused for consolidating the world's best instruction and sharing resources across institutions.

## Key Information

- CS50's online path: Extension School streaming → RealVideo/RealAudio → iPod/iTunes podcasting via RSS → video iPod, before MOOCs (Coursera/edX, 2012).
- Malan rejects bite-sized 3–5 minute chunks (they drive engagement but not necessarily educational outcomes) in favor of 3-hour lectures that students can pause and rewind.
- The "world's best instructor for everyone" consolidation doesn't happen partly due to institutional pride and fear of being "put out of business."
- COVID was a missed opportunity: he could not get Harvard on board with cross-campus course sharing (Harvard/Stanford/UCLA/Yale/MIT).
- What people pay for at Harvard is credentialing, support, and networking — not the knowledge itself, which "does society no good to withhold."
- He wishes for a "buffet of educational materials" schools can adopt or adapt rather than reinventing wheels.
