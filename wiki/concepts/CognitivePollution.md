## Definition
Cognitive pollution is the contamination of digital exhaust fumes that occurs when AI writes to your data sources. Once AI-generated content mixes with your own digital activity, you can no longer distinguish which patterns are yours and which are the AI's — you're observing a human-AI hybrid rather than your own cognition.

## Key Information
- Term used by Šimon Podhajský to describe what happens when AI writes to source systems
- The moment AI writes to your data sources, "the exhaust fumes are contaminated"
- You can no longer tell which patterns in your digital activity are authentically yours
- The observer effect (being watched changes behavior) is different — it's mediated by the human, not automated
- Read-only AI avoids cognitive pollution by never writing back to source systems
- "There's an argument to be made that you don't want the AI to write your draft in the first place, that you should reclaim your agency"
- Represents a philosophical argument for read-only AI beyond just security concerns
