## Definition
Generalization is the ability of a trained model to perform correctly on settings, objects, and environments it has not seen before — per Sergey Levine, the central hard problem in robotics.
## Key Information
- "The hard thing in robotics was always generalization."
- Demos usually do not reveal the level of generalization: rehearsed acrobatic feats are less impressive than mundane tasks done reliably with unseen objects and environments.
- Generalization is "a property of many trials, not of one trial," which is why it is hard to show in a single video; live demos and long time-lapses are attempts to make it legible.
- Figure's live-streamed package-sorting demo is an example Levine considers a genuine demonstration of generalization.
- Two roadmap milestones target generalization: (1) a robot that improves with autonomous experience in a novel setting, and (2) transferring common sense to recover from unexpected situations (e.g., knowing not to put spatulas in the oven).
- The biggest risk to single-digit-year timelines is that robots must reach reliability/generalization "higher than what we typically expect from LLMs," because a robot must act autonomously rather than be iteratively re-prompted.
