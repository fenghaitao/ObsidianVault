## Definition

The "SaaS apocalypse" is the narrative — discussed and largely rejected by [[Max Schoening]] (Head of Product, [[Notion]]) — that AI-assisted "vibe coding" will let companies and individuals build their own internal tools instead of paying for SaaS products like [[Notion]] or Salesforce, making traditional SaaS obsolete.

## Key Information

- Schoening's framing of the debate: "There's all this talk about we will not need SaaS tools any longer. We will build all our own tools. We don't need Salesforce... I'm going to build my own notion."
- His core rebuttal: most people don't actually want to maintain the full stack of software themselves. He compares this to food: "People don't want to go hunting either. They just want to go to Costco and have the steak in styrofoam packaging" — i.e., people want the outcome, not the maintenance burden.
- Cites [[Brett Taylor]]'s framing that "software is like a garden" — it needs ongoing tending — and argues the "as a service" part of SaaS is really what people are paying for: maintenance plus a team of specialists thinking hard about a specific problem, which he doesn't see going away.
- Personal anecdote: Schoening has tried "rebuilding Notion in a weekend" himself, partly to probe his own frustrations with the product — reinforcing his view that most people don't actually want that project.
- 2010s-era SaaS specifically, he argues, was often "a very fancy form around a spreadsheet" that guided people toward correct usage (less malleable than a raw spreadsheet, but that constraint was the value). He expects that layer to erode as tools become more general and AI-assisted, per [[Malleable Software]], while specialized/compliance-heavy tools remain "as a service."
- Cites the [[Joanna Stern]] anecdote (a journalist who tweeted that thanks to Notion AI she finally understood and could use Notion) as evidence that AI can make historically hard-to-adopt general tools more accessible, rather than replacing them outright — Notion "wasn't SaaS in the traditional way" but AI now functions as a built-in tutor for it.
- Predicts the overall shape of the market will resemble "the cloud wars": individual layers commoditize over time, but businesses will still resist locking into a single provider, so multi-provider "as a service" specialization persists — draws an explicit analogy to [[Heroku]] vs. [[Kubernetes]] (Heroku replaced the ops team; Kubernetes made the ops team "superheroes" while preserving cloud choice, and won on that basis despite worse UX).
- Bottom line: "I think to some degree the SaaS apocalypse is greatly exaggerated. At the same time, are things going to stay the same? Of course not."
