## Definition

[[Jeetu Patel]]'s explicit rejection of the standard management advice "praise in public, criticize in private": instead, be direct and critique openly in public (in a mode of problem-solving, not posturing), while reserving private moments specifically to build and reinforce trust.

## Key Information

- Patel's reasoning: public praise-only cultures create "asymmetry" — dashboards look green while growth stagnates — because nobody names what's actually broken. Being direct in public forces the team to face facts and solve problems together.
- This only works if enough trust has already been established that people feel safe being challenged openly — the private side of the equation is about deliberately building and signaling that trust (being generous, not "stingy," with words of support privately).
- Explicitly contrasted with Ben Horowitz's "sandwich" critique pattern (nice — critical — nice), which Patel avoids in favor of direct, adult communication.
- Compared by Lenny to radical candor's "care personally, challenge directly" framing — Patel agrees this is functionally similar.
- Complements [[Jenny Wen]]'s (episode 23) observation that a team comfortable "roasting" its leader is a sign of psychological safety that coexists with high standards — both frame safety and directness as mutually reinforcing, not opposed.
