## Definition

Term coined by [[Mark Pincus]] as the belief-driven counterpart to a Minimum Viable Product (MVP): a product you launch because you already believe, based on evidence, that it is going to be a hit — as opposed to an MVP, which is launched merely because it's "viable" (i.e., might make it, per unfounded hope).

## Key Information

- Pincus argues the word "viable" in MVP is itself the problem — "viable is where hope comes from" — and should be replaced with a standard of belief/evidence.
- Distinguishes two different reasons to put a product in market: (1) to learn (a genuine, small-scope test, fine to expect it might fail) vs. (2) to launch, which should only happen once you believe — not hope — it will be a hit.
- Companion concept to "[[Kill Hope Before Hope Kills You]]."
