# Founder as Craftsperson

The idea, articulated by [[Qasar Younis]], that being a founder is a craft to be learned through practice — the first startup is like the first wobbly table a woodworker builds.

## Core Idea

"If this was a woodworking podcast and you said the first table you built was wobbly, you wouldn't say 'go work at Crate & Barrel.' You'd say 'that's the first table. We're going to keep at it.' Being a founder is its own muscle."

## Key Points
- Qasar: "The first time you're going to do a startup, for the first three years, it's a zero. Just rid yourself of the expectation that it's going to be successful."
- The first startup is about learning and building the muscle — not about being an overnight success
- Qasar's third company (Applied Intuition) is his most successful — this pattern is common
- Some VC funds focus almost exclusively on multi-time founders for this reason
- The best ideas often come from low expectations, playing around, and tinkering
- "You have to hold these conflicting views in your head: it's deeply very important, and you should give it your all, AND it's also not that important"
- The tension is hard to reconcile: "You're in the proverbial war... what the hell are these people talking about having fun? This is hard."

## Practical Advice
- If after 2 years the market isn't giving you a more specific path, consider resetting
- The foundation (co-founders, market, life phase) may be wrong — not the table
- "It's very easy to pivot before you raise money and before you have employees. Nobody cares."
