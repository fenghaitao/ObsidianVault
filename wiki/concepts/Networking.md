## Definition

Networking is the practice of building professional relationships. Rahul Pandey reframes networking as genuine curiosity and sharing what you're working on, rather than transactional relationship-building. He credits this approach with creating many of his career opportunities.

## Key Information

### Rahul's Approach to Networking
- He "hesitated to use the word networking" because he never thought of what he was doing as networking
- His approach has two components: genuine curiosity about what people are doing, and being thoughtful about what you share
- Genuine curiosity: "I genuinely am interested in what you're doing. How are you doing? How are you enjoying it or not enjoying whatever you're up to?"
- This approach has always felt natural to him, not manufactured

### The Best Way to Develop Relationships
- The best way is not by asking a ton of questions or asking for mentorship
- Instead: share something you've worked on that might be interesting to the other person
- "Showcasing what you've been up to and why you have a unique insight or a unique project" creates gravity — people gravitate toward you
- This makes it a two-way street rather than a one-sided ask

### Networking as Luck Manufacturing
- Keeping in touch with people is a way to "manufacture luck"
- Rahul's debug tool at Meta came from talking to people and understanding their workflow
- His job hops came from being "in the know on what are the people doing that I respect"
- This is a "repeatable algorithm or repeatable process that's not dependent on luck"

### Social Media as Networking
- Posting on social media is a form of networking — "you are putting out content and people can see it and resonate with it"
- It creates luck because you don't know who's going to see your post, but people will think of you when opportunities arise
- Both Rahul and Ryan are active on social media as a deliberate networking strategy
