## Definition

"Picking Up on Executive Threads" is the skill of recognizing and acting on subtle cues or breadcrumbs that executives leave — offhand comments like "I wonder if..." or "Have you considered..." — and responding quickly before the moment passes. The best people take the bait; those who don't lose the executive's faith.

## Key Information

- Execs often leave subtle breadcrumbs of opinion rather than clear-cut asks
- "I wonder if I'm thinking about, have you considered?" — these are invitations to engage
- "The best people take the bait and the worst people don't"
- **Positive example:** When Rachel Woolen mentioned design reviews would need rethinking, Kev (head of design at Webflow) had a Loom with a framework ready within an hour
- **Negative example:** Tamar Yehoshua asked four times to see "top 10 use cases on X." The fourth time, she got frustrated because no one had picked up on the cue
- Missing these threads causes the executive to lose faith that you'll follow up on what they believe is important
- If you wait a week for follow-up items, the exec has moved on and you've missed your chance
- **Calibration question:** "How strongly do you feel about this?" or "Does this trump our current priorities?" — helps distinguish between passing thoughts and mandates
