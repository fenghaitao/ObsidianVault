## Definition
Capability Extrapolation is the methodological approach of projecting AI capability trends forward using multiple independent evidence sources with different strengths and weaknesses, rather than relying on any single measurement method.

## Key Information
- Joel Becker advocates a triangulation strategy using multiple evidence sources with "different pros and different cons"
- Evidence sources include: benchmark evaluations (time horizon), randomized controlled trials (RCTs), in-the-wild transcripts, and fuzzy-goal environments (AI Village)
- Each source has important limitations: benchmarks may not capture real-world complexity, RCTs have small sample sizes, in-the-wild data is observational not experimental, fuzzy-goal environments have elicitation issues
- The goal is to "pull that rabbit out of the hat one more time" — find additional evidence sources that provide independent signal
- Extrapolation must account for potential breaks in trends: compute slowdowns, software-only singularities, or intelligence explosions
- Considerations like "building on prior work" and "mergeability" may reduce effective time horizon compared to raw benchmark scores
- The approach is explicitly humble: Becker acknowledges that all predictions assume no unpredictable technological advances
