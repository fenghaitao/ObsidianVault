## Definition

[[Sherwin Wu]]'s bullish, underrated-opportunity thesis: most of the world's economically significant work is repeatable, high-determinism "business process" work (support scripts, standard operating procedures, back-office operations), not the open-ended, non-repeatable knowledge work (like software engineering) that Silicon Valley focuses on and that tools like Codex are built for.

## Key Information

- Wu's distinction: software engineering is open-ended and non-repeatable (you don't build the same feature twice), which is exactly why agentic coding tools thrive on it — but this describes a comparatively narrow slice of the real economy.
- Most jobs outside tech follow standard operating procedures with little room or desire for deviation (e.g., a utility company support line following fixed policies) — a completely different shape of work than what most AI coding tools are optimized for.
- Wu argues this category is systematically underrated by the tech industry specifically because it's outside "our wheelhouse" and rarely discussed on X/Twitter, even though it may be comparably large or larger in economic terms than software engineering.
- The opportunity: build AI/agent tooling specifically for automating deterministic, data-integrated business processes across enterprise systems, not just for open-ended coding-style tasks.
- Connects to Wu's broader observation (see [[Bottom-Up AI Adoption]]) that most real-world AI deployments fail not because of model capability but because of a Silicon-Valley-bubble mismatch between how tech insiders assume AI gets used and how ordinary non-technical employees actually need to use it.
