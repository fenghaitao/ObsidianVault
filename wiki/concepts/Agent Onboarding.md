## Definition

Agent onboarding is the process of setting up a new AI agent — defining its identity, granting it access to tools, and establishing how it will work with you. In OpenClaw, this happens through an interview-style conversation where the agent asks "Who am I? Who are you?" and builds its identity from the answers.

## Key Information

- **The OpenClaw onboarding flow:**
  1. Agent comes online: "Hey, I just came online. Who am I? Who are you?"
  2. User defines the agent's role and purpose
  3. Agent conducts an interview: asks about goals, constraints, preferences, context
  4. Agent builds its soul file (identity.md) from the conversation
  5. Agent starts discovering tasks it can help with
- Claire Vo compares this to an ideal employee onboarding: "If you think about the onboarding experience of an agent, this is a very interesting one where it just says 'Who am I? And who are you?' And we're going to figure this out together."
- "This isn't just metadata. This is the start of figuring out who you are."
- Voice notes (ramble mode) are recommended for onboarding — higher bandwidth than typing
- **Employee mental model for onboarding:**
  - Provision the agent its own email and calendar (like provisioning an employee)
  - Give it access/permissions to your accounts (not your passwords)
  - Start with read-only, progressively grant more
  - "I'm hiring an employee. If I were to hire an employee, I'd provision them an email address, I would give them a calendar."

## Why It Matters

- The quality of onboarding directly determines the quality of the agent's performance
- The interview format is innovative: contrasted with "the old mental model of structured fields and bios"
- "If you can make your agents effective, then you get promoted into human management" — agent onboarding as management training
