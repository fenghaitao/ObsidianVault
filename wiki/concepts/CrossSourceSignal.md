## Definition
Cross-source signal is the insight that emerges from combining data across multiple disconnected sources that were never designed to talk to each other. It is the core value proposition of read-only AI systems like Fulan — "the cross-source signal is the product."

## Key Information
- Core insight: "Your email client doesn't know what you journaled. Your task manager doesn't know what you're browsing. The cross-source signal is the product."
- No single source tells you about intention-action gaps, attention drift, or relationship decay — these only emerge from cross-referencing
- Example: combining Vivaldi browser reading history with Clay CRM contacts to suggest who in your network might want to discuss specific articles
- Four data sources combined in the reading-discussion demo: browser history, CRM, and two others — none designed to talk to each other
- The cross-source ability is what the three top use cases (intention-action gaps, attention drift, relationship decay) have in common
- Represents the positive side of the mosaic effect — the same aggregation that creates security risk also creates value
