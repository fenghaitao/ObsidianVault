## Definition

A framework used by [[Matt MacInnis]] (CPO, [[Rippling]]) comparing the search for product-market fit to drug discovery: a company builds a product the way a drug company synthesizes a compound, and the market either has (or lacks) the "binding receptors" for it — a fact that marketing cannot change.

## Key Information

- Core claim: "the market is immutable" — no amount of tweeting, LinkedIn posting, or advertising changes whether the market structurally wants your product; marketing can raise *awareness*, but the underlying reception is effectively predetermined ("fate has already decided the outcome").
- MacInnis explicitly warns against the failure mode of shipping a product, observing a lack of success, and then trying to "market your way through" the gap — arguing this is doomed if the binding receptors genuinely don't exist.
- He calls the mindset shift "liberating": instead of trying to convince the market to want something, a founder's job becomes finding the right "drug" (product) for receptors that already exist.
- Cited alongside a related quote (attributed to an unnamed VC): "product-market fit is a thing you absolutely know when you see it, and therefore if you don't absolutely know it, you don't have it" — which MacInnis connects to his own repeated false belief, during his years at [[Inkling]], that the company had achieved PMF when in hindsight it clearly hadn't.
- Product launches themselves ("big fat pull-the-slingshot-back launches") are described as "the teeniest thimble of water in the ocean of noise about startups" — nearly irrelevant to whether a product ultimately succeeds, versus building "brick by brick, bottom up."
