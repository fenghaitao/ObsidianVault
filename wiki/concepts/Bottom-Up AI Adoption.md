## Definition

[[Sherwin Wu]]'s observation that successful enterprise AI deployments require both executive-level buy-in *and* genuine bottom-up adoption from actual employees — top-down mandates alone (without employees who are excited, evangelizing, and building best practices organically) tend to produce negative-ROI deployments.

## Key Information

- Anti-pattern: an executive mandate ("we're going AI-first," tied to performance reviews) with no bottom-up champions leaves employees knowing they're supposed to use AI tools but with no one to learn from and no organic best practices — Wu says he's observed this repeatedly and suspects it explains a lot of negative-ROI AI deployments.
- Wu's read on why this happens: Silicon Valley, X/Twitter, and software engineering are all "bubbles" — most people in the world (and most companies) aren't AI-native or following model releases, so the sophisticated internal practices tech companies take for granted (skills files, MCP, agent frameworks) are foreign to most non-technical employees, who are often just asking a model the most basic possible questions.
- Wu's recommended fix: identify or staff a dedicated internal "tiger team," not necessarily software engineers — often the person is a technical-adjacent, non-coding "Excel wizard" type (e.g., a support or operations lead) who gets genuinely excited about the tools — and empower them to explore capabilities, apply them to specific workflows, run demos/hackathons, and evangelize internally.
- Confirmed even inside OpenAI itself: despite being built to be AI-centric from the start, real internal adoption only accelerated once individual employees could apply Codex-style tools directly to their own idiosyncratic work, not from top-down mandate alone.
- Reinforces [[Surgical Team Model]]: Wu frames this as another instance of his broader management practice of identifying and empowering the highest performers/most excited adopters rather than spreading effort evenly.
