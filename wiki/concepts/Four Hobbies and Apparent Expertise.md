## Definition

Four Hobbies and Apparent Expertise is a Marc Brooker blog-post framework — a 2x2 matrix with "doing vs. discussing" on one axis and "the hobby vs. the gear" on the other — that contrasts real expertise against visible-but-shallow apparent competence.

## Key Information

- The quadrants describe a trade-off between expertise and visibility: pure doers gain deep expertise but little visibility, while pure discussers (interested in the gear and in talking about it) gain apparent competence without depth.
- Overlaid on software: the "hobby" is coding and the "gear" is the dev setup/tooling; quiet coders may know everything but be invisible, while loud discussers may be visible without practicing much.
- Brooker considers 100% at either end a failure mode; he personally lands ~75/25 practitioner-to-communicator (sometimes 80/20), and says great engineers span ~90/10 to ~50/50.
- Pure doers also risk working on the wrong problem with their head down in the IDE.
- Long-term it's better to be underrated than overrated; being overrated feels good in the moment but is rarely sustainable.
