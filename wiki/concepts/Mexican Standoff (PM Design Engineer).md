## Definition

[[Marc Andreessen]]'s named framing (raised across several Lenny's Podcast episodes) for the three-way tension between product managers, designers, and engineers, each of whom now believes AI lets them absorb the other two roles.

## Key Information

- Andreessen's core claim: all three are actually correct — AI is now good enough at coding, design, and product management that each role really can expand into the others.
- His resolution: rather than one role "winning," the individuals who become AI-augmented combinations of two or three roles become the most valuable — see [[Skill Combination Premium]]. He predicts stovepipe job titles ("coder," "designer," "PM") may dissolve into something like "I build products" or "I tell the AI how to build products."
- Notes the same standoff exists in Hollywood among director/writer/actor, with AI-augmented "auteurs" as the parallel superpowered-individual outcome.
- Reactions from other guests to the same framing, raised independently by Lenny across episodes:
  - [[Evan Spiegel]] (episode 12): calls the dynamic "highly dysfunctional" if actually happening at a company, though acknowledges the underlying convergence of skills.
  - [[Keith Rabois]] (episode 15): argues the converging skill across all three functions is CEO-like judgment ("what are we building and why"), not any one discipline "winning" — see [[Role Collapse]].
- Also connects to [[Jenny Wen]]'s "block-shaped" strong-generalist hiring archetype (episode 23, [[Design Hiring Archetypes]]) as a practical hiring response to the same convergence.
