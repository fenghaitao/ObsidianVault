## Definition

"Engineering Manager as Surgeon" is Sherwin Wu's management philosophy, inspired by The Mythical Man-Month. The manager's role is to support engineers like a surgical team supports a surgeon: anticipating needs, unblocking them before they ask, and handing them "scalpels" before they know they need them.

## Key Information

- Inspired by The Mythical Man-Month (Fred Brooks, 1970s), which predicted software engineering might evolve toward a "surgeon" model
- The original prediction: one expert surgeon does the core work while everyone else supports (nurses, residents, fellows)
- Sherwin's adaptation: software engineering isn't literally like surgery (it's more collaborative), but the manager should act like a surgical support team
- The manager's job: look around corners, unblock people (especially from an organizational perspective), have the "scalpel" ready before the surgeon asks
- "If the surgeon needs a scalpel but the manager already has a scalpel ready for them, that's the best case scenario"
- In the AI era, this is even more important: as engineers crank out PR after PR, the main bottleneck becomes organizational/process-oriented, not technical
- Sherwin's concrete practice: spend more than 50% of management time with top performers, unblocking them and making sure they have everything they need
- AI tools may help managers anticipate blockers: asking ChatGPT hooked up to company knowledge "what are the active blockers on my team?"
