## Definition

A psychological technique where you give someone a positive reputation that they feel compelled to uphold, whether they deserve it or not. By framing someone as already being an advocate or ally, you create a social trap: they must either confirm the positive reputation or explicitly deny it (which is socially awkward).

## Key Information

- Jacob: "We give someone a positive reputation that they want to withhold whether they deserve it or not."
- Example with a recruiter: "I want to thank you for being an advocate for me and for respecting that I won't share compensation figures right now. I appreciate that."
  - The recruiter is now trapped: they must either say "No, I don't respect you" (socially awkward) or confirm the reputation
- Example with a female CEO: "I appreciate that you've always been an advocate for women and you support one another. That's something I respect about you."
  - Then if the comp comes in lower than a man's, you can say: "You said we treat this fairly. Prove it."
- Example with a CMO who lost an offer: "It's clear to me that you take care of women. I made a mistake with my coach's advice." — shared identity, positive reputation
- The technique works because denying a positive reputation is psychologically uncomfortable
- This is a form of emotional (pathos) persuasion rather than logical (logos) argument
