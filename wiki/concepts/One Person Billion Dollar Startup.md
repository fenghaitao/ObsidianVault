## Definition

Idea (credited by [[Sherwin Wu]] to possibly [[Sam Altman]]) that AI-driven individual leverage could let a single person build a billion-dollar company; Wu argues the more important story is in its under-discussed second- and third-order effects.

## Key Information

- Second-order effect: if one person can plausibly build a billion-dollar business, it's also far easier for anyone to build a much smaller, vertically-specific business — Wu predicts a boom in small, bespoke, vertical-specific software companies (10-20 startups each serving a narrow niche), potentially producing a new "golden age of B2B SaaS."
- Wu's rough taxonomy: for every one-person billion-dollar company, there might be dozens of $100M companies and thousands of $10M ones — the latter being "life-changing" outcomes for individuals even without venture-scale returns.
- Third-order effect: if many companies end up as small, high-leverage, low-headcount operations built on outsourced bespoke software, Wu speculates the venture ecosystem itself changes — fewer opportunities for the 100x/1000x return outliers VCs need, alongside a handful of large infrastructure/platform players supporting everyone else, even as outcomes for high-agency individual founders improve.
- Counterpoint raised by [[Lenny Rachitsky]]: he's skeptical a single person (without significant contractor help) can actually run a billion-dollar business alone, since customer support load doesn't scale down just because AI helps generate the product — a scaling bottleneck AI hasn't yet solved. Wu's response: the support burden itself may get outsourced to other small AI-native startups built specifically to serve that niche (e.g., dedicated support tooling for podcasters/newsletter operators), reinforcing the "bespoke software boom" prediction rather than contradicting it.
- Related fourth-order effect (Lenny): as the number of things competing for attention explodes, distribution and existing audience/platform become increasingly valuable — see [[Distribution Moat]].
