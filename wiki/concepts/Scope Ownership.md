## Definition

Scope Ownership is the state of being recognized as the definitive go-to person for a specific area of a codebase or product. It is the hallmark of readiness for promotion from mid-level (L4) to senior (L5) engineer.

## Key Information

### How Ricky Achieved Scope Ownership
- Ricky described the biggest shift from L4 to L5 as "taking more and more ownership and getting to a point where I could confidently say I owned a certain scope or certain area"
- The "buzzword" is being the **go-to person** for a scope of area
- He achieved this by deeply understanding a portion of the codebase and pushing out many projects with high impact in that specific area
- He picked an area in ads that "didn't look that pretty" and he wanted to make it better — the aesthetic motivation led to impact

### From Knowing to Leading
- At L5, Ricky owned a space and did projects with impact in it
- At L6, the shift was from knowing a space to leading it: "how can I push the boundaries of that scope of area, challenge the status quo, and have a larger influence over that scope of area"
- He got "a lot of points for leading and pushing the boundary of that area"
- The progression: know the space → lead in the space → create new scope within the space

### Avoiding Scope Competition
- On Ricky's team, engineers typically picked their own projects, so he didn't face scope competition
- He acknowledged that on other teams, competing for scope can be a real challenge
- His strategy was to find his own projects rather than wait for assignments
