## Definition

Environment Placement is the deliberate strategy of physically positioning oneself in environments that maximize learning, opportunity, and second-order effects. Jia Chen applied this to hackathons, office hours, and her startup journey.

## Key Information

- Jia attended office hours for hard classes even when she had no specific questions — she passively acquired information from others' questions and professor's offhand exam hints
- She applied the same principle to hackathons: being physically present at hackathons in person was more effective than remote participation
- For her startup, she moved to Silicon Valley to be in "startup land" — maximizing the second-order effects of being in the right environment
- She considered transferring from Michigan State University to a California school to be closer to the startup ecosystem
- The principle: "Environment placement is pretty big" — where you are physically affects what opportunities, connections, and information reach you
- This connects to her broader philosophy of placing yourself where network effects are strongest

### Jake Bolam's Framing
- Jake's closing advice "go where you're valued" applies environment placement to teams, not geography: join a team that appreciates your strengths
- He sees people join teams where their strengths don't matter (a front-end specialist on a backend team) and get bad ratings despite huge output
