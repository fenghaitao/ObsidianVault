## Definition

Executive coach [[Rachel Lockett]]'s set of practices for maintaining a healthy co-founder relationship, treated as structurally similar to a marriage but with far less institutional support — she cites a stat that roughly 65% of startups fail due to co-founder conflict.

## Key Information

- **Why it's under-supported**: unlike a marriage (where couples therapy is a normalized institution), co-founder conflict is a private matter people rarely discuss with their board or peers, even though it directly threatens the business.
- **Shared self-awareness tools**: Lockett uses the Enneagram to give co-founders common language for their tendencies. A recurring generalized pattern: CTOs skew skeptical, fact-seeking, self-sufficient; CEOs skew toward big-picture vision-selling and influence, often ahead of what the company has actually built — an inherent, productive tension ("a dance") that co-founders should name explicitly rather than experience as unexplained friction.
- **Co-founder "vows"**: an explicit, conscious commitment (paralleling wedding vows) about how the partners will work together — how decisions get made, how conflict gets handled, what culture they're building — ideally established early, not left implicit. Lockett co-authored a related article/template with First Round covering weekly, monthly, and annual check-in questions.
- **Dedicated relationship time, separate from running the business**: regular check-ins outside day-to-day operations — a biweekly dinner or lunch, a quarterly in-person check-in — explicitly to ask "how's this working for you? Are we still aligned on vision and strategy? What's gone unsaid that we need to talk about?" Framed with the same "balcony vs. dance floor" metaphor as the "[[One-Page Plan]]" cadence.
- **When it's already broken**: co-founders typically seek coaching either proactively (wanting to build something great from the start) or reactively (already frustrated, unsure whether to continue — "end it or send it"). Lockett's approach: use a 360 to surface honest feedback from both sides, then help each founder see what they've been missing from the other and how isolating leading without that support has been. Outcomes vary — some pairs recommit and rebuild a working cadence; others reach clarity that one founder should exit, which she frames as still a successful outcome (clarity over unconscious, ongoing frustration).
- **Any evolved facilitator can help, not only a paid coach** — an HR leader or general counsel with strong people skills can sometimes hold the same space, though outside support is generally useful for holding both parties' frustration and empathy simultaneously.
- Related individual-conflict tools: [[Non-Violent Communication (NVC)]] for the specific conversations that arise within the co-founder dynamic; [[Jerry Colonna]]'s "how are you complicit" framing for entering disputes with humility.
