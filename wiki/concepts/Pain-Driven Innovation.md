## Definition

Tony Fadell's framework for deciding what's worth building: start from real (often long-habituated) customer pain, then ask whether a new technology has just become available to solve that pain in a genuinely different way.

## Key Information

- Origin: Fadell says he always starts from pain — "what are people's pain right now, or you can see it on the horizon" — a question sharpened through his long relationship with [[Hermann Hauser]] (creator of Acorn Computer and co-founder of ARM), who asked Fadell how he decides what's worth building.
- Two-part formula: (1) identify genuine pain, often one that's been "evolved" around rather than solved (a limitation baked in since a product's original creation), and (2) find the "why now" — a new technology that has just crossed a threshold enabling a fundamentally different solution.
- **Nest example**: the pain was wasted energy/money and unusable programmable thermostats (50% of a home's energy bill, arcane "VCR-like" programming); the new technology was applying machine learning so the thermostat could learn user patterns automatically, justifying a $249 price that could save $800-1,200/year.
- **iPod example**: the pain was the lack of portable, all-your-music access; the enabling technologies were newly viable portable mass storage, MP3/digital music, and new lithium battery chemistries, combined with low-power ARM processors.
- **iPhone example**: the enabling convergence was multi-touch, emerging Wi-Fi ubiquity, the promise of 3G, digital cameras/video, and existing services like YouTube — none of which existed together when BlackBerry (a "texting machine") was designed.
- Innovation, in Fadell's view, usually requires reinventing the whole system around the pain point, not just the product — e.g., Nest's changed distribution (third-party installers, not retail) and purchase model, or the iPod/iPhone's bundled services (iTunes, App Store).
- Contrasted with General Magic, which built impressive smartphone-like technology roughly 15 years before its time — a cautionary example of having genuine pain/vision but the wrong "why now."
