## Definition

Lenny Rachitsky is the host of Lenny's Podcast and author of Lenny's Newsletter, a popular publication about product management, growth, and technology.

## Key Information

- Host of Lenny's Podcast, where he interviews leaders in tech and product
- Author of Lenny's Newsletter at lennysnewsletter.com
- Newsletter has 1.2 million subscribers (as of episode 21)
- Annual newsletter subscribers get a "Product Pass" with a year free of 19 products including Lovable, Replit, Bolt, Notion, Linear, Devin, PostHog, Superhuman, Descript, Whisper Flow, Perplexity, Warp, Granola, Magic Patterns, Raycast, ChaPPT, Mobin, and Stripe Atlas
- Elena Verna holds the record for most appearances on his podcast (four times)
- Has a contact form at lennyrachitsky.com
- Previously worked as an engineer for 10 years, then as a product manager
- Tried Atlas (OpenAI's browser) and tweeted feedback about the AI-only search experience, noting there was no way to switch to standard search
- Expresses optimism about the people building AI tools, noting they're "always just so awesome"

### Personal Background
- Real name was Leonid; parents changed it to Lenny when they became US citizens. Family nickname: Lonya (Russian) or Lonchik
- Moved to the US from Ukraine; born in Odessa
- Married to Michelle Rial for about 10 years; they met on HowAboutWe, a dating platform
- Has a son named Jude (almost 3 years old as of episode 21)
- Has misophonia (bothered by certain sounds, especially chewing)
- Has face blindness (prosopagnosia) — bad at remembering faces and names
- Enjoys being recognized by fans, finds it flattering. Has never had a bad experience
- Identifies as a Jewish atheist

### Career Journey
- Has had four careers: engineer, product manager, founder, and now podcaster/newsletter writer — he calls this his "fourth career"
- Describes "the most interesting careers" as "winding" with "starts and stops and failures and successes"
- Worked at Airbnb for 7 years; left with plans A-D, none of which included the newsletter
- First wrote on Medium about what he learned at Airbnb; Brian Chesky shared it with the whole company
- Early side projects: Atheist Spot (atheist news site), U torials (tutorials platform), and Local Mind (startup sold to Airbnb)
- Local Mind was built on Foursquare and Gowalla, allowing users to ask questions of people checked in anywhere
- Friend Lee Jacobs encouraged him to pursue writing, noting the rare overlap of enjoying something and people valuing it
- After 9 months of writing every week, applied the Lindy Effect and added a paywall; it worked within a month
- A psychedelic experience in Joshua Tree gave him the repeated phrase "I have wisdom to share" for 3 hours, giving him confidence
- Describes the content treadmill as "the Indiana Jones boulder is chasing me constantly"
- Still loves the work: "I can't imagine doing something more fulfilling and interesting"

### Stress and Happiness
- Probably more stressed than he appears, but less stressed than average; credits genetics (~70%) and learned techniques
- Took a University of Pennsylvania online course on the psychology of happiness
- Key insight: everyone has a baseline happiness level; you can improve it through positive thinking and optimism
- Exercise doesn't make you happier but brings you out of the negative
- Used self-talk ("it's going to be okay") during the scariest moment of his life — Michelle's C-section complication
- Most stressful business moment: Product Pass fraud attack where fraud rings (mostly in China) exploited API vulnerabilities
- Misses having coworkers but is "trying very hard to never have full-time employees" to keep things simple

### Product Management Philosophy
- Defines PM as: impact, collaboration, judgment, alignment, coordination
- Working definition: "your job as a product manager is to deliver business impact by prioritizing and solving the most impactful business problems"
- Believes a PM is essentially a mini CEO — channeling what the CEO would do on a specific product or feature
- Says product management is all about influence, and parenting is basically the same
- Applies PM principles to parenting: reads books, uses charts and star systems
- Believes the best stuff comes from actual experience, not pontificating

- Has also interviewed Jeffrey Moore (Crossing the Chasm) and Jen Abel (pricing expert) on related topics
