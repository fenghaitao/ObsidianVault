## Definition

Lenny Rachitsky is the host of Lenny's Podcast, a show on product, growth, and engineering leadership. He interviews guests such as [[Fiona Fung]] about how software teams operate in the AI era, and runs "Lenny's product pass," a sponsorship offering free access to AI products for newsletter subscribers.

## Key Information

- Previously worked at Airbnb; recalls Airbnb's growth-era culture work, including founders repeatedly emphasizing culture/values in all-hands meetings, and a Sheryl Sandberg fireside chat where she framed fast growth and hiring as "a good problem to have" compared to stagnation.
- Frequently surfaces recurring cross-episode themes in his interviews, e.g., "latent demand," "initiative"/"agency," and the quote "the cave you fear contains the treasure you seek" (used repeatedly across episodes).
- Was previously an engineer for about 10 years before moving into product/media.
- Has interviewed other Anthropic Claude Code/Cowork-team members, including [[Boris Cherny]] and [[Cat Wu]], both cited by Fung as being among the podcast's most-listened-to episodes.
- Interviewed [[Adam Mosseri]] (Head of Instagram) in a separate episode about taste, authenticity, and judgment in an AI world; speaks Russian and references his own experience learning a second language when discussing the value of "putting yourself out there."
- Has also had [[Benedict Evans]] and [[Kevin Weil]] (former CPO at OpenAI) as guests, per references made during the Mosseri episode.
- Runs "Lenny's product pass" (mentioned as "lennisproduct.com" in the Mosseri episode transcript), a free-year offer for AI products exclusively for Lenny's newsletter subscribers.
- Show has two recurring segments referenced across episodes: "fail corner" (guests share a major professional failure) and "hot seat corner."
- Interviewed [[Tony Fadell]] (co-creator of the iPod, iPhone, Nest; author of *Build*) in an episode on building real "taste" and how AI changes product-building; had said Fadell had "been near the top of my wish list" of guests since starting the podcast.
- Interviewed [[Eric Ries]] (author of *The Lean Startup* and *Incorruptible*) about why successful companies get corrupted by "financial gravity" and how founders can structurally protect their mission (public benefit corporations, mission trusts); Ries had appeared on the podcast once before.
- Continues to run "Lenny's Product Pass" (lennysproductpass.com), offering a free year of AI products to newsletter subscribers, as the recurring pre-roll pitch across episodes.
- Interviewed [[Benedict Evans]] (episode 07) about his "AI is eating the world" presentation, prompting Evans to unpack the "task vs. job" framework, model-commoditization thesis, and his "presume radical uncertainty" stance on the AI platform shift; also asked Evans about anti-AI backlash and how he's raising his kids given AI-driven career uncertainty.
- Interviewed [[Dr. Becky Kennedy]] (episode 28), clinical psychologist and CEO of [[Good Inside]], on applying parenting frameworks ([[Power Of Repair]], [[Connecting Before Correcting]], [[Behavior Vs Identity]], [[Most Generous Interpretation]], [[Sturdy Leadership]], [[Boundaries Vs Requests]], [[Resilience Over Happiness]]) to leading adults at work; opens the episode by citing a rant from a prior guest, [[Ryas Doshi]], that "most adults in the corporate environment are really just babies in disguise," and references a past episode with [[Peter Deng]] about language shaping thought.
- Interviewed [[Lazar]] (episode 27), [[Lovable]]'s first official "vibe coding engineer," about the rise of professional vibe coding as a career, after [[Elena Verna]] (Lovable's head of growth, and a prior podcast guest) mentioned working with a professional vibe coder in her own episode. Recalls a prior interview with [[Michael Truell]] (Cursor's CEO) about Cursor becoming "the layer after code," and connects it to Lazar's description of the AI-agent-conversation layer.
- Interviewed [[Sam Lesson]] (episode 32), partner at [[Slow Ventures]] and former VP of Product at [[Facebook]], in an unconventional episode devoted entirely to Silicon Valley etiquette — introductions, conversations, hygiene, dress, dining, scheduling, communication, meetings, and exiting — organized around Lesson's "[[Low Heart Rate]]" framing and [[Abundance Mindset]]. Shares his own self-reported face blindness/trouble remembering names in solidarity with Lesson's name-forgetting etiquette tips, and references listening to a [[Tyler Cowen]]/[[Alison Gopnik]] podcast episode about how children learn table manners through experimentation.
- Interviewed [[Aishwarya Naresh Reganti]] and [[Kiriti Badam]] (episode 33) on why most AI products fail and how to build them successfully, covering [[Non-Determinism In AI Products|non-determinism]], the [[Agency Control Trade-off]], and their "[[Continuous Calibration Continuous Development]]" framework. Cites [[Dan Shipper]]'s claim that the top predictor of AI-adoption success is whether the CEO personally chats with ChatGPT/Claude daily, and recounts an anecdote about [[Jason Lemkin]] replacing most of his sales team with AI agents.
- Interviewed [[Jason Lemkin]] (episode 35), founder/CEO of [[SaaStr]], for a second time (their first conversation, ~18 months earlier, on building a sales org, became a widely shared "legendary" episode) — this time on SaaStr's transition from a ~10-person sales team to 1.2 humans plus 20 AI agents. Mentions he previously used [[Delphi]] (Lemkin corrects the transcript's rendering, "Deli") to build his own AI clone, "Lennybot" (lennybot.com), which ingests every episode/piece of content Lenny has produced and includes a voice mode; discusses his own experience being a "top 1% user" of tools like Replit vicariously through Lemkin's account, and references a prior Lovable episode with [[Elena Verna]] and a prior guest referred to only as "Jen" (name garbled in the transcript's ASR — not linked here pending confirmation) who said she writes outbound emails "artisanally" without AI tools.
- Interviewed [[Molly Graham]] (episode 34) in a "greatest hits" episode collecting her scaling/career frameworks — "[[Give Away Your Legos]]," "[[Bob The Monster]]," "[[J Curve Versus Stairs]]," the "[[Waterline Model]]," "[[Six Rules For Goals And Alignment]]," "[[Serve The Business Not The People]]," "[[Escalation Is A Tool]]," and the "[[Fifty Percent Growth Rule]]." Recalls his own time at [[Airbnb]], including a [[Sheryl Sandberg]] fireside chat framing fast growth/hiring as "a good problem to have"; references a recent interview with [[Matt MacInnis]] on comfort-as-warning-sign and escalation, and a separate anecdote about a young PM named Zevy at Wix who reframed being a "10x PM" as being a "10x learner"; repeats the recurring quote "the cave you fear contains the treasure you seek."
- Interviewed [[Matt MacInnis]] (episode 36), CPO and former longtime COO of [[Rippling]], on a set of deliberately contrarian leadership frameworks — [[Deliberate Understaffing]], [[Extraordinary Results Require Extraordinary Effort]], [[Alpha Beta Hiring And Process Framework]], [[The Pickle (Product Quality List)]], [[Never Quit Is VC Propaganda]], [[Binding Receptor Theory of Product-Market Fit]], and [[Compounding Plus Power Law Plus Entropy]] — plus MacInnis's argument that point-solution SaaS and AI startups are structurally disadvantaged against data-rich bundled platforms.

### Personal background (episode 21 — interviewed by his wife, Michelle Rial)

- Born in Odessa, Ukraine; official US first name is Leonid (shortened by his parents to "Lenny" upon becoming citizens). Was a software engineer, then product engineer, then product manager before leaving [[Airbnb]] after 7 years to explore startup ideas.
- Started his newsletter (now ~1.2 million subscribers) as a side effect of writing about lessons learned — his first post ("What I learned at Airbnb") went viral on Medium and was shared company-wide by [[Brian Chesky]]; a friend, VC [[Lee Jacobs]], encouraged him to double down once it was clear he both enjoyed the writing and that it resonated. Added a paywall ~9 months into weekly Substack writing (partly triggered by COVID-era uncertainty about his Airbnb equity), citing the "[[Lindy Effect]]" as part of his reasoning to commit further.
- Describes following "pull" — repeatedly noticing something is working/enjoyable and leaning into it rather than following a pre-set plan — as the actual mechanism behind starting the newsletter, the paywall, and other decisions; explicitly frames this as the opposite of having had "do this crazy thing" as any kind of original plan (his real Plan A-D were all more conventional: start another company, join a startup as first PM, join a big company as PM, or become an advisor/consultant).
- Reports genuinely enjoying the work but describes an "Indiana Jones boulder" feeling of constant weekly-content pressure, and no clear long-term answer for what a "Substack writer/podcast" career looks like decades out.
- Has misophonia (distress at specific sounds, especially chewing) and self-reports mild face blindness/trouble remembering names, which has become more pronounced as public recognition from the podcast has grown.
- Took a University of Pennsylvania positive-psychology course that shaped his stress-management approach: a "baseline happiness" model (everyone has a baseline they return to after both good and bad events; the real lever is raising that baseline via optimism/reframing, not chasing single high moments) and the finding that exercise doesn't directly increase happiness but prevents dipping into a negative baseline.
- Recounts two of his most stressful moments: (1) a large 2025 promotional Lenny's Product Pass offer (free year of Cursor, Lovable, Bolt, Replit, V0) that attracted organized fraud rings (largely traced to China) exploiting API loopholes, requiring an engineer (Este) to patch holes around the clock; (2) the birth of his son, where an epidural migrated upward during his wife's C-section, stopping her heart/lungs and requiring emergency intubation — a ~1-in-50,000 complication that resolved safely.
- Earlier side projects, in retrospect unrelated-seeming precursors to his current work: [[The Atheist Spot]] (a Reddit-style news site for atheists), Tutorials-for-you-by-you site "U torials" (pre-Wikipedia/pre-YouTube, in his own description), and [[Local Mind]] (a startup layered on Foursquare/Gowalla letting users ask real-time local questions of people checked in nearby), which was acquired by Airbnb and led to his own Airbnb tenure.
- Describes his approach to product management, when asked to define it for a non-technical audience, as: impact, collaboration, judgment, alignment, coordination, organizing, planning, outcomes — and reiterates his view (previously voiced regarding [[Keith Rabois]]'s framing) that a PM should function like a "mini CEO," thinking about what makes the whole business succeed, not just their own scope.
- Credits practitioner-sourced, real-experience-based writing (most current newsletter posts are guest posts from people sharing one specific hard-won lesson) as the core reason for the newsletter's success — explicitly contrasted with generic "pontificating from the clouds" advice.
