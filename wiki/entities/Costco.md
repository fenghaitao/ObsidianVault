## Definition

Warehouse-retail company cited by [[Eric Ries]] in *Incorruptible* as a "governance fortress" case study — a company widely assumed to have no special legal protection against shareholder-primacy pressure, which turns out, on close inspection, to be structurally protected.

## Key Information

- Ries says people used to cite Costco to him as an example of a company that succeeds *without* any of the governance protections described in his book — "so much so that I was surprised to learn it was embodied in a governance fortress" when he actually checked.
- Its board reportedly sees its role as "a bulwark against pressure rather than as an amplifier of financial gravity," which Ries says explains its ability to resist outside pressure to cut costs/quality.
- Ries quotes a critical line leveled at Costco — "Costco takes money that rightfully belongs to shareholders and instead invests it in improving the customer experience" — intended as a criticism, which Ries reframes as exactly what a mission-protected company is supposed to do.
- Presented (with a caveat) as a single-entity structure: the governance rules are written directly into Costco's own structure, rather than delegated to an outside trust/foundation — which Ries contrasts with multi-entity structures (e.g., Novo Nordisk's foundation, Anthropic's Long-Term Benefit Trust) that have a renewable mechanism for restoring protections chipped away by repeated attacks.
