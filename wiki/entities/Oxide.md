## Definition

Oxide Computer Company is the rack-scale server company that Bryan Cantrill co-founded with Steve Tuck in 2019, focused on general-purpose CPU compute, storage, and networking built from first principles.

## Key Information

- Founded in 2019 after Cantrill and Tuck, who had worked together at Joyent, decided to build "the computer that we want to build" — a rack-scale machine with their own board design, switch, DC bus bar, software, and low-level software stack.
- The idea crystallized after seeing Facebook's Open Compute "Tioga Pass" hardware and realizing enterprise could get a similar first-principles rack-scale machine.
- Oxide deliberately stays on general-purpose CPU compute rather than GPUs/accelerators, because Nvidia's proprietary whole-stack stance conflicts with Oxide's transparency-at-the-hardware-software-interface approach; the two doors were "compete with Nvidia" or "partner with Nvidia," neither of which Oxide wanted.
- A famous VC's promise to fund "literally anything" turned into a refusal to back a computer company; Cantrill used the rejection to get an introduction to a founder who had failed on a similar thesis, learning that Oxide's minimum viable product is "a rack" and took 3+ years to build.
- Oxide's organizational model, in Cantrill's framing, is a heist movie: a safe cracker, getaway driver, and demolitions expert whose skills arrive at the right moment — a team-first culture built so each member can "do the thing I think is impossible, together."
