## Definition

Entrepreneur and author best known for *The Lean Startup* (2011), the most influential startup methodology book of its era. Fifteen years later he published *Incorruptible: Why Good Companies Go Bad and How Great Companies Stay Great* (May 2026), which shifts focus from building a successful company to protecting one after it succeeds.

## Key Information

- Author of **The Lean Startup**, which popularized concepts like the minimum viable product (MVP), build-measure-learn, and treating business assumptions as testable hypotheses. He argues top AI labs (OpenAI, Anthropic, Claude Code) operate on classic lean-startup principles today (shipping small "research preview" experiments, not knowing in advance what would become popular) even though they don't use the branded terminology.
- Author of **Incorruptible**, framed as the natural sequel to The Lean Startup: "the lean startup was about helping you build a successful company; this book is about helping you protect what you've built."
- Core thesis of Incorruptible: successful companies are usually destroyed not by competition but by **[[Financial Gravity]]** — an internal force that drags organizations toward mediocrity/extraction once they succeed — and this is preventable through the combination of **ethos** (a clear, written purpose) and **integrity** (structural governance mechanisms). See **[[Ethos And Integrity]]**.
- Built a long-term stock exchange (LTSE) and has advised many companies on governance structures; describes himself as "an eccentric collector of alternative governance ideas."
- Personally advised Dario and Daniela Amodei when they left OpenAI to found Anthropic, walking them through the risks of standard governance and discussing what became Anthropic's public benefit corporation status and Long-Term Benefit Trust. He says he takes no credit for Anthropic's success — "all the credit to Dario and Daniella and the whole team."
- Popularized/named several frameworks discussed in Incorruptible: **[[Harder Is Easier]]**, the **[[Figure It Out Principle]]**, the **[[Culture Bank]]** (Todd Park rule), **[[Mission Guardian]]**, **[[Spiritual Holding Company]]**, and **[[Mission Controlled Company]]**.
- Draws on historical case studies including Novo Nordisk (Nordisk Insulin Laboratorium), Zeiss, Cloudflare, Costco, Patagonia, Vectura, Groupon, Google, and Vital Farms to argue the pattern of corporate corruption/protection is empirical and centuries old, not a modern ESG fad.
- Book website: incorruptible.co (offers implementation guides and a secret cut chapter for mailing list subscribers).
