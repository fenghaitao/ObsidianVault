## Definition

Symbian was the company that built the Symbian OS, a smartphone operating system before smartphones were mainstream (~2001). It was David Singleton's first employer after university.

## Key Information

- Built mobile phone operating-system software around 2001, when smartphones were not yet a thing; Singleton joined largely because he liked the people, then realized they were working on technology he believed would be profound
- Singleton started as a software engineer building core parts of the Bluetooth stack and PC Connect software
- In his final years he worked as a "technical consultant": still an IC, but embedded with handset manufacturers (Panasonic, Samsung, Nokia) turning the Symbian OS into actual products — an early predecessor to today's forward-deployed engineer
- Seeing how different companies' team cultures shaped outcomes (Samsung ran duplicate competing teams; Nokia planned everything in far more detail) sparked his lifelong interest in how teams are organized
- Symbian also had a heavy-handed "process library" (part of ISO 9001 certification) that nobody actually followed, seeding Singleton's later skepticism toward process-for-process's-sake
