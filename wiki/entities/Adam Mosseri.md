## Definition

Adam Mosseri is the Head of [[Instagram]], a role he has held since 2018 after taking it over from Instagram's founders, [[Kevin Systrom]] and [[Mike Krieger]]. He is a designer turned product manager turned executive, previously the designer/lead behind the early [[Facebook]] News Feed and the Facebook ranking algorithm.

## Key Information

- Started his career as a design manager, then became a PM at Facebook when the PM on his first project ([[Facebook Home]]) quit; taught himself carriers, OEM certification, and Android/OS internals in the process — he calls it his biggest learning experience despite the project's failure.
- Designed and led the 2009 Facebook News Feed redesign and later built the Facebook ranking algorithm team.
- Took over Instagram from founders [[Kevin Systrom]] and [[Mike Krieger]] roughly 8 years before this interview (~2018).
- Known for being a public, accessible face for Instagram/Meta controversy — deliberately chose to engage publicly (starting on Twitter while running News Feed) rather than stay silent, believing debate about the platform "was going to happen with or without us."
- Argues [[Taste]] — an instinct for what AI is and isn't good at — is the most valuable and scarce skill in an AI-saturated world, and is personally "long on designers" because they tend to have taste.
- Describes Instagram/Meta's 2026 shift from large specialist teams to small generalist "[[Pods|pods]]" led by a new "[[Product Staff|product staff]]" role.
- Core hiring criteria (stable over time): grit, being a quick learner, and self-awareness (ability to take feedback). Newer emphasis: curiosity and willingness to "put yourself out there" and make mistakes publicly.
- Frames vision as "an articulation of the world/state of the product you want to get to" and strategy as "an opinionated path to achieve that vision" that must be reasonably contestable — see [[Vision Vs Strategy]]. Believes AI strategy advice is only useful when heavily steered with real business constraints, not asked for "lazily."
- Describes the best product leaders he's worked with as curators (of ideas, people, and teams) rather than singular "idea machines"; team chemistry/trust is "more art than science" but matters as much as individual competence.
- On Instagram's ranking algorithm: says a common misconception is that the algorithm has detailed semantic understanding of users; in reality it mostly relies on illegible high-dimensional embedding vectors. Championed a feature called "[[Your Algorithm|Your Algorithm]]" that uses an LLM to translate embedding-space clusters into human-readable descriptions of a user's inferred interests, and lets users adjust them.
- Believes AI-generated content will be a net tailwind for Instagram: as synthetic content becomes abundant, people will increasingly seek out human creativity, authenticity, and point-of-view — plans to label AI content/accounts rather than filter out AI content itself. Favorite AI content creators mentioned: "Plastic Dream Sequence" and "If Only AI."
- Says Instagram intentionally does not use token-spend leaderboards for engineers; treats AI compute/token budget as a constrained resource similar to GPUs, headcount, and opex budgets. See [[Token Budgeting]].
- Recounts two major public failures: [[Facebook Home]] (Android/HTC hardware fork, his first PM project) and building the first version of [[Reels]] on top of [[Stories]] (2019) instead of as a standalone product — a decision he says left Instagram out of position when [[TikTok]] exploded during the pandemic.
- Discusses handling public backlash (including the Cambridge Analytica lawsuit era and the 2021 feed/video-viewer redesign backlash) by trying to proactively communicate trade-offs rather than expecting to "win over" critics.
- Has three kids (ages 10, 8, 6); enforces screen-time boundaries (earned iPad time, e.g. via homework), approves apps himself, and has started teaching his 10-year-old to "[[Vibe Coding|vibe code]]" (using Claude Code) to build AI literacy.
