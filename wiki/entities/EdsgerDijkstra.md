## Definition
Edsger Dijkstra was a pioneering computer scientist who observed the first software crisis in the late 1960s/early 1970s. He noted that as hardware power grew, society's demand for software grew proportionally, leaving programmers to bridge the gap.

## Key Information
- Observed the original software crisis in the late 1960s/early 1970s
- Famously stated (paraphrased): "When we had a few weak computers, programming was a mild problem; now we have gigantic computers, programming has become a gigantic problem"
- Noted that as hardware power grew by a factor of a thousand, society's wants for software grew in proportion
- His observation is used by Jake Nations to frame the recurring pattern of software crises across generations
- His generation faced the crisis by creating the discipline of software engineering
