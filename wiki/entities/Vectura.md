## Definition

UK inhaler-therapeutics company (spun out of the University of Bath), publicly listed on the London Stock Exchange, cited by [[Eric Ries]] in *Incorruptible* as a real-world cautionary tale about **[[Shareholder Primacy]]** and the danger of never having written mission-protective provisions into a corporate charter.

## Key Information

- Made inhaler therapeutics for asthma and COPD; went public on the London Stock Exchange.
- [[Philip Morris]] bid to acquire Vectura at 165 pence per share (reportedly seeking to diversify beyond nicotine, framed by Ries as "we know a lot about inhaling things"); a private equity firm separately bid 155 pence per share; the board also had the option to remain independent, with no operational problems forcing a sale.
- The UK public and the British Thoracic Society publicly opposed the sale on the grounds that a tobacco company owning a respiratory-health company was self-evidently value-destroying, but Vectura's board concluded they had a fiduciary duty to accept the highest bid and did so.
- Within roughly three years of the ~£1.1 billion acquisition, Philip Morris took a $900 million write-down on Vectura and disposed of the company for parts; per Ries, "it doesn't exist anymore."
- Used by Ries as proof that the seemingly hypothetical "what if the most evil company you can imagine tried to buy yours" scenario is not hypothetical — it is a legally live obligation under standard shareholder-primacy corporate charters.
