## Definition

Philip Su is a software engineer who was promoted to Distinguished Engineer (IC9) at Meta, one of the few to reach that level. He previously spent 12 years at Microsoft, later joined OpenAI, and is known for his deliberate career choices including switching between IC and management tracks multiple times.

## Key Information

- Philip Su was one of the few engineers promoted to IC9 (Distinguished Engineer) at Meta, which is three levels above Staff (E6)
- He started his career at Microsoft, sprinting through the ladder to the equivalent of E7 at a young age, crediting luck, talent, and hard work
- At Microsoft, he had a sleeping bag in his office, slept at the office regularly, and followed Da Vinci's polyphasic sleep theory (waking at 3 AM to code, sleeping again at 6 AM)
- He was on the Tablet PC team at Microsoft with three distinguished engineers on a team of ~20 people, describing it as rare talent density
- He switched between IC and management tracks at least six times in his career, keeping the door open both ways by continuing to code even while managing
- Four separate times, a previous manager of his asked to report to him — he became their manager
- He was promoted to E9 as Site Director of Meta London, growing the office from 12 engineers to ~400-500 people over 4-5 years
- When switching back to IC at Oculus, he was deliberately re-leveled down from E9 to E7 (the level he joined at 8 years earlier), which he welcomed
- His philosophy: "it feels so much better to be performing well at a level than it feels to be barely treading water at a level"
- He joined OpenAI because he believed AI would be as big or bigger than the internet's impact on humanity, and he preferred joining the market leader
- He described OpenAI (~120 engineers) as having the highest talent density of any group he'd worked with
- Philip is ethnically Chinese and came from a culture where managers were more respected, which initially drove him toward management
- He bought coffee for the entire company at Facebook on multiple occasions, including spending over $23,000 on his departure
- He gave 100% of his Facebook IPO earnings to charity, feeling it was luck rather than something he deserved
- He founded Outeré, a Seattle nonprofit building free software for global health (funded by Gates Foundation), deployed in Nigeria, Kenya, and South Africa for malaria rapid testing
- He created "The Great Post Explorer" inside Facebook, a tool to rank and expose timeless internal posts
- He produced the podcast miniseries "Peak Salvation" about working at Amazon's flagship warehouse over peak season
- His writing is widely respected within Meta; his essay "As Good As It Gets" was legendary internally
- He describes himself as a generalist, not a specialist, and believes generalists are more valuable at small companies
- He believes he was a "bad manager" early on — at 24, he had to fire a 40-year-old, and was too immature to manage people more experienced than him
- He identifies giving feedback as his biggest management weakness — he pulls punches and downplays importance
- His advice to his younger self: spend more time clarifying personal values before pursuing goals; don't be "the dog that caught the car"
