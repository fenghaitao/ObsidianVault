## Definition

Early-2000s payments company where [[Keith Rabois]] served as EVP Business Development and Policy; famous for the "PayPal Mafia" — an unusually dense concentration of talent (assembled largely by [[Peter Thiel]] and [[Max Levchin]]) that went on to found or fund a generation of major tech companies over the following 25 years.

## Key Information

- Had roughly 254 people in its Mountain View office at acquisition; Rabois estimates the org contained 12-17 "barrels" (people who could independently drive an initiative to completion) — an unusually high ratio he credits as the root of the Mafia's outsized long-term impact. See [[Barrels And Ammunition]].
- Hiring model relied heavily on Peter Thiel's and Max Levchin's personal/Stanford networks rather than open interviewing — effective given an unusually talent-rich network, though Rabois cautions it doesn't generalize to companies without one.
- Rabois's own early hiring struggles at PayPal (roughly 50/50 good-to-mediocre hires) and the resulting feedback from [[David Sacks]] (about demonstrating hiring "leverage") led him to develop the [[Undiscovered Talent]] philosophy that shaped the rest of his career.
