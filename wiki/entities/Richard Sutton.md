## Definition

Richard Sutton is a famous AI researcher known for "The Bitter Lesson" — an essay arguing that general methods that leverage computation ultimately outperform human-knowledge-based approaches in AI.

## Key Information

- Sutton appeared on a podcast episode with "Dark Cesh" (likely Dwarkesh Patel) where he discussed how LLMs may be approaching a dead end
- Sutton argued that LLMs may plateau because of the way they learn
- Edwin Chen is in the camp that believes something new beyond LLMs will be needed to reach AGI — aligned with Sutton's view that models need to mimic the many different ways humans learn
- Boris Cherny cites the Bitter Lesson as a core principle for Claude Code's design: always bet on the more general model; scaffolding gains (10-20%) get wiped out by the next model version
- Boris's corollary: "the more general model will always outperform the more specific model"
