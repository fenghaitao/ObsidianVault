## Definition

[[Snapchat]]'s AR hardware platform, first launched in 2014 as a camera-off-the-phone device; evolved over 12 years (adding depth-sensing, a display, and — as of the 2024 generation — a full developer operating system) into "Specs," a consumer AR glasses product launching the same year as episode 12.

## Key Information

- Original 2014 motivation: get the camera out of people's pockets so they could capture "snaps" more naturally in the world.
- Roadmap: added a second camera for depth, then a display to overlay digital content, then (2024 generation) a full operating system letting third-party developers build full-featured software for the device, in preparation for consumer launch.
- Design philosophy (per [[Evan Spiegel]]): anchors AR content in the physical world/field of view rather than using a small heads-up corner display — Spiegel is critical of heads-up-display-style glasses (e.g., looking down/away from the person you're talking to to check a notification).
- Framed by Spiegel as central to Snap's [[Software Is Not A Moat]] strategy — vertically-integrated hardware is far harder for competitors to replicate than software features.
- Includes multiplayer/shared AR experiences designed to bring people together rather than isolate them individually (contrasted with solo VR headset use).
- Spiegel's kids are described as beta testers for the product.
