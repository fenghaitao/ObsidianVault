## Definition

Ephemeral (24-hour), chronologically-ordered content format originally invented by [[Snapchat]] (per [[Evan Spiegel]]); [[Instagram]] later built its own widely-copied version, which had significant momentum by 2019 and led Instagram to build the first version of [[Reels]] on top of it.

## Key Information

- Origin (Snapchat, per Spiegel): designed in response to two contradictory customer signals — people wanted an easy way to mass-share (they were asking for a "send all" button), but disliked the pressure of permanent, publicly-metriced, reverse-chronological social feeds. Spiegel's team synthesized both into something new rather than building literally what was requested: ephemeral, un-metriced (no public likes/comments), and told in forward chronological order (unlike the reverse-chronological norm of the era).
- Instagram's version had a relatively low read-through rate — more Stories content posted than most users had time to consume — which meant most early Reels content posted through Stories went unseen before disappearing.
- Building [[Reels]] on top of Instagram's Stories (instead of as a standalone product) is cited by [[Adam Mosseri]] as a major strategic mistake that left Instagram out of position against [[TikTok]]'s 2020 growth.
- Widely copied across the industry after Snapchat's invention — one of several Snapchat-originated formats (alongside swipe-based navigation and camera-as-primary-interface) that became industry standard.
