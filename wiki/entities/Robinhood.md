## Definition

Robinhood is a commission-free stock and crypto trading app. Jay Jo worked there as a software engineer during the GameStop saga and its July 2021 IPO.

## Key Information

- Jay signed at Series C (~100 people) and joined at Series D; the early team had exceptional caliber — about a third of his new-grad class later raised venture money
- Rapid scaling exposed engineers to many systems and career opportunities, giving competent engineers domain expertise by default
- The on-call "overnight batch" (trade settlement and related batch jobs after each market close) was brutal, sometimes running to 4–5am and dangerously close to business issues
- Its codebase accumulated tech debt from rapid scaling and wasn't architected in the most stable way
- On Jan 28, 2021, Robinhood paused buys on meme stocks (GameStop, AMC, etc.) because T+2 settlement required billions in collateral, not to manipulate prices; public messaging was poor
- IPO'd in July 2021, unwinding employees' option "handcuffs"
- For Jay, Robinhood compensation netted roughly an order of magnitude more than equivalent big-tech years
