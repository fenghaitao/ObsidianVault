## Definition

AI researcher referenced via a tweet [[Lenny Rachitsky]] cites in episode 13, describing the divide between people who tried early chatbots (like early ChatGPT/Claude), found them unimpressive, and stayed cynical about AI's potential, versus people who've experienced AI's full power through coding tools and see something very different. Per [[Simon Willison]] (episode 17), originated the term "vibe coding."

## Key Information

- His tweet is used by Lenny to frame [[Cat Wu]]'s advice: actually use current AI tools for real work rather than judging AI's capability from an old first impression.
- Per Simon Willison: Karpathy's original definition of "vibe coding" was building software purely by vibes — not looking at the generated code at all, playing with the result, and iterating by feel. Karpathy framed this as good for fun/prototyping specifically; Willison argues the term later expanded beyond that original scope to cover all AI-assisted programming, which Willison thinks devalues it — see [[Vibe Coding]] and [[Agentic Engineering]].
- Per [[Lazar]] (episode 27): says he personally began vibe coding in July 2024, roughly seven months before Karpathy coined the term in early 2025, and had already been teaching it via YouTube and a paid course before it had a name — cited as evidence the practice existed and was being professionalized before the term caught on.
- Per [[Alexander Imbiricos]] (episode 39, Codex product lead): recounted for tweeting that [[GPT-5.1 Codex Max]] solves in an hour the "gnarliest bugs" he'd otherwise spend hours stuck on — cited as praise underscoring the model's step up in difficult-bug-solving capability.
