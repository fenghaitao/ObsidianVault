## Definition

Aristotle was an ancient Greek philosopher whose principles of rhetoric — ethos, pathos, and logos — are applied by Jacob Warwick in negotiation. Jacob notes that too many executives lean on credibility (ethos) and logic (logos) while neglecting emotion (pathos), which is where many deals are actually won.

## Key Information

- Ancient Greek philosopher (384-322 BC)
- Developed the three modes of persuasion: ethos (credibility), pathos (emotion), logos (logic)
- Jacob Warwick applies these in negotiation:
  - Ethos: credibility and authority
  - Pathos: emotional appeal — "where a lot of deals get unfair"
  - Logos: logical argument
- Jacob also references kairos: the rhetorical principle of opportune timing
- Jacob: "Too many executives lean on credibility and logic because we're trying to do that. We don't show emotion. We don't show the authentic side of ourselves in corporate"
