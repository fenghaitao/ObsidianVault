## Definition

Heroku is a cloud application-deployment platform where [[Max Schoening]] previously led design. Later acquired by Salesforce. Cited in this episode as an early example of a "[[Tiny Core]]" product and as a cautionary example in the "cloud wars" analogy Schoening uses for [[SaaS Apocalypse|SaaS's future]].

## Key Information

- Schoening: `git push heroku master` was Heroku's tiny core — a single simple command that turned "a thing on my computer" into a live URL at a time when deploying apps was genuinely hard. He calls it "the first Vercel."
- Cloud-wars analogy: [[Kubernetes]] ultimately became more successful than Heroku even though Schoening thinks Kubernetes was a worse user experience — because Heroku's pitch was "we'll replace your ops team," while Kubernetes's pitch was "we'll make your ops team superheroes, and we won't lock you into one cloud." He argues businesses ultimately want choice/optionality, which Kubernetes offered and Heroku didn't.
- Used as the basis for Schoening's prediction that SaaS will evolve similarly: individual layers commoditize over time, but businesses will keep resisting single-provider lock-in, so multi-provider "as a service" specialization persists.
