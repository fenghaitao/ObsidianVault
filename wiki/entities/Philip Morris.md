## Definition

Tobacco company used by [[Eric Ries]] in *Incorruptible* as the running hypothetical for "the most evil company in the world" test he applies with founders and leaders, and as the real-world acquirer of [[Vectura]].

## Key Information

- Ries's recurring thought experiment with founders/leaders: imagine the company you consider most evil (his own hypothetical, drawn from his pulmonologist father's view, is Philip Morris) offering to buy your company for $1 more per share than it's worth — most people instinctively say no, especially once told the buyer intends to use the acquisition to sell cigarettes to children; yet under standard shareholder-primacy corporate charters, the board would have a fiduciary duty to say yes.
- In reality, Philip Morris did acquire the UK inhaler-therapeutics company Vectura for roughly 165 pence per share (reported by Ries as ~£1.1 billion total), reportedly seeking to diversify beyond nicotine; within about three years Philip Morris took a $900 million write-down and disposed of Vectura for parts.
