## Definition

Oracle is the enterprise software company that acquired Sun Microsystems in 2009, a move Bryan Cantrill describes as an "invasion" and a traumatic event for Sun's engineers.

## Key Information

- While IBM and HP were jockeying to buy Sun punitively (each trying to sabotage the other), Oracle swept in and bought the company; the acquisition itself was not what Cantrill found controversial, but Oracle's culture was.
- Cantrill describes Oracle as "an octopus that knows how to feed itself" — focused on its own profitability, building a natural monopoly over its customers, asphyxiating competition, and raising the rent.
- Oracle, in his account, made promises to customers it did not keep and never earned their trust; "if you ask customers, 'do you trust Oracle?' the answer will be no."
- Sun, by contrast, always strived to do right by customers and never lied to them; that contrast left Cantrill feeling ashamed to work at Oracle, and he quit within roughly 45 days.
- During the acquisition, some Sun engineers literally lined up to be laid off for the severance, while new Oracle colleagues were resentful that some who quit got nothing.
