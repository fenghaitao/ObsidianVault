## Definition

Rahul is a software engineer and content creator who went from E3 to E6 at Meta in three years, a trajectory similar to Ryan L. Peterman. He is the founder of Taro, a company focused on career growth for software engineers, and runs a YouTube channel and teaches a course called "senior to staff."

## Key Information

- Panelist on the "Getting To Staff" discussions (Sept 2023 and Feb 2024)
- Founder of Taro, his own company which sponsored the Sept 2023 panel
- Previously at Meta/Facebook for four and a half years, where he went from Senior to Staff Engineer
- Before Meta, spent two and a half years at Pinterest, where he was rejected for promotion twice
- Prior to Pinterest, was at a startup that got acquired by Pinterest
- His staff promotion at Meta came from two projects: an internal debug-ability tool (saving thousands of engineering hours/year, becoming a platform for others) and re-architecting a calling system
- Advocates a Portfolio Of Bets approach: have 2-3 projects rather than betting everything on one
- Emphasized starting with the problem rather than the solution: "What are the most burning problems in this org?"
- Went from E3 to E6 at Meta in three years
- Runs a YouTube channel as his primary content platform
- Teaches a course literally called "senior to staff"
- Referenced the StaffEng.com chart for leveling framework
- Noted that developing the ability to see what matters and what is impactful is critical for senior engineers
- Emphasized looking at org incentives and published team plans to identify high-impact projects
- Worked on ads projects unrelated to his team to find substantial revenue opportunities
- Advocated for being explicit about promotion at every level, even asking about the level after next
- Observed that infrastructure teams have more senior promotion opportunities past L5
- Noted that product teams have fewer spots for L6+ because impact comes from leading large teams of engineers
- Reported working 50-60 hours per week during his staff promotion push, though acknowledged some get promoted on 40 hours
- Quoted by Steve Huynh: "Bad news that's delivered early is just news; bad news that's delivered late is terrible news" — advice on proactively surfacing issues with managers
- Spent 5 years at Stanford doing undergrad and master's, was a research assistant in Yuri Lusco's lab
- Joined the Stanford spinout startup Cos as employee #2 right out of college, despite having offers from Google, Facebook, Microsoft, Twitter, and Square
- The startup was acquired by Pinterest after 8 months in an acqui-hire; Rahul had to re-interview to get the offer
- Deliberately pivoted from recommendation systems to Android mobile development at Pinterest to be closer to the product and build side projects
- Was rejected for L3→L4 promotion twice at Pinterest, winning on appeal the second time after clarifying a misunderstanding about project duration
- A staff-level mentor from Facebook at Pinterest taught him to create sandbox Android apps for faster iteration, which was the key mindset shift that got him promoted
- Joined Meta as IC5 (senior), a promotion from his level at Pinterest through job hopping
- Worked on Portal, Meta's hardware video calling device, in the Building 8 division
- His IC6 promotion at Meta came from two projects: a debug tool that saved hundreds of engineering hours/month, and migrating Portal's video calling infrastructure back to Messenger's shared RTC infrastructure
- Served as TLM (Tech Lead Manager) at Meta but found the hybrid role unsustainable long-term
- Left Meta to found Taro with co-founder Alex, whom he met on the Portal team
- They went through Y Combinator, which Rahul recommends as a no-brainer for first-time founders without existing VC networks
- Published Android tutorials on YouTube starting in 2019, which gave him signal to leave and start a company
- Believes much of his career was luck, but that luck can be manufactured through genuine curiosity, keeping in touch with people, and sharing what you're working on
- Core career insight: "There are no gatekeepers" — you don't need permission to learn, build, or teach
- Advises new grads to take the standard path (big brand name company) unless they have exceptional opportunities and social proof
- Recommends job hopping early in career, but notes it becomes counterproductive at staff/principal levels where impact comes from relationships and trust
