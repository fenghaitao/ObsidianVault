## Definition

Zach Wilson is a data engineer and content creator who worked at Netflix, Airbnb, and Amazon. He runs DataExpert.io and teaches data engineering and data science.

## Key Information

- Featured in a solo career interview with Ryan L. Peterman (Nov 2024), detailing his journey from IC3 at Facebook to Staff Engineer at Airbnb by age 26
- Dual major in CS and Applied Math; started career as a data analyst at Think Big Analytics, where he learned Hadoop
- Interviewed at Google twice for Mobile roles and was rejected both times before a Facebook recruiter reached out via LinkedIn
- Hired at Facebook as IC3 Data Engineer in 2016; promoted to IC4 in just 5 months because Facebook recognized he was mis-leveled
- At Facebook, worked on the Growth team (Notifications) under manager Jender; developed the Reachability metric to prevent notification spam, built the first cross-app growth dashboard, and made the notification ML pipeline 90% cheaper
- Received two consecutive "Greatly Exceeds" ratings as IC4 but was denied E5 promotion because management questioned sustainability of his performance (working 50-60 hours/week)
- Left Facebook after being denied transition from Data Engineer to Software Engineer without a down-level, despite writing 90% of the JavaScript on his team
- Joined Netflix through former manager Jender, who hired him as Senior Data Engineer; transitioned to Senior Software Engineer within 6-12 months
- At Netflix, was thrust into a staff-level role interfacing with 11 teams, which led to burnout and a mental health break
- Netflix collapsed the Data Engineering org in 2019, firing Jender and the entire leadership chain; Zach quit with nothing lined up
- After a year off, started creating content on LinkedIn in December 2020 and interviewed at Facebook, Google, and Airbnb
- Leveraged competing offers to negotiate a Staff Engineer title at Airbnb (job description required 10+ years; he had 6)
- At Airbnb, completed a Pricing and Availability pipeline migration in one quarter (planned for four quarters), earning an "Exceeds" rating at staff level while working ~40 hours/week
- Argues interviewing is the highest-leverage career skill; more important than on-the-job impact
- His advice to his younger self: develop soft skills earlier, do not be one-dimensional, prioritize health, and do not eat dinner at work
- Panelist on the "Getting To Staff" discussions (Sept 2023 and Feb 2024)
- At Netflix, worked on a project called Asset Inventory — a graph database (Knowledge Graph) describing all applications, code bases, data sets, and employees at the company and how they're connected
- The Asset Inventory project addressed Netflix's microservices security challenge: with 3,000-5,000 apps, each with potential vulnerabilities, a unified view of the infrastructure was critical
- At Netflix, also wrote a Groovy script that cut deploy times by removing unused dependencies, saving 1-7 minutes per deploy (30-60 minutes per engineer per day)
- At Airbnb, was tech lead on pricing and availability (Marketplace) as a product-focused data engineer
- Staff title came from job hopping to Airbnb — Netflix didn't have a formal Staff title at the time, but the work was at staff level
- After Airbnb, left to do content creation full-time for about six months before the Sept 2023 panel
- Distinguishes between Vertical Impact (direct business impact) and Horizontal Impact (enabling team to move faster); staff engineers should do both
- At Airbnb, realized after six months that a stakeholder-driven certification project was a diminishing return and successfully advocated to deprioritize it
- Noted that at Airbnb, his stock grant of $1.5M over 4 years dropped to ~$600K when share price fell from $210 to $80
- At Amazon, worked from 2012 to 2021 and benefited from Amazon's stock growth
- Runs DataExpert.io with free content including SQL coding challenges
- Has been promoted only once in his career (Junior to mid-level); every other promotion came from job hopping
- Emphasizes learning to be concise as a communication skill, citing LinkedIn's algorithm as a training ground for conciseness
