## Definition

Social/mobile gaming company founded by [[Mark Pincus]] in 2007, starting from a deliberately small Facebook poker game. Creator of [[FarmVille]], [[CityVille]], [[Words With Friends]], [[Mafia Wars]], and [[Zynga Poker]]; became the proving ground for the [[Proven Better New]] product framework.

## Key Information

- Started in 2007 when video gaming was a $23B industry (today ~$280B); Pincus bet on "latent demand" for games among people who didn't consider themselves gamers.
- Produced 8 massive hits out of 10 major game launches — an unusually high hit rate credited to focus on two things: (1) mission ("connecting the world through games") expressed as adding social dimensionality — an "invest, express, connect" design loop — and (2) obsession with retention over virality.
- Reportedly the only consumer company tracking [[Day 365 Retention]] at the time; also built a proprietary [[Active Social Network]] (ASN) metric measuring reciprocal social interactions (turns taken, gifts exchanged) as a leading indicator of long-term retention.
- Case study in Proven Better New: for a FarmVille expansion pack ("English countryside"), instead of spending a $10M ad budget on outside advertising, the team tested art/marketing variants directly on the game board and ended up selling $19M in early-access "keys" — turning an advertising afterthought into product signal and revenue.
- Zynga's PMs correctly predicted that [[Sid Meier]]'s social-civilization Facebook game would fail despite great game design, because its first-time-user-experience wasn't a proven-quality onboarding flow — an example of "proven" being necessary even when "new" is excellent.
- Was, in Pincus's telling, "a victim of its own success": games were so viral on Facebook that non-playing friends found them annoying, even though the underlying products (FarmVille, CityVille) had the company's best installs/engagement/retention numbers. Words With Friends and Zynga Poker proved more enduring because they successfully transitioned to mobile.
- Internal management culture under Pincus included heavy micromanagement (e.g., two-hour daily standups tracking every team member's tasks at up to 50 employees) and the "[[Teaching Hospital Model]]" of mentorship.
