## Definition

Sam Lesson (referred to in the transcript as "Sam Lesson," likely the AI-transcribed rendering of Sam Lessin) is a partner at [[Slow Ventures]], former VP of Product at [[Facebook]], and two-time startup founder (one company was acquired by [[Airbnb]]). He is [[Lenny Rachitsky|Lenny's]] guest for an episode dedicated entirely to Silicon Valley etiquette, drawing on a book and in-person classes he has developed on the subject.

## Key Information

- Started teaching etiquette almost by accident: "it started as many things to do with a tweet that got escalated into an event... escalated into a book and a bunch of other stuff."
- Core framing: etiquette exists at the intersection of "hilarious and useful" — humor cuts through noise, but only works if there's "a deeper truth" underneath it.
- Central thesis (2025/2026 context): as software commoditizes and everyone is asking others to trust them with data and business, etiquette — knowing how to meet people where they are, build trust, and mirror expected behaviors — becomes a genuine competitive tool for founders, not a frivolous distraction. See [[Low Heart Rate]].
- Coined/popularized the framing that etiquette is "a skill for how to show up in a room with a low heart rate," contrasted with a scarcity-driven, "this is my one shot" transactional mindset. See [[Abundance Mindset]].
- Ran an in-person etiquette class for [[Y Combinator]] founders at the Four Seasons (certificates of completion, wardrobe consultants, caviar/wine tasting, plus core material on eye contact, handshakes, and low-heart-rate presence) — done partly "tongue-in-cheek" because [[Garry Tan]] "got very mad about this."
- Runs [[Slow Ventures]] alongside partner [[Kevin Colleran]], who is "maniacal" about junior-associate etiquette gestures (e.g., always offering to clear coffee cups when meeting with LPs).
- Married to [[Jessica Lessin]], founder of [[The Information]]; co-hosts a low-key podcast called *More or Less* with his wife and friends [[Dave Morin]] and Brett Morin, which he says "we would do... if no one was listening."
- Has three young children (ages 8, 6, 4) whom he is informally teaching etiquette at home, with mixed and often comic results (his six-year-old now stands whenever his wife comes to the table).
- Built [[Lettermeme]], a personal AI news aggregator that converts newsletters into daily cartoon summaries — his answer in the episode's "AI Corner" segment; built first via vibe coding (Cursor, DigitalOcean, Cloudflare) and later handed off to engineer friends to scale.
- In "Contrarian Corner," argues seed VCs backing AI-branded startups will lose enormous money because such businesses are too capital-intensive for seed-stage economics and are fundamentally commoditizing; cites [[OpenAI]]'s seed investors earning "only" ~25x on a $500B valuation as evidence even the era's defining company was a mediocre seed bet. Distinguishes this from believing every startup should use AI as infrastructure. Frames the AI hype cycle as the latest "Terra Nova" (frontier) narrative — see [[Terra Nova Narrative]].
- Life motto: "Carthage must burn," from a poster made during a Facebook hackathon in the Facebook Home era, evoking the intensity of that period.
- Lightning-round book recommendations: *[[The Ancient City]]* (via [[Marc Andreessen]]'s suggestion), *[[Man's Search for Meaning]]*, *[[Area 51 (book)|Area 51: An Uncensored History]]*, and especially *[[The Lessons of History]]* by [[Will Durant]] ("probably the most approachable non-obvious book I love").
- Reachable via email ("lesson[at]gmail") or his personal site with a writing-trained chatbot; says he has no formal newsletter signup page as a "contrarian growth strategy."
