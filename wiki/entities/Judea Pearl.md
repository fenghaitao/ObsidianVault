## Definition

Judea Pearl is an Israeli-American computer scientist and Turing Award winner best known for pioneering Bayesian networks and the formal theory of causality (the do-calculus and the Ladder of Causation).

## Key Information

- Interviewed by Ryan L. Peterman; grew up in pre-1948 Mandate Palestine and was educated by German emigré professors fleeing Hitler, who taught science chronologically and humanistically.
- Studied electrical engineering at the Technion and at Brooklyn Poly, while working at RCA Laboratories (David Sarnoff Research Laboratory) in Princeton on superconducting computer memories.
- Discovered the "Pearl vortex" — a permanent current that circulates in thin superconducting films — an early physics result that became his "footstep into immortality."
- Later ran an R&D group at Electronic Memories on plated-wire memory, then joined UCLA's computer science department in 1969/70 without even applying.
- Proved that alpha-beta pruning is optimal in game-tree search, a result that surprised Donald Knuth (transcribed "Klo").
- Developed Bayesian networks with Azaria Paz (transcribed "Aaria Paz"), grounding the graphoid theory that links the axioms of conditional independence to graph separation.
- Concluded that probability captures association but not causation, and built the mathematics of cause and effect — the do-calculus and the Ladder of Causation (association → intervention → counterfactuals).
- Argues LLMs summarize human-interpreted internet knowledge rather than investigating raw data, so they cannot reach AGI alone; advocates combining them with causal calculus plus an innate curiosity/drive for control.
- Advocates world models — deriving answers from a few axioms rather than memorizing question-answer pairs — and urges students to "rebel against your professors."
