## Definition

Electric vehicle and robotics company led by [[Elon Musk]]; maker of the [[Optimus]] humanoid robot and self-driving car software. Discussed as an example of both humanoid-robot development and supply-chain verticalization.

## Key Information

- Optimus is cited (alongside [[Figure]] and [[1X Neo]]) as one of the leading humanoid robot programs, though [[Caitlin Kalinowski]] considers all current humanoids to be advanced prototypes rather than at-scale products.
- Musk is credited with verticalizing Tesla's supply chain, which let the company redesign a PCB in record time to adapt to different silicon when a needed chip became hard to source — contrasted with the catastrophic redesign risk a company with a more classic supply chain would face in the same situation.
- Tesla's self-driving software is used as a case study of trust formation in autonomous systems: Lenny recounts his wife going from resistant to unwilling to drive any other car, and Kalinowski contrasts this with the harder trust problem for home robots (no "existence proof" the way Waymo has for safety data).
- An anecdote is shared about a self-driving Tesla changing a destination mid-drive based on a connected AI assistant's suggestion (see [[Nat Friedman]]).
- Per [[Qasar Younis]] (episode 22): Tesla's approach to autonomy ("L2++") — few sensors, cheap compute, no HD maps — is contrasted with the [[Waymo]] approach (many sensors, heavy compute, maps); Younis predicts L2++ becomes ubiquitous and near-free globally within 5-7 years, following the trajectory of car nav systems giving way to free CarPlay/Android Auto. See [[L2 Plus Plus Vs L4 Autonomy]].
- Younis, a Tesla owner, describes becoming "a huge convert" to its self-driving feature over just the past few months, from nerve-wracking to reliably good.
