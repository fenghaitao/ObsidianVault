## Definition
John Ousterhout is a computer scientist and author of "A Philosophy of Software Design," a book that provides principles for designing good software systems. His concepts of deep modules, shallow modules, and complexity are foundational to understanding software design quality.

## Key Information
- Author of "A Philosophy of Software Design"
- Defined complexity as "anything related to the structure of a software system that makes it hard to understand and modify the system"
- Introduced the concept of deep modules: modules with lots of functionality hidden behind a simple interface
- Introduced the concept of shallow modules: modules with little functionality but a complex interface
- Argued that good codebases have relatively few large deep modules with simple interfaces
- His work is used by Matt Pocock to argue that well-structured codebases are essential for effective AI-assisted development
- Deep modules make codebases testable because you can test at the simple interface without needing to understand internal complexity
