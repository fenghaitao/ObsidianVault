## Definition

Molly Graham is a management/scaling advisor and community-builder best known for the "[[Give Away Your Legos]]" career framework. She was an early employee at [[Google]] (2007) and [[Facebook]] (2008-2013), an early operating exec at [[Brett Taylor]]'s startup [[Quip]], and ran the first two years of the [[Chan Zuckerberg Initiative]]. She now runs [[Glue Club]], a community for leaders in fast-scaling, changing companies, and is the featured guest of episode 34 of [[Lenny's Podcast]] (a "greatest hits" collection of her frameworks).

## Key Information

### Career/scaling background (episode 34)
- Started in tech in 2007 at [[Google]] the week the iPhone launched; her communications department grew from 25 to 125 people in 9 months — her first direct experience of hypergrowth.
- Joined [[Facebook]] in 2008 (following [[Sheryl Sandberg]] and Elliot Schrage), when it had 80 million users, $270M revenue, and 500 employees — "smaller than MySpace," widely assumed to be heading toward a sale to Microsoft. Left in ~2013, six months after the IPO, when Facebook had 5,500 employees, $5B revenue, and over a billion users.
- Left Facebook to join [[Brett Taylor]]'s pre-launch startup [[Quip]], running everything outside product/engineering; the company was later sold to [[Salesforce]]. Describes this as teaching her the very different experience of building from zero-to-one versus "holding on for dear life" inside a scaling company.
- Helped [[Mark Zuckerberg]] and [[Priscilla Chan]] start the [[Chan Zuckerberg Initiative]], joining when it had 30 people (the week it acquired two companies) and growing it to 250 people within a year.
- First job out of college: led 75-day wilderness expeditions in Patagonia and Alaska for the [[National Outdoor Leadership School]] (NOLS), where she first learned the "[[Waterline Model]]."
- Describes her career pattern as only taking jobs she's "highly unqualified for," and cites this deliberate discomfort as the source of her "[[J Curve Versus Stairs]]" framework.

### Frameworks originated or popularized (episode 34)
- **[[Give Away Your Legos]]** — her signature framework: repeatedly hand off the scope you've mastered as a company scales, rather than clinging to it.
- **[[Bob The Monster]]** — externalizing the emotional reactions (fear, territoriality, impostor syndrome) that accompany organizational change into a named "monster," so as to notice but not act on them; her rule of thumb is to give any such reaction two weeks before treating it as signal rather than noise.
- **[[J Curve Versus Stairs]]** — the case for jumping into roles you're underqualified for (originally drawn on a whiteboard for her by [[Chamath Palihapitiya]]) instead of climbing predictable "stairs"; includes the idea of embracing being a "professional idiot" during the resulting fall.
- **[[Waterline Model]]** — diagnosing team problems structural-first ("snorkel before you scuba"); she teaches this in [[Glue Club]] and first learned it at NOLS.
- **[[Six Rules For Goals And Alignment]]** — six rules for goal-setting drawn largely from Facebook's three-goal (growth/engagement/revenue) discipline, including "[[Strategy Should Hurt]]" (a phrase she credits to [[Claire Hughes Johnson]]).
- **[[Serve The Business Not The People]]** — her mantra for hard people decisions (firing, reorgs): ask what you'd do with no emotions involved, then do that, optimizing only the kindness of delivery.
- **[[Escalation Is A Tool]]** — a rule she attributes to [[Mark Zuckerberg]]: escalate together (not "tattle") the moment two equal-power people are stuck.
- **[[Fifty Percent Growth Rule]]** — headcount growth beyond 100%/year causes silent role duplication and net slowdown; the "happiest" growth rate is ~50%/year.
- **[[Founder Personality Defines Culture]]** — ~80% of a company's culture is set by founder personality, not declared values; contrasts Google ("two PhD students' paradise") with Facebook ("a 19-year-old hacker's dorm room"). Her version of "founder mode": build a company that would make the decision the founder would make when not in the room.
- **[[Do Not Promise What You Cannot Control]]** — never promise stability/titles you don't control; cites Claire Hughes Johnson's "letter bomb" metaphor for broken promises.
- Also discusses: firing well is as important a skill as hiring well (the best hirers are right only ~50% of the time); invest disproportionate energy in high performers via incremental "experiments" that expand their scope, not just in strugglers.

### Career-identity arc (episode 34)
- After ~10-15 years "proving" herself (title-chasing, what she calls "LinkedIn crushes" — jobs exciting to announce publicly but not to actually do), Graham turned down multiple C-suite offers, repeatedly asking herself "what does this get you that you don't already have?"
- Now runs [[Glue Club]] (glueclub.com) and writes a Substack called "Lessons" — describes her current focus as building safe spaces for leaders to learn, grow, and find sanity amid organizational chaos.
- Credits colleague [[Amie Vora]] (now head of product at [[Anthropic]]) with the framing "all advice is just someone telling you what they did" as her own guiding stance on giving advice.

### Prior wiki cross-reference (episode 18, Claire Vo)
- Cited in the Claire Vo episode as originator of the "[[Waterline Model]]," referenced there via a guest post on [[Lenny Rachitsky]]'s newsletter, used as an analogy for diagnosing underperforming AI agents.
