## Definition

Figma is the design/prototyping tool discussed by [[Max Schoening]] (Head of Product, [[Notion]]) as having an ambiguous usage trend among Notion's design team as AI coding tools rise — some designers use it less in favor of AI-driven prototyping, others still rely on it heavily.

## Key Information

- Schoening initially raises Figma usage as "trending down" within the design org, then walks this back: "I'm actually not positive that Figma is trending down. I think it's more that there is... two camps" — he floats the possibility of a Jevons-paradox effect where Figma usage is actually increasing even as vibe coding also increases.
- Notes his own aversion to "rivalry discourse" framings (e.g., Figma-vs-coding as a zero-sum fight), by analogy to his dislike of the "for Anthropic to win, OpenAI needs to lose" narrative.
- Early example of designers-writing-code at Notion: designing chat interfaces directly in Figma felt like working with a "dead fish" (a static image of something inherently dynamic) — referencing [[Brett Victor]]'s talk "Stop Drawing Dead Fish" — which motivated Notion's shift to prototyping chat UIs in a small LLM-friendly codebase instead.
- Cited as part of the broader [[Automation Vs Augmentation]] question: is direct manipulation (Figma-style) going away because agents now do the manipulating, or does faster inference bring direct manipulation back in a new form?

### Figma Make (episode 28 — Dr. Becky Kennedy)

- [[Dr. Becky Kennedy]] (CEO of [[Good Inside]]) describes using "Figma Make" as her primary tool for turning a product idea (e.g., a new onboarding flow) into a full visual prototype — UX design, copy, tone — rather than pitching it verbally, because she doesn't like having an idea she "can't fully see out." Her team knows this is how she thinks and will sometimes discard the resulting build entirely after seeing it, which she's fine with.
- She notes Andrew Hogan, who works on insights at Figma and is "a big Good Inside member," offered to have her record a testimonial video for the Figma team about how much she uses Figma Make.
- She also plays around with [[Replit]] and [[Lovable]] (shown to her by her team), and uses [[Claude]] and [[ChatGPT]] for rapid, unstructured ("vomit everything out") prompting.

### Episode 27 — Lazar's design-taste anecdote

- [[Lazar]] ([[Lovable]]'s first official "vibe coding engineer") recounts trying to copy a seemingly simple gradient background out of a Figma file made by one of Lovable's designers, only to discover the "simple" gradient was actually 50 layered colors at varying opacity levels — a formative moment in his understanding of the gap between "good enough" and genuinely world-class design craft. See [[Taste]].

### Per episode 41 ([[Tomer Cohen]], LinkedIn CPO)

- Sponsor segment for "Figma Make": pitched as a code-backed prototyping tool that uses a team's existing design building blocks/system, letting non-engineers build functional prototypes rather than only leaving feedback on static designs.
- Cohen notes LinkedIn experimented with multiple design-agent tools (Figma, Subframe, Magic Patterns) for its [[Full-Stack Builder Model]] design agent, finding different teams gravitated to different tools depending on function and prior familiarity — an unresolved tension LinkedIn is still working to converge.
