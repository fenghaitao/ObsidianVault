## Definition
Tony Zhao is a robotics researcher who was lead author of the ACT (Action Chunking with Transformers) / ALOHA paper, with Sergey Levine as a co-author.
## Key Information
- Levine credits Zhao with "99.9% of the work" on the ACT paper (a "shameless plug" since Levine is a co-author).
- The paper's insight was not a new mathematical technique but "putting together the right pieces": a low-cost bimanual setup built from ~$7,000 hobbyist arms (Trossen Robotics, transcribed "Trusson Robotics") with a leader-follower teleop rig.
- Showed that with a fairly straightforward transformer-based model, one could collect demonstration data for extremely dexterous tasks (replacing batteries in a remote control, putting a shoe on a mannequin foot for an assistive task) previously thought to require sophisticated, expensive hardware.
- He open-sourced the ACT code, which became a standard "starter kit" for robotic learning.
