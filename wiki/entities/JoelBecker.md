## Definition
Joel Becker is a researcher and member of technical staff at METR (Model Evaluation and Threat Research), where he works on measuring AI capabilities and reconciling benchmark evidence with real-world economic impact studies.

## Key Information
- Presented on the gap between benchmark-style AI capability measurements and economic-style field experiments
- Co-authored METR's time horizon paper measuring AI ability to complete long tasks and the RCT paper on AI's effect on developer productivity
- Noted that the remarkably steady exponential trend in time horizon was "much more steady than we were anticipating"
- Expressed skepticism that developers simply need more familiarity with AI tools to see productivity gains
- Openly acknowledged that the RCT result could be wrong ("we messed something up") and that the study is small
- Also involved in hiring at METR for research engineers, research scientists, and director of operations
- Presented a follow-up talk exploring compute-capability proportionality, monitored vs. unmonitored capabilities, and new evidence sources for triangulating AI capabilities
- Discussed METR's unpublished greenfield hackathon that found only ~4 percentile point improvement with AI allowed vs. disallowed
- Identified data science as a frontier where AI may struggle more than software engineering due to messy corporate data environments
- Endorsed the analogy that AI models are like neurodivergent individuals: extremely capable at specific tasks but struggling with a world designed for humans
