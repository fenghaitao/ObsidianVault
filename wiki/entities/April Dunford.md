## Definition

April Dunford (rendered "Jenna Ael" earlier in this episode's transcript, an ASR mis-hearing) is a positioning expert and prior [[Lenny's Podcast]] guest, cited by [[Lenny Rachitsky]] for her advice on framing sales pitches around competitive advantage rather than pain/problem-solving.

## Key Information

- Her cited tip: rather than pitching "here's the pain/problem we solve," focus on "here's how you'll be better than your competitors" — since customers respond more to relative competitive advantage than to problem-solution framing alone.
- Connected by [[Jeanne DeWitt Grosser]] to her own stat that ~80% of customers buy to avoid pain/reduce risk rather than chase upside — Dunford's and Grosser's framings both push against defaulting to an "art of the possible" upside pitch, which mostly resonates with fellow founders rather than typical enterprise buyers.
- Also cited (via a career-risk framing Rachitsky recalls from her episode) for the idea that adopting a new vendor is a significant career bet for the buyer — if it goes badly, it can hurt the buyer's own standing, which is a major hidden factor in enterprise buying decisions.
