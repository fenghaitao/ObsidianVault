## Definition
Sergey Levine (transcribed "Sergey Lavine" in this transcript) is a leading robotics and machine learning researcher, professor, and co-founder of the robotics company Physical Intelligence.
## Key Information
- Described as "one of the world's leading robotics researchers."
- Co-founder and leader at Physical Intelligence, where he works on general-purpose robot foundation models.
- Academic home is UC Berkeley (his colleague Stuart Russell worked on AI safety "over a decade ago").
- Early in his career he ran Google's "arm farm" project — a conference room full of robot arms grasping millions of objects — which learned general grasping but was hard to take "to the next level."
- Co-author (with lead author Tony Zhao) of the influential ACT / ALOHA paper on low-cost imitation learning.
- Core views: scale works but you must "scale the right thing"; robotics is still in the pre-scaling "puzzle piece" stage; the hard problem is generalization; data is like an education program, not a fungible commodity; reinforcement learning can fine-tune the last few percent of reliability.
- Skeptical of "robots as mechanical people" and of "end of human labor," drawing analogies to the proliferation of personal computers and to coding agents that empower rather than replace people.
- Reflecting on his younger self, his advice is to "take prior knowledge more seriously" — broad prior knowledge scaffolds learning rather than something to reject in favor of learning from scratch.
