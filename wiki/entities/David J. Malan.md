## Definition

David J. Malan is a Harvard professor best known for transforming CS50 into a world-class, free online computer-science course. The episode title transcribes his name as "David J Malan" (no period); the correct spelling is "David J. Malan."

## Key Information

- First took CS50 as a sophomore in 1996 under Brian Kernighan, coming from a government concentration.
- Taught "Introduction to Computers and the Internet" at Harvard Extension School as a senior in 1999, filmed on VHS.
- Earned a PhD in computer science at Harvard, then took over CS50 in 2007, expecting to fill in "for just 1 year" — 19 years ago.
- Championed putting CS50 online, from RealVideo/RealAudio streaming to iPod/iTunes podcasting and later video.
- Philosophy: use "memorable moments" (phone-book tearing, students sorting on stage) to make material latch onto memory; much of his energy comes "from a place of insecurity" about a bored audience.
- Kept C in CS50 because it sits close to the hardware and forces students to build data structures themselves, teaching first principles.
- Spent a sabbatical semester as "professor in residence" at GitHub, advocating for educational use cases in tools like VS Code.
- Biggest regret: not calming down and exploring more (e.g., dramatic arts, archaeology) earlier.
