## Definition
John Hennessy (Stanford) and David Patterson (UC Berkeley) are the two computer architects who championed RISC together and co-authored "Computer Architecture: A Quantitative Approach," the standard graduate textbook that popularized a quantitative, measurement-based approach to the field.
## Key Information
- Patterson calls Hennessy "my friend John Hennessy at Stanford"; the two jointly argued against the prevailing sophisticated (complex) instruction sets of the 1970s-80s, believing compilers could handle simpler instruction sets.
- Their textbook came out in 1990 and ran at least three editions before power was discussed at all, because Dennard scaling kept microprocessors at tens of watts through that period.
- Hennessy later wrote his own career-advice book from his Stanford presidency; Patterson relays one lesson from it — "you're only going to be remembered for the five or six things you've done in your life, not for the hundreds of little things."
- Together they represent the shift from intuition-based computer architecture ("how many angels on the head of a pin") to a quantitative, evidence-based discipline.
