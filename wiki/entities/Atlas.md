## Definition

Atlas is OpenAI's AI-native web browser, designed to provide contextual AI assistance by being embedded in the rendering engine rather than relying on accessibility APIs or screenshots. It launched on Mac first, with a Windows version in development.

## Key Information

- Built by OpenAI as a first-party browser to enable contextual AI assistance
- The motivation: rather than hacking other desktop software with varied accessibility support, or relying on slow/unreliable screenshots, Atlas is in the rendering engine and can extract whatever it needs to help
- Alexander Imbiricos worked on Atlas for a period but no longer works on it directly
- The vision is to enable "contextual actions" — like video games where pressing X near an object does the right thing, Atlas surfaces AI help at the moment you need it
- Part of the vision is to avoid push notification fatigue (thousands of AI notifications per day) by surfacing help contextually instead
- Users have clear control: open things in the AI browser when you want AI help, use your regular browser when you don't
- The Atlas team are power users of Codex; tasks that previously took 2-3 weeks for 2-3 engineers now take 1 engineer, 1 week
- Building a browser is "really hard" — the team built many difficult systems
- The Windows version is in development, and the team is helping make Codex better on Windows
- Lenny Rachitsky tried Atlas but found the AI-only search experience lacking, preferring to sometimes just Google; OpenAI later added standard search as an option
