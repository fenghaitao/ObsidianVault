## Definition

Travel/hospitality company founded by [[Brian Chesky]], referenced as an example of belief-driven (not hope-driven) product launches.

## Key Information

- Per [[Benedict Evans]] (episode 07): used, alongside [[Uber]], as an example of a company that "redefines the question" rather than just doing "the old thing but more" — cited as one of Marc Andreessen's classic examples that a company doesn't have to sell software to the incumbent industry (Airbnb doesn't sell software to hotels) to hugely disrupt it.
- Evans notes Airbnb's actual measured market impact on hotels is comparatively marginal (unlike Uber's impact on taxis) — citing his wife's business-travel needs (hotel amenities, room service, gym, predictable location) as a case where Airbnb structurally isn't a substitute, illustrating his broader point that "it depends" and value migration is never as clean as the simple platform-shift narrative suggests.
- Per [[Keith Rabois]] (episode 15): an early investor, he cites Chesky's Airbnb pitch as a formative example of trusting founder insight over customer research — the number of Craigslist listings offering a room for rent (a modest but "real" number, ~30 in the Bay Area) was, to Rabois, stronger evidence than any customer survey could have provided, since it reflected actual behavior rather than stated intent. Rabois also cites Chesky's post-success operating style ("founder mode": relentlessly pushing the team even when metrics are strong) as a model for offsetting organizational complacency.
- Per [[Sam Lesson]] (episode 32): acquired one of Lesson's startups. During the sale process, his [[Slow Ventures]] partner [[Kevin Colleran]] made a point of building a strong relationship with Airbnb's EA and office manager, which Lesson credits with materially helping the deal go smoothly — cited as an etiquette example ("the gatekeepers... it helps you get the thing").
