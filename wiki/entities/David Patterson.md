## Definition
David Patterson is a Turing Award-winning computer architect, UC Berkeley professor, and co-author (with John Hennessy) of the standard textbook "Computer Architecture: A Quantitative Approach." He championed RISC and, working for Google, led the Tensor Processing Unit (TPU) effort.
## Key Information
- Introduced as a Turing Award winner "famous for his contributions to computer architecture," with whom the host discusses the RISC vs CISC debate and modern GPU/TPU architecture.
- Was a co-proponent of RISC with his friend John Hennessy at Stanford; together they argued against the prevailing complex instruction sets of IBM and DEC in the 1970s-80s.
- Co-authored the textbook "Computer Architecture: A Quantitative Approach" with Hennessy; it came out in 1990 and didn't even discuss power as an issue for its first three editions (the third appeared around 2000), because Dennard scaling kept microprocessors at tens of watts.
- Works for Google; credits Google as the first big company to grasp ML/AI's potential and fear being swamped with demand, leading it to build the custom TPU hardware that debuted in 2016.
- Career at UC Berkeley: he went from a UCLA graduate student straight to Berkeley professor and recalls impostor syndrome — "I'm probably not going to get tenure, so I should just have a good time" — which he says gave him the right attitude early on.
- Personal background: as a small "youngest kid" he wrestled in high school and college, which he credits for physical self-confidence and the courage to stand up for ideas — "fortune favors the bold" — while cautioning that "friends come and go but enemies accumulate."
- Advice distilled across his talks ("How to give a bad talk," "How to have a bad career") and his paper "Life Lessons from the First Half Century of My Career" (16 lessons): put family first, optimize for happiness over wealth, make time for fun, and concentrate on finishing a few big things.
- Relationship advice ("nine magic words," three sentences no substitutions): "I was wrong. You were right. I love you." He has been married 59 years.
- His one stated regret: as SIGARCH chair in the 1990s he was unaware that men were harassing young women at the conferences; had he known, he would have intervened — he notes Sarita Adve (transcribed and garbled as "Serita") also recalled being unaware.
