## Definition

Ivan Sutherland (transcribed "Ivan Southerntherland") is the computer scientist who created Sketchpad in the early 1960s, a groundbreaking interactive computer graphics program.

## Key Information

- Creator of Sketchpad, a light-pen graphics program from the early 1960s that could draw like a modern CAD program and apply constraints (e.g., "this line must be perpendicular to that line") that even modern CAD tools only added recently.
- Casey's "Big Oops" talk argues Sutherland solved a hard architectural problem (operating on one property across many different objects) in assembly and punch cards with no editing or debugging tools.
- Casey reads Sketchpad's architecture as effectively an entity-component system — a "good architectural idea" whose DNA was present "right away" but which the OOP era failed to carry forward, instead deriving its hierarchies-and-encapsulation takeaway via Alan Kay's reading.
- Casey credits Sutherland and Sketchpad with the constraint/geometry features he says AutoCAD only got around 2007.
