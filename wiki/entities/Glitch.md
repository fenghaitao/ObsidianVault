## Definition

Glitch was a video game developed by Stewart Butterfield's company that was ultimately shut down. The internal IRC-based communication tool used to build Glitch became the foundation for Slack.

## Key Information

- The internal IRC tool used by the team was iteratively improved and became the prototype for Slack
- Slack had an "enormous advantage" from being developed over several years without explicitly working on it — only doing the minimum features that were "absolutely guaranteed to be successful"
- When Glitch was shut down, the company still had ~$9 million left and the team still enjoyed working on the game
- Butterfield felt he had "exhausted every non-ridiculous long shot idea to make it commercially successful"
- Shutting down Glitch was emotionally difficult: users lost their community, identities were lost, and people lost their jobs
- Andy Grove's book uses Glitch and Slack as an example of a "smart fold" — when expected value diminishes to the point where the alternative looks more attractive
