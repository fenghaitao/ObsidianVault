## Definition

Autonomous vehicle company cited by Tony Fadell as an example of a "hardware platform" business — an electric car loaded with sensors and software — that could become a platform for further innovation (e.g., delivery). [[Cat Wu]] separately cites it as her favorite recently-discovered product.

## Key Information

- Described by Fadell as an electric car with extensive sensors — "an incredible hardware platform with an incredible software platform," even if not as visually streamlined as a smartphone.
- Cited as an example of why atoms-plus-software companies have durable differentiation and staying power that pure software-only companies increasingly lack.
- Fadell speculates it will become a platform for other applications beyond ride-hailing, such as delivery.
- Cat Wu is a twice-daily rider (commuting to/from Anthropic): values not feeling rushed at curbside pickup and being able to take work calls privately without the social awkwardness of doing so in front of a human driver — worth a 2x price premium to her over Uber/Lyft. Notes it has changed workplace vernacular at Anthropic ("is Waymo here?" replacing "call a rideshare").

- Per [[Qasar Younis]] (episode 22): its approach to autonomy (many sensors, heavy compute, high-fidelity maps — "L4") is contrasted with Tesla's cheaper "L2++" approach; Waymo's product works better but only within a geographically constrained, heavily-mapped area. See [[L2 Plus Plus Vs L4 Autonomy]].
- Per [[Edwin Chen]] (episode 40): took his first ride shortly before the episode and called it "magical," saying it "really felt like a living future" — cited in the lightning round as a recently discovered product he loves.
