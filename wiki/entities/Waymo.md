## Definition
Waymo is a self-driving car company (subsidiary of Alphabet/Google) that operates autonomous ride-hailing services. It is cited as a real-world example of AI operating in the physical world.

## Key Information
- Used as an analogy for how AI agent evaluation works: Waymo's development followed an iterative eval loop — first driving straight roads, then left turns, then left turns with pedestrians, each requiring curated datasets of hard examples.
- The same eval-driven development approach used in self-driving applies to building AI agents today.
- Operates in San Francisco and other cities, expanding to the entire Bay Area
- Used as an analogy in METR's talk: self-driving got close quickly but the last bit of reliability took much longer — similar pattern may apply to AI coding agents

- In the 2024 AI Engineer Summit keynote, Waymo was used as the canonical example of the long timeline from demo to production: the first neural-network-driven car was built in 1988, Andrej Karpathy took his first demo drive in 2013, and Waymo received its first driverless permit in San Francisco in the 2020s. The point: going from demo to production takes time, and the same applies to LLM applications.
