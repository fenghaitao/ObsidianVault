## Definition

Food-delivery company [[Keith Rabois]] invested in early, cited as his central case study for why founder insight (not customer surveys) drives great consumer businesses. See [[Undiscovered Talent]] and Rabois's broader skepticism of customer research.

## Key Information

- Founding pitch (from Tony Xu and Evan, as recounted by Rabois): the key data point wasn't customer demand surveys but the fact that 93% of restaurants in the US didn't offer delivery — a number Rabois found immediately compelling ("seems like it should be higher than seven [percent that did]").
- Rabois connects the idea to [[Andrew Mason]]'s (Groupon founder) famous quip that phones should have two buttons, "I'm bored" and "I'm hungry" — DoorDash, in Rabois's framing, is the "I'm hungry" button.
- Rabois explicitly says no customer ever told the founders they wanted "a button on their phone to order delivery" — the validation instead came from talking to restaurants (would you display this placard?) and running small experiments on delivery economics/density, not asking end consumers what they wanted.
- Cited (with [[Square]]) as an example where Rabois's "don't talk to customers" advice applies even in a marketplace/SMB context, not just pure consumer.
