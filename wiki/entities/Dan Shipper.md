## Definition

Dan Shipper is CEO and co-founder of [[Every]], a media/software company he describes as one of the most AI-forward startups, built around live-using and writing about the newest AI tools as they emerge. He is a repeat guest on [[Lenny's Podcast]].

## Key Information

- A year before this episode, Dan predicted (initially as an "offhand hot take") that people were sleeping on [[Claude Code]], especially for non-engineering work — a prediction that proved highly accurate and helped fuel Every's product [[Cora]] and a popular newsletter post.
- Describes Every's method for spotting trends: don't prognosticate, "live in it" — everyone at Every (now ~30 people, up from 15 a year earlier) is an AI early adopter across engineering, design, writing, editing, sales, and customer service; Every gets early/beta access to models as a reviewer and "tastemaker."
- Coined/uses the "[[Reach Test]]" — whether you organically reach for a tool each morning — as his personal signal of genuine product-market fit for an AI tool.
- Main predictions in this episode: (1) work bifurcates into a company-wide "super agent" (reached mostly via [[Slack]]) plus (2) most work happening inside a computer-use surface like [[Codex]] or [[Claude Cowork]]; (3) the "[[SaaS Apocalypse]]" and "[[AI Job Apocalypse]]" are both overblown; (4) he is "super bullish" on product managers and full-stack designers; (5) the "[[Forward Deployed Engineer]]" role is here to stay because "[[Automation Is A Lie|automation is a lie]]" — every agent needs a human; (6) [[Codex]] has overtaken [[Claude Code]] as the leading work surface (his own daily driver); (7) CLIs as a primary interface are already fading in favor of GUIs for non-programmer work; (8) AI-generated writing (plans, strategy docs, email) will become normal and welcomed.
- Personally flipped from believing "everyone will have their own personal agent" (inspired by trying [[Open Claw]] internally at Every) to believing companies will run one shared "super agent" instead, because personal agents are too high-maintenance for most people to sustain without a dedicated human tending them.
- Built his own "[[Senior Engineer Benchmark]]" using his product [[Proof]] (which he vibe-coded and which broke in production after launch, giving him "vibe coder elbow" from over-work); used it to show GPT-5.5 is the first model willing to rewrite bad code from first principles rather than patch around it.
- Wrote an earlier essay called "the allocation economy" ([[Allocation Economy]]) arguing that human work with AI increasingly resembles management — spending a lot of time overseeing, correcting, and directing AI "reports."
- Cites internal Every examples: [[Marcus]] (PM running [[Spiral]]), [[Nitesh]] (forward deployed AI engineer running the internal agent [[Claudie]]), and COO [[Brandon Gall]] (coined "[[Computer Errands]]").
- Personal/lightning-round notes: recommends Annie Dillard's *The Writing Life* (mandatory reading at Every), Churchill's WWII history, and *The Rigor of Angels*; current obsession is *The Power Broker*; life motto: "do things worth writing about and write things worth reading"; describes Codex as the most underrated AI tool right now and says he would switch tools again if a competitor pulled ahead.
- Per [[Lenny Rachitsky]] (episode 33, referencing this same episode): cited as having said the number-one predictor of AI-adoption success is whether a company's CEO is personally "chatting with ChatGPT, Claude, whatever" many times a day — echoed in that episode via the [[Rackspace]] CEO's daily AI-learning habit (see [[AI Product Success Triangle]]).
