## Definition

*Incorruptible: Why Good Companies Go Bad and How Great Companies Stay Great* — [[Eric Ries]]'s 2026 book (releasing May 26), the subject of this podcast episode, arguing that successful companies are destroyed not by competition but by **[[Financial Gravity]]**, and that the antidote is combining **[[Ethos And Integrity|ethos and integrity]]**.

## Key Information

- Framed as the sequel to *[[The Lean Startup (book)|The Lean Startup]]*: that book was about building a successful company, this one is about protecting what you've built.
- Structured, per Ries, "like a mystery, a double mystery": (1) why has this corporate-corruption pattern persisted for hundreds of years when markets supposedly select for value creation, and (2) if it's inevitable, why are there durable exceptions (Anthropic, Costco, Patagonia, Novo Nordisk, Cloudflare, Zeiss)?
- Part One is titled "the shape of the abyss" — the grim case studies (Vectura, Whole Foods, Groupon, Vital Farms, Google, Johnson & Johnson) that Ries says founders must study before they can appreciate the exceptions.
- Coins/formalizes numerous named frameworks discussed across the episode: [[Financial Gravity]], [[Harder Is Easier]], the [[Figure It Out Principle]], [[Culture Bank]], [[Invisible Leader]], [[Mission Guardian]], [[Spiritual Holding Company]], [[Mission Controlled Company]], [[Torchbearers]], [[Director's Oath]], [[Mission Protective Provisions]], and [[Shareholder Primacy]].
- Cites academic evidence that companies protected by durable governance structures (industrial foundations, perpetual purpose trusts) are six times more likely to survive to year 50 and show superior return on invested capital versus conventional shareholder-primacy companies.
- Website: incorruptible.co — offers a mailing list with implementation guides, advanced implementation guides, reader's guides, and a chapter cut from the original manuscript.
