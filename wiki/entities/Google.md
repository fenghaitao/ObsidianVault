## Definition

Google is a multinational technology company focused on search, online advertising, cloud computing, and software. It is one of the FAANG companies.

## Key Information

- Karly mentioned interviewing at Google and being asked if she wanted to work on ads, which she declined because she didn't care about the domain
- Panelists discussed that Google's stock was "about back to where my grant was" at the time of the panel, illustrating equity compensation volatility
- Google was referenced as an example of a company where leveling is strict and titles from outside are often adjusted downward
- the panel discussed that working on high-impact products at Google (like ads) could be good for promotion, but personal passion for the work matters more. Zach interviewed at Google for a Software Engineer role and received a Senior offer (around $300K), which was significantly less than Airbnb's Staff offer. Zach previously interviewed at Google twice for Mobile roles early in his career and was rejected both times.
- Lee worked at Google as an IC Staff Engineer, first on Google Chat and then on Gmail working on notifications
- Google's leveling: L5 is Senior, L6 is Staff — the level transition discussed in the panel

- Ricky spent his entire 7-year career at Google in the ads organization, progressing from L3 to L6, and noted that Google has no "up or out" policy — you can stay at senior (or even mid-level) as long as you want
- Google's rating system runs on a 6-month cycle with ratings like "meets expectations" and "exceeds expectations"
- Promotions at Google are employee-initiated but require manager support; there is an ongoing calibration process
- New grad compensation at Google was around $180K in 2017, with sign-on bonuses
- Steve Huynh noted that many former Amazon engineers who couldn't make the SDE3 to Principal jump at Amazon went to Google and thrived as Staff, Senior Staff (E8), or Principal — two levels past staff
- At the UCLA talk, Google's compensation was compared to Meta's on Levels.FYI — Meta's numbers were much higher because Meta's stock was "ripping recently"
- Google has no "up or out" policy, which is more relaxed than other companies; Ryan noted that "up or out" is not as scary as it sounds — 90%+ of people make it to those levels
