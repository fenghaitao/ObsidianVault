## Definition

Technology company cited by [[Eric Ries]] in *Incorruptible* as a "don't be evil versus the quarterly report" case study — a company with genuinely good founding intentions whose informal ethos eroded because it was never backed by the same structural rigor as its financial reporting obligations.

## Key Information

- Ries made a study of blog posts written by long-tenured Google employees upon leaving — a whole genre, with roughly 50 examples he says are collected and linked in the book — describing a wistful, sad arc around the loss of the "don't be evil" ethos.
- "Don't be evil" was removed from the company's website, then only survived in the employee handbook, then was removed from the handbook entirely; Google has since been sued twice over conduct alleged to break the "don't be evil" pledge and settled both suits.
- In a conversation with a 13-year Google employee, Ries elicited that the employee was near-certain (100%) Google's next quarterly report would be filed on time, but far less certain (~90-95%) that Google wouldn't ever, say, have a self-driving car accidentally kill someone and have to manage the fallout — illustrating that the company has an "unbelievably expensive apparatus" ensuring quarterly reporting happens flawlessly, while "don't be evil" was never backed by any comparable enforcement mechanism, just a slogan.
- Cited (alongside OpenAI, Anthropic, Cohere, Palantir) as one of the major AI labs on a Vatican AI-governance panel Ries participated in, none of which use "standard" shareholder-primacy governance.
- Cited as one of the big tech companies that declined to help pro-democracy protesters defend their websites against state-sponsored hacking (a request [[Cloudflare]] accepted for free).

### Per Benedict Evans (episode 07)

- Cited as one of the "big four" incumbents (with Amazon, Apple, Facebook) hard to see a near-term problem for through the AI platform shift — though Evans notes mobile-era history shows incumbents can still be blindsided (Yahoo Mail failed to make the jump; for Google itself, mobile "didn't change anything").
- Cited as using existing distribution (not necessarily product superiority) to push [[Gemini]] adoption — "for a normal person," Evans says there's no discernible difference between Gemini and ChatGPT, so distribution is what wins users at the margin.
- Its marketing tagline for on-device AI features ("coming soon to our most powerful devices") is used by Evans as a wry example of how narrow initial AI feature rollouts on Android actually are (i.e., meaning it won't work on most Android phones).
- Cited via Google I/O's Android/Gemini integration announcements (replacing Chromebooks with "Gemini books," new Android intelligence features) as part of the distribution race with Apple/OpenAI.
- Referenced as an example (alongside Apple) of a company whose search/mobile product was never displaced despite skepticism at the time ("a search company with a weird logo — what's that got to do with mobile?").

### Per Qasar Younis (episode 22)

- Cited as a case study in organizational momentum blinding even dominant companies: in the late 2000s/early 2010s Google was Silicon Valley's "apex predator" (a billion dollars a month in cash flow, vastly larger than the emerging Facebook) yet could not successfully fight Facebook in social media — "Google is not Facebook," per Younis's "how does a gorilla learn to fly? by not being a gorilla" analogy.
- Used to argue that unconscious cultural/organizational momentum, not lack of talent or resources, is often what prevents a company from responding to a market shift — a dynamic Younis says can also strike much smaller companies whose founders are only slightly off course.

### Per episode 33 (Aishwarya Naresh Reganti and Kiriti Badam)

- Cited as one of 50+ companies where [[Aishwarya Naresh Reganti]] and [[Kiriti Badam]] have led or supported AI product deployments; also cited as a prior employer of Badam's before his current role on [[Codex]] at [[OpenAI]] (alongside [[Kumo]]).
- Its research lab, [[Google DeepMind]] (led by [[Demis Hassabis]]), is cited by Reganti as combining image-model, LLM, and world-model ("Genie") work — evidence for her 2026 prediction of a major leap in multimodal AI understanding.

### Per episode 34 (Molly Graham)
- [[Molly Graham]]'s first hypergrowth employer: joined in 2007 the week the iPhone launched; her communications department grew from 25 to 125 people in 9 months.
- Central example, alongside [[Facebook]], of Graham's "[[Founder Personality Defines Culture]]" thesis: under [[Larry Page]] and [[Sergey Brin]], Google "felt like two PhD students' paradise" — campus-like, ideas valued over shipping — in direct contrast to Facebook's "19-year-old hacker's dorm room."
- Cited by Graham for reportedly rewriting its entire codebase every eight years — evidence for her point that "what you know today is way less valuable than what you can learn by tomorrow," underpinning her "[[Give Away Your Legos]]" framework.
