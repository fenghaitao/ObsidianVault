## Definition

Stephen Cook is a computer scientist who, together with Leonid Levin, defined NP-completeness and proved that satisfiability is NP-complete — the Cook–Levin theorem.

## Key Information

- The "original papers of cook and leaven" defined NP-completeness and proved that NP-complete problems exist, using satisfiability as the first example.
- The core intuition: computation is local — a machine manipulates bits with local operations, so its time evolution can be encoded as local Boolean constraints, which is why any NP computation reduces to satisfiability.
- Father of James Cook, whose work (with Ian Mertz) was an essential ingredient in Ryan Williams's time-space breakthrough.
