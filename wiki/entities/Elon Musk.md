## Definition

Founder/CEO associated with [[Tesla]] (including the [[Optimus]] humanoid robot) and Starlink/SpaceX. Cited by [[Caitlin Kalinowski]] as an example of disciplined engineering trade-off thinking and supply-chain verticalization.

## Key Information

- Kalinowski says Musk is known for defining explicit engineering trade-off ratios (e.g., the value of a gram of weight versus cost), which lets a team resolve design trade-offs quickly once the ratio is set.
- Cited as the person behind verticalizing supply chains at Tesla and (per Kalinowski) at Starlink, which she describes as effectively taking in silicon/raw materials and shipping finished chips/products, giving much greater resilience to supply-chain shocks than companies with more classic, outsourced supply chains.
- When Tesla couldn't source a needed chip, Musk's team reportedly redesigned the PCB in record time to adapt to different available silicon — cited as an example of the payoff of vertical integration.
- Also referenced via Tesla's self-driving software (see [[Tesla]]) as an example of building consumer trust in autonomous systems over time.
- Per [[Sam Lesson]] (episode 32): cited as an example of narrative/storytelling-driven valuation — "if the storytelling of Elon allows [[SpaceX]]... to be worth 1.4 trillion dollars to the public market, guess what? The money powers are going to do great with all this narrative-driven... religion" — used in Lesson's Contrarian Corner argument about AI-startup seed investing. See [[Terra Nova Narrative]].
