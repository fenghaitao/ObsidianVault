## Definition

Lovable is an AI app-building ("vibe coding") platform/company where [[Lazar]] works as the first official "vibe coding engineer." Employees and enterprise customers use it to build both internal tools and shipped, customer-facing products via natural-language prompting rather than hand-written code.

## Key Information

- Described internally (per Lazar) not as a company but as a mission/idea: enabling anyone to build, not just consume, via prompting an idea into existence.
- Described elsewhere in the podcast (via [[Lenny Rachitsky]], episode 21) as one of the fastest-growing startups in history.
- Product features referenced: a voice-dictation input function, per-project "project knowledge"/rules settings (for agent.md/rules.md-style persistent instructions), version control/revert support, and support for exporting a project's code to [[GitHub]].
- Has shipped features users previously had to work around manually (e.g., generating an actual video from a prompt, building Chrome extensions or desktop apps) — Lazar notes non-technical users discovered these were possible before Lovable officially supported them.
- Enterprise adoption: per Lazar, at least half of S&P 500 companies reportedly have employees using Lovable to some extent; several unnamed enterprise customers (in healthcare, finance, telecom) use it heavily for internal tools even when they can't yet push AI-built software fully to production.
- Internal 2025 company motto: "[[Demo Don't Memo]]" — build a working prototype in Lovable instead of writing documents/specs and sitting in meetings to align stakeholders.
- Built and vibe-coded its own Shopify integration marketing templates and its own merch store using the product itself (Lazar built both).
- Is hiring across roles, including for vibe-coder-style positions; Lazar notes candidates have successfully applied by sending a Lovable-built app instead of a traditional resume.
- Cited by [[Lenny Rachitsky]] (episode 21) as one of the products included in "Lenny's Product Pass" free-year newsletter subscriber offer, alongside Replit, Bolt, Cursor, and others.
- Product capability trajectory: Lazar contrasts what Lovable could do in January of the prior year (needed a team of engineers to turn a 4-hour Lovable prototype into a production build six to seven months later) against January 2026, when he describes the product as "ages ahead" in functionality.
- [[Dr. Becky Kennedy]] (CEO of [[Good Inside]]) separately mentions playing around with Lovable after her team showed it to her, alongside [[Replit]] and Figma Make, as part of her workflow for turning product ideas into visual prototypes.
- Per [[Zevi Arnovitz]] (episode 31): one of the "opinionated builder" tools (alongside Bolt, Base 44, Replit, v0) in the middle stage of his [[Exposure Therapy Onboarding]] path — easy to start with, but he graduated off it once he needed more control than its guardrails allowed.

### Per episode 35 (Jason Lemkin)

- Cited by [[Jason Lemkin]] as a hyper-growth AI-native company so demand-flooded that competitors have to actively pick which leads to respond to; recounts a former colleague (now running sales at [[Bolt.new]]) who says Bolt "closed a seven-figure deal we stole from Lovable because no one called them back at Lovable" — used as Lemkin's illustration that even category leaders lose deals purely to slow follow-up, not product quality. See [[Plays Vs Playbooks]].

### Per episode 38 (Elena Verna, Head of Growth)

- Founded by CEO [[Anton Osika]] and co-founder Fabian Hedin; launched officially in the third week of November 2024 (having previously existed as "GPT Engineer"). Passed $200M ARR before its one-year anniversary — one of the fastest ramps to that milestone in history — with growth accelerating (100M ARR in ~7-8 months, then another 100M in the next 4 months). Over 8 million users have tried the product; ~100 employees at time of episode (tripled from 30 six months prior), recently raised a Series B at a $6B valuation.
- Revenue is treated as a byproduct, not the optimization target: Verna says the team actively discusses giving more product away for free even at the cost of near-term revenue growth rate, because the goal is maximizing usage/market share, with monetization tuned afterward.
- Retention: subscriber/paid retention is "on par with" other B2B SaaS benchmarks Verna has worked at ([[Miro]], [[Dropbox]], SurveyMonkey, Netlify, Amplitude) — not exceptional — but net dollar retention is strong because active builders buy more credits, and the team currently prioritizes engagement retention over paid retention as its north star.
- Cost structure: Verna estimates AI companies broadly operate around ~40% margins (versus 80-90% for classic high-margin SaaS); Lovable has very low headcount costs (100 people supporting $200M ARR), minimal paid marketing spend, and a small sales team focused only on inbound "hand-raiser" enterprise interest — freeing budget to fund free-credit giveaways as a growth/marketing line item instead.
- Growth-team structure and levers: growth engineers led a Shopify e-commerce integration and voice-mode input — work Verna says "would usually never come out of growth" — while the core agent/product team owns activation (the first-generation prompt-to-app experience) as a central product-quality obsession, not a growth-team funnel-optimization problem.
- Culture/quality bar: "this is not lovable" is the internal phrase used to flag anything — a bug, a rough interaction — that falls short of the product's namesake quality bar, triggering immediate (no-sprint-queue) fixes.
- Community: runs a Discord community with hundreds of thousands of members and community managers; also runs an ambassador program. Influencer marketing outperforms paid social by roughly 10x for the company.
- Hiring: prioritizes passion/agency/autonomy over pedigree; uses paid work trials and probation periods; explicitly hires people comfortable "creating clarity out of chaos" rather than people who need clarity provided to them.
- "[[She Builds]]": a recurring women-only hackathon (shebuilds.lovable.app) offering unlimited free Lovable access for 48 hours, aimed at closing an estimated adoption gap (Verna cites third-party data suggesting only ~20% of Lovable's users are women) and surfacing hyper-local, non-venture-scale problems (eldercare, household organization, community groups) that would never have justified traditional software investment.
- Internal AI-native workflow: prototypes specs directly in Lovable before/alongside written documentation (e.g., recreating a pricing-page screenshot with requested changes, then handing the Lovable prototype to engineering); running its first company-wide internal hackathon on Lovable itself.
- See [[Product Market Fit Treadmill]] and [[Minimum Lovable Product]] for the two central strategic frameworks Verna introduces in this episode.
