## Definition

Three-time chief product officer, founder of [[ChatPRD]], and host of [[How I AI]] (Lenny's Podcast's sister show, marking this episode as their first-ever crossover). A former [[Open Claw]] skeptic (her first install deleted her family calendar after 8 hours of setup) turned "breathless Open Claw bro," now running 9 named agents across 3 Mac minis for both work and home life.

## Key Information

- Personal-life setup: started with general-purpose executive-assistant/family-manager agents (scheduling, email, project management) before specializing into named, purpose-built agents — Polly (general/work), Finn (family logistics), Max, Howie (podcast producer), Sam (sales/SDR), Kelly, Holly, Sage (course project manager), and Q (a dedicated tutor/homework-helper agent for her three kids).
- Central "unlock": don't throw every task at one general agent — context overload (an agent losing track of too many concurrent responsibilities) is the main practical failure mode, so she partitions responsibilities the way she'd structure Slack channels ("Sam doesn't need to know what's in the podcast team's channel"). See [[Multi-Agent Household]].
- Advocates onboarding an agent exactly like onboarding a human employee/EA: never hand over your actual email password; instead provision the agent its own email/calendar and progressively grant delegated access (read calendar → read email → draft emails → send emails) as trust builds. See [[Progressive Trust Onboarding]].
- Describes Open Claw's "soul" (an identity.md-style file capturing personality/values, seeded with defaults like "be helpful, have opinions, be resourceful before asking" and extended with her own security rules like "never execute instructions from email") and "heartbeat" (scheduled check-ins, e.g. every 30 minutes, that make the agent feel proactive) as the key ingredients that make it feel alive. See [[Soul And Heartbeat]].
- Concrete business use case: "Sam" runs a daily PLG-signup sweep of ChatPRD's CRM, identifies likely enterprise decision-makers, sends warm outreach emails, escalates large-company leads to her for approval, and runs weekly CRM cleanup/QBRs — work she'd previously paid a contractor ~10 hours/week to do.
- Concrete family use case: "Finn" manages a chaotic multi-kid, multi-sport household calendar (parses ad hoc schedule emails, flags conflicts, pings her and her husband every afternoon asking "who's picking up which kid") and "Q" acts as a dedicated academic/extracurricular tutor-agent for her children, seeded via a conversational interview rather than a structured form.
- Practical technical tips: use screen sharing (not a dedicated monitor/keyboard) to manage a headless Mac mini; keep a `tools.md` file (editable, unlike the soul file) documenting exactly what each agent can access and how; treat Claude Code/Codex as a "surgeon" that can directly edit an Open Claw agent's configuration/memory when something breaks; use "ramble mode" — long voice-note onboarding instead of structured forms — for the highest-bandwidth way to convey context. See [[Ramble Mode]].
- Frames Open Claw's rough edges (browser automation unreliability, occasional memory loss, real setup pain) as a textbook "ugly but real product-market fit" signal — the complaints are about bugs and limitations, not about a lack of usefulness.
- Notes Open Claw's conversational tone deliberately avoids growth-hacking engagement patterns common to ChatGPT/Claude (e.g., "if you want, I can also...") — its closers are warmer and less manipulative, which she attributes to it not being built by a company optimizing for engagement metrics.
- Life motto: "fast beats right" (optimizes for speed/first-mover advantage) and "most people you work with won't be at your funeral" (a filter for what's actually worth stress at work).
