## Definition

Aishwarya Naresh Reganti ("Ash") is an AI product consultant and former AI researcher (early researcher roles at Alexa and Microsoft; author of 35+ research papers). Together with [[Kiriti Badam]], she has led or supported 50+ AI product deployments across companies including [[Amazon]], [[Databricks]], [[OpenAI]], and [[Google]], and co-teaches a top-rated AI product-building course on [[Maven]].

## Key Information

- Co-created the "[[Continuous Calibration Continuous Development]]" (CCCD) framework with Kiriti Badam, and co-articulates the episode's two foundational concepts — [[Non-Determinism In AI Products]] and the [[Agency Control Trade-off]] — as the reasons AI product development differs fundamentally from traditional software.
- Runs a consulting practice helping enterprises with AI transformation, training, and strategy; observes that 2025 execution remains "all over the place" industry-wide because the field is only three years old with no established playbooks, and that old PM/engineer/data-team handoffs are breaking down in favor of tighter, joint review of agent traces.
- Introduces the "[[AI Product Success Triangle]]" (great leaders, good culture, technical progress), citing the [[Rackspace]] CEO's daily AI-learning habit as a leadership exemplar and warning against FOMO-driven cultures that make subject-matter experts unwilling to help calibrate AI systems.
- Attributes the "[[Semantic Diffusion]]" term to Martin Fowler to explain why "eval" now means different, sometimes incompatible things to different practitioners (data-labeling companies, PM commentary, benchmark-checking).
- Names "[[Supervisor-Subagent Pattern|multi-agent systems]]" as "misunderstood" rather than overhyped: naive peer-to-peer agent decomposition ("gossip protocol") rarely works reliably, while a supervisor-agent-plus-sub-agents pattern does.
- Calls coding agents "underrated" given low penetration outside the Bay Area, and jumping across new AI tools "overrated" relative to obsessing over the underlying business problem — echoing her closing advice to "be obsessed with your customers, be obsessed with the problem... AI is just a tool," and her claim that 80% of effective AI engineers/PMs spend their time deeply understanding workflows and data rather than building flashy models.
- Predicts 2026 will bring a leap in multimodal AI understanding (not just generation), enabling AI to process messy real-world data like handwritten documents and scanned PDFs that current models struggle with; cites [[Demis Hassabis]] and [[Google DeepMind]]'s combined image/LLM/world-model direction as a signal of where this is headed.
- Lightning round: recommends *When Breath Becomes Air* by [[Paul Kalanithi]] as her most-recommended book; enjoys rewatching *[[Silicon Valley (TV Show)|Silicon Valley]]* for how "eerily similar" it is to the current AI wave; names [[Whisper Flow]] as her favorite recently discovered product; life motto from her father: "they told it couldn't be done, but the fool didn't know it, so he did it anyway."
