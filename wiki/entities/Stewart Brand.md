## Definition

Stewart Brand is the author of *How Buildings Learn*, cited by [[Max Schoening]] alongside the "timeless way of building" idea as an architectural analogy for [[Malleable Software]]: the best-fitting homes (or software) are often not designed top-down by an expert but adapt slowly, over time, to how their actual occupants live.

## Key Information

- Schoening: "the timeless way of building and Stewart Brand's... how buildings learn — I think idea is also that it's very likely that the best homes for you are not actually built by an architect. They are the thing that over a long time adapt to exactly how you would love to lead your life and they learn over time" — noting this kind of physical adaptation is costly (you'd have to "rip out a wall"), unlike software, where adaptation can in principle be cheap.
- Invoked in the same breath as [[Dieter Rams]]'s critique of overly precious, museum-piece design that isn't actually useful/adaptable.
