## Definition

Michael Stonebraker (transcribed in the episode as "Mike Stonbreaker") is a Turing Award-winning computer scientist famous for fundamental contributions to database systems, including creating Ingres and Postgres and later founding StreamBase, Vertica, and DBOS.

## Key Information

- Hired at Berkeley after his PhD; Eugene Wong mentored him from 1971 and they started Ingres in 1972, which earned Stonebraker tenure in 1976.
- Created Postgres with an extensible type system after Ingres's hardcoded types couldn't support GIS shapes or custom date math (bond-market calendars).
- Wrote the 2004 "One Size Fits All" argument and the 2011 paper (with David DeWitt et al.) showing distributed databases beat Hadoop; publicly disputed Google's MapReduce and eventual consistency.
- Founded DBOS in 2023 from the academic idea of replacing the upper half of Linux with a database system.
- Published the "Beaver" text-to-SQL benchmark showing LLMs score ~0% on real data warehouses (10% with RAG, 35% with FROM/JOIN clauses).
- Career advice: "think outside the box," pick a problem that isn't going with the flow, find a mentor, and for new PhDs take the most prestigious job possible; warns computer science may not be a growth industry going forward.
