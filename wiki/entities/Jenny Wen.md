## Definition

Head of design for [[Claude Cowork]] at [[Anthropic]] (previously head of design for [[Claude]]/[[Claude Code]]); before that director of design at [[Figma]] (leading FigJam and Slides), and earlier a designer at Dropbox, Square, and [[Shopify]]. Argues in her own episode that the traditional design process is dead, replaced by a split between fast implementation support and short-horizon (3-6 month) vision-setting.

## Key Information

- Core thesis (from a September conference talk, "don't trust the design process"): the classic research→diverge→converge design process is dead, forced by how fast engineering now moves (engineers "spinning up their seven Clauds"). Design work is stratifying into two modes: (1) supporting implementation/execution — often just letting an engineer scrap-build an idea rather than making a polished mock first — and (2) short-horizon (3-6 month, down from 2-10 year) vision-setting, often expressed as a prototype rather than a storytelling deck.
- Quantifies her own time shift: a few years ago ~60-70% mocking/prototyping, ~20% engineering collaboration, ~10% coordination; now ~30-40% mocking, ~30-40% pairing/jamming directly with engineers, plus a new slice actually implementing/shipping code herself.
- Still uses traditional methods (user research, surveys, prototyping) but in different proportion; still uses [[Figma]] specifically for divergent exploration (trying 8-10 different directions) and fine visual/interaction detail work, since coding tools are comparatively linear/single-direction. Also still uses an IDE (VS Code) for last-mile CSS/polish work even as engineers move toward CLIs/agents.
- Coined "building trust through speed": ship early (clearly labeled "research preview," as with [[Claude Cowork]]), accept it will be flawed, but commit publicly to rapid, visible iteration based on user feedback — the way you avoid losing trust when shipping early is by visibly, continuously improving afterward, not by waiting for polish.
- On AI and taste: expects AI's sense of taste/judgment to keep improving, but argues the truly hard part of building software was rarely the building itself — it's someone being accountable for disputed decisions about what to build — and that accountability layer persists even if AI can present the options.
- Describes chat/terminal interfaces as durable (not a temporary stop before something else), because the open-ended, always-available nature of chat is what makes it flexible — but expects most specific/frequent interactions to be increasingly served by model-generated UI widgets on top of chat, not replacing it.
- Deliberately stepped back into IC design work at Anthropic for about a year (after briefly managing a team) specifically to build hands-on fluency with the AI-driven change in her own discipline — argues managers who don't stay close to the actual work can't fully empathize with how much it's changed, similar to how some engineering orgs rotate new EMs through IC work first.
- Led [[Claude Cowork]]'s design: publicly it "took 10 days" to ship, but that compressed a much longer internal exploration (many prototypes of to-do-list widgets, multiple-choice question UI, onboarding) — the 10 days was the final push once the team decided the moment had come, not the full design process. Describes Cowork as "Claude with hands" or "Claude that turns your garbage into something nice."
- Uses "the [[Legibility Framework]]" (credited to VC [[Evan Tana]]) to describe her role at Anthropic as spotting internally-generated prototypes/ideas that have real energy behind them but aren't yet legible/articulable, then giving them form — her example: an internal "Claude Studio" prototype that looked confusing to her at first but ultimately seeded both the skills framework and key parts of Cowork's UI.
- Hiring philosophy: three archetypes she prioritizes — the "strong generalist"/block-shaped person (multiple skills each ~80th percentile), the deep T-shaped specialist (top ~10% in one area, e.g. strong enough engineering to work close to the model, or elite visual/icon craft), and the "craft new grad" (early-career, humble, unusually fast learner, unburdened by legacy process assumptions). See [[Design Hiring Archetypes]].
- Management philosophy: argues seemingly "low-leverage" tasks (dogfooding personally, fixing a small bug, making someone's anniversary card) are often secretly high-leverage because they signal that a senior leader is genuinely close to the work and cares — cites [[Mike Krieger]] personally submitting PRs as an example. Encourages psychological safety measured partly by whether a team feels comfortable affectionately "roasting" her verbal tics in critique sessions, paired with maintaining high standards (a stance she likens to radical candor).
- Personal/lightning-round notes: recommends *The Power Broker* (Robert Caro) and *Insomniac City* (Bill Hayes); enjoyed *A Sentimental Value* and *The Pit* season 2; has used the photo-sharing app Retro for two years, valuing its weekly-only, ad-free, count-free design; life motto "it is what it is"; uses Claude Cowork to mine years of personal notes/interview notes into a self-authored "design craft" hiring rubric she didn't know she could articulate.
