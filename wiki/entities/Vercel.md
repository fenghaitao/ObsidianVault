## Definition

Web deployment/hosting platform ("Verscell" in the transcript's phonetic rendering), named in this episode's [[WorkOS]] sponsor ad read as one of the "winning companies" powered by WorkOS's enterprise-readiness APIs, alongside [[OpenAI]], [[Cursor]], [[Perplexity]], and [[Plaid]].

## Key Information

- Cited in the recurring WorkOS ad-read customer list across multiple Lenny's Podcast episodes as a company using WorkOS's drop-in single sign-on, SCIM, RBAC, and audit-log APIs to become enterprise-ready.

### Per episode 35 (Jason Lemkin)

- One of Lenny's Podcast episode 35's two sponsors: Vercel's "v0" web-development assistant, pitched as letting product managers, designers, and developers build working prototypes (with database integration, screenshot import, GitHub sync) rather than just docs/ideas for reviews.
- Cited repeatedly by [[Jason Lemkin]] as a flagship example of a hyper-growth AI-native company that deliberately keeps its sales team small for cultural reasons even though it could grow faster with more salespeople — "even if Replit only goes from zero to 200, it could have been 220 with a smaller sales team." Lemkin says this preference for staying lean is shared broadly among leading AI company CEOs.
- Cited as adding a leader referred to as "Janine" (full name/role not given in the transcript, not linked here) to its go-to-market team, which [[Jason Lemkin]] reads as a signal Vercel is moving toward more enterprise-focused sales even as it stays lean — part of his broader claim that "everyone eventually goes enterprise."
- Named alongside [[Replit]] and [[ElevenLabs]] as a hyper-growth company so demand-flooded that "half our job is picking which [leads] to respond to" (a description Lemkin attributes to a former colleague now running sales at [[Bolt.new]], discussing the broader hyper-growth AI cohort Vercel belongs to).

### Per episode 42 ([[Jeanne DeWitt Grosser]], COO)

- Grosser (formerly CPO at [[Stripe]]) is COO, overseeing marketing, sales, customer success, revenue ops, and field engineering; the GTM organization builds its own internal AI agents (a "lead agent," a Gong-transcript-based "dealbot") directly on Vercel's own platform (AI Gateway, workflows/workflow SDK, fluid compute, sandboxing) as a "customer zero" for the company's own product.
- The lead agent let Vercel shift 9 of 10 inbound SDRs to outbound work in ~6 weeks while holding lead-to-opportunity conversion flat; costs roughly $1,000/year to run versus well over $1M in prior SDR salaries. See [[Go-To-Market Engineer]].
- Vercel's first three GTM engineers were former front-end developers who had become sales engineers.
- Segments its customer base by company size, growth potential, web-traffic/"promote" rank (a smaller company with outsized traffic gets enterprise-level attention), and workload type (e-commerce vs. SaaS vs. crypto) — used to explain, for example, historically low SaaS-company penetration (since migrating an existing large SaaS codebase to Vercel is a heavy lift) versus growing AI Cloud interest from SaaS companies adding AI-native features.
- Underwent a pricing restructuring (around August of the prior year) splitting a previously bundled SaaS-like price into separate Pro and Enterprise SKUs, moving startup-relevant features out of Enterprise into self-serve — credited with meaningfully accelerating PLG-funnel growth.
- Internal culture motto per Grosser: "Vercel builds Vercel with Vercel," reflecting heavy internal dogfooding of its own product across every team, including GTM.
