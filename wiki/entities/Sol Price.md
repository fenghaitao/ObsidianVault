## Definition

Founder of Price Club (predecessor/forerunner tied to [[Costco]]'s retail-warehouse model), cited by [[Eric Ries]] in *Incorruptible* as the model for the "fiduciaries" framing — "customers first, employees second, shareholders last."

## Key Information

- Ries says he deliberately starts the book with Sol Price rather than with trendy consulting language ("stakeholders," "culture"), preferring the old-fashioned word "fiduciary": the question every leader must answer is who their fiduciaries are, i.e., who they would rather die than betray.
- Price's stated ordering — customers first, employees second, shareholders last — is presented as one legitimate answer (contrasted later with Peter Drucker's inverse ordering: employees first, customers second, shareholders last); Ries's point is not which ordering is correct, but that leaders must commit to one in writing.
- Referenced again near the end of the episode in the "this is not a party, this is a wake" story about a founder ousted by investors, where Ries invokes "he's like Saul Price" in describing founders whose ousting effectively erases their standing in the company they built.
