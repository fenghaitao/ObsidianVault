# Qasar Younis

Co-founder and CEO of [[Applied Intuition]], a $15B physical AI company. Former COO of [[Y Combinator]]. Third-time founder.

## Background
- Born on a farm in Pakistan, grew up in Detroit/Warren, Michigan
- Started career as an engineer at [[General Motors]], then at [[Bosch]]
- MBA from Harvard Business School — first exposure to high finance and the world of private equity
- COO at [[Y Combinator]] under president [[Sam Altman]]
- Lived in Japan; influences include Japanese philosophy of maintenance and cleanliness

## Applied Intuition
- Founded ~2016-2017 as third company after two prior startups
- Company has never spent any money it raised — profitable and sustainable for nearly 10 years
- 1,000+ engineers
- 18 of top 20 automakers are customers, plus Department of Defense, construction, mining, trucking companies
- Core philosophy: **radical pragmatism**

## Philosophy
- **"Our best work is done alone and quietly."** — Intentionally stayed under the radar for ~10 years, focusing on product and customers rather than public presence
- Only joined Twitter/X in 2026 at urging of [[Marc Andreessen]] and [[Elad Gil]]
- First tweet got 2M views, quote-tweeted by Marc Andreessen as "the best AI CEO nobody knows"
- **Reading old books:** Recommends reading pillars of human ideas — philosophy, history, biographies — rather than new content. Time filters out noise.
- **Taste in leadership:** Believes many Silicon Valley CEOs lack taste because of narrow life experiences. Broad exposure (travel, working in large orgs, diverse reading) develops better judgment.
- **Emotions:** Generally not helpful in making rational business decisions. Remove the emotional filter to see the "raw image" of the decision.
- **First startup as practice:** Treat the first company as learning the craft of being a founder.

## Key Views
- **Physical AI:** The real AI revolution in the next 5-10 years will be in farming, mining, construction, trucking — not just software
- **AI fear = misunderstanding:** The root of AI anxiety is not understanding the technology's limitations
- **China comparison:** American companies can't be compared apples-to-apples with Chinese ones (like [[Huawei]]) because Chinese companies are extensions of the state, not profit-driven entities
- **Industrial Revolution parallel:** AI will bring net positive change to humanity, despite downsides, just as the Industrial Revolution did
- **The score takes care of itself:** Focus on the craft, not the metrics
