## Definition

Co-founder and CEO of [[Applied Intuition]], a ~$15B autonomy-software company he calls "the most important under-the-radar AI company." Born on a farm in Pakistan, raised in Warren, Michigan; started his engineering career at GM and Bosch before founding several companies, including a stint as COO of [[Y Combinator]] under [[Sam Altman]].

## Key Information

- Applied Intuition is his third company; he argues explicitly that his third attempt being the most successful is not a coincidence — founders should expect their first company to be "a zero" and treat early failures as building the muscle of being a founder, not a verdict on their potential.
- Deliberately kept Applied Intuition quiet and under-the-radar for roughly a decade, guided by the company value "our best work is done alone and quietly" — modeled more on Berkshire Hathaway than a typical loud Silicon Valley startup.
- Recently began posting on X (his first tweet got ~2M views and was quote-tweeted by [[Marc Andreessen]]), at the urging of Andreessen, [[Naval Ravikant]] (an Applied Intuition investor), and [[Elad Gil]] — reasoning that as the company grows more influential he has a responsibility to share his views more broadly, even though he personally gets no emotional satisfaction from public-facing work.
- Frames his life philosophy around "radical pragmatism": every one of Applied Intuition's values reduces to this, and founder advice should always be checked against whether it actually applies to your specific situation (e.g., staying quiet only works because he's already known/trusted in the ecosystem).
- Describes an MBA at Harvard as his first real exposure to extreme wealth, but says his most formative experience was over a decade working inside large bureaucratic organizations (GM, Bosch) — seeing "the bowels" of institutions as an employee, which he argues most founders never experience and which shapes better leadership and culture.
- His central thesis: the real AI revolution over the next 5-10 years is in [[Physical AI]] — farming, mining, construction, trucking — not consumer software; sees public anxiety about AI/robots as rooted in unfamiliarity rather than real danger (see [[Nunchuck Robot Fallacy]]).
- Advocates strongly against broadly "pumping the brakes" on frontier technology, arguing that slowing growth to mitigate feared harms disproportionately damages the most economically marginalized people it's meant to protect.
- On China: argues Chinese tech giants like [[Huawei]] must be understood as extensions of the state rather than ordinary companies, making direct company-to-company comparisons (e.g., Huawei vs. Apple) misleading. See [[China As State Extension]].
- Describes Applied Intuition's decision culture as deliberately emotion-minimized: anyone should speak up regardless of seniority, the best idea wins regardless of source, and once a decision is made the company moves decisively. See [[Best Idea Wins Culture]].
- Heavy reader with a public reading list; philosophy is to read old, well-filtered books on subjects you know nothing about, seeking material that changes your existing framing on the world. See [[Read Old Books Heuristic]].
- Runs a "cleaning zen" at the office (no-shoes policy, staff clean their own space) inspired by time living in Japan, which he connects loosely to a broader ethos of discipline and craft; notes Applied Intuition has never spent any outside capital it has raised.
