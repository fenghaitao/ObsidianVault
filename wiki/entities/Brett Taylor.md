## Definition

Brett Taylor is cited by [[Max Schoening]] for the framing that "software is like a garden" — it requires ongoing tending — used by Schoening to explain why the "as a service" part of SaaS (maintenance plus specialist attention) remains valuable even as raw implementation gets cheaper via AI. He is also the founder of [[Quip]], where [[Molly Graham]] was an early operating executive before its sale to [[Salesforce]].

## Key Information

- Quoted by Schoening in the context of rebutting the "[[SaaS Apocalypse]]" narrative: "Brett Taylor says this too. Software is like a garden. You need to tend to it and the thing you pay for in the as a service is the maintenance and a bunch of specialists thinking really hard about a problem."

### Per episode 34 (Molly Graham)
- Founded [[Quip]]; Graham joined a couple of months before launch and ran everything outside product/engineering, describing the experience of building with him from zero-to-one as very different from (and lonelier than) scaling inside an already-fast-growing company like [[Facebook]] or [[Google]].
- One of the "very high-performing founder CEOs" Graham says she's worked closely with, alongside [[Mark Zuckerberg]], [[Sheryl Sandberg]], [[Larry Page]], and [[Sergey Brin]] — cited as another example supporting her "[[Founder Personality Defines Culture]]" thesis.
