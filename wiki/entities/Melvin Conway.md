## Definition

Melvin Conway is the author of the early paper behind Conway's law, the idea that the structure of a system mirrors the communication structure of the organization that built it.

## Key Information

- Wrote the very early paper (1960s, or possibly 1950s) that lays out Conway's law, which Casey calls the one software-engineering "law" he would bet on.
- The paper's mechanism: when a problem is partitioned across workers whose inter-worker communication is slower than their internal computation, the design decisions about who builds what lock in slower iteration across those boundaries — so the product shows the boundaries ("products look like the org chart").
