## Definition
Alan Kay is a computer scientist and pioneer of the personal computing revolution at Xerox PARC. In the keynote, he is cited for the technique of projecting Moore's Law into the future to build expensive, unmarketable prototypes that would become viable when hardware caught up — a strategy recommended for LLM application builders.

## Key Information
- Worked at Xerox PARC during the development of foundational personal computing concepts
- His team's core technique: project Moore's Law forward, then build expensive/slow/buggy systems to experience future capabilities ahead of time
- This approach is presented as the model for LLM builders: design for capabilities that will be economical within 12-30 months
- The framing: "the best way to predict the future is to look at the past, find people predicting the present, and copy what they did"
