## Definition

~$15B autonomy-software company co-founded by [[Qasar Younis]] and [[Peter Ludwig]], described as "basically Waymo or Tesla but without the hardware." Adds AI/autonomy software to vehicles — cars, tractors, planes, submarines, mining rigs, trucks — for 18 of the top 20 automakers, major global construction/mining/trucking companies, and the Department of Defense.

## Key Information

- Nearly a decade old with 1,000+ engineers; has never spent any outside capital it has raised, which Younis attributes partly to a frugal, disciplined office culture (e.g., staff clean the office themselves).
- Deliberately stayed under-the-radar/quiet for most of its history, guided by the core value "our best work is done alone and quietly" — a Berkshire Hathaway-style posture rather than a typical loud Silicon Valley startup.
- Company values were derived empirically rather than aspirationally: after gaining early traction, the founders wrote down the actual reasons the company was succeeding and turned those into values, including "radical pragmatism," "our best work is done alone and quietly," "move fast, move safe" (decisiveness, assessed in manager reviews), "never disappoint the customer," "technical mastery," "high output matters," and "laugh a lot."
- Culture is deliberately emotion-minimized in decision-making: anyone should speak up regardless of seniority, and the best idea wins regardless of who proposed it — but once a decision is made, the company moves decisively. See [[Best Idea Wins Culture]].
- Alumni ("Applied Intuition is creating a founder class in itself") have gone on to start their own companies.
- Has a no-shoes office policy and weekly "cleaning zen," influenced by Younis's time living in Japan.
- Serves as the commercial backbone for the [[Physical AI]] thesis: intelligence added to existing, already-engineered heavy machinery (tractors, mining rigs, trucks) rather than built from scratch as humanoid robots.
