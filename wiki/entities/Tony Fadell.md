## Definition

Product builder and investor who co-created the iPod, iPhone, and Nest Learning Thermostat; author of the book *Build*; founder of The Build Collective; designer in residence at MIT. Was previously part of the General Magic team.

## Key Information

- Co-created some of the most influential consumer hardware products in history: the iPod, iPhone, and Nest thermostat; co-author of 300+ patents.
- Was part of the legendary General Magic team (subject of the documentary *General Magic*), which built precursor smartphone technology roughly 15 years too early.
- Wrote *Build*, a widely cited book for product builders that popularized the "builder" identity over separate designer/engineer/PM roles, and discusses topics like opinion-based decisions, the customer journey, and storytelling.
- Founder/investor at The Build Collective (buildc.com), a deep-tech investment and advisory firm; has had a portfolio of 200+ companies at times, spanning environment, societal benefit, and health.
- Named the inaugural designer in residence at MIT (Media Lab / Media Academy of Design), mentoring students on customer-journey thinking.
- Core philosophy: start from real customer pain, then find genuinely new technology to solve it differently (used for the iPod, iPhone, and Nest).
- Argues 1.0 products require opinion-based ("taste") decisions from a small team of "tastemakers," since there's no analog data for genuinely new categories.
- Warns against "cognitive surrender" to AI coding/design tools — cites the leaked Claude/Anthropic source code as an example of brittle, under-architected AI-generated software.
- Personally invested early (pre-hype) in deep-tech/chip companies like Groq and Cerebras, and advises portfolio companies including Simbe Robotics, Great Parrot, and Recursion.
- Advocates for ethical restraint in product design — avoiding addictive engineering and calling for "digital nutrition" regulation akin to junk-food labeling.
