## Definition

Adam Ernst is a Distinguished Engineer (IC9) at Meta who built iOS infrastructure that impacted the entire company — most notably the Core Data replacement ("mem models"), the declarative UI framework ComponentKit, and the failed cross-platform framework ComponentScript.

## Key Information

- Founded a middle-school software company called "CosmicSoft" that sold online testing software written in REALbasic (a Visual Basic-inspired, cross-platform language) and accepted payment via mailed checks and an early e-commerce service that issued unlock codes.
- Joined Facebook in 2012 as an E5, weeks before the IPO, during the company's shift away from the HTML5 "Faceweb" app toward native code.
- Earned E6 by replacing Apple's Core Data at iOS-team scale with an immutable "mem models" system that eased reasoning about thread safety and mutations.
- Created ComponentKit around 2014 after manager Lee Byron (GraphQL co-inventor) asked him to think through how React's concepts could work in an iOS-first way.
- Spent ~2 years on ComponentScript, a technically excellent cross-platform framework that he later called "a total and complete failure" and killed himself.
- Reviewed ~1,600 diffs in six months (~14 per workday), treating code review as an organic way to influence other engineers.
- Stayed on the iOS domain throughout his career, "rolling with" reorgs and building deep-plus-broad knowledge by diving "eight levels deep" into any blocking system (e.g., GraphQL codegen, Buck).
- Always knew management wasn't for him because he loves writing code and considers himself best at communicating with engineers about concrete technical problems.
- Admires engineers with distinct strengths: Dustin Shahitapor (prolific coder), Wei Han (fixer), Michael Bolan (project starter), Bob Baldwin and Oliver Ricard (communication), Nolan O'Brien (alignment and the Apple relationship).
