## Definition

Founder and CEO of [[Meta]] (renamed from Facebook to lean into the VR/metaverse vision). Cited by [[Caitlin Kalinowski]] as a leader who ran the company's technical decision-making process unusually well. [[Molly Graham]], who worked closely with him at [[Facebook]] and the [[Chan Zuckerberg Initiative]], cites him as the primary example of her "[[Founder Personality Defines Culture]]" thesis and "[[Escalation Is A Tool]]" rule.

## Key Information

- Per Kalinowski, ran the technical side of Meta very well: decisions were pushed down to the lowest level possible to maintain speed, with clear review structures ("if you can make the decision without the review, you will do that").
- Together with CTO [[Andrew Bosworth]], was closely involved in technical decisions — able to read ~20-page reports, grok trade-offs, and meaningfully contribute to technical discussions at scale (repeated many times per month).
- Renamed Facebook to Meta to lean into VR/the metaverse as a bet on the future; Kalinowski notes Meta spent roughly $10 billion on this VR push.
- Used by [[Sam Lesson]] (episode 32) as the go-to example of a universally-recognized famous person when illustrating etiquette around meeting celebrities: walking up and asking "who are you" is "ridiculous" over-sycophancy in the other direction is equally wrong; the right approach treats them as a normal person while showing awareness of who they are.

### Per episode 34 (Molly Graham)
- Central example of "[[Founder Personality Defines Culture]]": Graham says "Facebook is Mark" — the company's shipping-above-all, "19-year-old hacker's dorm room" culture was set by his personality, not by any deliberately declared value; she recalls him saying one thing at an all-hands that outweighed months of her own deliberate culture work ("like somebody threw a boulder into the pond").
- Source of "[[Escalation Is A Tool]]": strongly believed people don't escalate enough; reframed escalation from "tattling" to "going up together" the moment two people of equal power/context are stuck, applied at both Facebook and the [[Chan Zuckerberg Initiative]].
- Co-founded the [[Chan Zuckerberg Initiative]] with [[Priscilla Chan]]; Graham helped run its first two years, describing it as just as chaotic and fast-scaling as Facebook despite philanthropy's calm reputation.
