## Definition
Fred Brooks is a computer scientist and author best known for "The Mythical Man-Month" (1975) and the paper "No Silver Bullet — Essence and Accident in Software Engineering" (1986). He also authored "The Design of Design" (2010). His work on software complexity is foundational to understanding the challenges of AI-assisted development.

## Key Information
- Author of "The Mythical Man-Month" (1975)
- Author of "The Design of Design" (2010)
- Published "No Silver Bullet" in 1986, arguing no single innovation would yield an order-of-magnitude improvement in software productivity
- Identified two types of complexity: essential complexity (the fundamental difficulty of the problem) and accidental complexity (everything added along the way)
- Argued the hard part of software is not the mechanics of coding but understanding the problem and designing the solution
- His framework is used by Jake Nations to explain why AI cannot solve the fundamental difficulty of software engineering
- Introduced the concept of the "design concept" in The Design of Design: the ephemeral shared idea of what you're building that exists between collaborators but cannot be captured in a document
- Introduced the "design tree" concept: walking down each branch of design decisions, resolving dependencies between decisions one by one
- Matt Pocock uses Brooks' design concept and design tree to explain why AI and humans need to reach shared understanding before coding
