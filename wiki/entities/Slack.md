## Definition

Slack is a workplace messaging platform founded by Stewart Butterfield, famously known as one of the early "consumerized B2B SaaS" products. It was acquired by Salesforce in one of the largest tech acquisitions of its time.

## Key Information

- Launched officially in February 2014
- Originated from the internal IRC-based communication tool used while building the game Glitch
- Famous for consumerized B2B SaaS approach — leaning into delight, experience, and craft
- Key product innovations mentioned: magic link authentication, do not disturb with elaborate rollout, shouty rooster (at everyone moderation), threads, fair billing
- Early growth driven by cross-pollination: employees who used Slack at one startup would advocate for it at their next company
- Had an SLA that gave customers 100x their money back for downtime (applied automatically, proactively)
- Offered free credits to businesses struggling during COVID
- Introduced fair billing — stopped charging for inactive seats
- Went public via direct listing with no lockup, structured to be employee-friendly
- Stewart Butterfield's mantra: "In the long run, the measure of our success will be the amount of value that we create for customers"
- Claude Code integrates with Slack for agentic workflows; Co-work can send Slack messages and manage reminders
- Boris uses Co-work weekly: every Monday it messages each engineer on Slack who hasn't filled out their status
- Jessica Fain was a PM at Slack, then Chief of Staff to CPOs April Underwood and Tamar Yehoshua, then led the core product team (channels, emoji, search, messaging)
- Implemented the "Customer Love Sprint" — a two-week sprint where engineers picked what to work on, shipping 65 improvements
- Had "Hey Stuart, what do you think?" sessions with Stuart Butterfield — early alignment conversations before designs were done
- Ali Rayl (early Slack employee) implemented the "Hey Stuart" sessions
- Noah Weiss and Ethan Eisman codified Slack's product principles
- Known for the "Stuart plus two more" rule: deliver exactly what Stuart asked for plus two alternative versions
- Had a culture of "painting the insides of the cabinets" — obsessive attention to product craft and usability
