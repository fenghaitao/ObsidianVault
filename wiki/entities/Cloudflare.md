## Definition

Internet infrastructure/security company co-founded by [[Matthew Prince]], cited at length by [[Eric Ries]] in *Incorruptible* as the primary case study for the **[[Harder Is Easier]]** principle and for how a mission ("make a better internet") can emerge organically rather than being imposed top-down.

## Key Information

- Founders came out of Harvard Business School reportedly "traumatized" by consulting jargon and deliberately avoided writing a mission statement or values statement for years, on the theory that "we're just making a firewall and putting it in the cloud" — yet Ries argues the company clearly had a mission the whole time, evidenced by its behavior.
- Defended pro-democracy protesters' websites for free (as free-tier customers) against state-sponsored hacking attacks when no other major tech company (including Google) would take the risk — done for no financial reward, purely because it was "the right thing to do."
- Its mission statement ("make a better internet") emerged bottom-up: engineers kept using the phrase casually until Matthew Prince was asked if it was the official mission and eventually had it formally adopted; the company's first formally documented value was "be principled."
- Key story: an engineer asked why the company charged for SSL/web encryption if "a better internet" should mean an encrypted internet. Rather than shutting the idea down (the normal middle-manager response), Prince said "let's figure it out" — the **[[Figure It Out Principle]]**. The team hand-rolled custom cryptographic software and renegotiated certificate-authority deals to make free universal SSL sustainable rather than bankrupting.
- Short-term, the free-SSL rollout depressed premium-tier conversion rates (reported to the board), but the company stuck with it; the resulting top-of-funnel growth increased by an order of magnitude, and Ries credits the resulting trust as central to Cloudflare becoming a $70 billion company and to the internet's default expectation of encryption today.
- Per [[Simon Willison]] (episode 17): cited (alongside [[Shopify]]) as hiring roughly 1,000 interns in 2025, since AI coding assistance collapsed the traditional month-long onboarding ramp to about a week — part of the broader pattern that AI most benefits senior and junior engineers, squeezing mid-career engineers hardest.
