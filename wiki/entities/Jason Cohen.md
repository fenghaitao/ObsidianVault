## Definition

Four-time founder (two unicorns, including [[WP Engine]]) and 20-year blogger at asmartbear.com (named after his earlier company, [[SmartBear]]); author of the forthcoming book *[[Hidden Multipliers]]*. His episode lays out a sequential diagnostic framework for stalled product/company growth — see [[Growth Stall Diagnostic Framework]].

## Key Information

- Writing philosophy: quality over consistency — publishes rarely (~150-200 posts he's proud of over ~18 years) but holds every piece to "the best I can do" rather than a regular schedule; acknowledges the tension between that standard and the risk of paralysis.
- Core growth-diagnostic sequence, applied strictly in order (fixing a downstream issue doesn't help if an upstream one is unaddressed): (1) logo churn/are customers leaving — see [[Logo Churn Ceiling]]; (2) is pricing correct — see [[Pricing Selects The Market]]; (3) are existing customers growing (NRR) — see [[NRR Compounding Fallacy]]; (4) are acquisition channels saturated — see [[Elephant Curve]]; (5) do you actually need to grow at all.
- On churn surveys: rejects multiple-choice cancellation reasons (found via an A/B test that respondents just pick whichever option is listed first) in favor of open-ended "what made you cancel?" phrasing (cites a Groove case study: 10% → 20% usable responses just from rewording "why did you cancel?" to "what made you cancel?").
- Rejects the idea of a single "root cause": uses a medical-examiner analogy (approximate cause of death — "stopped breathing" — versus the actual chain of causes) to argue that reasons like "too expensive" or "project ended" are almost always approximate causes masking a deeper, more actionable issue (e.g., a missing integration, or having targeted a volatile customer segment).
- On AI's use in this analysis: LLMs are good at summarizing themes from open-ended survey responses but bad at surfacing the specific, non-average actionable details — his workaround is asking the model for themes, then asking it to extract every specific supporting detail per theme with attribution.
- Advocates focusing detection/prevention efforts on onboarding by default: cites YouTube retention curves as an analogy — small percentage improvements at the very start of a funnel compound into disproportionately large improvements at the end.
- Endorses "create value for the customer, then split it with them" as the root frame behind pricing and NRR — measure how much value a customer perceives they're getting (ideally a number, but qualitative proxies are fine) and let willingness to pay follow from that, rather than treating price as an isolated lever.
- On channel saturation: recommends deliberately asking "do we know which channels are saturated?" — most companies don't, and default to "add a feature, hope marketing can push harder," which doesn't work once channels are actually saturated. Cites Constant Contact's in-person small-business workshops and HubSpot's agency-channel pivot (grew to 50% of revenue) as examples of genuinely new channels, not incremental tweaks to existing ones.
- Final diagnostic question — "do you need to grow?" — reframed as existential rather than mechanical: sometimes the answer is no (maximizing profit over revenue, as many bootstrapped companies including 37signals do), but for people who are personally built to build/innovate, stagnation itself may be the real problem ("if you're not growing, you're dying" applied to the person, not just the company).
- Contrarian take ([[AB Testing Skepticism]]): most A/B testing doesn't work, because (a) most people can't/don't test the things that actually matter (strategy, vision — "you don't A/B test whether Uber is a good idea"), and (b) statistical false-positive rates dominate results when testing rare/small effects without real rigor — cites a Shopify CTO's admission (from a separate Lenny's Podcast episode) that ~a third of their measured effects vanish under holdout-group re-testing.
- Personal/lightning-round notes: recommends *On Writing Well* (William Zinsser) and *Crossing the Chasm* (Geoffrey Moore, a prior podcast guest); loves *ER* (all 15 seasons, rewatching with his 16-year-old daughter) and *The Pit*; uses Whisper Flow and Anker charging gear; life motto "be yourself, everyone else is taken" (often misattributed to Oscar Wilde); co-won the 2017 Ernst & Young Entrepreneur of the Year (Central Texas) with WP Engine CEO [[Heather Brunner]], whom he considers a genuine late-joining co-founder.
