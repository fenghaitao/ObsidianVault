## Definition

Early-20th-century management theorist (contemporary of [[Frederick Winslow Taylor]]) whose work — decades ahead of its time — is presented by [[Eric Ries]] as the intellectual origin of the **[[Invisible Leader]]** concept central to *Incorruptible*'s closing argument.

## Key Information

- Wrote (circa 1920) ideas Ries says would sound strikingly contemporary if attributed to a 2026 thinker: "we need to focus on power with, not power over"; "the superior and the subordinate together obey the law of the situation"; the hallmark of a leader is whether they can create more leaders.
- Coined the concept of the **invisible leader**: illustrated by her example that Mr. Rowntree, owner of the Rowntree Chocolate Factory, is not really the factory's leader — its true, invisible leader is the shared sense of common purpose he is skilled at instilling in the people who work there.
- Was largely erased from management history for most of the 20th century (as a woman writing radically ahead of her contemporaries) and only rediscovered and republished in the 1990s; Peter Drucker later called her "the prophet of management."
- Ries closes the episode by recommending her as final "homework" for listeners.
