## Definition

Tony Hoare (Sir Charles Antony Richard Hoare, transcribed "Hora") is a major computer-science figure whom Casey Muratori groups with Dijkstra and Knuth as "the three amigos" of the structured-programming era.

## Key Information

- Casey describes Hoare ("Charles Antony Richard") as "another massive figure in computer science," one of the "three amigos" (Hora, Dystra, Canuth) who wrote to each other and are central to computing history.
- Found in Hoare's papers a note from the 1960s describing an idea he "decided not to pursue" — after Peter Naur dismissed it at a conference ("making multipass compilers is easy; I just wrote a nine-pass one") — that with hindsight "pretty much describes static single assignment form" (SSA).
- Casey speculates Naur's dismissal may have accidentally set compiler science back ~15-20 years by discouraging Hoare from pursuing what would have been a very important idea.
