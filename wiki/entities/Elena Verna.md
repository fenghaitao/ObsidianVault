## Definition

Elena Verna is Head of Growth at [[Lovable]] and a record four-time [[Lenny's Podcast]] guest. Previously led growth at [[Dropbox]], [[Miro]], SurveyMonkey, Netlify, and Amplitude. She mentioned working with a professional vibe coder ([[Lazar]]) in an earlier appearance, which prompted [[Lenny Rachitsky]] to invite Lazar onto the show directly (episode 27); episode 38 is her own dedicated interview on Lovable's growth.

## Key Information

- Hired Lazar early on to help execute her growth ideas at Lovable, valuing his "mindset, velocity, and ownership" over a traditional technical background.
- Per Lazar, she was the one who ultimately decided to hire him after he had already been building in public (YouTube, LinkedIn) and effectively doing the job before being formally brought on.
- Joined Lovable roughly 6 months before this episode (the company grew from 30 to 100 people in that span); says only 30-40% of her 15-20 years of growth experience transfers to Lovable's category, versus the 80% she historically felt confident bringing to a new role — the rest requires reinvention, not localization of known patterns.
- Now spends roughly 95% of her time innovating on new growth loops/features versus 5% on optimization — the inverse of her historical ratio — because most of her frameworks were built for optimizing existing user journeys, which she says is "just not worth our time" at Lovable's stage.
- Central frameworks/observations from episode 38: the "[[Product Market Fit Treadmill]]" (PMF must be recaptured roughly every 3 months as both LLM capability and consumer expectations shift); "[[Minimum Lovable Product]]" (MVP's "viability" bar is obsolete — the differentiator is now an emotional "wow" reaction); Lovable's growth secret sauce of giving the product away aggressively (free credits for any user's hackathon) despite thin AI margins (~40%, versus 80-90% for classic SaaS), because it converts users into unpaid distribution agents; building in public via constant, narrated shipping (functions as resurrection, re-engagement, and being-heard strategy simultaneously); and treating activation as the core agent/product team's obsession rather than a growth-team optimization surface.
- Cites [[Bangaly Kaba]]'s "[[Adjacent User Theory]]" and worries the vibe-coding category is entirely focused on retaining early "pioneer" adopters, leaving the much larger "adjacent user"/"latent majority" segment unconverted — risking the category becoming permanently associated with "just for techies" or "just for prototyping."
- Cites a framework from [[John Cutler]] (transcribed "John Cuddler"): software categories move through capability → value → scale stages; she argues vibe coding is still very much in the capability-discovery stage, with what's possible changing every 1-3 months.
- Hiring philosophy at Lovable: prioritizes passion/"fire in the belly," high agency, and high autonomy over pedigree or brand-name prior employers; runs paid work trials; deliberately hires for comfort with chaos.
- On work-life integration: rejects the concept of "balance" in favor of moment-to-moment prioritization, choosing whichever of work/family she'll least regret not prioritizing in the moment; protects sleep, health, and family time as non-negotiables, and considers taking real time off part of doing her best creative work.
- Wrote a post titled "I'm worried about women in tech": cites data suggesting only ~20% of Lovable's users are women (via third-party autofill data, since Lovable doesn't collect this directly), despite the brand's deliberately warm, approachable design; runs "[[She Builds]]," a recurring women-only hackathon with unlimited free Lovable access, to help close the adoption gap. Also observes that AI-native new graduates and ex-founders (rather than traditional corporate hires) are becoming unusually sought-after hires in AI companies.
- Personal AI-usage notes: uses Lovable itself to prototype internal specs/mocks (e.g., recreating a pricing page from a screenshot before handing it to engineering); uses ChatGPT's deep-thinking mode for brainstorming, Granola for meeting summaries, and Whisper Flow for voice-to-text throughout her day.
- Writes her own newsletter at elenaverna.com.
