## Definition

Snapchat is a messaging and camera app company founded by Evan Spiegel, originally started in a small house ("the blue house") in Venice Beach, Los Angeles. The Snapchat-director guest led its camera team as a senior director.

## Key Information

- The guest joined as roughly the 100th employee, and Snapchat grew to about 3,000 employees within two years
- He chose Director of Camera (the first page of Snapchat) instead of Director of Data, learning iOS and Android from scratch for about two months
- Its culture was surprisingly similar to Bay Area/Facebook culture (move fast and break things, done is better than perfect), partly because many early employees came from the Bay Area or Seattle
- CEO Evan Spiegel holds a pixel-level performance bar and deeply values personal connections and the company's engineers
- Snapchat began in "the blue house," a small house in Venice Beach; Spiegel later bought it back and began hosting board meetings there
- Spiegel reportedly told a leader that "no matter what happens, you should never fire the first 15 engineers," because they are the company's founding members
- The guest credits the first two years at Snapchat as among the happiest of his career, spent learning new things daily

- Ryan Olson: Instagram Stories was built because everyday sharing was "evaporating" to Snapchat's ephemeral format; Instagram's unfair advantage was that people already had their friend graph on it, and it added UX touches Snapchat lacked (tap-left to navigate back, hold-to-pause).
