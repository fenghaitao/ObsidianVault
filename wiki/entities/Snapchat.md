## Definition

Social media company founded by [[Evan Spiegel]] and [[Bobby Murphy]]; over 1 billion monthly active users and $6B+ annual revenue, one of the only consumer social apps launched in the last 15 years to endure. Invested heavily in hardware ([[Spectacles]]/Specs) to build durable differentiation beyond easily-copied software features — see [[Software Is Not A Moat]].

## Key Information

- Invented [[Stories]] (ephemeral, un-metriced, chronological sharing), swipe-based navigation, the camera-as-primary-interface pattern, and screenshot-notification detection — features widely copied across the industry (e.g., by Instagram/Threads and TikTok).
- Early distribution insight: unlike bigger networks competing on total connections, Snapchat grew by connecting users to their *closest* friends, on the theory that's where most social value concentrates — a deliberate alternative to raw network-effect scale.
- Runs an intentionally small (9-12 person), flat, non-hierarchical design team that acts as a genuine shipping bottleneck for quality/cohesion — see [[Design As Bottleneck]] — organized per org-design theory in Safi Bahcall's *Loonshots* (see [[Loonshots]]).
- Did not hire its first product manager until ~200 employees; Spiegel's early view was that designers (not PMs) should own product direction, generalizing from his own product-design-plus-engineering dialogue with Bobby Murphy.
- Has ~200 million monthly players across its gaming surface; 25 million Snapchat+ subscribers (over $1B revenue run-rate); building "Specs" AR glasses (successor to Spectacles) after 12 years of hardware investment, launching the same year as this episode.
- Uses AI internally for automated code review (bug detection), agent-assisted debugging from user-submitted crash reports, and a "go-to-market agent" that can draft a product spec, risk analysis, and launch materials from a single product idea — built primarily on [[Claude]].
- Describes itself as tech's "middle child" — much bigger than Pinterest/Reddit, much smaller than Meta/Google — see [[Middle Child Positioning]].
- Not yet net-income profitable as of this episode, due to heavy hardware/platform investment; Spiegel calls this "a crucible moment" requiring the core ad business to prove durable profitability alongside the Specs launch.
