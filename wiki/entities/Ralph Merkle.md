## Definition

Ralph Merkle is a computer scientist who independently invented the key-distribution half of public key cryptography and later earned his PhD at Stanford under Martin Hellman.

## Key Information

- As an undergraduate (later a master's student) at UC Berkeley, in fall 1974 he proposed his key-distribution "puzzle" idea as one of two term-project ideas for CS 244 (computer security); he also proposed a second, more mundane idea.
- The professor's scanned handwritten note said the mundane "idea number two" looked much better, and another professor told him "see me today"; Merkle instead dropped the course and pursued the key-distribution idea on his own.
- Submitted a paper to CACM (Communications of the ACM); editor Susan Graham rejected it, noting the absence of references — implying "has no one thought of doing anything like this before?" (the answer was no).
- His paper appeared in 1978, even though submitted slightly before Diffie-Hellman's; Hellman helped him rewrite it.
- Hellman recruited Merkle to Stanford in summer 1976 as a research assistant (RA) with tuition covered; Merkle completed his PhD under Hellman's supervision.
