## Definition

Facebook Marketplace is the peer-to-peer buy/sell product at [[Meta]] that [[Fiona Fung]] started and took from idea to launch. It now generates over $100 billion in GMV (gross merchandise value) annually.

## Key Information

- Fung led the launch strategy region-by-region, prioritizing a delightful product experience before expanding.
- Early metric lesson: in one launch region, the team initially gated expansion heavily on raw "number of sellers," but Fung noticed people were still successfully finding the items they needed even with fewer sellers, because the region had a smaller number of high-volume "power sellers." The team adjusted the gating metric accordingly — cited as a broader lesson about not blindly following a metric that no longer reflects the actual goal.
- Launch to Latin America (Chile) underperformed other regions; a small on-the-ground research trip (Fung plus two others, testing with local Android phones) revealed the feed loaded poorly over the region's slower LTE connections — a growth blocker that had not surfaced through remote data analysis alone.
- Fung continued personally dogfooding Marketplace even after leaving the team — e.g., discovering a new resale-scam vector firsthand when trying to sell a used MacBook Air.
- Per [[Boris Cherny]] (episode 25): originated from [[Latent Demand]] — around 2016, ~40% of posts in Facebook Groups were people buying and selling items, "abusing" a product not designed for that. Meta first built special-purpose buy/sell groups, then Marketplace itself.
