## Definition

Facebook is [[Meta]]'s original social media platform. [[Adam Mosseri]] worked there before moving to [[Instagram]], designing and leading the early Facebook News Feed and later running the team that built the Facebook ranking algorithm. It is also discussed by [[Mark Pincus]] as a case study in his "[[Cocktail Party Distribution]]" thesis about social products losing and gaining "adrenaline"/productivity value over time. [[Molly Graham]] worked there 2008-2013 (following [[Sheryl Sandberg]] from [[Google]]) and cites her tenure there as the origin of most of her scaling/management frameworks.

## Key Information

- Mosseri joined as a design manager; his first project as a self-declared PM was [[Facebook Home]], which failed but taught him a huge amount about carriers, OEMs, certification, and Android/OS internals.
- Mosseri led the redesign of Facebook's News Feed, which launched in March 2009; the first public comment on the redesign was a derogatory, hateful comment directed at him personally as the designer — an early formative experience in handling public backlash.
- Historically required designers to pass a technical/programming loop before Facebook gave up on that requirement because it was too hard to hire for; Mosseri notes he has only recently been able to "program again" (with AI coding tools) for the first time in roughly a decade.
- Started being active on Twitter while running News Feed specifically because that's "where journalists really lived," believing public debate about the platform was going to happen regardless and the company should participate.
- Per [[Mark Pincus]]: started as "just an app to check out girls and guys at Harvard" — a humble, non-ambitious original premise, per Pincus's "being less ambitious" principle. Gave users massive "social productivity" (staying in the loop with 300-1000+ friends), one of Pincus's examples of a "cocktail party" product whose core value was productivity/lead-generation rather than pure entertainment.
- Pincus reports NPS data showing that when people quit Facebook and Instagram, their net promoter score swung from +35 to -35 — evidence that consumer social has "lost the adrenaline" and people are proud to announce leaving it.
- Zynga's games (FarmVille, CityVille, etc.) were built as viral Facebook-platform products; their virality on Facebook eventually annoyed non-playing friends.
- [[Sam Lesson]] (episode 32) was VP of Product at Facebook before becoming a partner at [[Slow Ventures]]; his life motto, "Carthage must burn," comes from a poster made during a Facebook hackathon in the intense, competitive Facebook Home era ("Google's coming for us... working literally 24 hours a day").

### Per episode 34 (Molly Graham)
- [[Molly Graham]] joined in 2008 when Facebook had 80 million users, $270M revenue, and 500 employees ("smaller than MySpace"; most people assumed it would be sold to Microsoft); left ~2013, six months after the IPO, with 5,500 employees, $5B revenue, and over a billion users.
- Ran on exactly three company goals for the five years Graham was there — growth (monthly active users), engagement, and revenue — the case study behind her "[[Six Rules For Goals And Alignment]]."
- Culture described by Graham as feeling like "a 19-year-old hacker's dorm room" — shipping above all else — in contrast to [[Google]]'s "two PhD students' paradise," per her "[[Founder Personality Defines Culture]]" thesis (Facebook's culture attributed almost entirely to [[Mark Zuckerberg]]'s personality).
- Origin of Graham's "[[Give Away Your Legos]]" framework: she describes reaching a point of "giving away my job every 3 weeks" as the company's scope kept expanding.
- Origin of her "[[J Curve Versus Stairs]]" framework: recruited by [[Chamath Palihapitiya]] off Facebook's HR team onto a mobile-phone hardware project she had no background in, approved by [[Sheryl Sandberg]].
- [[Mark Zuckerberg]]'s "[[Escalation Is A Tool]]" rule (escalate together rather than "tattle") was established here and later carried to the [[Chan Zuckerberg Initiative]].
