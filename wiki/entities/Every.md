## Definition

Every is an AI-forward media and software company led by CEO [[Dan Shipper]]. It publishes writing about AI and building products, and runs roughly six internal software products, with everyone in the company — including non-technical staff — using tools like [[Codex]], [[Claude Cowork]], and [[Claude Code]] to do their work.

## Key Information

- Grew from about 15 people to almost 30 in the year between Dan Shipper's two podcast appearances; includes engineers, designers, writers, editors, salespeople, and customer service staff, all described as AI early adopters.
- Runs internal products including [[Proof]] (a markdown editor Dan built), [[Cora]] (an email agent), and [[Spiral]] (a writing app run by PM [[Marcus]], formerly of Axios).
- Runs an internal consulting practice, supported by an internal agent called [[Claudie]] that runs the whole practice; [[Nitesh]], an AI engineer, spends much of his time as a "forward deployed" operator directing Claudie in Slack.
- COO [[Brandon Gall]] coined the term "[[Computer Errands]]" for personal (non-work) uses of agents, like ordering groceries.
- Adopted [[Open Claw]] (a personal AI agent harness) early and broadly, but found it too fragile/high-maintenance for most employees to sustain without dedicated care — a key data point behind Dan Shipper's prediction that companies will converge on one shared "super agent" rather than many personal agents.
- Did its end-of-2025 quarterly planning entirely through Notion agents: each employee talked to an agent about their goals and results, which then produced AI-generated strategy reports for each team, cross-referenced against overall company strategy.
- Publishes external guides that are explicitly agent-co-authored and meant to be read by both humans and AI agents (so a reader's own agent can ingest and later apply the guide's details).
- Continues to grow SaaS spend year-over-year despite heavy internal AI/agent use — cited by Dan Shipper as evidence against the "SaaS apocalypse."
