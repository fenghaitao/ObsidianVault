## Definition

Andrew Ambrosino is the product and engineering lead for the [[Codex]] app at [[OpenAI]]. He describes his career path as designer → engineer → product manager → founder, and now oversees the desktop app effort that is merging Codex with [[ChatGPT]].

## Key Information

- Prior to OpenAI, he was a startup founder in a heavily regulated industry; that company was eventually sold for parts. He also worked at another startup building AI tools in a locked-down, regulated industry. He describes both experiences as long stretches of "failure" before things started working out (roughly 10–15 years).
- Leads the Codex org: roughly double-digit numbers of engineers, about half that on design, and a smaller number of dedicated product people — deliberately a lean, mostly-IC team with large scope per person and little formal management layering.
- Champions the concept of "[[Taste]]" as the key differentiator for high performers now that implementation is cheap: the ability to curate, steer, and judge signal from noise among many parallel AI-generated attempts.
- Coined/uses the "[[Zone Defense]]" analogy for how product people on his team divide up coverage of the problem space instead of clustering on the same ideas.
- Describes heavy internal [[Dogfooding]] of the Codex app by his own team (including using Codex to manage the very release process of Codex features) as central to the org's practice.
- Discusses the history of the Codex app: started as a CLI, then a developer-focused desktop app (explicitly not an IDE, view-only code), then added an in-app browser, computer use, and artifact creation (the "Codex for almost everyone" release around May), and is now merging with ChatGPT into a general knowledge-work app.
- Recounts that the Codex app released in February would have "absolutely failed" if released as-is in November — the only material difference was underlying model capability, not the product shape.
- Uses Codex extensively in his own work: daily briefs synthesized from ~3,000 Slack channels, automations/scheduled tasks that he can steer conversationally, an internal deep-research workflow, and a spreadsheet-modeling workflow — and built an automation to filter spammy emails using Codex's computer-use capability to configure a Google Cloud Pub/Sub API integration.
- Believes the "PRDs are dead, prototypes are in" narrative is partially wrong: the right medium (document vs. prototype) still depends on what you are trying to communicate or de-risk; documents remain useful for establishing product clarity even though prototyping is now cheap.
- Cautions against fully dissolving product/design/engineering roles — believes each discipline still has a real skill component and best practices, and that eliminating the "product role" entirely (as some companies claim to be doing) is "a terrible idea."
- Notes OpenAI's use of the "member of technical staff" title convention (see [[Member Of Technical Staff]]) as one visible sign of blurring role boundaries, tracing the practice back to Xerox (and a company called "Up There").
- Mentions a colleague, Alexander, who holds a master's degree in computer science and exemplifies the design-team members who also write code (illustrating [[Role Collapse]] within the Codex org) — possibly [[Alexander Imbiricos]], who separately appears as Codex's product lead in episode 39, though this isn't confirmed in either transcript.
