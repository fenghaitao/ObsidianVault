## Definition

Manuel Blum is a computer scientist who, with Silvio Micali, wrote a fundamental paper framing randomness relative to the observer's computational power.

## Key Information

- The Blum–Micali coin-toss thought experiment: three experiments keep the coin toss identical, but the observer upgrades from bare human, to human + laptop, to a Cray supercomputer with sensors and cameras.
- Conclusion: the same physical event looks full-entropy to a weak observer and fully predictable (zero-entropy) to a powerful one — entropy/randomness depends on the observer, not just the event.
- This observer-centric view underlies the complexity-theoretic definition of pseudorandomness and derandomization.
