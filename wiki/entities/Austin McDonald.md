## Definition

Austin McDonald is a former Meta hiring committee member who led mobile (iOS and Android) hiring across the company, and a behavioral-interview coach. (Transcription note: the transcript introduction renders his first name as "Audrey" in one place; the correct spelling is "Austin," matching the episode title.)

## Key Information

- Served as a Meta hiring committee chair-like figure who led iOS and Android recruiting across the company and conducted hundreds of interviews.
- Could not be promoted to M2 without demonstrably solving complex recruiting problems; his key project was improving diversity hiring via better interview preparation, plus clarifying candidate-data quality for Meta's applicant tracking system.
- Emphasizes that everything in the hiring process is evaluative — even the recruiter intro call and the final "do you have questions?"
- Says leveling starts with years of experience and previous title (since Meta's levels aren't public), and that candidates from non-FAANG companies — even big, business-oriented ones — are often downleveled significantly.
- Argues lying on a resume is possible but "much harder than people expect" because phone-screen scope checks and highly calibrated onsite interviewers catch vague or disconnected stories.
- Teaches the "decode, select, deliver" loop for behavioral interviews and prefers the CALR framework (context, actions, results, learnings) over STAR.
- Positions story scope, not relevance, as the number-one selection criterion, so candidates exit an interview having told the highest-scope stories that best represent them.
- Promotes the "leveling rubric" — levels are really about ambiguity, cascading from task (new grad) → feature (mid) → project (senior) → goal (staff) → organization (principal) → industry (distinguished).
