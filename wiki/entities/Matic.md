## Definition

Consumer robot vacuum/mop company (referred to as "Madic" in the transcript's phonetic transcription) founded by Mehul Nariyawala. Used throughout episode 09 of [[Lenny's Podcast]] as a concrete example of the complexity of a modern consumer robotics supply chain.

## Key Information

- Both [[Lenny Rachitsky]] and [[Caitlin Kalinowski]] own and praise Matic devices personally.
- Its CEO/founder raised the question, relayed by Lenny, about a "meteor" of memory (RAM/DRAM) price increases coming for consumer hardware and robotics/[[Physical AI]] companies — the starting point for the episode's discussion of the memory price shock.
- Kalinowski estimates a device like this likely has 50-150 major parts (more once you count every small component like PCB capacitors, likely thousands total), including wheels, vacuum motor, mop reservoir, a SLAM-based mapping system (kept on-device rather than sent to the cloud, for privacy), wireless modules, an SoC, RAM, and PCBs.
- Used as the example for why "all it takes is one piece to not be available and your whole thing is screwed" — a missing diecast part is recoverable in a few months, but a missing SoC/silicon or unavailable RAM requires a catastrophic full board redesign.
