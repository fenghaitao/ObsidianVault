## Definition

Lee is an engineer and engineering manager with experience at Amazon, known for writing about tech career topics and mental health. Lee is autistic, has ADHD, depression, and anxiety, and writes openly about neurodiversity in tech.

## Key Information

- Panelist on the "Getting To Staff" discussions (Sept 2023 and Feb 2024)
- Started software engineering career in 2004 after undergraduate degree
- Worked at smaller companies doing Healthcare Informatics and nonprofit fundraising before entering big tech
- Started at Amazon in 2012 as an SDE2; moved into people management and was promoted to L6 (Senior Engineer equivalent) as a manager
- Later left Amazon and joined Facebook/Meta as an L6 Staff Engineer (IC)
- After a difficult experience at Meta, moved to Google about a year and a half before the Sept 2023 panel as an IC Staff Engineer
- Worked on Google Chat, then moved to Gmail working on notifications
- His most technically complex IC work at Amazon involved writing a custom class loader to solve Android's method limit problem, allowing multiple DEX files in an app before Google supported it — this was for Amazon Video (now Prime Video)
- Tried to get promoted to Senior SDE3 three times at Amazon and failed all three; first try to get to L6 as a manager also failed
- Emphasized that promotions are "trailing" at most big tech companies — you perform at the level first, then get promoted
- Advocated for being explicit about promotion interest even if you don't think you're ready — just ask for perspective on what's missing
- Stressed that "your code cannot advocate on your behalf" — relationship building and visibility are essential
- Asks one off-topic question every day in team chat rooms to build social connection; is known as "the question person" across Google
- Three leadership modes: leading from the front (finding the path and bringing people along) vs. leading from the back (pointing people in the right direction)
- Believes asking the right question at the right time is one of the most powerful tools — being in the right place to hear a decision and call out risks can save months of Dev effort
- Advocated writing your promotion document before you think you're ready: "Write it right now and take it to people and say why would you reject this"
- Is autistic and has ADHD, depression, and anxiety; writes openly about neurodiversity in tech
- Learned to soften communication style: ask questions instead of making declarative statements
- Believes one of the highest measures of intelligence is not what you say but what questions you ask
- Writes on LinkedIn and Substack about tech career and mental health
- Noted that working on "force multiplying" through people leadership allowed achieving staff-level impact without working more than 40 hours a week
