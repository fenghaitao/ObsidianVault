## Definition

Professional negotiator (runs [[Think Warwith]]) who works behind the scenes advising senior tech executives, professional athletes, and Hollywood celebrities on comp, bonuses, M&A, and severance — never negotiating directly himself, describing his approach as "fingerprintless." Has helped clients secure over $1 billion in additional compensation.

## Key Information

- Estimates that a simple, non-aggressive pushback ("what's the chance there could be a little more?") produces roughly a 20% improvement on offers almost universally, across income levels; with his direct coaching, he targets ~40% average movement, with cases of 100-400% increases by breaking salary bands entirely (typically via up-leveling a role, e.g. senior director re-scoped to VP).
- Observes that product managers, engineers, and designers (more introverted/thoughtful profiles) tend to negotiate worse than marketers/revenue leaders (more extroverted profiles), despite product having driven much of tech's value over the last 25 years.
- Core philosophy: negotiation isn't adversarial "politics" but a collaborative value exchange — help the counterparty (a founder/executive) understand what value you create, since companies already extract far more value from top performers than they pay them, especially as W-2 employees rather than owners.
- Advocates negotiating in person or by video call, never by email — since written text strips tone and a stressed reader (rushing through airport security, stuck in traffic) can misread even well-worded pushback; also advocates negotiating with the person who actually controls the P&L/budget, not through a recruiter (a "game of telephone" that loses nuance).
- Advocates deliberately choosing a favorable meeting environment/time — a technique he calls "[[Home Field Advantage]]" — and always taking extra time before responding to signal scarcity and reduce rushed mistakes ("haste equals risk").
- Central pitch technique, "[[Sell The Vacation]]": rather than pitching credentials, ask discovery questions to surface a stakeholder's actual pain, then vividly describe the future state after that pain is solved (with the candidate positioned as the one who got them there) — makes competing candidates' credential-based pitches look weak by comparison.
- Recommends never anchoring to a number too early in a process (since a role's scope often expands mid-interview, "scope creep," and an early number gets used against you later); when directly pressed for a number, deflects with "I don't discuss compensation until we're ready to make an offer" or "what did you have in mind?"
- Warns against "[[Never Split The Difference|splitting the difference]]" reflexively when a counterparty's offer differs from a request — cites a severance case where accepting a "mistaken" halved number without pushing back cost a client $90,000+, and a Hollywood writer case where an agent's insufficiently aggressive counter-anchor (settling at $1M instead of pushing past $1.3M) left money on the table.
- Uses "[[Reciprocity Imbalance]]" as a relationship-building tactic: proactively making warm introductions for a counterparty (without being asked) creates an unspoken social debt that increases the odds of favorable treatment later.
- Frames negotiation power as fundamentally about information and timing (citing the social-deduction game Werewolf, where a small informed minority reliably beats an uninformed majority) — companies negotiate constantly and know salary data at scale, while most individuals negotiate only a handful of times in their careers, which he argues should be a source of confidence (once you understand the imbalance) rather than intimidation.
- On execs specifically: recommends reframing a candidate's job search using whatever professional framework they already know (sales process, marketing positioning, PRD-writing) rather than treating "negotiation" as a foreign academic skill, since curiosity and empathy — core professional skills people already have — are the actual foundation.
- Founding personal motivation: grew up with instability/trauma and found negotiation psychology (fight-or-flight dynamics visible in boardrooms) a lower-stakes version of dynamics he recognized from childhood; now runs the Cody Dieruf Foundation (supporting rural Montana families affected by cystic fibrosis, motivated by his own son's diagnosis) and publishes free negotiation scripts/courses via "Execs in the City" specifically to make this knowledge accessible rather than gatekept.
- Recommends books: *Influence* (Robert Cialdini), *You Can Negotiate Anything* (Herb Cohen), *Negotiation Made Simple* (John Lowry), and *Radical Candor* (Kim Scott, credited with helping him learn assertiveness).
