## Definition

Xavier Leroy is the creator of OCaml and the leader of CompCert, a formally verified C compiler; he is a French computer scientist who worked at INRIA on programming languages and formal verification.

## Key Information

- Creator of OCaml (its predecessor), hired at INRIA on a research position for those early versions and type-system work.
- Led the multi-year effort to prove CompCert — a C compiler — correct, showing its assembly output is faithful to the source C (no miscompilation introduced by the compiler).
- Describes OCaml as both "a fine functional language" and "a fairly decent systems programming language" with a predictable cost model.
- Views Rust as "the finest language... for manual memory management," but stresses manual memory management is not always faster.
- Calls JavaScript "the ultimate dynamic language" and dislikes its runtime-redefinable semantics and introspection ("security nightmare").
- Generally skeptical of generative AI: "every new line of code is a liability"; concerned humans cannot review the volume of AI output.
- Was initially reluctant to rewrite OCaml's garbage collector/allocator for multicore; work was done mostly by OCaml Labs at Cambridge, while he helped settle the memory-model design.
- At ~60, he reflects he may have specialized in programming-language research a little too early and urges a broad computer-science background.
