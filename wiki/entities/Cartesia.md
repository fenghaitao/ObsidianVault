## Definition

Cartesia is a voice-AI company co-founded by Stanford PhDs from Chris Ré's lab (including Albert Gu) where Nimit Sohoni works as an AI researcher. The transcript occasionally garbles the name as "Cartesian."

## Key Information

- Mission: build the next generation of voice AI and a platform for it; flagship product is text-to-speech, plus speech-to-text and voice agents.
- Its research arm focuses on long context, multimodality, continual learning and memory, and test-time compute, targeting real-time, always-on intelligent assistants.
- Main competitor is Eleven Labs (about an 18-month head start); Nimit says Cartesia leads on latency and cost and has strong voice cloning, and pushes toward end-to-end (speech-to-speech) systems.
- Built on state space models (co-founder Albert Gu authored Mamba) and hybrid models; published "H-nets," which learn tokenization boundaries from raw characters rather than a fixed two-stage tokenizer pipeline.
- Nimit joined as an early employee because the founders were his Stanford labmates and because a startup can be more nimble and exploratory than a big lab's groupthink-prone environment.
