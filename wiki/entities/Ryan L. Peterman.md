## Definition

Ryan L. Peterman is a software engineer and content creator who hosts panels and writes about career progression to Staff Engineer. He is known for going from E3 to E6 at Meta in three years, one of the fastest such trajectories.

## Key Information

- Hosted this "Getting To Staff (IC6) at FAANG" panel in February 2024
- Hosted an earlier "Getting To Staff (IC6) at FAANG" panel in September 2023 with panelists Zach Wilson, Lee McKeeman, Carly, and Rahul
- At Meta, went from E3 (new grad) to E6 (Staff) in three promotions over five years — one of the fastest such trajectories
- His staff promotion project was a large efficiency project that made video processing at Instagram 94% cheaper for basic encodings
- Key to his promotion was creating scope: finding the opportunity, convincing people it was a good idea, getting buy-in, and leading execution through others
- Worked on the Instagram product at Meta
- Writes on Substack and LinkedIn about career progression to Staff Engineer
- Runs a YouTube channel focused on career growth topics
- Teaches a course called "senior to staff"
- Referenced the StaffEng.com resource for the IC ladder progression chart
- Grew up in Orange County, Southern California, with no AP Computer Science offered at his school; did not know what CS was until college
- Attended UCLA and started as an undeclared engineer, declared as Electrical Engineer, then switched to Computer Science and Engineering in junior year
- Interned at Bloomberg in New York during junior year, where he finished his project quickly and spent the rest of the summer grinding LeetCode
- Failed every single onsite interview for full-time roles despite aggressive LeetCode practice, ultimately receiving only two offers: Bloomberg return and Amazon
- Joined Amazon to stay on the West Coast, receiving the offer through an unusual SAT-like logic puzzle interview process
- At Meta, went through Boot Camp and chose an Instagram infrastructure team, but the manager had no headcount — Ryan had to beg and convince him to find headcount, demonstrating the importance of agency in seeking out the best opportunities
- As a junior engineer, he was an "absolute workhorse" churning out code, submitting up to 10 diffs a day during his most coding-heavy period, and worked until the latest shuttle at 9:27 PM
- His staff promotion project was a compute efficiency optimization for Instagram video encoding that was impactful because COVID increased platform demand; the optimization itself was trivial — eliminating redundant work
- Transitioned to TLM (Tech Lead Manager) with 70% IC / 30% manager split, then eventually pivoted to a full org leader (traditional manager) role
- Also switched to management because he felt growing to IC7 would make him a "snowflake" — narrowly useful only at very large companies, with limited transferability
- Hosted a November 2024 podcast episode interviewing Ricky, a Staff Engineer at Google who reached L6 by age 28, covering accelerated promotion strategies, the trade-offs of seniority, and the Manager vs IC decision. Also hosted a November 2024 podcast episode interviewing Zach Wilson about his career journey from IC3 at Facebook to Staff Engineer at Airbnb by age 26, covering job hopping, interviewing leverage, manager trust, and the trade-offs of accelerated career growth.
- Hosted a January 2025 podcast episode interviewing Evan King about his rapid career growth to Staff Engineer at Meta by age 25, creating a dual-perspective narrative by interweaving his own parallel career experience. Also hosted a March 2025 podcast episode interviewing Steve Huynh, a former Amazon Principal Engineer, covering Amazon's interview culture, performance management, the SDE3-to-Principal promotion gauntlet, and career advice. Also hosted an April 2025 podcast episode interviewing Rahul Pandey about his career journey from a Stanford spinout startup to Staff Engineer at Meta by age 28 and founding Taro. Also hosted an April 2025 podcast episode interviewing Jia Chen, a college student who won 21 hackathons with a 70% win rate, covering hackathon strategy, agency, content creation, and founding Sprint.dev. Also gave a talk at UCLA in May 2025 with Ricky about industry secrets for students, covering the engineering ladder, internship success, promotion strategies, luck vs. agency, imposter syndrome, big tech vs. startups, and the importance of soft skills and asking questions.
