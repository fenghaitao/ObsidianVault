## Definition

Claude Shannon is a mathematician and the founder of information theory, whose theorem ties the security of a secret to its entropy.

## Key Information

- Shannon's theorem, as Wigderson cites it, says "the quality of your password is as good as the entropy in it."
- This distinguishes generating secrets (passwords) — which require true, information-theoretic randomness independent of any observer's compute — from fooling a computationally bounded test.
- For secrets, unlimited computational power is irrelevant: the demand is genuinely that a bit be half-heads/half-tails.
- Martin Hellman cites Shannon's 1949 paper (published in the Bell System Technical Journal) connecting information theory to cryptography; Peter Elias handed it to Hellman at MIT, convincing him his information-theory PhD could apply to cryptography.
