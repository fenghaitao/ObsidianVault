## Definition
Claude Shannon was a pioneering computer scientist who in 1949 wrote the seminal paper "Programming a Computer to Play Chess," proposing two types of chess engines: Type A (brute force search) and Type B (selective/intuitive). Referred to by the speakers as "the OG Claude."

## Key Information
- 1949 paper envisioned two approaches to computer chess: Type A engines that search all possible moves, and Type B engines that selectively pick the best moves
- Assumed Type B would be necessary because computers were too weak for exhaustive search — but computers quickly improved, making Type A viable
- His classification framework remains relevant: Deep Blue and Stockfish are Type A, while AlphaZero represents Type B
- The Play Magnus team referenced Shannon to contextualize the evolution from brute force engines to modern LLM-based chess explanation
