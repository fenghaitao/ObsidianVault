## Definition
Xerox PARC (Palo Alto Research Center) was the legendary R&D division of Xerox that pioneered many foundational concepts of personal computing, including the graphical user interface, the mouse, and networked computing. In the keynote, it is cited as the model for how to plan for the future: project capability trends forward, build expensive prototypes now, and be ready when the economics become viable.

## Key Information
- Pioneered the graphical user interface (GUI), mouse, Ethernet, laser printing, and object-oriented programming
- Alan Kay and colleagues at PARC adopted the technique of projecting Moore's Law forward to build systems that were initially too expensive and slow for market, but allowed them to design for and eventually create the future of computing
- This technique is recommended for LLM application builders: use projected cost reduction trends to design applications that will be economical in 12-30 months
- The lesson: you may not be able to sell it now, but you can "live it" — design and prototype for future capabilities
