## Definition

mathlib is Lean's community-built mathematical library — the corpus of formalized definitions and theorems that lets users state (and then prove) mathematical problems in Lean.

## Key Information

- Began around 2017 ("the beginning of the Lean mathematical library"), and is central to why "Lean became popular first with math."
- Provides the definitions (for example, real numbers) needed simply to *state* a problem; de Moura says that with mathlib, writing IMO problems in Lean is now "the easy part."
- A "massive library and massive community" is one of Lean's competitive advantages: to state open conjectures you first need the concepts available in a library.
- Enables collaborative, trust-free mathematics: because you don't have to trust someone else's proof (Lean can fill holes and check), large numbers of people can work together.
- Underpins AI math: systems that solve IMO-style problems rely on mathlib to translate problem statements into Lean before attempting proofs.
