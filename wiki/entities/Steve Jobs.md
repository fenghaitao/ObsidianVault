## Definition

Co-founder of Apple, cited by [[Mark Pincus]] as an exemplar of the "[[Close To The Metal]]" management principle and as the person who saw an MIT touchscreen demo (later realized as the iPhone) at a TED conference. Also recalled at length by [[Tony Fadell]] as the model of opinion-based, "taste"-driven leadership and obsessive storytelling during iPod/iPhone development.

## Key Information

- Pincus recounts attending a TED conference where an MIT team demoed an early touchscreen on a giant whiteboard; Jobs was reportedly obsessed, spending the whole session with the team — cited as the origin of the "new" element (touch) that, combined with proven phone/iPod patterns, became the [[iPhone]].
- Cited alongside Jeff Bezos as an example of a CEO who insisted on micromanaging pixel-level details (e.g., picking out conference-room carpeting) rather than delegating core product/UX decisions — the "[[Close To The Metal]]" principle.
- Per Tony Fadell: made the final opinion-based call to go with an on-screen (multi-touch) keyboard over a physical keyboard for the iPhone, despite inconclusive data on both sides ("We are going this way"), and told dissenters they could work on a different project if they weren't on board — Fadell's example of "benevolent dictatorship" / taste-driven decision-making.
- Refused Windows connectivity for the first iPod ("over my dead body... this is going to help us sell more Macs"), a call later reversed once low sales proved the Mac-only strategy wrong; the iPod's eventual Windows support is credited by Fadell with helping save Apple from near-bankruptcy.
- Refused a stylus for the iPhone/iPad, insisting on finger-first design ("your finger is good enough"); a stylus was later added via a "skunk works" project and became a valued feature without displacing finger-first design.
- Rehearsed and refined the story of the iPhone daily for roughly 2.5 years before launch, testing the pitch on trusted people, so his keynote delivery appeared effortless; held that "the best marketing just tells the truth."
- Shut down a proposal to add adult content to the iTunes Store/video offering ("Is that the kind of world you want your kids to grow up in?") — cited by Fadell as an example of needed ethical leadership in product design.
- Per [[Eric Ries]] (Incorruptible): cited as a "harder is easier" exemplar — Jobs reportedly fought with people over the internal layout of wires inside a computer that customers would never be permitted to open or see, an obsession with quality for its own sake. Ries also cites Jobs's practice of holding skip-level meetings specifically with "[[Torchbearers|torchbearers]]" throughout the org (people simply committed to doing the right thing) rather than only his direct subordinates.
- Per [[Matt MacInnis]], who spent roughly eight years at [[Apple]] under Jobs: coined (per MacInnis) the internal term "the death march" for the practice of throwing engineers immediately into building the next iPhone version the moment the current one shipped, with no break — cited by MacInnis as formative for his own view that sustained organizational intensity, not episodic effort, produces extraordinary outcomes (see [[Extraordinary Results Require Extraordinary Effort]]).
