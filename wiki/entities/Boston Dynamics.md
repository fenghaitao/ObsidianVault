## Definition
Boston Dynamics is a robotics company famous for highly acrobatic humanoid and legged-robot demonstrations built on sophisticated hardware and traditional control.
## Key Information
- Its classic demos show "very sophisticated, very carefully designed hardware" with a traditional control approach, and comparatively less emphasis on the decision-making side.
- Levine distinguishes two jobs: controls is "when you have to control the robot body," while AI is "when you have to take into account what goes on outside of the robot."
- A backflip on flat ground mostly requires dealing with the robot itself, whereas picking up a coffee cup off a table requires understanding the rest of the world.
- The field's shift from Boston Dynamics-style feats to learned manipulation reflects that hardware is now "good enough," and the bottleneck is the decision-making loop that reacts to the environment.
- A hand-designed controller that performs a sophisticated behavior is a "proof of existence" that a learnable, generalizable control law exists — "parsimonious does mean generalizable."
