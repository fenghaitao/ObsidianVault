## Definition

Founder of [[Airbnb]], cited twice by [[Mark Pincus]]: as an example of "collecting winnings, not making bets" (launching only when you already believe it's a hit) and of doing things "in non-scalable ways," working hands-on with the team.

## Key Information

- Pincus: "If you talk to Brian Chesky about his launches, he already knows he has a hit. He's not launching it to find out if people like it" — illustrating the belief-vs-hope distinction in [[Kill Hope Before Hope Kills You]].
- Cited alongside Jeff Bezos and Zuck as a founder who does non-scalable things by hand with the team rather than delegating.
- Per [[Keith Rabois]] (episode 15): an early investor's key evidence for the Airbnb opportunity was Chesky's own observation of actual Craigslist room-rental listings, not customer surveys. Rabois says he and Chesky are "usually in sync" on management philosophy, citing Chesky's "founder mode" of continually pushing pace even when the company is already succeeding — since Rabois observed morale actually dropping among top performers during rare lulls, not rising.
