## Definition
Bell Labs was the storied AT&T research laboratory in New Jersey where Unix, C, and C++ were created.

## Key Information
- Bjarne calls it "the best place in the world" for applied math and engineering at the time, "probably twice as many computer scientists as MIT."
- The home of Unix, C, and much of the theory behind them when Bjarne arrived.
- Research philosophy: "hire the best people you can find and don't tell them what to do" — a researcher's job was "do something interesting in a year's time" and report on one sheet of paper in ≥9-point font.
- This "fairly anarchic" model produced most of the famous Bell Labs output; other parts of the building produced optical fibers, wireless technology, charge-coupled devices (cameras), and cellular phone systems.
- Hiring was informal: chat with people like Dennis Ritchie and Sandy Fraser, then they recommend you to the director.
- Its density of talent gave Bjarne impostor syndrome at first; frequent lunches and open doors were how he learned.
