## Definition

4,000 Weeks is a book by Oliver Burkeman built on the premise that the average human lifespan is roughly 4,000 weeks, which reframes time as scarce and makes each week feel more precious and consequential.

## Key Information

- Philip Su names it as his top book recommendation for people earlier in their career who are still figuring out how to plan things, crediting it with helping him "take a broader look around."
- The core framing he takes from it: visualize today's biggest goal for the next several years, then "pretend you've just reached it — what do you do next?" A good answer means you will be fine; Philip admits he did not have one.
- Philip connects it to Tim Urban's well-known "life in weeks" graphic, in which every week of a whole life is drawn as a single box.
- It helped Philip see that many of his decisions were hard because his personal values were not clear.
- He ties the book to the "day after" problem — athletes who reach NFL/NBA stardom have built no skills in other domains and have nothing planned for after they peak.
