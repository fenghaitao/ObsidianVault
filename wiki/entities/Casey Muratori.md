## Definition

Casey Muratori is a video game technology developer (best known for a licensed character animation system written at Rad Game Tools and for work on The Witness) and a self-described amateur computer historian whose talks trace the origins of famous programming advice.

## Key Information

- Calls his historical research "dumpster diving" — not derogatory: "it's actually a very enjoyable experience" to pick through computing history, and every dive yields mind-boggling amounts of previously unknown context.
- Gave two computer-history talks: one on the history of "premature optimization is the root of all evil" (at the Better Software Conference, the second of two such talks) and "The Big Oops: Anatomy of a 35-Year Mistake" about Sketchpad.
- Grew up with computers at home because his father worked at Digital Equipment Corporation; learned to program as a child.
- Interned at Microsoft around 1995; the manager the interns were assigned to had a "massive flame out" and left the week before arrival, so his internship became "pretty lame" (e.g., an ANI cursor loader for animated Windows cursors).
- Left the Microsoft path to join a startup with Microsoft alumni he admired (including Chris Hecker), then worked at Gaspired Games, then Rad Game Tools.
- At Rad Game Tools wrote (almost entirely himself, except Jeff Roberts' texture compressor) the character animation system released in 1999; the second version (~2001) is still largely unchanged and is still used in Baldur's Gate 3 (Larian).
- Contributed a little to The Witness (Jonathan Blow), specifically the walk system; calls the game "absolutely fantastic" and says the design was good though parts of his implementation were "janky."
- Credits Chris Hecker with "basically complete credit" for teaching him to read technical papers (SIGGRAPH proceedings) and to use Citeseer — the skill that made his history talks possible.
- Says he is "absolutely terrible at understanding game design"; his strength and interest is engine technology and low-level engineering.
- Career advice he'd give his younger self: get into low-level programming earlier, and don't be afraid to say "I don't understand this — please explain it to me."
