## Definition

Co-founder of [[PayPal]]; assembled most of the company's non-technical hires largely through his personal/Stanford network. Taught [[Keith Rabois]] the "[[Undiscovered Talent]]" philosophy of building a company around people the market hasn't correctly evaluated yet. Also originated the "[[Determinate Vs Indeterminate Optimism]]" 2x2 framework, discussed at length by [[Marc Andreessen]].

## Key Information

- Quit practicing law after 5 months and 4 days, per Rabois — cited (alongside [[David Sacks]], who never practiced law) as evidence that leaving law quickly for tech correlated with better outcomes among PayPal's founders.
- Rabois recalls Thiel teaching him the undiscovered-talent lesson literally while jogging around the Stanford campus early in his PayPal tenure.
- Per Marc Andreessen (episode 29): long-time critic of "no progress in atoms, only in bits" — Andreessen says he's come around considerably to this view, since regulatory/cartel structures prevent the physical world from changing as fast as software has. Also originated the determinate/indeterminate optimism framework Andreessen uses to describe founders versus VCs.
- Debated Andreessen (referenced by Lenny) on whether technological progress would continue or stall — Andreessen was proven right on continued progress in bits, but now credits Thiel's narrower point about stalled progress in atoms.
- Per [[Lazar]] (episode 27, professional vibe coding): attributes to a person he names as "Peter Steel" (almost certainly Peter Thiel, rendered phonetically in the transcript) a recent remark that anything deterministic — a clean X-input-equals-Y-output math problem — is where AI will most directly displace human work, contrasted with human-relational/emotional-judgment work, which he argues stays valuable longer.
