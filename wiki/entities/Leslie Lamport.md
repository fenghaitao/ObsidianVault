## Definition

Leslie Lamport is an American computer scientist and Turing Award winner known for foundational contributions to distributed systems — the bakery algorithm, logical clocks and the "happens before" relation, the Byzantine generals problem, and Paxos — and for creating LaTeX.

## Key Information

- Won the Turing Award for his contributions to distributed systems.
- Holds a PhD in mathematics, which shaped his preference for written proofs and invariants.
- Started with a buggy two-process mutual-exclusion solution that taught him "concurrent programs are hard to get right and you needed a proof that they were correct."
- Invented the bakery algorithm, "Time, Clocks, and the Ordering of Events" (with logical clocks and the happens-before relation), the Byzantine generals framing, and Paxos.
- Spent a month in the Netherlands in 1976 with Edsger Dijkstra (via colleague Carel Scholten), which yielded a variant of the bakery algorithm as its one published result.
- Built LaTeX from TeX macros to write his book.
- Worked mostly in industry; says abstraction — not raw intelligence — is his real gift, and only recognized it in his last decade.
- Coined the maxim: "if you think you know something but don't write it down, you only think you know it."
- Declines to second-guess his past: "I shouldn't waste time trying to answer questions that I don't have to answer."
