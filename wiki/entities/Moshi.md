## Definition
Moshi is the first full-duplex speech-to-speech conversational model, developed by Kyutai and released approximately 2 years ago (circa 2024). It handles true bidirectional conversation where both parties can speak simultaneously, including back-channeling and interruptions, while maintaining conversational coherence.

## Key Information
- Developed by Kyutai, a non-profit AI research lab
- First speech-to-speech model designed for conversation
- Only full-duplex speech-to-speech model as of May 2026
- Full duplex: can handle simultaneous speaking, back-channeling ("mhm"), coughing, interruptions
- Model can start answering before the user finishes speaking
- User can talk over the model and it considers what was said
- Very robust to noise and multiple speakers
- Strong conversational flow — described as "impossible to match"
- Major limitation: "very stupid" — no tool calling, no observability, no real paralinguistic understanding
- Could talk for a few minutes before becoming pointless
- Impossible to use in production due to lack of observability and safety detection
- Nvidia later published PersonalPlex based on Moshi
- Part of Kyutai's open research output alongside speech-to-speech translation and Pocket TTS
