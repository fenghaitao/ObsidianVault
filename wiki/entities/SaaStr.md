## Definition

SaaStr is the "world's largest community for B2B founders," founded by [[Jason Lemkin]] as a blog in 2012 after he sold his prior startup to Adobe. It runs SaaStr Annual (a ~10,000-person event, held since 2015/2016) plus seven European events, does eight figures of revenue a year, and became the case study for Lemkin's AI-agent go-to-market transition (transcribed phonetically in the source as "Saster"/"Zaster").

## Key Information

- History: started as a blog documenting Lemkin's mistakes post-Adobe acquisition; ran informal meetups before "anyone did this stuff"; first big meetup in 2015; grew to 10,000 attendees a year at SaaStr Annual; 12 years/12 events in the US plus 7 in Europe (~20 major events total, ~3,000-4,000 talks across the smaller ones).
- Business model: sells conference sponsorships (avg. $70-80K, though some run $500K+ for multi-year deals) and tickets (a few hundred dollars up to ~$2,000 for late-deciding VCs); has ~100 sponsors; Lemkin also personally invests (~$200M lifetime, ~10x return) into founders from the community, though he says the investing "turned into a business" somewhat unintentionally.
- Database scale used to justify AI GTM: ~400,000 contacts (vs. Lenny's 1.2M newsletter subscribers) — Lemkin's point is that even a company with "only" 30,000 website visitors or leads (regardless of only having 200-300 paying customers) has enough volume to make agentic GTM worthwhile.
- Pre-AI GTM team: 2-3 SDRs and up to 5 AEs (8-9 people total) managing both the high-touch sponsorship sales cycle and the high-volume/self-serve ticket sales, plus lapsed-lead reactivation, using different vendors/workflows for high-end vs. low-end segments.
- The AI transition trigger: at SaaStr Annual (the ~10,000-person event) two long-tenured, well-paid salespeople quit on site; Lemkin, having already seen a general-purpose digital-clone agent ("Deli"/[[Delphi]]) close a $70K sponsorship autonomously, decided "we're done hiring humans in sales."
- Post-transition team: 1.2 humans (one full-time AE plus [[Amelia]], "Chief AI Officer," at 20% time) plus 20 AI agents, occupying the 10 desks that used to be GTM staff, each labeled with its agent's name (e.g., "Reply" for [[Replit]], "Quali" for [[Qualified]], "Arty" for [[Artisan]]); net productivity is "about the same" as the 10-person human team but far more efficient.
- Also builds and gives away its own free tools for the community, entirely on [[Replit]] (built by Lemkin himself, a self-described "top 1% user" who cannot code): a startup-valuation calculator (used ~800,000 times in 90 days), a pitch-deck reviewer (reviewed ~3,000 decks), and others — explicitly separate from its GTM stack, which was bought, not built in-house.
- saastr.ai hosts all of SaaStr's built agents/tools publicly ("it's all bulleted out, you can copy us").
