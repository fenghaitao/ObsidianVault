## Definition

Daily-deal email company, once one of the fastest-growing private companies in America, cited by [[Eric Ries]] in *Incorruptible* — via founder [[Andrew Mason]]'s account — as a cautionary tale about incremental erosion of a founding principle under ROI pressure.

## Key Information

- Built around and went public on the premise of sending exactly "one email a day" with a deal.
- After going public, executives/employees repeatedly pressured Andrew Mason to send more emails to "make the quarter," framing it in lean-startup-adjacent language ("shouldn't we run an experiment? what does the data say?"). Mason ran the experiment; two emails a day made more money, so he "couldn't say no" — and the frequency crept from two, to three, to eventually eight emails a day.
- Ries says he has heard nearly identical "email frequency" erosion stories from many other CEOs — used as the general pattern for how ROI-based incrementalism destroys a founding principle one small, individually-justifiable step at a time.
