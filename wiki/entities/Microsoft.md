## Definition

Microsoft is the company where [[Fiona Fung]] spent 11 years early in her career, working on the Visual Studio editor team and later on TypeScript, after joining in the early 2000s.

## Key Information

- Fung joined Microsoft's Visual Studio team in the early 2000s, her first exposure to using an IDE (she came from a Unix/terminal-debugging background at IBM and initially did not know what Visual Studio was).
- She worked on the Visual Studio editor team, using the VS editor to build the VS editor itself — the origin of her long-standing commitment to [[Dogfooding]].
- In that era, software shipped on physical CDs, creating hard, fixed deadlines that forced heavy upfront planning — a contrast Fung draws with today's continuous-shipping, AI-accelerated development environment.
- Also ran the teams that built TypeScript, per this source's framing of her background.
- Rapid engineer feedback loops existed inside the VS team itself (engineers were heavy users of their own product) even before social media made fast customer feedback broadly available.
- Per [[Benedict Evans]] (episode 07): used as the historical example of a platform company that gained real leverage "up the stack" via Windows — the browser wars showed Microsoft could use distribution to force adoption (of Internet Explorer over Netscape), but Evans notes winning the browser war ultimately "didn't matter" because value moved further up the stack anyway. Evans's central open question for AI is whether foundation models get Windows-style stack leverage or end up as commodity infrastructure more like AWS.
- Steven Sinofsky (a former Microsoft Windows executive, later at [[Andreessen Horowitz]]) is quoted by Evans: incumbents always try to frame a disruptive new technology as "just a feature" of their existing product — "sometimes they're right, sometimes it's a feature."
