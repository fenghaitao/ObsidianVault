## Definition

CS50 is Harvard's introductory computer-science course, transformed by David J. Malan into a world-class, free online course that teaches problem-solving via Scratch, C, Python, JavaScript, and an AI week.

## Key Information

- Malan first took it in 1996 under Brian Kernighan (Margo Seltzer taught it before him); he took over in 2007.
- Went online through Harvard Extension School (1999 VHS filming), RealVideo/RealAudio streaming, then iPod/iTunes podcasting via RSS, then video iPod.
- Lectures are deliberately ~3 hours (not bite-sized MOOC chunks), which Malan defends because online you can pause/rewind; he targets "memorable moments" (phone-book binary search, students sorting on stage) each class.
- Keeps C as the foundation because it is close to hardware and forces students to build hash tables/tries themselves; the week 5 hash table shrinks to a one-line Python dict in week 6.
- Includes a one-hour AI introduction (timed with family weekend) and a "virtual rubber duck" (cs50.ai) built on OpenAI and Microsoft Azure APIs, deliberately less helpful than ChatGPT.
- Historically disciplines 5–10% of the class each semester for academic dishonesty; AI has made it harder to produce a "smoking gun."
- Final projects encourage and welcome Claude, ChatGPT, or Gemini, which are otherwise not allowed for assignments.
- Has been offered in parallel at Yale and Oxford; MIT's OpenCourseWare inspired its open-courseware mission.
- Malan's goal is "not to output programmers, but engineers and educated citizens."
- Reiterated in July 2026: C is "a pretty small language" with a small standard library unless you add third-party code, so most things must be built yourself; it balances English-like syntax over lower-level primitives and sits just above assembly code.
- Week 5 has students build hash tables and covers singly/doubly linked lists, tries, trees, and abstract data types like stacks and queues; week 6 "whittles" that down to a one-line Python dictionary, scaffolding higher-level languages.
- Malan frames the C-first value as threefold: more-informed data-structure design decisions, first-principles diagnosis of what could go wrong, and a scaffold to higher-level languages.
