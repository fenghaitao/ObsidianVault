## Definition

Lazar (full surname given only phonetically in the transcript as "Yavanovich") is [[Lovable]]'s first official "vibe coding engineer" — a full-time employee whose job is to vibe code internal and external products all day. He has no software-engineering background and has never written a line of code beyond a few manual console logs.

## Key Information

- Started in Lovable's growth team, brought on by [[Elena Verna]] (head of growth), and has since expanded into go-to-market and internal enterprise/community tooling; describes his scope as unusually wide across departments because the role "complements so many things."
- Shipped both internal tools (e.g., a feature-adoption matrix) and public-facing products, including Lovable's Shopify-integration marketing templates and Lovable's own merch store (fully vibe coded, including physical shirts sold from it).
- Background before Lovable: a forestry engineer by training (not software), plus blue-collar jobs (waiting tables, working at Subway) and early startup roles in community management and social media/distribution — frames his non-linear path as directly useful ("slumdog millionaire" analogy).
- Says he began vibe coding in July 2024 — about seven months before [[Andrej Karpathy]] coined the term "vibe coding" in early 2025 — and taught it via a YouTube channel and paid course for three or four months before the term existed.
- Core philosophy: not having a technical background is an advantage because it makes him "positively delusional" — assuming everything is possible until proven otherwise — while pairing that with self-awareness about needing clarity and good prompting to make it real.
- Spends roughly 80% of his time in planning/chat mode and only 20% executing/building, because he believes the scarce, valuable skill is clarity and judgment, not raw code output; he deliberately reads only the agent's natural-language output, never the underlying code.
- Developed a personal debugging escalation framework he calls "[[4x4 Debugging Framework|4x4]]" (try-to-fix button → console-log awareness layer → external diagnostic tool like Codex/Claude → revert and re-prompt, then teach the lesson into a rules file).
- Built a stack of living planning documents for every project (master plan, implementation plan, design guidelines, user journey, tasks.md, and a rules.md/agent.md instructions file) to keep the AI agent's limited context/token window continuously supplied with the right information — see [[Token Budgeting]].
- Recommends starting any new project with four parallel first attempts (voice brain-dump, typed prompt, a pulled design reference from [[Mobbin]] or [[Dribbble]], and an actual code/component snippet from a library like [[21st.dev]]) to reach clarity fast.
- Credits exposure to Lovable's designers (Felix, Nad, Abby) for his biggest personal upskill — recounts discovering that a seemingly simple gradient he tried to copy from Figma was actually 50 layered colors with varying opacity, reshaping his sense of what "world class" design requires.
- Believes coding is being commoditized ("coding is going to be like calligraphy") while judgment, clarity, taste, and design remain durably valuable; predicts PMs and then designers are the biggest relative winners from AI.
- Argues elite software engineering remains necessary and scarce — for maintaining/scaling codebases and infrastructure as everyone builds more (citing repeated Cloudflare outages as an example) — even as building the application layer itself becomes broadly accessible.
- Describes his own career path as becoming a job "by building in public": you don't need a company to hire you as a professional vibe coder — you can hire yourself first by sharing your work (his YouTube channel, LinkedIn posts, and public course) before being formally hired. Elena Verna hired him after he was already effectively doing the job.
- Notes some job candidates have gotten hired at Lovable by sending a Lovable-built app instead of a resume.
- States he is a member of the "top 0.1% elite level" of vibe coders and refers to himself as a future "rapid engineer" as the vibe-coding/coding distinction dissolves.
- Per [[Elena Verna]] (episode 38): connected with her over social media and joined part-time to help scale her personal vibe-coding output, including building a set of Shopify-Lovable templates for the Shopify integration partnership and vibe-coding the first version of Lovable's "[[She Builds]]" hackathon site and application process. Verna describes him as "part technical" and says a non-technical person can fill this role if they're genuinely skilled.
