## Definition

Jeanne DeWitt Grosser (rendered "Jean"/"Gan Grosser" in the transcript's ASR) is COO at [[Vercel]], overseeing marketing, sales, customer success, revenue ops, and field engineering. She previously built [[Stripe]]'s early sales team from the ground up as Chief Product Officer there, and started her career on Google's original Gmail team in 2004.

## Key Information

- Defines "go-to-market" broadly as any function that touches a customer or makes a dollar (marketing, sales, technical sales/sales engineering, customer success, support, partnerships) — arguing AI is now driving consolidation of the roughly 17 historically hyper-specialized GTM roles back into a more integrated life cycle.
- At Stripe (~2017), led "Project Rosalind" — an early, largely unsuccessful attempt (with data scientist collaborator Ben Salman) to build a company-attribute database for templated, "Mad Libs"-style personalized outbound email; the same underlying idea now works well with modern AI, per her Vercel rebuild of the concept.
- Coined/champions the "[[Go-To-Market Engineer]]" role: a technical hire (often an ex-sales-engineer) who builds custom internal AI agents replicating and exceeding top human performers' workflows. Vercel's first three GTM engineers were all former front-end developers who had become sales engineers.
- Built Vercel's "lead agent," which let the team shift 9 of 10 inbound SDRs to outbound work in ~6 weeks while holding lead-to-opportunity conversion flat; also built "dealbot," a real-time Gong-transcript-based coaching agent evolved from a lost-deal post-mortem tool.
- Central strategic framework: "[[GTM As Product]]" — since technical product differentiation narrows over time, the experience of being sold to becomes the differentiator; treats the full customer journey (awareness through multi-year retention) as a product to be intentionally designed, citing Stripe's hands-on payments-architecture whiteboarding first sales call as an example.
- Multi-dimensional segmentation framework: beyond company size, layers in growth potential (critical for consumption pricing), traffic/"promote" signals (e.g., a smaller company with outsized web traffic gets enterprise-level attention), and workload/business-model type (B2B vs. B2C vs. platform/marketplace at Stripe; e-commerce vs. SaaS vs. crypto at Vercel).
- Cites a stat that ~80% of customers buy to avoid pain/reduce risk rather than chase upside — positioning around risk avoidance resonates more broadly (especially in enterprise) than "art of the possible" upside pitches, which mostly land with fellow founders. Connects this to a related point from a separate episode with [[April Dunford]].
- States a goal for GTM orgs to be "equal parts revenue-driving and R&D" — since a sales team may talk to more customers per week than an entire product-research function, translating that feedback into structured signal makes GTM an extension of product management. Litmus test for a strong AE: it should take 10 engineers 10 minutes to realize the AE isn't a PM.
- Praised by former colleagues [[Claire Hughes Johnson]] and [[Kate Jensen]] for this product/engineering fluency.
- On PLG: still a strong default starting motion for non-enterprise-first products, but has a ceiling — companies that delay building a real sales motion hit a growth wall; recommends hiring a first salesperson around ~$1M ARR once a repeatable sales process (not just founder-led evangelist selling) exists.
- On pricing: treats pricing as its own product discipline; cites Vercel's shift from a bundled SaaS-like price to an unbundled Pro/Enterprise split (moving startup-relevant features into self-serve) as a major recent PLG growth driver.
- On hiring/comp: favors a "diversified portfolio" sales team mixing traditional sales backgrounds with consulting/banking backgrounds; flags that rigid, annually-locked sales compensation plans conflict with the need to pivot quickly as products change (Vercel's AI Cloud didn't exist when this year's sales comp plan was written).
- Life motto: "when the going gets tough, the tough get going" (from her mother), paired with "where there's a will there's a way"; also cites a sales-training phrase, "yeses are great, nos are great, maybes will kill you." Was a (self-described unremarkable) competitive collegiate diver, crediting the sport for her obsession with precision, replicability, and comfort with repeated failure.
