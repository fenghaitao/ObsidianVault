## Definition

Company [[Keith Rabois]] was an early investor in; cited as one of the generation-defining companies (alongside Stripe and OpenAI) whose early employees reportedly faced outside skepticism/mockery before it succeeded. See [[Ugly Baby]]. Per [[Jason Lemkin]] (episode 35), also the originator of the modern "forward deployed engineer" concept and, per [[Marc Benioff]], the model for letting enterprise customers go live before they pay.

## Key Information

### Per episode 35 (Jason Lemkin)
- Described as "obviously the single most successful public B2B company at the moment" (Lemkin notes Databricks and others may catch up in the next wave of IPOs).
- Originated the "forward deployed engineer" title/model, per Lemkin, though at a different scale than most companies: Palantir's version involves teams like "Gary Tan and an army of folks" embedded on-site for six months on nine-figure deals — Lemkin contrasts this with the smaller-scale forward-deployed model most SaaS/AI companies (including SaaStr) actually need. See [[Forward Deployed Engineer]].
- Cited (secondhand, via Lemkin quoting [[Marc Benioff]]) as the company Salesforce's CEO most envies — not just for its high deal sizes, but because every Palantir customer can go live before paying, which Benioff says he wishes Salesforce could replicate but can't yet at Salesforce's scale.
