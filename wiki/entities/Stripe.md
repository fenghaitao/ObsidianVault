## Definition

Company [[Keith Rabois]] was an early investor in; cited as one of the generation-defining companies (alongside Palantir and OpenAI) whose early employees reportedly faced outside skepticism/mockery before it succeeded. See [[Ugly Baby]].

## Key Information

- Rabois notes his mother was skeptical of most of his tech career but did appreciate Stripe specifically, and repeatedly lobbied him to invest before he eventually did.

### Per episode 42 ([[Jeanne DeWitt Grosser]])

- Grosser built Stripe's early sales team from the ground up as its Chief Product Officer, running it under an "efficiency is leverage" operating principle that kept headcount deliberately lean (e.g., 4 SDRs where most companies would staff 30).
- Circa 2017, she and data scientist [[Ben Salman]] built "Project Rosalind" (named after DNA-mapping scientist Rosalind Franklin) — a database with a row per company on the planet and columns of firmographic attributes (e.g., business model: marketplace vs. vanilla payments) meant to power templated, "Mad Libs"-style personalized outbound emails; the false-positive rate never got good enough to work well at the time, though the same underlying concept works today with modern AI. Salman later went on to ZoomInfo and founded an AI-native GTM startup pursuing a similar idea.
- Segmented its customer base along company size, growth potential (crucial given Stripe's consumption-based pricing — a 200%-year-over-year grower merited more attention than an 8% grower), and business model/workload type (B2B vs. B2C vs. platform vs. marketplace) — each combination implying different Stripe products to sell (e.g., a B2B platform needed Stripe Connect and Stripe Billing; a B2C business needed consumer payments like Apple Pay).
- Reframed the first sales call from a standard discovery Q&A into a hands-on payments-architecture whiteboarding session, where prospects drew their own technical stack — leaving them with a useful artifact and a sense of genuine collaboration, while Stripe learned deeply about the prospect's existing setup and competitive stack. See [[GTM As Product]].
- Launched Stripe Billing with a premium pricing strategy by default, then killed the associated free trial after realizing integration friction meant customers would stick around regardless — an example Grosser cites of avoiding a "premium by default" pricing mistake.
- Grosser was at Stripe for nine years; she recounts customers who didn't buy during one cycle often returning several years later during a subsequent buying cycle.
- Praised by former Stripe colleagues [[Claire Hughes Johnson]] and [[Kate Jensen]] for building a sales org with unusually deep product/engineering fluency — her litmus test: it should take 10 engineers only 10 minutes to realize an account executive isn't a product manager.

### Per episode 43 ([[Rachel Lockett]])

- [[Rachel Lockett]] was an HR leader at Stripe; under CEO Patrick Collison's push to retain top talent, she built a coaching/retention program for the company's top ~50 tech-side executives, using engagement scores and coaching circles, and found even highly capable, committed leaders were often deeply exhausted.
- Stripe's talent bar, per Lockett: "would you enthusiastically rehire this person for the same role?" — a binary, clarifying question used in place of vaguer performance judgment calls. See [[Enthusiastic Rehire Test]].
