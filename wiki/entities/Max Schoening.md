## Definition

Max Schoening is Head of Product at [[Notion]]. He was previously a product manager at Google, ran the design team at [[Heroku]], and was a design leader and engineer at [[GitHub]] (under [[Nat Friedman]]). He is a two-time startup founder and is described by [[Lenny Rachitsky]] as one of the most successful AI-forward product leaders and one of the deepest thinkers on how AI reshapes how software is built and used.

## Key Information

- Credited internally at Notion with pushing designers and PMs to start writing and shipping code, originating from a small "playground" codebase he and another designer built (inspired by Bret Victor's "Stop Drawing Dead Fish" talk) to prototype chat UIs in code instead of static Figma mockups.
- Explicitly does not care whether designers/PMs ship production code — he values coding because it forces people to "interrogate the material" (e.g., understanding agent loops) rather than because of shipping utility. See [[Agency]] and [[Role Collapse]].
- Central thesis: **agency**, not technical skill or job title, is what separates people who thrive from people who stall as AI collapses the skill floor. See [[Agency]].
- Champions **[[Malleable Software]]**: software should adapt to users' interests rather than a vendor's fixed design; cites his ongoing collaboration with [[Jeffrey Litt]] and [[Ink & Switch]] on this problem.
- Skeptical of the "SaaS apocalypse" narrative (see [[SaaS Apocalypse]]) — argues most people want the maintenance/specialization SaaS provides, not the burden of building/maintaining their own tools.
- Articulates a "tiny core" theory of great products — one exceptional, narrow capability beats feature accumulation. See [[Tiny Core]].
- Uses internal Notion principles "[[Obviously Good]]," "[[Incremental Correctness]]," and "[[Shots On Goal]]" to describe how the company balances shipping speed against quality.
- Describes taste as trainable like a model: repeated cycles of making something, predicting audience reaction, and getting feedback — see [[Taste]].
- Founded a document-collaboration startup in 2014 that lost a term sheet from True Ventures the same week Notion pivoted into the same space; spent significant time polishing an editing experience (including markdown folding, later reinvented in Obsidian) while Notion's much rougher early editor won anyway — cited as his biggest professional "fail," a lesson that working diligently on the wrong thing (editing polish vs. the tiny core) doesn't pay off.
- Notion's token spend policy under his purview is currently unlimited/unmonitored by design, on the view that during a new capability's early period it's more valuable to let people explore than to optimize cost — while expecting ROI conversations in 6–12 months. Related to [[Token Budgeting]].
- Book recommendations: *Code* by [[Charles Petzold]] (how computers actually work), *Tools for Conviviality* by [[Ivan Illich]] (technology that empowers vs. technology that is "industrial"/de-humanizing), and *Seeing Like a State* (on the false legibility organizations create for themselves).
- Recommends the [[Ghostty]] terminal emulator, built by [[Mitchell Hashimoto]].
- Personal motto: "The universe is change and life is what you make it" (attributed to Marcus Aurelius).
- Closing hot take: "We already have universal basic income. It's called knowledge work" — argues most knowledge work isn't strictly economically necessary for subsistence, and humans will keep inventing reasons to stay "in the loop" alongside AI agents.
