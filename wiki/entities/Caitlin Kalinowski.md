## Definition

Hardware and robotics leader; worked on the unibody [[MacBook Pro]] and was technical lead on the [[MacBook Air]] and Mac Pro at [[Apple]] (2007-2012), led the VR hardware team at [[Meta]] (Rift, Quest 2) and then the AR glasses hardware team (including [[Orion]]), and most recently helped build [[OpenAI]]'s robotics and hardware division from scratch before departing. Guest on episode 09 of [[Lenny's Podcast]], "Why the next AI boom is physical AI."

## Key Information

- At Apple (2007-2012): thermal lead on the first MacBook Pro, then led successive iterations of the MacBook Air (including the wedge-shaped mass-production version) and the cylindrical Mac Pro. Credits Apple's culture of examining "why we're building it this way" (e.g., finishing the back of a cabinet even though no one sees it) for training a generation of hardware leaders now spread across the industry.
- At Meta: ran the VR hardware team (Rift, Quest — including reducing Quest 2's price/cost to make it the best-selling VR headset of all time, which required removing cameras, components, and changing materials), then led the AR glasses hardware team, including Orion, Meta's most advanced AR product.
- At OpenAI: helped build the robotics and hardware division from scratch and attracted top robotics talent. Left after publicly tweeting disagreement with the speed, governance, and lack of defined guardrails around OpenAI's announcement of a Department of War deal — she says she still respects the people and the robotics team, and hoped her decision would make it easier for others to voice their own boundaries.
- Core thesis of the episode ([[Physical AI]]): once AI's ability to do purely digital/knowledge work saturates, the next frontier is the physical world — robotics, manufacturing, industrialization, and eventually space.
- Views humanoid robots ([[Humanoid Robots]]) as still advanced prototypes; safety (mass, compliance of impact) is a primary design constraint, and "at scale" (hundreds of thousands to millions of units) is far off, gated mainly by supply chain (magnets, [[Actuators]], silicon, RAM) rather than by AI capability.
- Advocates for U.S. re-industrialization and independent supply chains (magnets, actuators, silicon) for both economic and military-security reasons; argues investment should shift toward drones over legacy platforms like aircraft carriers, citing Ukraine as the model of rapid, iterated drone warfare.
- Has advised startups to pre-buy memory (RAM/DRAM) to hedge against an AI-datacenter-driven memory price shock hitting consumer hardware and robotics.
- Believes current AI (LLMs, video models) cannot yet do real [[CAD]] (dense, solid engineering geometry understanding friction/weight/contact); wants a "Codex for hardware engineering," and thinks proprietary CAD data (a major moat for manufacturers) is the biggest bottleneck to building one.
- Learned how to design robots that feel "human" and non-threatening (soft appearance, telegraphing intent, acknowledging people entering a room) from HRI researcher [[Leila Takayama]]; compares this design discipline to what [[Pixar]] and [[Disney]] do with character animation.
- Hardware-building principles she teaches: set goals/KPIs early and don't move them; design and prototype the hardest/riskiest part first; over-invest iteration in the parts users touch most; use off-the-shelf parts when prototyping, go custom for mass production when needed; "you never have more time than you think you do."
- Shares a hardware failure story from Quest 2 development: a camera reduction (5 to 4, for cost) caused a spec-interpretation mismatch between mechanical and computer-vision teams, discovered at EVT (Engineering Validation Test) and fixed via a bracket redesign without slipping the ship date.
- On hiring for zero-to-one hardware/robotics teams: favors generalists who can transfer skills across fields, blends senior builders/scalers with "AI-native" engineers in their early 20s, and prioritizes mission alignment plus a gut-level sense of motivation and drive.
- Reflects on lessons from working with [[Steve Jobs]] (unwavering excellence bar), [[Mark Zuckerberg]] and [[Andrew Bosworth]] (fast, well-delegated technical decision-making), and [[Sam Altman]] (push to think in orders of magnitude bigger — "why not 100x?").
- Personal notes: hired a PhD tutor to study the Western canon (Greek tragedies, Herodotus) via poet Joseph Brodsky's reading list; enjoys the clothing brand [[Vollebak]]; owns a [[Matic]] robot vacuum.
