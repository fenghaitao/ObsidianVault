## Definition
John Backus was the designer of Fortran who, in his Turing Award lecture, argued programming should be liberated from the von Neumann style in favor of functional programming.

## Key Information
- Designer of Fortran, "a quintessentially imperative language."
- His Turing Award lecture (1977/1978), "Can programming be liberated from the von Neumann style?", proposed functional programming and introduced an idiosyncratic language called FP.
- Also suggested designing new hardware to execute functional languages — a thread that led to machines like Symbolics' Lisp machine and the SKI machine.
