## Definition

Head of engineering for [[OpenAI]]'s API and developer platform; previously built home-pricing models at [[OpenDoor]]. Gives a broad view of AI's near-term trajectory given that most AI startups build on OpenAI's APIs.

## Key Information

- At OpenAI: 95% of engineers use [[Codex]] daily; 100% of PRs are reviewed by Codex; engineers using Codex heavily open ~70% more PRs than those who don't (a widening gap). He personally still writes some code but says using AI tools is now easier than manual coding even for a manager.
- Uses *Structure and Interpretation of Computer Programs* ("the wizard book") and *Fantasia*'s "Sorcerer's Apprentice" as his central metaphor for AI-native engineering — see [[Wizard Book Analogy]].
- Describes engineers increasingly acting as managers of fleets of Codex agents (10-20 parallel threads), and — citing *The Mythical Man-Month*'s "surgical team" prediction — models his own management philosophy on making each top performer feel like an empowered "surgeon," with the manager as support staff clearing organizational blockers. See [[Surgical Team Model]].
- Describes an internal OpenAI team experiment maintaining a 100% Codex-written codebase with no manual "escape hatch," surfacing that most agent failures trace back to insufficient context/documentation rather than model capability — pushing them to encode more "tribal knowledge" into repos via docs, code structure, and skills/AGENTS.md files.
- Names the "one-person billion-dollar startup" as one of the most under-analyzed second/third-order effects of AI leverage — see [[One Person Billion Dollar Startup]].
- Argues AI's biggest underrated opportunity is [[Business Process Automation Opportunity|business process automation]] (repeatable, high-determinism enterprise operations) rather than open-ended software engineering, since most of the world's work outside Silicon Valley looks nothing like coding.
- Observes many companies get negative ROI from AI deployments when adoption is purely top-down (executive mandate) without genuine bottom-up champions; recommends companies staff a dedicated internal "tiger team" to build excitement and best practices organically. See [[Bottom-Up AI Adoption]].
- Restates Boris Cherny's "build for where the models are going, not where they are today" almost verbatim as his own standing advice, plus a companion quote from a FinTool founder, Nicholas: "the models will eat your scaffolding for breakfast" (citing the rapid obsolescence of vector-store-era tooling). See [[Build For The Model 6 Months Out]].
- Describes OpenAI's deliberate platform-neutrality stance (every model shipped in a first-party product also ships in the API; competitors are not blocked) as a direct expression of its mission to spread AGI's benefits to all of humanity, since OpenAI alone can't reach everyone. See [[Platform Neutrality Mission]].
- Predicts (12-18 months out): models coherently completing multi-hour (eventually possibly full-day) tasks, per the METR benchmark trend; and a major leap in native audio/speech-to-speech multimodality, which he considers underrated relative to coding given how much of the world's work happens via voice.
- Personal/lightning-round notes: recommends *There Is No Antimemetics Division* (qntm), *Breakneck* (Dan Wang), and *Apple in China* (Patrick McGee); enjoys anime (*Jujutsu Kaisen* season 3); loves Ubiquiti home networking gear; life motto "never feel sorry for yourself"; from his Opendoor days, cites proximity to high-voltage power lines, floor-plan quality, and curb appeal/front doors as underrated home-price variables.
