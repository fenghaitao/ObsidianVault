## Definition
James Cowling is a distributed-systems engineer who was Dropbox's most senior engineer and is now co-founder and CTO of Convex. His career spans transactional/consensus research, Dropbox's Magic Pocket storage system, and building a higher-level backend abstraction platform.
## Key Information
- PhD at MIT under Barbara Liskov; master's thesis on Byzantine fault tolerance and a well-known paper, Viewstamped Replication Revisited.
- PhD thesis was the Granola algorithm for distributed transaction coordination using one-shot / independent transactions; pre-dated and was cited by Google's Spanner paper.
- Joined Dropbox in 2012; became the most senior engineer there, leading the Magic Pocket storage system and the migration off S3.
- Has two parallel career threads: abstractions (how to design simple models/APIs for complex problems) and large-scale transactional systems.
- Started Convex with co-founder Jamie Turner after eight years at Dropbox, leaving amicably.
- Believes the best engineering comes from a deep understanding of why, and that simple systems are harder to design than complex ones.
- Self-describes as a "strategic, collaboration, simplicity, abstraction engineer" whose raw coding ability plateaued around level four while his scope and perspective grew.
- On the AI era: argues senior engineers remain in high demand because architecture, simplicity, and design are still human domains; stresses that "coding and engineering are very different things."
- After Magic Pocket shipped, the small team dispersed: Jamie Turner went on to redesign the sync protocol and desktop client, while Cowling moved to the file system and distributed databases.
- Argues hard technical problems are a recruiting asset — they attract engineers who enjoy them, who then "cycle off" to other parts of the system once solved.
- On owning infra: building such a system is "like having a baby… you've got to raise the baby" — you own it and must keep investing after launch, not declare victory.
