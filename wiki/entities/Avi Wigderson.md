## Definition

Avi Wigderson is a mathematician and theoretical computer scientist who won both the Turing Award (2023) and the Abel Prize (2021), and is interviewed here about complexity theory, randomness, zero-knowledge proofs, and quantum computation.

## Key Information

- Introduced in the episode as a "Turing award and Abel Prize winner" — uniquely honored across both computer science and mathematics.
- Interviewed by Ryan L. Peterman about his field.
- Holds the intuition — "probably of almost all members of the theoretical computer science community" — that P ≠ NP.
- Co-developed the Nisan–Wigderson pseudorandom generator with Noam Nisan.
- Co-authored (with Oded Goldreich and Silvio Micali) the GMW result that every provable statement has a zero-knowledge proof.
- Describes theoretical computer science as "lucky to have two parents," living in both mathematics (theorems/proofs) and computer science (motivated by computation).
- Says the field's real work is "modeling things," more than solving problems — creating definitions and models (like his notion of randomness) that give rise to surprising theorems.
- Predicted zero-knowledge proofs would never be implemented because the protocol is so costly — and says "I was wrong," given how important ZK became in the real world.
- On motivations: he works because he enjoys the activity of thinking and learning from failures; most days he "fails to do what [he] wanted to do."
- Advice to researchers: "work on things they enjoy the most" and experiment broadly early on; "what you like most is what you are better at."
- Ryan Williams recommends "Avi's book" as a nice introduction to complexity theory (among his recommended readings for learning the field).
