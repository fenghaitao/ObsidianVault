## Definition

Managing Director at [[Khosla Ventures]]; former EVP Business Development and Policy at [[PayPal]] (part of the "[[PayPal|PayPal Mafia]]"), COO of [[Square]], VP of Corporate Development at [[LinkedIn]], and co-founder of two companies. Early investor in [[Stripe]], [[Palantir]], [[Airbnb]], [[YouTube]], [[DoorDash]], [[Ramp]], and dozens of others. A repeat [[Lenny's Podcast]] guest known for blunt, contrarian operating and investing advice.

## Key Information

- Has not used a computer since September 2010, when he began running his work entirely from an iPad (inspired by [[Jack Dorsey]] running Square from an iPad); does everything from phone, watch, or iPad.
- Central talent philosophy, learned from [[Vinod Khosla]] and reinforced by his PayPal experience with [[Peter Thiel]] and [[Max Levchin]]: "the team you build is the company you build." Talent density, not market/product/technology, is what makes or breaks a startup.
- Improved his own hiring after critical feedback from [[David Sacks]] (that he wasn't demonstrating hiring "leverage"); learned to identify talent he already knew well (poaching internally) before developing techniques for evaluating strangers — including "ruthless referencing" (e.g., 20 references per senior hire) and asking candidates "if you were CEO of your current company, what would you do differently?"
- Popularized the "[[Barrels And Ammunition]]" framework: only a small number of people in any organization ("barrels") can independently drive an initiative from inception to success; hiring more people without adding barrels just increases coordination tax without adding throughput.
- Advocates building companies around "[[Undiscovered Talent]]" rather than competing for already-proven hires, since startups can't win a bidding war and undiscovered people (often younger, with less "data" attached to them) offer disproportionate value.
- Contrarian take: hates talking to customers for consumer/SMB products (though not enterprise, where a real decision-maker exists) — believes customer feedback is frequently subconscious and misleading, citing Airbnb and DoorDash as businesses built on founder insight/observation, not customer surveys.
- Practices "[[Criticize In Public]]": gives negative feedback to team members in front of colleagues rather than privately, believing it builds collective awareness and collaboration rather than shame; explicitly rejects psychological safety as a management value ("high-performance machines don't have psychological safety, they're about winning").
- Views the CEO's core job as "offsetting complacency" — pushing harder specifically when a company is succeeding (since success breeds complacency and talented people get demotivated when "skating"), and shifting to a supportive, non-critical coaching mode when a company is struggling.
- On the future of PM/design/engineering: believes traditional PM roles (multi-quarter roadmaps built from customer inputs) "make no sense" going forward; the durable skill across all three functions converges on CEO-like judgment — "what are we building and why" — with business acumen as the differentiator. Believes design and code are "merging" as disciplines. His response to [[Marc Andreessen]]'s "[[Mexican Standoff (PM Design Engineer)]]" framing.
- Investing philosophy: purely founder-driven ("does this founder have a non-zero chance of changing an industry or the world?"); looks for founders who can articulate a path to durable "[[Accumulating Advantages]]," even if unproven; treats being laughed at by fellow VCs as a positive signal (the "[[Ugly Baby]]" heuristic).
- Identifies early-stage company "tempo"/speed as his strongest predictive signal of eventual success (citing Square, Fair, and Ramp as examples of unusually fast problem-to-shipped-solution cycles); also favors companies that grow senior leadership internally (promote-from-within) over hiring "value preservation" executives externally.
- Personal/lightning-round notes: recommends *The Upside of Stress* by Kelly McGonigal and the *Nuremberg* trial dramatization; loves Eight Sleep (also an investor); life motto "no days off" (missed workouts only 7 days in 7 years); former litigator for 4.5 years before entering tech.
