## Definition

Tyler Cowen is an economist whose view — that society is largely not capped by (raw) intelligence — [[Max Schoening]] paraphrases in support of his skepticism that ever-smarter frontier AI models matter much for most knowledge work.

## Key Information

- Schoening: "I think society is largely not capped by intelligence. I think Tyler Cowan [sic] says something similar. I don't want to put words in his mouth." Used to support Schoening's "retina display" analogy for model intelligence — past a certain capability threshold, more raw intelligence stops being the binding constraint. See [[Automation Vs Augmentation]].
- Also referenced (in the Fiona Fung episode, per the existing [[High Agency High Accountability]] wiki page) in connection with the observation that people thriving in the AI-tooling era are distinguished by initiative/agency rather than waiting to be assigned work.

- Per [[Lenny Rachitsky]] (episode 15, [[Keith Rabois]] conversation): cited for a public statement arguing that now is the time to work harder, since AI is eating away at the value of not staying current — raised in the context of widespread career anxiety and long hours among "AI-pilled" workers.
- Per [[Lenny Rachitsky]] (episode 32, [[Sam Lesson]] etiquette episode): referenced as the host of a podcast episode featuring [[Alison Gopnik]], discussing how children learn practical skills (like fork use) through experimentation rather than instruction — raised during a dining-etiquette tangent about Lesson's own young children's table manners.
