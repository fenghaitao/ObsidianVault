## Summary

Online platform for coding interview preparation, providing a battery of test cases and automated evaluation. Referenced as a contrast to the old-fashioned way of interview preparation—when Steve Huynh was preparing for his SDE transition, no such platforms existed and he had to manually collect interview questions and generate his own test cases.

## Key Details

- Provides automated test case evaluation for coding problems
- Did not exist when Steve Huynh was preparing for his SDE interview at Amazon in the mid-2000s
- Part of the "cottage industry" of interview prep that has emerged around big tech hiring
- Used by candidates to prepare for coding interviews at FAANG companies

### David Ronca on LeetCode

- Ronca: "I personally don't love lead code" — it shows how fast someone writes code for a problem they've likely practiced, not real engineering ability.
- Concedes large companies like Meta and Google have "no choice" but to use a structured bar like LeetCode at their hiring volume.
- Counterexample: engineers who "nail" LeetCode and canned system-design questions but then do "terrible" engineering work and make poor engineering decisions.

### Ryan Williams on the three-sum question

- The episode opens with the host calling three-sum "the most popular LeetCode question" and asking Ryan Williams about it, using LeetCode-style algorithmic interviewing as the entry point to his research.
- Williams walks through the O(n³) brute force and the classic O(n²) two-pointer solution before revealing that three-sum can be solved even faster than n².
