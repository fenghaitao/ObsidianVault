## Core Summary

Ryan L. Peterman interviews Carey Nachenberg, a cybersecurity engineer who rose to Fellow (Symantec's senior-most technical rank) before moving to Google X, Lyft's self-driving team, and a UCLA lecturing role. Carey's career was built on self-directed "project taste" — picking problems with big business impact where the company had gaps — and on an ongoing fight with imposter syndrome that twice kept him in comfortable places. His core lesson: intelligence is overrated; communication, collaboration, and focusing on the company's outcomes matter far more.

## Key Points

- Started as Symantec's (Peter Norton Group) first intern in 1992 and left in 2016 as the company's senior-most engineer (Fellow, roughly four levels above staff)
- Was never assigned work — he picked impactful projects by looking for gaps the company needed and no one else was stepping up to fill
- "Project taste" means finding work with big business impact that advances the company's or division's outcomes, not just your own
- Recruited into Google X by a former Symantec COO; joined as Principal Engineer after being down-leveled from a Symantec "level 10 / VP" because Google won't hire unseen fellows
- Built Chronicle's Backstory — a Google-scale cybersecurity log index ingesting petabytes and turning 30-minute queries into ~2-second pivots
- At Lyft, designed an end-to-end neural-network driving architecture wrapped in a safety "guard rails" layer that overrides unsafe network decisions; hand-coded robotics heuristics were "a dead end"
- His Lyft project credit was partly taken by others — he learned to "toot your own horn," claim only the specific parts you did, and record metrics while they're fresh
- Imposter syndrome kept him at Symantec out of fear he couldn't make it elsewhere; an external invitation to interview is what finally broke the inertia
- At X he saw a 200+ IQ engineer stuck at L4 because of poor communication and low business impact — intelligence is not destiny
- Teaching philosophy: lecture to the 30th–50th percentile (modeled on himself), use empathy, humor, and color; he now allows LLMs for learning but not projects after watching autocomplete hinder understanding
