## Core Summary

Ryan L. Peterman interviews Jake Bolam, a Principal Engineer (IC8) at Instagram, about his path from IC6 to IC8 and the "leading through others" archetype. Jake traces his career from a slow remote onboarding at Facebook Groups, through a deliberate move to Instagram web, to leading a multi-year backend migration to Meta's main stack that drove his two promotions. He lays out a pragmatic philosophy: do whatever it takes, lean into your strengths, shield your time, and modulate code review depth by risk rather than reviewing everything uniformly.

## Key Points

- Jake joined Meta as an IC6 on Facebook Groups, had a slow remote onboarding but a supportive manager helped him get a "meets all" rating during PSC
- He kept being pulled from front-end product into front-end and then backend infrastructure at every company; he eventually embraced impact over stack (Instagram web had <100 engineers vs 2-3k on backend)
- IC6 = tech lead of one team (~10-12 people); IC7 = leading multiple teams; IC8 = an even larger blast radius, scaling with the importance of the problem
- His IC7 promotion came from a 2-year infra migration delivered mostly in ~6 months by a motivated team, which freed resources to ship ads on Instagram web in 3 months
- His IC8 promotion came from a project most of the org thought was impossible/stupid: migrating Instagram's backend to the main Meta stack, framed in year one as "fix the devx between the stacks"
- He uses "server champions" (a cross-org group of ~40 product-team leads) as his most important feedback channel
- To protect work-life balance at IC8 he shields ~50% of his week as focus blocks, clears Wednesday/Friday of meetings, and batches 1:1s on Monday afternoon
- His code review philosophy: review depth scales with risk — thorough on core/prod, light and trust-based on gated leaf components; he prefers "how do I accept this diff" over "how do I reject it"
- He intentionally spends 20-30% of his time on work he'll never get PSC credit for (helping people, org contributions)
- Career advice: "go where you're valued" — find a team that likes your strengths rather than staying where your strengths are ignored
