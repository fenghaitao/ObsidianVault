## Core Summary

Former Stripe CTO David Singleton traces his path from Symbian (pre-smartphone OS) through ~12 years at Google — rising from junior engineer to VP and running Android Wear — to Stripe's CTO, and finally to co-founding Dev Agents. The conversation centers on management as its own craft: hiring without whiteboard interviews, staying connected to real engineering work even as a leader, and designing organizational processes deliberately.

## Key Points

- Started coding as a kid building an invoicing system for his parents' business, which showed him software's real-world impact
- At Symbian he worked as a technical consultant embedded with handset makers — an early forward-deployed-engineer role that taught him how team culture shapes outcomes (Samsung's duplicated-mission races vs Nokia's detailed planning)
- Joined Google post-IPO expecting a short stay but stayed ~12 years by re-learning every 18-24 months; did "six different jobs," including mobile, ads (to learn how Google made money), and Android
- As a young IC, removed a mobile redirect on google.com wasting ~2.5s per user, saving on the order of "70 years of user time every year" — an early lesson in agency and impact
- Was pitched the Tech Lead Manager role instead of product management; he kept one foot in code (built the first Google Translate iPhone app on 20% time) and calls the IC-plus-manager split workable only if deliberately structured
- Leadership practice "engineeration" (engineer + vacation): about once a quarter, take a few days to join a team and complete a small real project, then write a detailed friction log
- Managerial lessons: "teams that are perfectly happy are rarely performing at their very best," the "balcony" habit of self-observation, and seeking "type two fun" (hard now, rewarding in hindsight)
- Ran Google London learning that far-away sites need complete missions and autonomy; a site lead's best work may be building relationships at HQ so the local team can run autonomously
- At Stripe he helped push reliability past five nines by introducing a systematic ops review, and replaced whiteboard interviews with realistic pair-programming — arguing big companies could do the same if they cared
- Values Stripe's "users first" operating principle and "meticulous in your craft"; culture is "the behaviors you accept," so leaders must model it and call out drift
- Left Google when he realized a year was identical to the year before ("unfinished business" done, no more learning), then co-founded Dev Agents to build a "next-generation operating system" for consumer AI agents
- First-principles thinking and condensed, peer-reviewed writing are his highest-leverage communication habits
