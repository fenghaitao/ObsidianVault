## Core Summary

In a 25,000-subscriber special, Ryan L. Peterman's friend Brandon interviews him about the podcast itself. Ryan started the show to practice speaking — a skill he calls high-leverage — with a built-in commitment to polish every word, and found product-market fit telling the "career stories" of hidden senior engineers. He also shares how he sources guests and how he balances the workload with a full-time job.

## Key Points

- Started podcasting to practice speaking, which he views as high-leverage for career and relationships; Toastmasters was good but too infrequent and lacked a forcing function
- Posting publicly is a commitment mechanism: knowing the audio will be published forces him to pour over every word, creating a feedback loop Toastmasters never gave him
- The inspiration came from Facebook's internal "career stories" written by senior ICs; the show brings on super-senior-but-unknown engineers to tell those stories
- The Philip Su episode's breakout ("the graphs all going vertical") showed there was product-market fit for these stories — it wasn't just a personal interest
- Since he is not yet a recognized brand, he builds trailers from the snappiest hooks to win over new YouTube viewers, while considering lighter intros for podcast apps where listeners already know him
- Sourcing hidden senior ICs: LinkedIn and Google don't surface them, so he asks the audience — which has surfaced guests from Apple, Google, and Microsoft
- The hardest podcasting-specific skill: suppressing verbal "uh-huh" acknowledgments while the guest is speaking
- Work-life balance is genuinely hard: he wrote his newsletter until roughly 4 a.m. on Thursdays for two years and was warned not to burn the candle at both ends
