## Core Summary

Ryan L. Peterman curates the best answers to his favorite interview question — "what advice would you give your younger self?" — from a year of interviewing CTOs and distinguished engineers at companies like Anthropic, Google, Meta, and Amazon. The compilation's recurring themes: clarify your values and what you actually want before climbing, avoid the emptiness of achieving an early goal you never truly examined, protect relationships outside work, lean into strengths and environments where you're valued, and trust yourself more.

## Key Points

- Roy Disney's line frames the whole theme — "decisions are easy when your values are clear to you" — so spend time clarifying what you actually want before committing
- "Dog that caught the car": a guest who joined Microsoft wanting to be a dev manager hit level 67 (near E7, his terminal level) young, then had no mental model for what came next and fell into a period of serious depression; warns to be sure you actually want the thing you want (Warren Buffett analogy)
- "Bending the reed vs breaking the reed": a sleeping-bag era in Seattle meant seeing a visiting fiancée only 11 PM–midnight at Denny's; be sure you're comfortable with other things breaking, because extreme focus requires it — and E7 at 30 vs 38 doesn't matter in the big arc
- People pleasing: examine whether you're climbing a ladder you chose or one that was put in front of you; the person you really need to please is yourself
- Trust yourself: early-career anxiety and impostor syndrome gave way to confidence built through projects succeeding and good feedback; several guests wished they'd had more audacity trust their judgment sooner
- Invest in relationships outside work: one guest spent years optimizing career but not "general life progression," and only after moving to LA (out of the tech bubble) found a much more fulfilled life
- "Don't be the best, be the only": rather than out-specializing friends in design or engineering, negotiate across many themes; being broadly competent made the combination unique and valuable
- Public speaking: a guest terrified of public speaking through school was fine pitching his own product (PostRank days) — passion and investment in the subject, not generic knowledge, made the difference
- "Always prefer high growth": Amazon's escalator (from 10,000 people to a million, revenue up ~80x) carried a career upward regardless of individual climbing — "my ladder was always an escalator"
- "Jobs are still with other humans": build relationships and a reputation (e.g., via LinkedIn) so opportunities come to you — Amazon called one guest, not the other way around
- Aim to be the person deciding what's right to build, not an "output machine" — your value is more than what you can type
- "Be more courageous and less embarrassed; you're going to fail a lot and it's fine" (one guest even suggests therapy for self-understanding)
- "Go where you're valued": a front-end specialist on a backend team earns bad ratings despite huge output; find a team that likes your strengths
- "Just use common sense": big companies accumulate inherited nonsense and misaligned incentives; trust yourself and develop your common sense
