## Core Summary

Nimit Sohoni, a Stanford PhD, former Citadel Securities quant, and now AI researcher at the voice-AI startup Cartesia, compares AI research and quantitative finance careers. His central claims: a PhD helps open doors but is not required for either field; the skill a PhD actually builds is research taste — finding the right problems; and quant, despite its reputation, often has better work-life balance than AI research. He also explains state space models versus transformers and why Cartesia bets on voice AI and co-designed model architectures.

## Key Points

- "90% of the battle in research is actually finding the right problems"; a PhD develops problem selection and research taste, not just execution.
- A PhD opens doors to academia, AI research, and quantitative finance, but the role's shape differs; engineering-heavy AI work (training/eval infra, data processing) does not require one.
- To build research taste: keep up with the literature (a curated X/Twitter feed of ML papers), start with small sub-problems and extend prior work, then mature into bigger, novel problems.
- He joined Citadel Securities after interning the summer before graduating with his PhD; he was curious about math-plus-computation careers (ML research, quant finance, quantum computing).
- Quant is unified by strong math (stochastic calculus is the "finance 101" backbone), with roles spanning alpha generation, monetization, risk, and data analysis; hedge fund vs market maker vs front/back office differ greatly.
- Quant work-life balance can exceed AI research because US trading hours cluster when people work; AI's intense competition leads many to work around the clock.
- Quant comp is not standardized like tech levels; it is bonus-weighted and driven by firm/team/strategy performance, exposure to alpha, and seniority — and accordingly more variable/risky.
- Finance is extremely secretive (alpha decays as more people know it); non-competes and garden leave (0 to ~2 years, paid to not work) are standard, and pods are intentionally uncorrelated.
- The top 1% of quants and AI researchers earn outlandishly (some "NBA-player salaries"); it comes down to skill, politics, and being in the right place at the right time.
- Top quant firms cited: Renaissance Technologies ("RenTech," the gold standard), Jane Street, Citadel, Jump Trading, Hudson River, plus elite smaller/secretive firms TGS, XTX, and Radix.
- He left Citadel for Cartesia — a voice-AI company (text-to-speech, speech-to-text, voice agents) — to rejoin AI and take startup risk after establishing stability; the co-founders were his Stanford labmates in Chris Ré's lab (including Albert Gu).
- Cartesia's main competitor is Eleven Labs; Cartesia differentiates on latency, cost, and quality (e.g., voice cloning) and pushes toward end-to-end systems.
- State space models compress context into a fixed-size state (like a brain), unlike transformers whose KV cache grows with sequence length (like a database); hybrid models interleave both, and SSMs suit low-information modalities like audio.
