## Core Summary

Laurent (a staff engineer and manager with experience at Stripe, Airbnb, Instagram, and Meta) reveals the untold rules of performance calibration and how to avoid getting stuck at senior, framing the senior-to-staff jump as a shift to independently finding, pitching, and solving problems. He shares management lessons — the "surprise factor" for reviews, situational leadership, and measuring developer productivity by the journey of a code change rather than lines of code.

## Key Points

- Joined Airbnb in 2016 as one of two test-infrastructure engineers, attracted by the people, location, and early-stage developer-productivity work; he had already specialized in developer productivity at Apple and Meta.
- Pitched his manager to become the team's manager (rather than waiting to be asked), starting with two reports; he learned the "untold rules" of calibration during management training.
- Passed a peer's first-one-on-one manager test by hearing all sides of a story before reacting — a core people-management principle.
- Built a calendar-defrag tool for his reports and ran a team-health initiative via a large Google Form, discovering their long stand-ups were "useful for the other people" but for nobody.
- Returned from management to IC after getting bored once the team largely ran itself; the management "secrets" (career framing, reviews, coaching, difficult conversations) made him a stronger IC and leader.
- "Surprise factor" metric: before a review, manager and report each write their expected rating and reveal simultaneously — a match passes, a mismatch fails; both negative and positive surprises are bad.
- Untold calibration rules are local and evolve as organizations mature (e.g., changing roles <2.5 months before review means an automatic "meets"; new grads can't be promoted before a minimum tenure).
- On disputing calibration rules: gather evidence and make your case, but first understand the story behind the rule — some are justified, some are stale precedents; discuss one-on-one rather than in the room.
- At Stripe he reframed measuring developer output from lines of code/PR count to the "journey of a change" (time and phases from branch to merge), which exposes where and why an org or person is slow.
- Scaled from tech lead of ~35 to ~140 engineers at Stripe by embedding in teams, never dictating or rejecting designs outright, and influencing via questions; scaled further through office hours and accepting he knew less than each domain expert.
- Situational leadership (introduced by Scott Renfro): coaching must match a learner's motivation × mastery quadrant — directing → selling/encouraging → supporting → delegating; a stuck bootcamp mentee unlocked only after he switched from directing to supporting.
- Senior-to-staff is a bigger mindset shift than staff-to-senior-staff: it requires owning the full cycle of identifying, pitching, and solving a problem end-to-end; find problems by logging friction ("channel your inner frustration").
- Recommends Radical Candor (Kim Scott) as the most influential management book he read, and advises trying management via an intern first.
- Airbnb, a designer-led company, hired people with firefighting/disaster-management backgrounds to run its incident handling and measurement — an outside-the-box move Laurent carried to later jobs.
