## Core Summary

David Fowler, a Microsoft Distinguished Engineer who joined as an intern and was promoted 11 times over 17+ years, explains Microsoft's leveling system and the arc of the projects (NuGet, SignalR, .NET Core) that carried him up the ladder. He frames his rise around permissionless agency, communicating with working code, and shifting from being "smart" to having impact.

## Key Points

- Microsoft levels run 59-70 (about two levels per title band); entry is 59/60, and principal spans three levels, so the experience depth inside a band is enormous
- Got the internship by showing a game demo at a career fair rather than résumé prestige; early interviews were puzzle-based (Mount Fuji, Die Hard jugs, counting gas stations)
- "Code is currency": build a working prototype first and show it — "it's not a PowerPoint, it's not a document"
- Built SignalR as a nights/weekends side project after being captivated by Google Docs' co-editing; it became an official product and his first real tech lead
- Was pulled onto .NET Core as one of its architects and had to scale from reviewing every change to trusting and empowering ~30 engineers
- His promotion to principal was the shift from individual output to outcomes and impact-through-others; "the things that get you to senior and maybe principal hurt you growing even further"
- The Distinguished Engineer promotion required a peer-review process and a body of sustained work (deferred impact), not one big year
- Felt max imposter syndrome discovering Guido van Rossum (Python's creator) is the same level; mentors reframed his goal away from "fix everything" toward amplifying a strength until he was superhuman at it
- Mentors told him to go ask the people who decide promotions what "the next level" means, rather than accept generic feedback
- Blocks eight hours a week for IC work even as a DE, and still codes daily by choice
- Observed the Satya-era culture shift: from competing duplicate teams and assertive interruption to collaboration, "raise your hand," and pulling in quiet voices
- Advice: before changing roles ask when the last reorg was; and at least once work on a project long enough to see the long-term impact of your decisions
