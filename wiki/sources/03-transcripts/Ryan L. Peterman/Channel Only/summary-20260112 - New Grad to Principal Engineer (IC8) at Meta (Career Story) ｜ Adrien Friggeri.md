## Core Summary

Adrien Friggeri tells his career story from new-grad PhD hire to Principal Engineer (IC8) at Meta, arguing that growth comes from spotting real problems, building useful tooling bottom-up, and aligning with managers and champions before promotion decisions. His path runs through Facebook's early data-science team, bringing A/B testing to Instagram, building the Bento notebook platform, a failed startup attempt, Clubhouse, and Meta's smart-glasses/hardware group, where he earned IC8 by carving out scope with overcommunication and pre-built consensus.

## Key Points

- Joined Facebook's pre-allocated "data science" team (early 2011-12), a ~20-person group of statisticians and early-ML researchers who built the A/B testing and data-measurement infrastructure rather than doing product analytics; his first project studied how rumors spread on social networks.
- First manager Cameron Marlow's advice: "software engineers have power at this company... you are a software engineer" — don't pigeonhole yourself into a narrower category.
- The rumor work found false rumors spread faster than true ones, and pointing out that a rumor was false did little to limit its spread.
- Brought A/B testing to Instagram as a naive IC4 with no mandate, hacking a Facebook API endpoint to log exposures from AWS and replicating the bucket-hashing logic; the system underpinned his IC5 promotion.
- Regards not officially joining Instagram (remaining on the data-science team as a consultant) as one of his biggest career mistakes.
- Built Bento, Meta's Jupyter-based notebook platform, starting from a personal library that collapsed data-pipeline boilerplate from ~300 to ~20 lines, then seeding adoption through new-hire boot camp/data camp and supporting legacy systems to build trust.
- Transitioned to a Tech Lead Manager role (TLM2, equivalent to senior manager/staff) around Bento, largely on his IC work and vision.
- On taking risk: align expectations with your manager and skip ahead of time, overcommunicate, and be ready to hear "no."
- Left Meta to attempt a startup, found he was not an entrepreneur (he preferred going deep technically to delegating the interesting parts), and effectively took a sabbatical instead.
- Boomeranged to Meta twice (drawn by managers James Pierce and then Vincent Hardy), and joined Clubhouse early to build its data stack, A/B testing, and notification-filtering ML.
- Back at Meta, pivoted from "the data guy" to product/hardware, leading all user "experiences" on an unreleased smart-glasses line; he did the work for about a year before asking for the retroactive "TL of experiences" label.
- Earned IC8 by pairing strong underlying impact with consensus built ahead of calibration — socializing the promotion over two cycles and enlisting a heavyweight D2 champion through monthly check-ins.
- Frames scope-taking as removing work off a colleague's plate with their consent, not "stealing scope"; overcommunication is his constant theme.
- Advises maximizing "luck surface area": be a good person, build trust, don't be afraid to ask (or to hear no), and take more shots.
