## Core Summary

Zach Wilson shares his career journey from being hired as an IC3 data engineer at Facebook to reaching Staff Engineer at Airbnb by age 26, with two of his three promotions coming from strategic job hopping rather than internal promotion. He emphasizes that interviewing is the highest-leverage, most important part of a career — more important than on-the-job impact — and that building a compelling "impact story" is essential for successfully negotiating higher levels. Zach also discusses the critical role of manager relationships, internal brand, self-advocacy, and the trade-offs between accelerated career growth and personal well-being.

## Key Points

- Interviewing is the most important career skill — it has more leverage than on-the-job impact because new companies don't see hours worked or negative data, only the impact story
- Zach was promoted from IC3 to IC4 at Facebook in just 5 months because the company recognized he was mis-leveled at hire, demonstrating that companies will correct leveling mistakes quickly
- Working more hours does not necessarily lead to promotion — Facebook told Zach he was greatly exceeding expectations as an IC4 but denied him E5 because they questioned whether his performance was sustainable
- Zach's transition to Netflix came through his former manager Jender, who hired him as a Senior Data Engineer based on their prior working relationship at Facebook — proving internal brand matters across companies
- Zach leveraged competing offers (Google, Facebook, Airbnb) to negotiate a Staff Engineer title at Airbnb when the job description required 10+ years and he had only 6
- At Netflix, Zach was thrust into a staff-level role he wasn't ready for (interfacing with 11 teams), leading to burnout and a mental health break — illustrating why companies resist promoting people with impact but without the behaviors
- The key behaviors that shift from senior to staff: selling a technical vision, deciding which problems to solve (not just solving them), working through others, and establishing better work boundaries
- At Airbnb, Zach delivered a major pipeline migration in one quarter that was planned for four quarters, quickly building internal brand as someone who can handle risky, complex problems
- Zach's advice to his younger self: develop soft skills earlier in lower-risk situations, stop being one-dimensional, prioritize health and fitness, and don't eat dinner at work (it's a company ROI strategy to keep you working longer)
- The "learning or earning" framework: if you're not getting at least one of these, leave — Zach prioritized learning over earning even at the staff level
