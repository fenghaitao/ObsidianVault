## Core Summary

Ryan Olson recounts rising from a failed Facebook interview to principal engineer (IC8) at Instagram in just a few years, and the craft and career lessons behind building Instagram Stories, the White Out redesign, IGTV, and IG Labs. He argues for small teams ("if you want to go fast, go small"), pairing with great designers, keeping senior engineers in code, and ultimately left to found Retro, a friends-first, ad-free social app.

## Key Points

- He failed his 2011 Facebook interview from nerves (on a question that was later banned), and it took him a while to bounce back; he later used beta blockers to manage interview anxiety and landed the full-time role.
- He interned at Flipboard (~50 people, ~4 interns) after an investor friend (who founded Insight Partners) connected him to co-founder Evan Doll; there he built and open-sourced FLEX, the iOS debugging tool later used at Facebook and Instagram.
- The Flipboard intern class included Dylan Field (later Figma) and Devin Finzer (later OpenSea) — both were determined to start companies.
- At Instagram (~10 iOS engineers after an internal faction war over AsyncDisplayKit vs vanilla iOS), early wins included a 20% cold-start improvement and an 80% crash-rate cut (which won the company's physical "axe" award).
- He led the 2016 White Out redesign with a designer: flat icon, white chrome, semantic colors, built behind a feature flag and merged incrementally; Mike Krieger said to skip A/B testing and ship, but Facebook leadership forced A/B tests the night before launch.
- He grew IC4 → IC5 (White Out) → IC6 (Stories) → IC7 (IGTV) → IC8 (IG Labs / tech lead director) — roughly one promotion per half at the start; he felt underleveled at hire and explicitly asked to jump IC4 → IC6.
- For Instagram Stories he cut the team down to a core of himself plus one other iOS engineer (with borrowed help), built it in 2–3 months at a 16–18 hour/day pace, and shipped touches like hold-to-pause; it stopped the flow of everyday sharing to Snapchat.
- Stories won partly because Instagram already had people's friend graph, and it added UX touches Snapchat lacked.
- IGTV — long-form vertical video pitched by Kevin Systrom and designer Ian Spalter — largely failed (long-form + vertical mismatch, little inventory) but informed Reels; he holds a patent on AI reformatting of landscape video.
- He co-founded IG Labs with a designer as a small, high-talent-density "Delta Force" team for new initiatives (e.g., collaborative posts); its talent later seeded Threads.
- He tried the tech lead director (TLD) role; he believes senior ICs and managers should keep coding ("skin in the game"), disagreeing with Facebook's strict IC/management separation.
- He left with Stories PM Nathan Sharp to build a product studio; Retro is a friends-only, ad-free, subscription photo journal (mutual friending, 250-friend cap, no algorithmic feed) that became popular in Taiwan.
- Career reflections: at a startup, satisfaction and learning are up while compensation is down; large teams carry hidden coordination/compile-time costs; for new grads, master the tools of your time (now AI tools) and build things for people.
