## Core Summary

A panel discussion hosted by Ryan L. Peterman featuring staff-level engineers (Zach Wilson from Netflix/Airbnb, Lee McKeeman from Amazon/Meta/Google, Carly from Activision, and Rahul from Meta/Pinterest/Taro) sharing their experiences and strategies for reaching the Staff Engineer (IC6) level. The panelists discuss the projects that got them promoted, how to balance delegation versus hands-on coding, the critical importance of relationship building and visibility, and heuristics for optimizing promotion chances. The central message is that staff engineering is about influence and multiplicative impact across multiple teams, not just individual technical brilliance — your "code will not speak for itself."

## Key Points

- Staff Engineer is the level above Senior Engineer (L5/E5/S3) characterized by influence and scope across multiple teams, not just individual technical delivery
- The projects that got panelists promoted all involved cross-team leadership, solving burning business problems, and enabling others rather than solo execution
- Multiplicative impact — making other engineers faster or more effective — is a hallmark of staff-level work and can be achieved through tooling, platforms, or Master Data sets
- A Portfolio Of Bets approach (2-3 projects balanced) is more reliable than betting everything on a single project, since many factors in big tech are outside your control
- Relationship building and visibility are essential — your code alone will not get you promoted; people need to know who you are and what value you bring
- Delegation versus hands-on work balance depends on your strengths, team composition, and the needs of the project; some staff engineers are "Code Machines" who execute at 4-5x individual productivity
- The Infrastructure Bias is real — building tools or platforms that other engineers depend on creates leveraged impact that is easier to measure than product-facing work, though this is partly a legacy bias
- Scope is discovered, not invented — ask what burning problems your manager, director, and teammates face, and solve those; start with the problem, not the solution
- Company hopping is a valid path to staff level when internal promotion opportunities are limited by organizational need or scope availability
- Communication skills — tailoring your message to technical and non-technical audiences — are critical for influencing decision-makers and building trust
- You should understand your company's promotion process early and write your promotion document before you think you're ready to identify gaps
