## Core Summary

Jay Jo, a Robinhood software engineer turned co-founder of Sei Network, traces a deliberately non-linear career — Facebook and Pinterest internships, four years at Robinhood, then founding a crypto company. He became jaded about big-tech promotion culture after a perfect 5/5 rating came with no promotion or pay change, and began "playing the game" by doing the minimum while still getting promoted. The GameStop short-squeeze saga and its T+2 settlement collateral crunch convinced him the financial system needs better rails, which became the founding thesis for Sei, for which he raised $35M.

## Key Points

- Careers are non-linear with step-function improvements, not smooth linear growth; a lucky "LeetCode and Chill" session covered the "number of islands" problem that literally appeared in his Facebook onsite
- What you value is a function of what people around you value — at UCLA the prestigious internships were Google and Facebook, not HFTs or pre-IPO firms
- The Facebook internship felt impersonal; Pinterest (as a Kleiner Perkins fellow) was a better, smaller, San Francisco-based culture with a close-knit intern class
- At Robinhood (signed at Series C ~100 people, joined at Series D), about a third of his new-grad class later raised venture money; scaling fast exposed engineers to far more systems
- You must choose what you're optimizing for: maximum TC upside is an early-stage startup, learning is a fast-growing smaller company, and he optimized for learning and growth over money
- Felt growth was slower than hoped after joining "too late" (post Series A/B); the on-call "overnight batch" of trade settlement was brutal (sometimes to 4–5am, dangerously close to business issues)
- Around the 9-month mark he earned a 5/5 rating with no promotion or compensation change tied to it, which made him jaded about "playing the game"
- Stayed 3.5 years partly due to a family death and a $400,000 cost to exercise options; the July 2021 IPO removed his "handcuffs"
- Financially Robinhood netted roughly an order of magnitude more than equivalent big-tech years, but timing luck mattered (2018 grad, the 2021 IPO wave)
- He still got promoted while coasting: in a 10x-growth company a competent engineer becomes load-bearing and a domain expert by default
- GameStop saga: short-seller buying pressure caused short squeezes; Robinhood paused buys on Jan 28, 2021 due to T+2 settlement collateral requirements (billions needed), not price manipulation — though they miscommunicated it
- The saga revealed an archaic financial system and inspired Sei: first a centralized-limit-order-book exchange done on-chain, later pivoted to building the underlying infrastructure
- Fundraising: $5M seed plus $30M strategic ($35M total), begun 3 weeks after Terra collapsed — "one of the worst times in the history of crypto"; a tier-1 lead investor (Multicoin) makes everything downstream easier
- Crypto value thesis: verifiability; strong today in payments and trading; stablecoins (USDC/USDT) for remittances and inflation-hedged savings; long-term a globally accessible "decentralized Wall Street"
- 2022 (starting Sei) was his highest-growth year; the hard skills were business/fundraising/marketing, not engineering — a competent L4-level engineer is sufficient founder-technical skill
- Advice to younger self: don't be so scared to leave — competent engineers face little downside, and founder reps make you more valuable afterward
