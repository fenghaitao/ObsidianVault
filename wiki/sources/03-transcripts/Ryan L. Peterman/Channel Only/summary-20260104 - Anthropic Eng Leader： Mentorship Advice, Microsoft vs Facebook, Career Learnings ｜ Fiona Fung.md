## Core Summary

Fiona Fung, an engineering leader who rose through the ranks of Microsoft and Meta and now supports Anthropic's Claude Code team, shares a career of lessons: manager-of-managers partnership and "trust but verify," mentoring versus coaching, dogfooding as a leader, and building a culture of kindness. She contrasts Microsoft's four-week sprints with Facebook's weekly ones and the "nothing is somebody else's problem" culture that drew her to Facebook Marketplace, and she explains how mission-orientation pulled her from a happy VR role at Meta to Anthropic.

## Key Points

- First job out of college used Visual Studio to build Visual Studio, where she learned dogfooding; she spent 11.5 years at Microsoft, staying through the JavaScript/TypeScript era to help ship TypeScript 1.0
- Left Microsoft in 2015 to join Facebook (Marketplace), recruited by a former Visual Studio coworker who became her first Facebook manager
- Three mission reasons for Facebook Marketplace: (1) affordability/commerce for most of the world where $5–10 and used goods matter, (2) environmentalism — giving goods a second/third/fourth life, (3) supporting local small businesses; she also wanted to learn to be an effective engineer outside the "Microsoft cocoon"
- Facebook Marketplace ran weekly sprints (vs Microsoft's four-week), first launched as www.fas.com; one aggressive-growth war room ran ~2 months and launched 75 experiments at once — a lesson to be realistic about duration, discuss team health, and be upfront with the team
- Manager-of-managers lesson: "divide and conquer" by asking managers what each of you is strong at, plus "trust but verify" — don't delegate so much that you lose touch with projects
- Mentoring tips: start with an explicit conversation about what the mentee wants (what does success look like at 3 and 6 months); distinguish mentoring (listening and dispensing advice) from coaching (acting as a mirror so they discover answers); the most effective goals are set by the mentee
- One-on-one advice for ICs: move status reporting to async (Slack or a living 1:1 doc) and save live one-on-one time for real conversations
- Pivotal feedback: when receiving constructive feedback, stay in "read-only mode" — listen without asking justifying questions; save questions for later and let yourself sit with the feedback
- On dogfooding: use the product you build to gain empathy and a pulse; as a manager it's her "maker time" (e.g., repro-ing floor-height bugs in Horizon OS); tips include integrating it into what brings you joy and holding weekly product sessions with the team
- On working with PMs: be upfront about the group's goal and each person's strengths, then divide and conquer; using the product a lot made her one-on-ones with PMs into productive product riffing
- Her badge-post sign-off: "in a world where you can be anything, be kind" — grounded in a COVID story where a report let her reschedule a one-on-one at the last minute to FaceTime her grandmother
- Culture at Facebook changed as it grew — "culture is not just a poster on a wall, it's through your actions"; she'd ask bootcampers choosing teams "what are you passionate about?"
- Joined Anthropic because everyone she met was mission-oriented ("build AI that benefits humanity with safety first"), echoing why Facebook Marketplace drew her in 2015; used a Sonnet-based tool internally and saw AI's transformation firsthand
- At Anthropic (2 months in) the mission is real DNA, and Claude Code's iterate-launch-feedback loop felt even faster than her Marketplace days
