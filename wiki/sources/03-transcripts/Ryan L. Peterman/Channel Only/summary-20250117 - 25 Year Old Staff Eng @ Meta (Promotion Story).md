## Core Summary

Ryan L. Peterman interviews Evan King, who reached Staff Engineer (IC6) at Meta by age 25 through three consecutive annual promotions. The conversation traces Evan's full journey: from high school AP Computer Science and founding a hacking club at Cornell, through Meta's boot camp and joining the Content Integrity org combating terrorism and graphic violence, to being handed tech lead of the graphic violence team as a junior engineer. Evan's IC6 promotion was anchored by his response to the Christchurch mosque shooting in 2019, where he helped create the Realtime Integrity team and built the Golden Set Recall evaluation system, eventually improving live suicide detection recall from 9% to mid-90s. After achieving staff, Evan left Meta to co-found a Web3 startup with his former manager, went through two acquisitions, and ultimately launched Hello Interview, a mock interview and interview preparation platform. Throughout the conversation, Ryan interweaves his own parallel experience growing quickly at Meta on an Instagram infrastructure team, creating a dual-perspective narrative on rapid career growth, the role of luck and agency, the importance of manager advocacy, and the tradeoffs between big tech depth and startup breadth.

## Key Points

- Rapid career growth at Meta does not require being a programming prodigy; both Evan and Ryan started CS relatively late (Evan in high school, Ryan in junior year of college).
- Doing work fast creates a "budget of time" to take on additional scope, which accelerates career growth; workflow optimization, effective code search, and not getting blocked are key accelerators.
- Manager advocacy is critical — Evan's manager proactively created opportunities (tech lead of graphic violence, the Realtime Integrity team) and advocated behind the scenes for his promotions.
- The most impactful solutions are often the simplest — Evan's breakthrough in live suicide detection came from using comment signals rather than sophisticated pixel/audio analysis, improving recall from 9% to 55% overnight.
- Meta's culture rewards impact over difficulty, meaning a one-line change with massive impact is valued more than a complex year-long project with similar impact.
- Influence without authority is essential at higher levels; Evan led teams where his level was not publicly known, forcing him to earn credibility through being right consistently.
- Leaving big tech for startups revealed how narrow big tech expertise can be; Evan had never set up a caching layer, configured a database, or written frontend code before starting his own company.
- Startup experience provides dramatically faster skill growth and breadth compared to big tech, though it comes with significantly more hours and stress, especially early on.
- Evan's biggest regret is not being more inquisitive about how things actually worked, opting instead to ship and move on; he recommends investing time in deep understanding.
- Beyond career growth, Evan advises investing in relationships and life outside work, acknowledging he underinvested in friendships and romantic relationships while hyper-optimizing for career.
