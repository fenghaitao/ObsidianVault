## Core Summary

James Everingham, former head of engineering at Instagram, traces a 30-year career — kicked out of Penn State and immediately hired as staff, through Borland, pre-IPO Netscape and the browser wars, leading Instagram engineering, Meta's Novi/Libra crypto project, Meta Dev Infra, and co-founding Guild. His recurring lessons are that relationships and "going where the smart people are" compound into outsized opportunity later, that distribution beats technology in the marketplace, and that small talent-dense teams with founder involvement beat large interdependent ones.

## Key Points

- Early career: at 18 he was kicked out of Penn State for never attending classes (a 0.0 GPA) and was hired weeks later as full-time staff because the university was already using his uploaded software; he took ~150 credits of advanced CS for free without ever getting a degree.
- The browser wars ended up being a distribution war, not a technology war: Netscape had a superior browser, but Microsoft bundled Internet Explorer free, and "if you have two products where the quality is pretty equal, people are going to just use the one that's the default."
- Netscape open-sourced its browser (a move suggested by Jamie Zawinski) to rally thousands of developers against Microsoft's resources; that code became the foundation of Mozilla, Firefox, and parts of Chrome. SSL export controls and censoring their own code were obstacles along the way.
- Instagram Stories shipped in ~3 months with a deliberately small founder-led team (Kevin and Mikey involved) that had clear ownership and no cross-org dependencies — instead of throwing a 20-person team at the Snapchat threat. Value: "do the simple thing first."
- CTO vs Head of Engineering: Mike Krieger stayed CTO (technology, roadmap, external brand) while Everingham took people/execution (hiring, org structure, performance reviews). They published their job descriptions when engineering hit ~100 people to remove confusion.
- Novi/Libra at Meta: he and product partner Kevin Weil were recruited into the crypto mission by David Marcus ("the mental virus"); the org grew to ~3,500 people but never shipped under political and regulatory pressure. In retrospect: "we probably should have just released it and asked for forgiveness."
- Guild: a control plane / governance layer for AI agents inside companies (identity, access, observability, circuit breakers) — a deterministic layer on top of non-deterministic AI. Preceded by Meta's Dev Mate, an agentic platform that went viral internally.
- On management: he is the "bottom of the org chart" servant-leader type, not a micromanager; micromanagement is a temporary tool to fix a broken team, then step back. Hiring smart people and thinking for them loses them.
- Career advice: go where the smart people are; offer help instead of asking "will you mentor me"; friends come and go but enemies collect; speak up more; max out your 401(k) on day one; time (not money) becomes the scarce commodity.
