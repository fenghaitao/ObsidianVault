## Core Summary

Ryan L. Peterman reflects on quitting Meta to work on his podcast full-time, a decision he admits never made sense on paper. Triggered by a reorg and funded by six months of savings, he set out to see if the podcast could work, only to nearly run out of money before sponsors Cursor and WorkOS signed on. He cut his burn rate by recording every episode himself — including shooting the Bjarne Stroustrup interview in his study with two iPhones — and nine months later the podcast sustains itself. His ergonomic-keyboard side project also funded within a day on Kickstarter, even though he expects it to lose money. His explicit frame: the goal was never money — otherwise he would have stayed at Meta — but the freedom to work on passion projects as long as they earn their keep.

## Key Points

- Made "the first decision that didn't make any sense on paper" — quitting Meta to pursue the podcast full-time, funded by six months of savings.
- A late-2025 reorg at Meta felt like the right opportunity to leave early and try the podcast for real.
- Doing everything alone (reviewing every second of footage plus deep research per guest) meant barely one episode per week, and "if I stopped, everything stopped."
- Four months in he still hadn't made a dollar; a strong flu left him bedridden and finally pushed him to take sponsors seriously, after which Cursor and WorkOS signed on and brought money in.
- He cut burn rate by recording episodes himself — the Bjarne Stroustrup interview was shot in his study against a wall of C++ books using two iPhones.
- Nine months in (against six earmarked), the podcast sustains itself, and the ergonomic keyboard raised the funds it needed within a day on Kickstarter despite being expected to lose money in aggregate.
- He'll keep going as long as the podcast covers itself and his living costs, asks for Spotify ratings (he usually struggles to stay in the top 50), and plans to ship the keyboard at the highest quality bar early next year.
