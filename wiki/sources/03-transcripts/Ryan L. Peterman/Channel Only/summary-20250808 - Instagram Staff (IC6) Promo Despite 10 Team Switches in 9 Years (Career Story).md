## Core Summary

Ryan L. Peterman interviews Sash, an Instagram staff engineer (IC6) who reached staff despite switching teams ten times in nine years. Sash optimized for fun and breadth over impact, drifting from Facebook "newsfeed delight" Easter eggs to experimental hardware, Instagram's blockchain/NFT team, and the early Threads prototype. His recurring lessons: prototypes are the highest-fidelity way to communicate, comfort with ambiguity is an asset, and breadth quietly compounds into a network.

## Key Points

- Joined a "newsfeed delight" team that shipped designers' discarded prototypes with no metrics; it disbanded after about six months but taught him to optimize for fun first
- His through-line was not optimizing for impact — he picked "irrational, high-risk" teams on vibe, accumulating a toolbox of skills, people, and connections he could build on later
- Moved to an experimental hardware team (AOSP/Android) with zero mobile-hardware experience and committed about two diffs in six months; the team was reviewed on de-risking directions, not landing code — big tech protects risk-takers from downside
- Reverse-engineered Apple's car Bluetooth messaging protocol with a Raspberry Pi to find the "upper-bound experience" unbounded by constraints, then worked backward to productionalize it
- Hardware teams have ruthless deadlines (something must ship to the factory); software ships continuously, which makes hardware timetables feel different
- Joined Instagram's blockchain team in London and shipped the NFT product; after Adam Mosseri's TED talk on decentralized creators, he partnered with Google, Spotify, Shopify, and Nike on cross-company incentives — until crypto winter killed the project
- Recruited onto Threads after spinning up a Mastodon instance next to an internal Instagram server and wiring them together over Christmas break, then building a text-only Instagram feed prototype; a working prototype created momentum where 500 docs couldn't
- Threads shipped from first prototype to public release in about five to six months, sped by a top-down "window of opportunity" (Musk acquiring Twitter) that made red tape magically vanish
- Built an internal RAG FAQ "support bot" during the GPT-3 era, which drove ~50 reach-outs from across the company — visibility through prototypes, not write-ups
- Left Meta to return to startups as a two-way-door decision; his manager's advice: don't fix all your weaknesses, position yourself so your strengths shine and weaknesses don't get in the way
