## Core Summary

Ryan L. Peterman interviews Stefan Mai, a former Amazon and Meta senior manager (M2) who now works on Hello Interview. Stefan gives a behind-the-scenes look at performance management — quotas, PIPs, and stack ranking — plus the gamesmanship between managers and orgs, and contrasts Amazon's disciplined, process-driven culture with Meta's open, malleable one.

## Key Points

- Joined Amazon in 2012 in early advertising (self-serve ads) and was immediately signed up for a six-to-eight month "death march" by an eccentric manager — emblematic of Amazon's rawest grind
- Transitioned to management "prematurely"; his early mistakes were soft-skills gaps (not understanding people's goals, weak storytelling), and he was uncomfortable for about 18 months before it clicked
- Advice to new managers: practice active listening and get people to open up, because the manager/peer power dynamic corrodes former bonds and the real information won't flow to a transactional "API" one-on-one
- Storytelling is a bedrock skill: critique the stories you already tell yourself, and paint your team's wins clearly so people feel like they're winning — morale follows the story
- His M2 promotion came after about seven managers in 3.5 years and building a 30-40 person org; leading other managers has hidden pitfalls (skip-level griping, "mutiny," and accidentally disintermediating a report)
- Kent Beck's explore/expand/extract: "explore" phases need risk tolerance and speed; "extract" phases reward discipline; Amazon culturally skews toward the lean, extracted end
- Amazon vs Meta: Amazon is disciplined, predictable, and process-heavy ("mechanisms" and fitness functions); Meta is open, collaborative, and malleable but less disciplined; Meta fights harder for its top quintile (discretionary equity), Amazon stays frugal
- Low-performer quotas (roughly 7-20% depending on company) exist to counter rater inflation; managers dread the "egg on your face" reversal of having to walk back praise
- PIPs are first a legal paper trail, but people genuinely can exit them; Amazon HR was "savage" about checking boxes, while Meta HR historically exhausted every avenue before firing
- Job hopping: early-career moves force generalization and growth; frequent senior-level moves are a red flag; the credibility snowball can also become a shackle if you get stuck as "the person who built that system"
- Advice to his younger self: find mentorship sooner, and lean into agency — write plans for what you want and the steps to get there, since the world is malleable to such plans
