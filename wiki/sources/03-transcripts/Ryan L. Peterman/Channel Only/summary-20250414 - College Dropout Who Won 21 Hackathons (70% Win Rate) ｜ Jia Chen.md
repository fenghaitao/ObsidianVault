## Core Summary

Ryan L. Peterman interviews Jia Chen, a college student who won 21 hackathons with a 70% win rate despite starting as a finance major with only one Python class. Jia shares her systematic approach to hackathon success: selecting teammates based on skills and GitHub activity rather than credentials, coming in with pre-researched ideas, connecting frontend and backend within the first few hours, and pitching to judges by focusing on the demo rather than technical details. She also discusses balancing school, hackathons, and content creation (growing to 50K followers) through time blocking, and recounts founding Sprint.dev after a chance angel investment at a Luma hot pot event, eventually landing seven YC companies as clients and reaching five figures MRR.

## Key Points

- Jia won 21 hackathons with a ~70% win rate, starting from a non-technical background (finance major) with only one Python class
- Her hackathon strategy: pre-research sponsor tools and ideas before coding, connect frontend and backend in the first few hours (not at the end), aim for a solid finished product over complexity
- Judges are often non-technical — focus on the demo and user interaction, not the tech stack; a flawless simple demo beats a complex unfinished one
- Team selection: choose teammates based on GitHub activity and relevant skills, not credentials (PhD, internships); look for scrappy people with a growth mindset who can maintain energy for 24 hours
- Agency is a core theme: she secretly rented a car and drove to Ohio for her second hackathon, won first place; she emphasizes taking initiative without waiting for permission
- She balanced 3.5+ engineering GPA, 15 hackathons in one semester, and growing to 50K social media followers through time blocking and prioritizing high-ROI content
- Her startup Sprint.dev (co-founded with Aiden Goolan) began when she shipped an MVP overnight, posted it on Instagram, and it crashed from too much traffic; later raised angel investment at a Luma hot pot event and VC funding
- She landed seven YC companies and 50 other companies as clients, reaching five figures MRR; her apartment was robbed but she kept working within the hour
- On college: "it works for some people, it gives them structure and discipline" but it "dulled down my creativity and my passion for learning"; she recommends setting win conditions before dropping out
- Key advice for students: build something people want to use, get projects out of localhost and onto the cloud, develop a personal brand for leverage, and place yourself in the right environment (e.g., moving to SF for startups)
