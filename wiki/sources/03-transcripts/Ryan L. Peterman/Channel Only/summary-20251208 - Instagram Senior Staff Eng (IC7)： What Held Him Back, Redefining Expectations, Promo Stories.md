## Core Summary

Marius Schultz, a Meta Senior Staff engineer (IC7) who specializes in frontend web engineering, explains how he grew from IC4 to IC7 by repeatedly "redefining expectations" — leading projects classified one or two levels above his title. He disentangles ratings (delivered impact) from lagging promotions (demonstrated next-level behaviors), shows how a single cross-functional expectations miss blocked one promo, and describes how he increases his "luck surface area" by positioning for opportunities. For senior engineers he advocates leveraged work in product infrastructure and strong ownership as the directly responsible individual.

## Key Points

- Burning passion for the web since 9th-grade HTML; famously read a CSS book ("Little Boxes") in one sitting on Christmas Eve; worked at a small Munich agency while studying CS
- IC4→IC5: greatly exceeded for multiple halves, but one promo was blocked by an "expectations miss" — he was too pushy and intense advocating ideas rather than collaborating cross-functionally
- Ratings measure the impact you delivered; promotions (lagging) measure whether you already exhibit the next level's behaviors — a gap means you're not yet ready
- The COVID half produced zero company-wide promotions, blocking his promo again; he kept doing IC5+ work anyway and was eventually recognized
- His "redefines expectations" rating came from leading, as an IC4, a project classified as IC6-level (10+ engineers plus PMs and designers) and launching it without major issues
- There is an IC4/IC5/IC6 (and higher) way to approach almost any problem; scope scales from task → small project → large project → org → system → company → industry
- Calibration uses "laddering": articulate the base work that earned "meets all," then the additional work that justifies "exceeds"
- Luck surface area: a senior engineer's paternity leave created a vacuum; because Marius had positioned himself as the web-platform lead, he was handed the IC6 project
- IC6 promo (another RE): a web project under a hard deadline; he flexed into a "product hybrid" role through ambiguity ("fog of war"), scoped and sequenced the work, then became the coding machine
- Switched to Instagram web (recruited by Jake Bolam) to stay aligned with web/quality work; rewrote the long-neglected notifications panel
- Leverage and "product infra": hardened the site with React error boundaries, classifying primary/secondary/tertiary errors and writing a company-wide note, then extended with lint rules so tooling does work on his behalf
- Threads web: joined a few weeks after kickoff as the sole web engineer (6–8 weeks solo), deliberately created a separate surface, built logged-out and logged-in clients, then hired and led the team as TL
- IC7 promo came primarily from Threads' success; he shipped ~2,000 diffs that year (~10/day) and made the attribution clear ("Threads web happened because I got involved")
- Strong ownership as the DRI: anticipate failure modes (DNS, infra, QA, feedback channels) and work backward to prevent them; "recursive accountability" plus a driving-teacher model of tech leading
- Advice to younger self: "things are going to be fine… chill a little bit" — he once forced himself to publish a blog post weekly, an intensity he now considers unhealthy
