## Core Summary

John Myles White, a former engineering director at Meta (PyTorch and Meta Superintelligence Labs), argues that the engineering labor market has flipped from undersupplied to oversupplied, making big-tech employment more stressful even as Meta runs effectively for shareholders. He dissects the promotion-driven culture that dominated Meta's AI infra and monetization orgs, where engineers shipped work they believed would fail because it was their "promo bar," and where real skill-building became decoupled from promotions. He also recounts his formative years building Meta's experimentation tools (Deltoid A/B testing), his role in the Julia programming language, and his case for statistical rigor.

## Key Points

- On Meta vs. MSL: he is bullish on Meta as a stockholder (run effectively for the bottom line) but bearish on being an employee — the possibility of layoffs is "the number one emotional thing that causes people stress."
- The supply of engineers is now "way oversupplied" (exacerbated by AI), so employers make fewer sacrifices to acquire/retain talent, affecting comp, perks, employee voice, and reorg calculus.
- Promotion culture: in AI infra, "there was not a single person... whose first and foremost goal wasn't promotion"; monetization hired with "a very detailed plan of work you will do in order to get promoted."
- Promo hacking's consequences: teams ship work they know is bad to hit the promo bar, goals are written so shipping something you intend to delete the next day counts as success, and systems get built maximally coupled to satisfy "affected 10 other systems" bars.
- "You have to play the game. It's totally irrational not to play the game" — unless a VP shuts it down, no one can unilaterally disarm; PyTorch was the counterexample where people loved engineering for its own sake.
- PyTorch held a higher promotion bar (its "eights" were as good as other teams' "tens"), making its people seem under-leveled yet credible to external teams and ultimately stronger engineers.
- Early career: joined the "data science" team that split into core data science and data science infrastructure; the "Emotional Contagion in Social Networks" PNAS paper became a PR disaster; he became de facto tech lead of A/B testing tools after senior engineers left.
- Deltoid is Meta's A/B testing framework; he says Statsig is essentially Deltoid re-built externally, and DataSwarm was re-built by its author as Airflow after he quit.
- Julia: a high-level language designed to match C speed (initially aimed at replacing MATLAB); R can be 1,000–10,000x slower than equivalent C because of pervasive dynamic checks and lazy evaluation.
- Statistics: he recommends Larry Wasserman ("hyper intense honesty") and "Introduction to Agnostic Statistics"; warns against treating "barely significant" as a win and shipping-to-delete goal pathology.
- Regret: never attended his skip-level leader Javi's office hours; advice to his younger self: have more confidence and ambition — most successful people "just tried and you didn't try."
