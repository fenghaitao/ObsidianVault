## Core Summary

An anonymous big-tech veteran posting as "Asian Dad Energy" gives an honest account of being laid off after 25 years in tech, walking through his path from engineering consulting to big-tech chief architect. He argues the current wave of layoffs is driven by companies cutting headcount to performatively justify AI investment, and shares lessons on flexibility, mentorship, engineering integrity, and communication.

## Key Points

- He discovered he was on a layoff list and voluntarily swapped himself to the top so a couple of junior engineers would survive the round, "buying them a matter of months."
- His conjecture on layoffs: in an AI arms race, companies cut headcount so revenue stays flat while costs drop, then market "AI-driven efficiency" to lift stock valuation — a performative, sector-wide trend, not just one employer.
- The layoff triggered a cocktail of anxiety, panic, excitement, fear, and hope; he was too mentally unsettled to negotiate despite a generous, well-structured severance (garden leave, severance months, PTO payout, education/equipment stipend).
- Career arc: CS degree → digital/engineering consulting (junior → senior → tech lead → architect → technical director) → big-tech senior enterprise/chief architect for his final several years.
- Consulting has a "get in, build, get out" model and a longer-term retainer model; fly-by-night projects leave clients with a dizzying, redundant patchwork of systems that digital transformation must stitch together.
- Senior consulting became selling a smooth digital-transformation vision to non-technical C-suite executives, and he burned out on the gap between the dream sold and the messy "sausage making."
- Engineering integrity: engineers are obligated to tell clients the truth ("your $20 million investment is a steaming pile of turds"); a firm's reputation is what prevents shipping junk.
- Technical consulting gets less pushback than management consulting, which is often blamed when client teams are thinned out or laid off.
- Big-tech compensation more than doubled or tripled versus consulting via bonuses, RSUs (vesting over 3–4 years), and an employee stock purchase plan (~15% discount); shares held through COVID ballooned in value.
- A mentor's most memorable lesson was "be flexible" and adapt to changing conditions; after 40 he noticed learning new frameworks taking noticeably longer.
- He started vlogging (as Asian Dad Energy) because it was easier than writing; the video went viral and felt therapeutic — he realized he wasn't alone.
- Advice: work hard, don't stop learning, stay flexible; for speaking, relax, don't over-identify with the subject, and explain it like the audience is five.
