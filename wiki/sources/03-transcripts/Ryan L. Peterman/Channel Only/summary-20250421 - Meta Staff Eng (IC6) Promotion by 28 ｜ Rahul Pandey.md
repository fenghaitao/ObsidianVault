## Core Summary

Rahul Pandey shares his full career journey from a Stanford research lab spinout startup (Cos) that was acquired by Pinterest, through two failed promotion attempts at Pinterest, to achieving Staff Engineer (IC6) at Meta by age 28. He then transitioned to a Tech Lead Manager role before leaving to found his own company, Taro. The conversation covers career decision-making frameworks (one-way vs two-way doors), the value of storytelling and social proof, the importance of mentorship and fast iteration loops, the differences between big tech and startup culture, and the mindset shift from seeking permission to taking initiative. Rahul emphasizes that much of his success came from luck, but that luck can be manufactured through curiosity, relationship-building, and putting yourself out there.

## Key Points

- Rahul joined a Stanford spinout startup (Cos) as employee #2 because he found the story too compelling to pass up, despite having offers from Google, Facebook, Microsoft, Twitter, and Square
- The startup was acquired by Pinterest after 8 months — an "acqui-hire" closer to failure than success, but it gave Rahul a higher-than-new-grad package and a great story
- At Pinterest, he did a career reset from recommendation systems to Android mobile development, and was rejected for L3→L4 promotion twice before winning on appeal
- A key mentorship from a staff-level Facebook transplant at Pinterest taught him to create sandbox environments for fast iteration, dramatically improving his velocity
- The second promotion rejection taught him that framing matters: a 16-month project was interpreted as lacking urgency, when the real story was different
- He switched to Meta (Facebook) as an IC5, then achieved IC6 (Staff) through two main projects: a migration of Portal's video calling infrastructure back to Messenger's shared infra, and a bottoms-up debug tool that saved hundreds of engineering hours per month
- Job hopping is effective early in career but becomes counterproductive at staff/principal levels because impact at those levels comes from relationships and trust
- He served as a TLM (Tech Lead Manager) at Meta but found the hybrid role unsustainable long-term, recommending it only as a transitional role
- He left Meta to found Taro with his co-founder Alex (whom he met on the Portal team), motivated by finding a trusted co-founder, financial stability, and signal from his YouTube tutorials
- Rahul's biggest career insight: "There are no gatekeepers" — you don't need permission to learn, build, or teach; this mindset shift came too late for him
- He estimates luck played a large role in his career, but believes luck can be manufactured through genuine curiosity, keeping in touch with people, and sharing what you're working on
- Advice for new grads: unless you have exceptional opportunities and social proof, take the standard path — go to a big brand name company first to build credibility
