## Core Summary

Ryan L. Peterman interviews "Simon," a Meta engineer from Sweden who grew from new grad (IC3) to Staff Engineer (IC6) in three years, earning two rare "redefines expectations" (RE) ratings and two discretionary equity grants along the way. Simon credits his long-time manager Bala and a revenue-focused ads payments team for the early momentum, and he dissects the difference between ratings (what you achieved) and promotions (how you achieved it at the next level's behaviors). After a brief, ill-timed switch to management, he returned to IC, finding that being a people-oriented IC6 gives him more optionality than being a manager.

## Key Points

- Simon joined as an IC3 returning intern and started effectively as an IC4 by skipping the tooling ramp (prior internship) and pre-learning React/TypeScript-style concepts
- His first-half promo to IC4 was driven by revenue impact — his projects brought in 3x the team's half goal — plus end-to-end ownership of why results landed or didn't
- RE ("redefines expectations") is Meta's rarest rating, above "greatly exceeds"; Simon earned two, one as an IC4
- Ratings and promos are correlated but not 1:1 — a promo requires demonstrating next-level *behaviors* (owning others' success), not just outsized impact
- His IC5 promo came when he stopped being responsible only for his own output and took responsibility for a pod's project and people
- His IC6 gap was accountability: "delegation is not abdication" — he learned to build systems (people + project breakdowns) that surface slipping projects and struggling people
- Discretionary equity (additional equity / AE) is extra RSUs a director grants top performers; Simon received it twice, often announced via an unexpected 15-minute calendar invite
- Written communication is the biggest lever: TLDR above the fold, numbers, and audience-tailored sections; most engineers bury the "why" in detail
- He briefly became a manager, then returned to IC after a chaotic period (manager in an accident, skip on leave, London exit, and overpromising two IC5→IC6 reports)
- As an IC6 who enjoys people work, he has more optionality in who/how he coaches than a manager, whose support is unconditional
