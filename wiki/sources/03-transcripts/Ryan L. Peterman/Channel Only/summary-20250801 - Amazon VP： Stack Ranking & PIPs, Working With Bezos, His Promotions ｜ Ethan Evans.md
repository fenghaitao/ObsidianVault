## Core Summary

Ryan L. Peterman interviews Ethan Evans, an Amazon VP who rose from being fired twice in the dot-com era to leading an 800-engineer organization. Ethan gives a brutally honest account of Amazon's performance management (stack ranking, unregretted attrition, PIPs) and shares the tactics — "strategically annoying" self-advocacy and "the magic loop" — that drove his own promotions, contrasting working with Jeff Bezos and Andy Jassy.

## Key Points

- Started wanting to work at Apple; went to Carnegie Mellon; his pre-Amazon career was a chain of high-growth startups where he was "made redundant" twice largely for being abrasive
- After being a "layoff of one," an interviewer asked why no company kept him if he was that valuable — he rebuilt his soft skills and studied workplace psychology
- Amazon reached out after he'd studied them as a Barnes & Noble competitor; hired as a senior manager, he rose to director then VP of an 800-person org
- Director promo: an outsized TiVo-partnership win ("without Ethan, we wouldn't have TiVo") plus politely pressuring his VP ("my career is very important to me")
- "Strategically annoying": have an opinion and push, but civilly — you can raise any topic with polite wording
- "The magic loop": a deal with your manager — "I'll do everything you need done; you make sure I'm rewarded for it"
- An early mistake: he promised a star engineer a promotion then missed the cycle out of process ignorance — know the dates; the manager is "the chicken," you're "the pig"
- Twitch integration: he gave up a team of 800 to advise CEO Emmett Shear with no authority, to practice influence and soft skills
- Bezos vs Andy Jassy: Jeff can gamble ("how much is in the bank? I might want to spend it"); Andy is a partner-style leader — Ethan found Jeff more inspiring and supportive
- Amazon's unregretted attrition (~5-7%/year) forces hard conversations managers otherwise avoid; most PIPs are "dishonest and psychologically unrealistic" — once on the list, start job hunting
- A manager can spin the same employee's facts two ways (e.g., heavy code review as valuable vs. "just churning") — HR won't rescue you; don't bring a knife to a gunfight
- Advice: prefer high-growth companies ("escalator, not ladder") and build relationships/reputation, since Amazon called him — not the other way around
