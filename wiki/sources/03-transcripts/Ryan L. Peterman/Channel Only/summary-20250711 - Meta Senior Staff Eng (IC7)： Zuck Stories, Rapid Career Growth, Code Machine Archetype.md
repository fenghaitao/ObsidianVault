## Core Summary

Ryan L. Peterman interviews Michael Novati, who joined Facebook in 2009 as an intern (when it had ~220 engineers) and rose to Senior Staff (IC7) as a self-described "coding machine." Michael shares early-company stories (working with Mark Zuckerberg on the 2009 reactions hackathon, the ripstick ban) and argues that raw output alone doesn't make a senior IC — taste and judgment do. He also weighs in on how LLMs will reshape the "coding machine" archetype and the profession.

## Key Points

- Joined as an intern in 2009 between undergrad and a planned PhD (UW HCI), drawn by a lucky perfect fit on internal collaboration tools
- Early Facebook culture was deeply engineer-driven: engineers had to be won over, and most internal tools were built from scratch
- "Move fast and break things" was about breaking norms and first-principles thinking, not reckless breakage; stability matters more as a company matures
- He wrote the internal "weekly navati" blog as an open/direct exercise, but in retrospect wouldn't repeat it — off-the-cuff posts on sensitive topics caused real friction
- The "coding machine" archetype describes single-person output that a team of 4+ senior engineers wouldn't accomplish (e.g., manually removing every "preparable" class from the codebase)
- He frames taste/judgment as the patina on a cast-iron pan: built slowly through reps and feedback, more important than raw output for reaching IC7
- On LLMs: they're the next step from Vim→VS Code, making experienced engineers with strong taste far more productive first; agentic flows may redefine what "software engineer" means
- Compensation wisdom: don't sweat single-digit offer differences or try to guess stock — prioritize a company where you'll fit and perform, since performance is more within your control
