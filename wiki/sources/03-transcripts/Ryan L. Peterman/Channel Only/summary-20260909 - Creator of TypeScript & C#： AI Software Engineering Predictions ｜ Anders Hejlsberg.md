## Core Summary

Anders Hejlsberg pushes back on maximalist AI-replaces-engineers predictions, arguing "AI will write 90% of code" is really a claim about sheer volume — AI already writes 100% of some no-code apps, and exploding output volume makes the prediction self-fulfilling — rather than about the super-high-quality, never-before-seen code that still demands human judgment. Because AI is at heart a stochastic machine that has memorized the internet, it can extrapolate but struggles with genuinely novel work like the TypeScript compiler, and someone still has to understand and take responsibility for whatever it produces. He predicts the craft of software engineering shifts from typing code to reviewing agents' output — more reviewing, less typing — and that juniors remain the pipeline that produces seniors.

## Key Points

- On "AI will write 90%+ of code within a year": it depends what you count — for certain classes of apps (no-code), AI already writes 100%; the sheer volume of code is exploding because AI writes so much, which makes the prediction "self-fulfilling."
- The real bar is super-high-quality code nobody has written before — novel algorithms or solutions — and it's not clear AI will write 90% of that.
- AI is "at heart a big stochastic machine that has memorized the entire internet"; it can extrapolate somewhat but struggles with what it hasn't seen, which is why it cannot write the TypeScript compiler (there is nothing like it in the training set).
- "Don't hand AI the keys": if you don't understand what is below, you can't fix it or vouch for it, and responsibility lands on the human — "they're going to sue you, not the AI."
- On why his view differs from Anthropic optimists: it's not that AI can't do amazing things, but that people extrapolate all the way to 100%; someone still has to understand the work and connect it to the business problem.
- If AI replaces junior engineers, "how do you ever get senior software engineers?" The pyramid has narrowed at the bottom — fewer people entering, advancing faster to supervisory roles.
- The craft is changing from typing lines of code to having agents type them while you review — more reviewing, less typing; some people are fantastic at that workflow.
- He has enjoyed typing code for 40–50 years and will keep typing the parts he likes, happily farming out tests to AI; reviewing is harder for him, and AI could make code review much more ergonomic.
