## Core Summary
swyx opens the AI Engineer Summit with a keynote declaring "war on slop" — low-quality, inauthentic, or inaccurate content that can be produced by both humans and AI. He argues that as token generation costs drop 100-1000x per year, the amount of taste required to combat slop is orders of magnitude larger than what's needed to produce it (swyx's Law of Anti-Slop). The positive message is that AI itself can be used to fight slop, through better prompting, curation, code maps for codebase understanding, computer use for automation, sub-agents to fight context rot, and modularity where humans design clear boundaries and AI codes everything in between.

## Key Points
- Slop is not exclusive to AI; humans can also produce low-quality, inauthentic, or inaccurate content
- The opposite of slop is "kino" — high-quality, tasteful, creative content
- Brandolini's Law: the energy needed to refute BS is orders of magnitude larger than needed to produce it
- swyx's Law of Anti-Slop: the amount of taste needed to fight slop is orders of magnitude larger than that needed to produce it
- AI can be prompted to avoid producing slop, with significant quality improvements
- Code slop creates tech debt and can expose private data of millions of users
- "No autonomy without accountability" — measuring AI by duration rather than code quality is sloppy
- Semi-Sync Value of Depth: keep human attention on hardest problems, commoditized work goes async
- Code Maps use AI to scale codebase understanding as a way to fight code slop
- Computer Use enables AI to autonomously operate complex apps including IDEs
- Sub-agents can fight context rot, a major theme at the conference
- Greg Brockman's modularity: keep clear boundaries on human-designed components, let AI code everything in between
