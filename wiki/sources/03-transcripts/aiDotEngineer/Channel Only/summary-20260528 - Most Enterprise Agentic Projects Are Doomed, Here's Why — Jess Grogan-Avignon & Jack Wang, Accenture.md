## Core Thesis
Jess Grogan-Avignon and Jack Wang of Accenture argue that most enterprise agentic AI projects are doomed not because of AI limitations, but because the enterprise "scaffolding" — the human-speed processes, governance, funding models, and delivery approaches — is fundamentally incompatible with machine-speed AI development. They present five enterprise tensions (speed, value, delivery, trust, moat) and prescribe: bet like a VC, upgrade governance for machine speed, and engineer for trust with feedback loops from day one.

## Key Points
- **Tension 1 — Speed**: Enterprise processes (security reviews, deployment approvals, data access, stakeholder meetings) are designed for human pace. AI can build in 2 weeks what takes 12 months to reach production due to alignment across infrastructure, security, AI gateway, data governance, and application teams. The real technical debt is years of underinvestment in engineering automation and CI/CD. Every human process must become adaptable, executable code.
- **Tension 2 — Value**: Traditional business cases assume scope, value, and cost are knowable upfront. With AI, these are often backwards — you learn by doing. When prototyping costs drop to near zero, entirely new capabilities become possible. AI achievers (12% of companies) see 50% higher revenue growth from doing entirely new things, not cost cutting. CFOs must think like VCs: back a portfolio of bets rather than demanding fixed guaranteed payback from individual projects.
- **Tension 3 — Delivery**: Agentic systems are non-deterministic with emergent behavior. They cannot be scoped like traditional feature builds or milestone'd like fixed programs. Delivery must shift to hypothesis-driven approach with small loops of build-evaluate-iterate, building statistical confidence. Teams need people comfortable with ambiguity who can translate statistical numbers into stakeholder confidence.
- **Tension 4 — Trust**: Completion of individual features is less valuable than the trust built over time. Trust operates like a deposit/withdrawal account with stakeholders. The exposure ladder for progressive autonomy: shadow mode (agent runs alongside, no effect) → advisory mode (agent recommends, human approves) → controlled autonomy (narrow, low-risk, kill switches) → full autonomy. Each step is gated by evidence in outcomes, not completion of activities.
- **Tension 5 — Moat**: In a recursive world where AI codes AI, anything can be cloned. Your ERP, CRM, SOPs (transactional memory) are a floor, not a fortress — every competitor has a version. The real moat is living memory: edge cases, corrections, emotional intent, and actual behavior at your specific scale. Every feature should either generate a feedback signal or deliver on what signals have taught you. Feedback is the only moat.
- **Prescription**: Start now. Deliver differently — measure in confidence, shape projects around hypotheses, run small loops. Make finance a transformation partner with portfolio-based investment. Make governance speed the CTO's top engineering problem. For CEOs: your moat is in what you are learning and compounding every day.
