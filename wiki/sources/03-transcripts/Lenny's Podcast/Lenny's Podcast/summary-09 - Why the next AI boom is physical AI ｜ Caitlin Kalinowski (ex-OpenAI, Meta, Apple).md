## Core Summary

Caitlin Kalinowski, former hardware leader at Apple, Meta, and OpenAI, argues that the next AI boom is physical AI. As AI saturates what can be done behind a keyboard, the frontier shifts to robotics, manufacturing, and the physical world. She explains why hardware is fundamentally harder than software: you only get to "compile" 4-5 times total (not daily), and part variance across millions of units means you must solve for the last half-percent. VR's real legacy was teaching the industry SLAM, depth sensing, and spatial perception -- technologies now critical for robotics. She also warns about the need to re-industrialize the US for military security, and that AR glasses (Orion) are the likely future of computing.

## Key Points

- VR's real value: taught the industry SLAM, depth sensing, spatial perception -- all now critical for robotics and physical AI
- Hardware "compiles" only 4-5 times total (vs. software compiling hourly); you must be conservative and get it right
- Part variance across millions of units means solving for the last 0.5% is the hardest part
- AR glasses (Orion): 70-degree field of view, waveguides and microLEDs not yet ready for mass production
- Computer science enrollment is declining; hardware/robotics enrollment is rising
- "In the next 2 years, we need to invest a lot more in drones than in aircraft carriers"
- Need to re-industrialize the US: "People that are your allies now may not be in the future"
- Sam Altman: "Why not 100x or 10,000x? You're thinking too small." Steve Jobs: unwavering bar for excellence
- Robotics needs to be "Pixar-like" -- non-threatening, soft, reactive to humans
- Memory prices are a "meteor" coming for consumer hardware and robotics
