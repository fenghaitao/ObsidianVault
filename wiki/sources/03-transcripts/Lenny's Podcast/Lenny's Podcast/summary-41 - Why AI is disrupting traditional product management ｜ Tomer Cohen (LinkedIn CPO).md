## Core Summary

Tomer Cohen, LinkedIn's CPO, argues that AI is disrupting traditional product management by collapsing the bloated, micro-specialized product development process into a "full-stack builder" model where individuals can take ideas from concept to launch across traditionally siloed functions. LinkedIn is piloting this model by re-architecting its platform for AI, building specialized internal agents (trust, growth, research, analyst), and driving cultural change through pod-based teams, new career ladders, and performance incentives. Cohen contends that by 2030, 70% of job skills will change, and organizations must become more nimble and adaptive to match the accelerating pace of change.

## Key Points

- The full-stack builder model empowers individuals to develop experiences end-to-end, combining skills across engineering, design, and product management, supported by AI agents.
- LinkedIn is replacing its APM program with an Associate Full-Stack Builder (APB) program, creating a formal career ladder with the title "full-stack builder."
- AI tools cannot work off-the-shelf at scale; companies must invest in re-architecting platforms, curating golden knowledge examples, and customizing agents to their unique context.
- LinkedIn has built specialized agents: trust agent (identifies harm vectors), growth agent (critiques growth ideas), research agent (trained on member personas), and analyst agent (queries the LinkedIn graph).
- Top talent is adopting these tools fastest, confirming that AI amplifies already-strong performers rather than leveling the playing field.
- Change management is critical — providing tools is insufficient; organizations must build incentive programs, showcase success stories, and adjust performance evaluations.
- Cohen identifies five irreplaceable human traits for builders: vision, empathy, communication, creativity, and judgment (taste-making), with judgment being the most important.
- The shift mirrors the desktop-to-mobile transformation and requires patience, up-front investment, and continuous communication of progress.
