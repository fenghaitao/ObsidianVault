## Core Summary

Andrew Ambrosino, product and engineering lead for Codex at OpenAI, reveals that 90% of OpenAI employees use Codex weekly -- not 90% of engineers, 90% of the entire company. The product development process has inverted: implementation is no longer the expensive part, taste and curation are. At OpenAI, everyone is agentic and building everything, leading to "90 different uncoordinated" prototypes for the same feature. The PM's job is now curation: what's good across all those attempts? He pushes back on "PRDs are dead" -- the real skill is picking the right medium (document vs. prototype) for the point you're making. He also reveals that Codex's quality bar is being set so high that opening it should be as natural as opening a browser tab.

## Key Points

- 90% of OpenAI employees (not just engineers) use Codex weekly; 5M+ weekly active users, 6x growth since January
- "Implementation is not the expensive part anymore. It's dare I say taste" -- the product process is inverted
- Everyone at OpenAI is agentic; leads to 90 uncoordinated prototypes for the same feature
- PM's role is curation: what's good across all attempts, what should be folded into other features
- "PRDs are dead, prototypes are in" -- Andrew disagrees: pick the right medium for the point you're making
- Prototypes now look production-ready, which can cause over-anchoring on the wrong direction
- Codex quality bar: "so high that there was never a hesitation to open this app... just like people open a browser tab"
- Merging Codex and ChatGPT: the vision is a unified super assistant
- AI is still bad at design because "the human aspect of taste is part of the feedback mechanism"
- Designer turned engineer turned PM -- embodies the role-merging trend
