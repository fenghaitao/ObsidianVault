## Core Summary

Jason Lemkin, founder and CEO of SaaStr, shares his firsthand experience replacing SaaStr's ~10-person human sales team (SDRs + AEs) with 20 AI agents and 1.2 humans — achieving roughly the same business performance. He argues that AI is rapidly displacing mediocre and mid-pack sales performers, predicting the classic email-based SDR and human BDR (lead qualification) roles will be "mostly extinct" within 12 months. The conversation covers practical advice for deploying AI sales agents, why training and human oversight are critical, the emergence of forward-deployed engineers as essential to AI GTM success, and how the best human salespeople who learn to orchestrate AI agents will become hyper-valuable ($250K SDRs managing 10 agents). Lemkin also explains the broader bifurcation of the B2B market: AI-native companies are experiencing unprecedented demand ("everyone in market at once"), while legacy B2B companies face evaporating demand and must embrace ruthless efficiency.

## Key Points

- SaaStr replaced ~10 human GTM staff (SDRs + AEs) with 20 AI agents + 1.2 humans (one full-time AE + Amelia, chief AI officer, spending 20% of her time on agent orchestration), achieving roughly equivalent business performance.
- AI is replacing the mid-pack and mediocre: the best salespeople will get AI superpowers, but the rest are at risk of displacement.
- Classic email-based SDRs and human BDRs/qualifiers will be "mostly extinct" within 12 months — AI can do the job better, 24/7, with instant response times.
- The key to successful AI agent deployment: train agents on your best salesperson's scripts and emails, iterate daily for 30 days, and never "set and forget."
- Forward-deployed engineers (FDEs) are essential — the vendor's willingness to help with training and onboarding is more important than raw feature comparisons.
- "Don't build it yourself" for GTM agents — buy from vendors who will support you, unless you're Vercel with dedicated GTM engineers.
- The "Plays vs Playbooks" distinction: all GTM plays (outbound, webinars, events, podcasts) still work, but the old playbooks are broken because growth has decelerated and demand has evaporated for legacy B2B.
- Bifurcation of the market: AI-native companies have 50%+ of prospects in-market at once (vs. traditional 3-5%), creating unprecedented demand, while legacy B2B struggles with evaporating demand.
- The orchestrator/human-in-the-loop role is critical and exhausting — agents work 24/7 including nights, weekends, and holidays, and someone must review outputs constantly.
- Agent fatigue is real: SaaStr may be at capacity with 20 agents and can't add a 21st due to the management overhead.
- For salespeople: embrace AI tools, become the best at working with agents, and lean into the transparency they bring; fighting it is career suicide.
- "If you can close on a text message, AI can close it" — AI can be "people people" too, and the "I'm a people person" defense is insufficient.
- The incognito mode test: experience your own product's support, sales contact, and onboarding as a fresh user to find the most painful gap, then deploy an agent to fix it.
- The best startup is often the one you're already working at — don't quit if you have happy customers; turn your existing company into your hot AI startup.
- SaaStr's agent stack: Deli (digital clone/support), Artisan (outbound), Qualified (inbound qualification), Agentforce (Salesforce, for lapsed lead reactivation), with 70% response rate on previously ignored leads.
- Salesforce is becoming the hub/database for AI GTM agents, creating a renaissance for the platform despite its age.
