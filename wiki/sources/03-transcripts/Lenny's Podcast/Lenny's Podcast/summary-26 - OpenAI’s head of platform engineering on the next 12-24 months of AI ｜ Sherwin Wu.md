## Core Summary

[[Lenny Rachitsky]] interviews [[Sherwin Wu]], head of engineering for [[OpenAI]]'s API and developer platform, on the state and near-term trajectory of AI-driven software engineering. Wu describes near-universal internal Codex adoption (95% of engineers daily, 100% of PRs reviewed by Codex) and frames the moment through two literary analogies: MIT's "wizard book" (SICP) plus Disney's Sorcerer's Apprentice for engineers-as-spell-casting-wizards who must avoid letting agents "go off the rails," and *The Mythical Man-Month*'s "surgical team" prediction for his own management philosophy of concentrating time on top performers. He elaborates several named ideas: the "[[One Person Billion Dollar Startup]]" and its under-discussed second/third-order effects (a possible coming "golden age of B2B SaaS"); the underrated opportunity in [[Business Process Automation Opportunity|business process automation]] versus open-ended coding; why many enterprise AI deployments get negative ROI absent genuine [[Bottom-Up AI Adoption]]; and OpenAI's "[[Platform Neutrality Mission]]" of keeping its API open and uncompetitive with third parties as an expression of its charter mission. He echoes Boris Cherny's "[[Build For The Model 6 Months Out]]" principle nearly verbatim as his own standing advice, adding a companion quote about models "eating scaffolding for breakfast." Personal notes include his prior work building home-pricing models at [[OpenDoor]] and recommended sci-fi/nonfiction reading.

## Key Points

- OpenAI internal Codex usage: 95% of engineers use it daily, 100% of PRs are Codex-reviewed, and heavy users open ~70% more PRs than light users (a widening gap).
- Uses SICP ("the wizard book") plus Fantasia's Sorcerer's Apprentice as his central metaphor: engineers as wizards casting powerful "spells" via natural-language incantations, who must avoid letting unsupervised agents run wild. See [[Wizard Book Analogy]].
- Management philosophy drawn from *The Mythical Man-Month*'s "surgical team" prediction: spend the majority of time on top performers, proactively clearing organizational blockers. See [[Surgical Team Model]].
- An internal OpenAI team maintaining a 100% Codex-written codebase (no manual "escape hatch") found most agent failures trace to insufficient context/documentation, not model capability — pushing more "tribal knowledge" into repos via docs and skills files.
- "[[One Person Billion Dollar Startup]]": Wu's elaboration of second/third-order effects — a boom in small, bespoke, vertical software startups; a possible "golden age of B2B SaaS"; and a reshaped VC ecosystem with fewer venture-scale outlier bets.
- "[[Business Process Automation Opportunity]]": most of the real economy is repeatable, deterministic business-process work, not open-ended coding-style knowledge work — an opportunity Wu says is systematically underrated by tech/Silicon Valley.
- "[[Bottom-Up AI Adoption]]": enterprise AI deployments need genuine bottom-up champions (often non-engineer "tiger team" members), not just executive mandates, or they risk negative ROI.
- "[[Platform Neutrality Mission]]": OpenAI keeps its API open and uncompetitive with third parties (every model shipped in a first-party product also ships in the API) as a direct expression of its mission to spread AGI's benefits broadly.
- Independently echoes "[[Build For The Model 6 Months Out]]" (Boris Cherny, episode 25) as his own standing product-strategy advice, plus "the models will eat your scaffolding for breakfast" (quoting FinTool founder Nicholas).
- Predicts (12-18 months): models coherently completing multi-hour, eventually full-day tasks; and a major leap in native audio/speech multimodality, an underrated domain relative to coding.
