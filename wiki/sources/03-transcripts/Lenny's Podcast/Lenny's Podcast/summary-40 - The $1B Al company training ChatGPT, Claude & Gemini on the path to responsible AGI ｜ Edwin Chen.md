## Core Summary

Edwin Chen, founder and CEO of Surge AI, shares how his bootstrapped data company reached $1B+ revenue in under 4 years with fewer than 100 people by providing high-quality human data to train frontier AI models like ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini. He argues that the AI industry is optimizing for the wrong objective functions — chasing benchmarks, leaderboards, and engagement metrics rather than truth and genuine human advancement. Chen emphasizes that data quality is not about rote checkboxing but about deep, nuanced judgment akin to taste and sophistication, and he warns that without course correction, we risk building AI that chases dopamine instead of meaningfully advancing humanity.

## Key Points

- Surge AI hit over $1B in revenue with under 100 people, completely bootstrapped with no VC funding, proving that small elite teams can build enormous companies
- The quality of data used to train AI models is the key differentiator — it requires deep understanding of what "quality" means within each domain, not just checking off boxes
- Most AI benchmarks are flawed or gameable, and optimizing for leaderboards like LM Arena often makes models worse at actual accuracy and instruction following
- AI labs face perverse incentives: researchers must climb leaderboards for promotion even when they know it degrades model quality
- Reinforcement learning environments are the next frontier in post-training, simulating messy real-world scenarios where models must act over long time horizons
- AI models will increasingly differentiate based on the values and objective functions chosen by their creators, not just raw intelligence
- Edwin advocates for building companies by staying heads-down and creating something uniquely yours, rather than following the Silicon Valley playbook of fundraising, pivoting, and blitz-scaling
- The industry risks optimizing AI for engagement and dopamine (AI slop) rather than for truth, creativity, and advancing humanity
