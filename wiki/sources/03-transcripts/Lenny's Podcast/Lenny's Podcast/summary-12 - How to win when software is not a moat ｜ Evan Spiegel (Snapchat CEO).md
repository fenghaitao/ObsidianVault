## Core Summary

[[Lenny Rachitsky]] interviews [[Evan Spiegel]], CEO and co-founder of [[Snapchat]], about why consumer social products almost never last and how Snap has stayed durable and inventive for 15 years. Spiegel's central thesis, [[Software Is Not A Moat]]: since software features are trivially cloned, Snap deliberately built harder-to-copy advantages — creator/developer ecosystems, a vertically-integrated AR lens platform, and now hardware ([[Spectacles]]/Specs) — rather than relying on network effects alone. He argues distribution, not product-market fit, is consumer tech's hardest problem today, and predicts AI will keep climbing "up the funnel" from code to strategy, leaving distribution as the one thing AI can't shortcut. The conversation covers the invention of [[Stories]] and screenshot-notification detection, Snap's late (~200-employee) first PM hire and philosophy that designers should own product direction, its intentionally small/flat design team run as a genuine shipping bottleneck (per [[Design As Bottleneck]] and Safi Bahcall's [[Loonshots]] framework), hiring purely on design portfolio, using [[Jobs To Be Done]] thinking to decide where to deploy AI agents, and Snap's self-description as tech's "middle child." Spiegel closes with a contrarian take that humanity (adoption/comfort), not technology, will determine how AI plays out.

## Key Points

- [[Software Is Not A Moat]]: features are easily copied (TikTok, Threads, Meta all cloned Snap inventions); durable moats come from ecosystems/platforms and vertically-integrated hardware, not code alone.
- Distribution, not product-market fit, is the central unsolved problem in consumer tech today — TikTok bought its way to distribution; Threads inherited Meta's; Snap's original edge was connecting people to their closest friends rather than maximizing total network size.
- AI is climbing "up the funnel" (autocomplete → writing code → reviewing/testing → ideas/strategy), which Spiegel argues makes distribution the next moat, since AI can't hand a startup an audience.
- [[Stories]] was invented by synthesizing contradictory user feedback (wanting easy mass-sharing, disliking permanent/judged feeds) into something new — ephemeral, un-metriced, chronological — rather than building the literal feature request.
- Snap's design team is small (9-12 people), flat, and a genuine shipping bottleneck by design — modeled on [[Loonshots]]' theory that companies need both a large hierarchical "franchise" arm and a small flat "innovation" arm in healthy tension.
- Snap waited until ~200 employees to hire its first PM; Spiegel's early view was that designers should own product direction and strategy, not just visuals.
- Uses [[Jobs To Be Done]] thinking to bring order to AI adoption: listing simple jobs for Snapchatters and advertisers, then deciding where agents fit versus where humans stay hands-on.
- Snap frames itself as tech's "middle child" — bigger than Pinterest/Reddit, smaller than Meta/Google — entering a "crucible moment" year with Specs launching and the ad business needing to prove durable profitability.
- Contrarian close: humanity, not technological capability, gates how AI actually gets adopted; expects meaningful societal pushback against AI as it advances.
