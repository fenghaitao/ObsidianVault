## Core Summary

Evan Spiegel, CEO of Snap, explains why Snapchat is one of the only consumer social apps to survive 15 years. The key insight: Snap learned 15 years ago that "software is not a moat" -- a lesson the AI industry is just discovering. Distribution is the hardest thing in consumer tech, and TikTok (money) and Threads (Meta's existing distribution) succeeded because they figured it out. Snap's strategy: build ecosystems (creator relationships, AR lens developers) and hardware (Spectacles, AR glasses) that are far harder to copy than software features. He predicts the next generational consumer companies will be built on new form factors like glasses.

## Key Points

- "Software is not a moat" -- Snap learned this 15 years ago; AI companies are discovering it now
- Distribution is the hardest problem in consumer tech; TikTok solved it with billions in subsidies, Threads with Meta's existing distribution
- Snapchat's early insight: connecting to close friends (not all friends) is where the majority of network value lies
- Snap's strategy against copying: build ecosystems (creators, developers) and hardware that are very hard to clone
- Snapchat Plus hit 25M subscribers, $1B+ revenue run rate -- Instagram just copied it with "Instagram Plus"
- 1B+ monthly active users, $6B+ annual revenue, 8B+ AR lens photos daily
- Predicts a "crucible moment" coming: societal pushback on AI changes, humanity matters more than technology
- Small design team constantly innovating: "If you want to have a good idea, you have to have lots of ideas"
- New form factors (glasses) will create opportunities for the next generational consumer companies
