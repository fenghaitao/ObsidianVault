## Core Summary

[[Lenny Rachitsky]] interviews [[Jason Lemkin]], founder/CEO of [[SaaStr]], about replacing SaaStr's ~10-person go-to-market team (2-3 SDRs, up to 5 AEs) with 1.2 humans (one full-time AE plus [[Amelia]], SaaStr's "Chief AI Officer," at 20% time) and 20 AI agents, after two well-paid salespeople quit on the spot at SaaStr Annual. Net productivity is roughly the same as with the human team — "not better, not worse" — but far more efficient, since the agents scale like software and work nights, weekends, and holidays. Lemkin argues the classic email-cadence SDR and inbound-lead-qualifier roles will be "mostly extinct" within a year, while the best 20% of AEs and leaders will gain superpowers and even command higher pay (he floats $250K/year SDRs who manage 10 agents instead of 10 people). The recurring practical thesis: agents only work if a human personally does the "ingestion, training, QA" loop — uploading data, training on your best person's material, correcting daily mistakes for about 30 days — and picks a vendor willing to provide hands-on "forward deployed engineer" support; vendors that just "turn the product on" fail. Lemkin frames 2026 as a bifurcated market where hyper-growth AI-native companies (Vercel, Replit, 11 Labs) can't service inbound demand fast enough, while legacy SaaS pushes AI for cost efficiency — both ends pushing GTM automation for different reasons.

## Key Points

- SaaStr went from ~10 GTM humans to 1.2 humans + 20 agents after two SDRs quit at SaaStr Annual; Lemkin decided "we're done hiring humans in sales," while stressing this displaced the "midpack and mediocre," not top performers he'd still hire.
- The agent rollout sequence: a general-purpose digital-clone agent ("Deli"/[[Delphi]], transcribed as "Deli" — see note in [[Delphi]]) for support and inbound, then outbound ([[Artisan]]) for lapsed high-value leads, then inbound qualification ([[Qualified]]) which hit a 24/7, weekend-responsive lead within hours of launch, then [[Salesforce]] Agent Force for reactivating leads sales had deprioritized (70% response rate).
- The repeatable training loop: upload your website/wiki/docs, let the agent ingest and "rag/vector" it, then spend roughly an hour a day for ~30 days correcting its mistakes (QA) until it performs like your best salesperson's cloned script — see [[Agent Training Loop]].
- Vendor selection should weight the [[Forward Deployed Engineer]] (implementation/support person) as heavily as feature comparisons; the two vendors that "offered to help the most" (Artisan, Qualified) succeeded where a vendor demanding $100K upfront, and one that was "scared of SaaStr" as a first customer, did not.
- Lemkin's central framing: "the plays all work — outbound, webinars, podcasts, events — it's the playbooks that are broken" because low ROI (not obsolescence) is why old tactics feel dead; see [[Plays Vs Playbooks]].
- 2026 GTM is bifurcated: previously ~3-5% of a category's prospects were "in market" in a given year; in many AI categories that's now over 50%, driving unprecedented simultaneous demand — see [[Everyone In Market At Once]].
- SDR/BDR email-cadence and inbound-qualification roles will be "mostly extinct" within 12 months; AE roles are ~70% safe today but could fall to 40-50% within a year; field/in-person sales and complex enterprise negotiation remain largely AI-resistant for now.
- AI-written outbound emails, once trained on a company's best human template, already beat the median human rep's effort — Lemkin argues most human sales emails are mediocre and untrained AI is compared unfairly against an idealized human standard.
- Managing 20 agents is a new full-time job (Amelia spends 10-15 hours/week reviewing outputs) — the emerging "chief AI/orchestration officer" role, ideally filled by an internal, nerdy, data-loving generalist rather than hired as an outside "GTM engineer" — see [[Chief Orchestration Officer]].
- Lemkin's actionable advice for anyone worried about their job: personally pick one AI agent/tool, deploy and train it yourself for a month (50-60 hours), and you become "hyper employable"; most people, including staff at a $10B+ public company SaaStr consulted for, have never actually done this themselves.
- Long-term vision: today's per-customer agents are siloed; the future ("hive mind," referencing the TV show *Pluribus*) is agents sharing data and learnings across all of a company's customers simultaneously — see [[Hive Mind]].
- AI won't shrink the sales profession overall — winners are growing so fast that even leaner GTM orgs will need more humans in absolute terms — but reps must become "geometrically more productive" (e.g., [[Owner.Com]]'s CEO Kyle is targeting $3-5M revenue per rep, versus $300-500K a few years ago).
