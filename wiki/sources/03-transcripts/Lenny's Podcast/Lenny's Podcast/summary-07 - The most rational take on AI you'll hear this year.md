## Core Summary

Benedict Evans, former a16z partner and independent analyst, argues that AI is "as big a deal as the internet or mobile -- and only as big a deal as the internet or mobile." We're in 1997: most stuff doesn't work yet, most of what people will do hasn't been built, and it's unclear how anything will work when it does. His contrarian take: the job apocalypse narrative is overblown. Every major technology wave automates jobs and creates new ones. AI companies themselves are increasing headcount. The key advice: don't stick your head in the sand with moral superiority about AI being evil -- dive in, understand what you can do with today's tools. The jagged frontier of AI capability means it's not intuitive where it works and doesn't.

## Key Points

- AI is as big as the internet or mobile -- and only as big (not bigger)
- Timeline: we're in "1997" -- very exciting, most stuff doesn't work yet, unclear how it will work
- Job apocalypse is overblown: every tech wave automates and creates jobs; AI companies are increasing headcount
- "People who say every big company will buy ChatGPT and fire everyone in two weeks are morons"
- You can't predict which jobs will be exposed: "you can't look at a senior partner at a law firm and say 17% of their work could be automated -- this is horseshit"
- Spreadsheet analogy: accountants seeing spreadsheets in late 70s was mind-blowing but didn't eliminate accounting
- Only 15-20% of 13-18 year olds are daily AI users; 60% don't use it at all
- Jagged frontier: where AI works and doesn't work is not intuitive
- Advice: don't stick your head in the sand; dive in and understand what you can do today
