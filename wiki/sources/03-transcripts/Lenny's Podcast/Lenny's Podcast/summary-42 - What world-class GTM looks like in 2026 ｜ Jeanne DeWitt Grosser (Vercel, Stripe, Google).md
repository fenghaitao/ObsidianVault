## Core Summary

Jeanne DeWitt Grosser (COO at Vercel, former CPO at Stripe) argues that world-class go-to-market in 2026 requires treating GTM like a product — designing a unique, human buying experience at every touchpoint — and leveraging AI agents via the emerging "GTM engineer" role to automate repetitive workflows, enabling sales teams to operate 10x faster. She emphasizes that as products commoditize, the experience of being sold to becomes a key differentiator, and that 80% of customers buy to avoid pain or reduce risk rather than to increase upside. The conversation covers practical tactics including segmentation frameworks, the build-vs-buy calculus for GTM tools, pricing strategy, and how to build a sales org that engineers and product teams respect.

## Key Points

- GTM encompasses every function that touches a customer or makes a dollar: marketing, sales, technical sales roles, customer success, support, and partnerships — not just sales and marketing.
- The rise of the GTM engineer (GTMEN) role enables companies to build AI agents that automate sales workflows (inbound lead qualification, outbound prospecting, deal analysis) with dramatic cost and speed improvements — e.g., one agent replaced 10 SDRs on inbound while maintaining conversion rates.
- Build-vs-buy is shifting: internal AI agents are often cheap and fast to build (~$1,000/year to run, built in weeks) and can be more effective than off-the-shelf tools because they capture esoteric company context.
- Gong transcripts fed into AI agents (e.g., "Dealbot," "Lostbot") reveal true reasons for lost deals (e.g., failure to demonstrate value vs. price) and provide real-time coaching to sales reps.
- Segmentation should go beyond size (SMB/Mid-Market/Enterprise) to include growth potential, traffic/usage (e.g., CrUX rank), and workload type (e.g., e-commerce vs. crypto vs. SaaS), forming a multi-axis framework.
- PLG is still relevant but has a ceiling; most companies eventually need to layer in a sales-assisted or sales-led motion to sustain growth and increase deal sizes.
- Pricing should be treated like a product: align value delivery with cost structure, avoid premium pricing as a default, and ensure the pricing model reflects how customers actually derive value.
- Sales hiring benefits from a diversified portfolio: mix traditional salespeople with consultants/bankers to create a richer learning environment blending sales skills with analytical rigor.
- The best sales orgs are equal parts revenue-driving and R&D — they translate customer feedback into actionable product roadmap signal, earning credibility with engineering teams.
- 80% of customers buy to avoid pain or reduce risk, not to increase upside; enterprise buyers are especially motivated by career risk avoidance.
