## Core Summary

Eric Ries, author of The Lean Startup, returns with his new book Incorruptible about why good companies go bad. The core thesis: success itself becomes a liability as the "force that no one controls but everyone obeys" drags organizations toward mediocrity. He uses Anthropic as a case study: Dario Amodei embedded safety into the corporate charter with a trust of AI safety experts who can appoint board members without equity, creating structural protection against corruption. The pattern is pervasive -- founders get ousted, brands get ruined by private equity cost-cutting, and even restaurant food quality becomes a casualty. The solution: "harder is easier" -- principled decisions that seem harder in the short term create unexpected long-term rewards.

## Key Points

- The Lean Startup was about building success; Incorruptible is about protecting what you've built
- Anthropic's charter: for-profit board members appointed by a trust of independent AI safety experts with no equity
- "Whenever you see Anthropic do the right thing, think about how much that's costing them"
- The pattern: success creates temptation to extract value, quality drops, customers and employees revolt, market share shrinks
- "Harder is easier": principled decisions create unexpected rewards; most leaders can't defend their principles because they've been taught ROI-based thinking
- People can "taste" ownership structure -- private equity takeovers are detectable in food quality
- AI companies are actually operating exactly like Lean Startup: ship MVP (research preview), get feedback, iterate
- "The more golden the goose, the greater the temptation to butcher"
