## Core Summary

Dan Shipper, CEO of Every, makes bold predictions about how AI will change work. He argues work will bifurcate into two modes: everyone will have at least one agent they delegate to (like OpenClaw), and most work will happen in environments like Codex or Claude Co-work that become the operating system. Key contrarian takes: the AI job apocalypse is not real, PMs and full-stack designers are super bullish, the SaaS apocalypse is "dumb" (agents increase SaaS users, not eliminate them), and CLIs are already over. The core human skill is creativity: models commoditize yesterday's competence, humans use that frozen competence to make something new.

## Key Points

- "The AI job apocalypse is not really a thing. I am super bullish on PMs and full-stack designers"
- Work will bifurcate: (1) everyone has an agent to delegate to, (2) most work happens in Codex/Claude Co-work as the OS
- "Automation is a lie. Every agent needs a human. We have so much automation and I also work way more"
- "The SaaS apocalypse is dumb. I would buy SaaS stocks right now. Agents increase the number of users of SaaS"
- CLIs are over: "We speed ran the CLI era. It was nice while it lasted, but I think CLIs are over"
- Models make yesterday's human competence cheap; humans use that to make something new and interesting
- Every doubled in size in the past year (15 to 30 people) despite being extremely AI-forward
- The "reach test": do you reach for the AI tool organically when you wake up?
- Predictions to be reviewed May 2027
