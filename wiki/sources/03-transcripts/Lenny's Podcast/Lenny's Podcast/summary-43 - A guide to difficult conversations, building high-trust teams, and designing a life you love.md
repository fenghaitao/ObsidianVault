## Core Summary

[[Lenny Rachitsky]] interviews [[Rachel Lockett]], an executive coach and former longtime HR leader at [[Pinterest]] and [[Stripe]], who works with CEOs, founders, and leaders on emotional/positive intelligence, resilience, courage, and building trusted, accountable teams. The conversation is built around live coaching demonstrations (Rachel coaches Lenny on-air twice) and covers a tight set of practical frameworks: the "[[GROW Model]]" and "[[Active Listening Three Levels|three levels of active listening]]" for coaching direct reports instead of always giving answers; "[[Non-Violent Communication (NVC)|non-violent communication]]" (observations → feelings → needs → request) for difficult conversations; the "[[Zone Of Genius]]" framework for identifying and designing around a person's natural gifts (targeting ~80% of work time in one's strengths) as the core antidote to burnout; Stripe's "[[Enthusiastic Rehire Test]]" for talent decisions; Alpine Investors' "[[One-Page Plan]]" (People First Operating Rhythm) for company-wide strategic alignment; and a set of "[[Co-Founder Relationship Practices]]" (vows, quarterly check-ins, personality-typing tools) for the notoriously under-supported co-founder relationship, which Lockett notes causes an estimated 65% of startup failures. Lockett's overarching thesis: most leaders (especially technical ones) over-rely on having all the answers, which trains their teams to bring every hard problem back to them instead of building their own capacity — and that professionals are not purely logical, so ignoring the emotional dimension of work relationships is itself a source of dysfunction and burnout.

## Key Points

- **Coach vs. advise**: leaders default to advising because they've been rewarded for having answers, but this trains teams to bring every hard problem back up the chain rather than developing their own judgment. Advise only when there's genuine urgency or a real skills gap; otherwise coach.
- **[[GROW Model]]**: four categories of coaching questions — Goal (what does success look like?), Reality (where are you stuck today?), Options (what paths could you take?), Way forward (what will you do next?) — used non-linearly, based on where the person actually needs help.
- **[[Active Listening Three Levels]]**: Level 1 (internal — listening through your own inner dialogue), Level 2 (focused — able to repeat back the words), Level 3 (global — hearing beneath the words: tone, body language, context, unspoken emotion). Great leaders operate at Level 3 when coaching, influencing, or pitching a vision.
- **[[Non-Violent Communication (NVC)]]**: Marshall Rosenberg's four-step framework for difficult conversations — Observations (a fact, no interpretation) → Feelings (a genuine emotion, not "I feel like you're being a jerk") → Needs (the underlying universal human need) → Request (a small, achievable olive branch, not a demand). The goal of any conflict is mutual understanding, not convincing the other person they're wrong.
- **[[Zone Of Genius]]**: people have more energy when operating in their natural gifts; Lockett's aspirational target is 80% of work time spent in one's zone of genius. Practical exercises: track the 5 things that gave you energy and the 5 that depleted it every night for two weeks; ask 5-10 people what your core strengths are; review your calendar for patterns of dread vs. excitement. Burnout is often a "slow leak" from spending too much time outside this zone, not simply overwork.
- **[[Enthusiastic Rehire Test]]**: Stripe's talent bar — "would you enthusiastically rehire this person for the same role?" A "no" doesn't mean immediate termination, but it does mean something must change (role change, performance plan, or exit).
- **[[One-Page Plan]]** (Alpine Investors' "People First Operating Rhythm," which Lockett has adapted in her own coaching practice): a single-page structure with vision/values in column one, strategic intentions/KPIs in column two, annual goals in column three, and quarterly goals in column four — so every prioritization conversation stays tied to the company's core strategy and vision. Paired with a regular cadence (quarterly reflection, "what's an inconvenient truth?") of stepping off "the dance floor" onto "the balcony" to reflect on what's working.
- **[[Co-Founder Relationship Practices]]**: co-founder conflict causes an estimated 65% of startup failures, yet gets almost no structural support (unlike a marriage, which has couples therapy as a normalized institution). Recommends: personality-typing tools (Lockett uses the Enneagram) to build shared language for role tendencies (e.g., CTOs skew skeptical/fact-seeking, CEOs skew optimistic/vision-selling — an inherent productive tension); explicit "co-founder vows" defining how the partners will work together, make decisions, and handle conflict; a regular cadence of dedicated relationship time (biweekly dinners, quarterly in-person check-ins) distinct from day-to-day business operations.
- Ties several frameworks together with recurring guests/concepts: Jerry Colonna's line "how are you complicit in creating the conditions you claim you don't want?"; the Stanford "Touchy Feely" course associated with Carole Robin; Alpine Investors' Graham Weaver (a prior podcast guest); and Netflix CTO Elizabeth Stone's similar use of the "enthusiastic rehire" framing.
- **Career-design examples**: cites Rahul Vohra (Superhuman) hiring a president to offload operational work he wasn't suited for, and HubSpot co-founder Dharmesh Shah's rule of never having direct reports — both framed as deliberate zone-of-genius design choices at the founder level.
- Personal anecdote: Lockett's own career pivot from product management (at a company called Remind) to executive coaching, prompted by colleague Zach Abrams telling her her "zone of genius" was people, not product strategy — Zach went on to lead product at Coinbase, Brex, and Bridge (acquired by Stripe), and remains her client.
- AI-in-coaching: uses Granola to take session notes and track patterns across a client relationship over time; uses ChatGPT to brainstorm retreat activities; is building (not yet shipped) an AI assistant with full client context (development plan, session notes, coaching frameworks) to give clients light-touch, between-session tactical support without replacing the deeper work of personal coaching.
- Lightning round: recommends *The 15 Commitments of Conscious Leadership* and *Designing Your Life* (Bill Burnett); enjoyed *K-pop Demon Hunters* with her daughter; loves Loom for scaling human-feeling training content; life motto is a Joseph Campbell quote about the path being one you make yourself, not one already laid out; reads Roald Dahl (*Witches*, *Matilda*) with her kids.
