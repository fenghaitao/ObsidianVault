## Core Summary

Stewart Butterfield, founder of Flickr and Slack, shares his mental models for building products people love. He argues that product success comes from prioritizing comprehension over friction reduction, developing taste and craft as competitive advantages, and obsessing over not making users think. The ultimate measure of success, he insists, is the amount of value created for customers, and leaders must actively supply their teams with "known valuable work to do" to avoid the trap of hyperrealistic work-like activities.

## Key Points

- Utility curves (S-curves) help teams assess whether a feature needs more investment to reach the "magic threshold" of value, or has already hit diminishing returns
- The goal of product design should be "don't make me think" — reducing cognitive load is more important than reducing clicks or friction
- Comprehension, not friction, is the real challenge for most products: users need to understand what your product is and what to do next
- "Owner's delusion" causes builders to overestimate how much attention and intent users bring to their product
- Taste can be developed and creates a critical competitive advantage — most companies don't invest in it
- "Tilting your umbrella" — small acts of courtesy and empathy in product design build emotional connections that drive word-of-mouth growth
- Parkinson's Law: work expands to fill the time available, and organizations naturally accumulate administrators
- "Hyperrealistic work-like activities" are superficially identical to real work but create no value; it's a leader's responsibility to supply known valuable work
- "We Don't Sell Saddles Here" — you must create the market and communicate the outcome, not just build the product
- Pivoting requires coldly rational decision-making after exhausting all realistic possibilities, despite the emotional humiliation
- Generosity in business (fair billing, employee-friendly policies, SLA credits) is both ethical and game-theoretically advantageous
- "In the long run, the measure of our success will be the amount of value that we create for customers"
