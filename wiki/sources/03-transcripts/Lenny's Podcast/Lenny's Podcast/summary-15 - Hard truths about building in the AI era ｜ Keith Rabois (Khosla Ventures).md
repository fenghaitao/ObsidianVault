## Core Summary

[[Lenny Rachitsky]] interviews [[Keith Rabois]] ([[Khosla Ventures]] Managing Director; former [[PayPal]], [[Square]], [[LinkedIn]] executive; early investor in [[Stripe]], [[Palantir]], [[Airbnb]], [[DoorDash]], [[Ramp]]) on talent, team-building, and a series of deliberately contrarian management/investing takes. Rabois frames "the team you build is the company you build" (learned from [[Vinod Khosla]]) as the first-principle behind everything else, and introduces "[[Barrels And Ammunition]]" — the idea that only a small number of people in any organization can independently drive an initiative from inception to success, so hiring more people without adding "barrels" just adds coordination tax. He advocates building around "[[Undiscovered Talent]]" rather than competing for already-proven hires, "[[Criticize In Public]]" as a management philosophy (explicitly rejecting psychological safety), and treats CEO complacency-offsetting as the core executive job. On AI and careers, he argues traditional PM roles no longer make sense and that PM, design, and engineering are converging on CEO-like judgment as the durable skill. His investing philosophy is purely founder-driven, uses "[[Accumulating Advantages]]" as a due-diligence question, and treats being laughed at by fellow VCs (the "[[Ugly Baby]]" heuristic) as a positive signal. He identifies early-stage company tempo/speed as his strongest predictive success signal.

## Key Points

- "The team you build is the company you build" (via [[Vinod Khosla]]) — talent density, not market/product/technology, is the root cause of startup success or failure.
- "[[Barrels And Ammunition]]": a small number of people ("barrels," often 2-17 per company) can independently drive initiatives to completion; extra hires without extra barrels only add coordination tax.
- "[[Undiscovered Talent]]": startups can't win bidding wars for already-proven hires, so the real edge is correctly evaluating people the market's "black box" hasn't correctly priced yet (often skewing younger, with less career "data").
- Hates talking to customers for consumer/SMB products (though not enterprise) — believes it's actively harmful, since customer feedback on subconscious purchase/behavior decisions is frequently misleading; cites Airbnb and DoorDash as founder-insight-driven, not survey-driven, businesses.
- "[[Criticize In Public]]": gives negative feedback to team members in front of colleagues, not privately — explicitly rejects psychological safety as a value ("high-performance machines... are about winning").
- CEO's core job is offsetting organizational complacency — push harder specifically when things are going well, since success breeds complacency and talented people get demotivated when "skating."
- Believes traditional PM roles "make no sense" going forward; PM/design/engineering converge on CEO-like judgment ("what are we building and why") as the durable skill, with business acumen as the key differentiator.
- Investing is purely founder-driven; asks whether a founder can articulate a path to durable "[[Accumulating Advantages]]," even unproven; the "[[Ugly Baby]]" heuristic treats being laughed at by fellow VCs as a positive signal.
- Early-stage company tempo/speed (time between identifying a problem and shipping a fix) is his strongest predictive signal of eventual success — cites Square, Fair, and Ramp.
- Favors internal promotion over hiring senior outside executives for "value creation" roles, reserving external senior hires for "value preservation" roles.
