## Core Summary

Keith Rabois, PayPal Mafia member and Khosla Ventures MD, shares hard truths about building teams and companies in the AI era. The core framework is "barrels and ammunition": barrels are the rare people who can drive an initiative from inception to success independently, and most companies have only 2-17 of them. Hiring more ammunition without expanding barrels just adds coordination tax. He argues for building companies on undiscovered talent, pushing harder when winning (complacency is the enemy), and that the CEO's single role is offsetting complacency. He also predicts the PM role will evolve into a CEO-like function of "what are we building and why."

## Key Points

- "The team you build is the company you build" (Vinod Khosla)
- Barrels and ammunition: barrels are people who can independently drive an outcome; ammunition supports them. PayPal had 12-17 barrels out of 254 people; a large company might have just 2
- To identify undiscovered talent, understand why standard hiring processes would miss them -- younger people have less data, making them harder for homogeneous evaluation systems
- Ask candidates: "If you were CEO, what would you have done differently?" to test strategic mindset
- Do 20+ references on senior hires; don't stop until you hit a negative reference
- 30-day feedback loop: ask yourself if you'd make the same hire decision again
- Push harder when winning, not when losing. When losing, be supportive. Success breeds complacency
- "High performance machines don't have psychological safety -- they're about winning"
- The best CEOs apply "relentless application of force" (Mike Moritz)
- Hasn't used a computer since 2010 -- runs everything from iPad, following Steve Jobs' lead
- PM role makes no sense in the future; the skill is being a CEO: "what are we building and why?"
