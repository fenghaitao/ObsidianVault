## Core Summary

Cat Wu, head of product for Claude Code and Co-work at Anthropic, reveals how Anthropic's product team ships faster than anyone else. The key is removing every barrier to shipping: features go from idea to users' hands in a week, timelines dropped from 6 months to 1 month to sometimes 1 day. The approach combines research previews (low-commitment launches for feedback), rigorous weekly metrics readouts so everyone understands the business, and tight cross-functional processes where marketing and DevRel can turn around announcements the next day. The PM role is evolving toward setting clear goals, creating repeatable shipping processes, and developing product taste -- as code becomes cheaper, deciding what to write becomes more valuable.

## Key Points

- Anthropic's product timelines went from 6 months → 1 month → sometimes 1 day
- Research previews: ship everything as an early, unbranded experiment to reduce commitment and get fast feedback
- Weekly metrics readouts with the entire team so everyone understands the business and can make decisions independently
- PRDs are used only for particularly ambiguous or infrastructure-heavy features; most features ship without them
- The PM role: "figuring out the path from where we are today to the vision 3-6 months from now"
- Cat and Boris Cherny are "80% mind meld" with blurred lines between PM and tech lead
- Product taste is the emerging critical skill: as code becomes cheaper, deciding what to build becomes more valuable
- Claude Code source code leak was a human error (human working with Claude on a PR, passed two layers of review)
- The hardest thing: "figuring out for the current model, how do you elicit the maximum capability"
