## Core Thesis

Jason Cohen, four-time founder of two unicorns (including WP Engine), presents a diagnostic framework for stalled product growth organized as a sequence of five questions that must be addressed in order: (1) Are customers leaving (logo churn)? (2) Is pricing and positioning correct? (3) Are existing customers growing (NRR)? (4) Are acquisition channels saturated? (5) Do you even need to grow? The framework reveals that churn sets a hard mathematical ceiling on maximum company size, pricing selects the market rather than simply following a demand curve, and the root of all sustainable growth is creating genuine customer value and then splitting it with them.

## Key Takeaways

1. **Logo churn is the worst problem** because cancellations grow automatically with company size (exponentially), while marketing grows only linearly. Formula: `max customers = new customers per month / cancellation rate`. A company adding 100 customers/month with 5% churn will never exceed 2,000 customers.

2. **"What made you cancel?" gets 2x more usable responses** than "Why did you cancel?" (Groove case study). Multiple-choice cancellation surveys are noise — randomize the list and all options are picked equally. "Too expensive" is almost never the real reason.

3. **Pricing selects the market.** Raising prices often doesn't change signup rates because you enter a different market segment. The microeconomic demand curve doesn't apply — cheaper isn't always better. A product priced too low looks cheap and inadequate to mid-market/enterprise buyers.

4. **Positioning can 8x your price.** The Double Down example: same product pitched as "cut AdWords costs in half" vs "double your leads." Selling growth (what the CEO values) commands far higher prices than selling savings.

5. **NRR must be >100% for public SaaS companies.** Median NRR at IPO is 119%. Only 2 of 100+ public SaaS companies have NRR below 100%. The math: a 20% loss from churn requires a 25% gain from upgrades just to break even.

6. **Marketing channels are elephant curves, not S-curves.** They decline after saturation. You can't just flog the marketing department — you need creative new channels (like Constant Contact's in-person workshops) or entirely new products.

7. **Onboarding is the highest-leverage point.** 50% of users drop off in the first 30 seconds (like YouTube retention). A small improvement at the front creates outsized gains at the end. Early churn is also super unprofitable because you never recoup acquisition costs.

8. **AB testing doesn't work on important things.** You can't AB test strategy, vision, or insights. Most mundane AB test results are false positives. Even Shopify's 100-person team sees a third of their positive test results disappear over time.

9. **"If you're not growing, you're dying" may apply to the person, not the company.** Sometimes stasis is fine (37 Signals). Sometimes you need to change jobs, sell the company, or start something new. The question is existential, not just financial.
