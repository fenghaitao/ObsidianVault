## Core Summary

[[Lenny Rachitsky]] interviews [[Jason Cohen]], four-time founder (two unicorns, including [[WP Engine]]) and 20-year blogger, on his sequential "[[Growth Stall Diagnostic Framework]]" for diagnosing (and reigniting) stalled product growth. The framework runs strictly in order: (1) [[Logo Churn Ceiling|logo churn]] — the worst problem, since cancellations compound as a percentage while marketing only grows linearly, creating a hard ceiling on company size; (2) [[Pricing Selects The Market|pricing/positioning]] — most companies underprice by default, and price determines which market segment buys at all, not just how many; (3) [[NRR Compounding Fallacy|net revenue retention]] — percentage losses and gains don't cancel arithmetically, so healthy NRR can mask an unsustainable logo-churn problem; (4) [[Elephant Curve|channel saturation]] — acquisition channels don't just plateau, they eventually sag, so genuinely new channels (not incremental tweaks) are often required; (5) whether the company/founder actually needs to grow at all, reframed as an existential rather than mechanical question. He also shares a contrarian take on why most A/B testing fails ([[AB Testing Skepticism]]), corroborated by a Shopify anecdote about vanishing effects under holdout testing.

## Key Points

- [[Logo Churn Ceiling]]: max sustainable customers = new customers/month ÷ churn rate; churn compounds with company size while marketing growth doesn't, so churn always eventually caps growth. Use open-ended survey wording ("what made you cancel?") and reject "too expensive"/"project ended" as true root causes — see [[Groove]] case study (10%→20% response rate from a wording change alone).
- [[Pricing Selects The Market]]: raising prices often doesn't reduce signups (and can increase them) because too-low pricing signals low quality to larger buyers who were never truly addressable at that price; the "Double Down" example shows the same product can earn 8x revenue purely from reframing pricing around what the buyer already values (growth) rather than cost savings.
- [[NRR Compounding Fallacy]]: a 20% loss needs a 25% gain to break even; NRR above 100% doesn't guarantee health if logo churn is severe enough — median SaaS IPO NRR is 119%, and companies below 100% NRR have poor outcomes.
- [[Elephant Curve]]: acquisition channels rise, plateau, then sag (not a simple S-curve) — cites magazine circulation and conference attendance as channels that looked healthy right before collapsing; recommends genuinely new channels (Constant Contact's in-person workshops, HubSpot's and WP Engine's agency channels) over incremental tweaks.
- Final question — do you need to grow? — sometimes no (bootstrapped, profit-maximizing companies like 37signals are a legitimate choice), but for builder-type founders, prolonged stagnation may itself be the real problem.
- [[AB Testing Skepticism]]: most A/B testing fails to test what matters (strategy/vision) and produces mostly false positives on smaller tests; corroborated by a Shopify CTO's admission that ~a third of measured effects vanish under holdout-group re-testing.
- Root philosophy tying it together: create genuine value for the customer, measure it (even qualitatively), and only then decide how to split that value via price — most growth problems trace back to this being unclear or undelivered.
