# Summary: The real AI revolution isn't software. It's farms, mines, and trucks. ｜ Qasar Younis

## Core Thesis

The real impact of AI in the next 5-10 years will be in **physical AI** — farming, mining, construction, trucking, and defense — not just software. These industries face severe labor shortages (aging farmers averaging late 50s, unfilled dangerous jobs) and autonomy is coming just in time. Qasar Younis argues that AI fear stems from misunderstanding the technology's limitations, and the net effect on humanity will be overwhelmingly positive, akin to the Industrial Revolution.

## Key Topics

### 1. Physical AI and the Future of Industry
- Applied Intuition is a $15B company that adds AI to vehicles: cars, tractors, planes, submarines, mining rigs, and military systems.
- 18 of the top 20 automakers are customers, plus the largest construction, mining, and trucking companies, and the Department of Defense.
- The real AI revolution will be in legacy physical industries, not just consumer software.
- Farmers average age is late 50s — in 10-20 years, many will retire with no replacement workforce.
- Mining, trucking, and construction are dangerous jobs people don't want. Autonomy fills the gap.

### 2. AI Optimism and the Industrial Revolution Parallel
- Net suffering in humanity should go down significantly because of AI, just as the Industrial Revolution brought broad access to healthcare, goods, and services.
- Self-driving cars will save 30,000+ lives per year in the US alone.
- Free or near-free mobility transforms lives — e.g., someone in Rwanda 2 hours from a hospital.
- Criticism of AI often comes from people living in very comfortable existences who don't see the benefits for those at the margins.

### 3. AI Fear = Misunderstanding
- The core root of AI fear is misunderstanding. Understanding the technology reveals its limitations.
- Example: nunchuck-wielding humanoid robots are pre-programmed motors, not sentient beings. Factory robots have been doing complex tasks for 25 years without generating anxiety.
- The human "monkey brain" fills gaps in understanding with fear — the same instinct that made ancestors fear rustling bushes.
- Advice: spend time learning about AI, see its edges, then actively make the technology be used for good.

### 4. Public Market AI Sell-off vs. Societal AI Anxiety
- These are two different things driven by different motivations.
- Hedge fund managers test AI by asking consultants to build apps in a week, then extrapolate that AI will disrupt billion-dollar companies. This drives sell-offs.
- The market overreacts because it doesn't understand the depth of existing products (integrations, edge cases, etc.).
- Qasar's view: public investors don't have the edge you'd assume — they're buying narratives, not doing deep technical analysis.

### 5. Autonomous Vehicles: L2++ vs. L4
- **Tesla/FSD approach (L2++):** Fewer sensors, no HD maps, cheaper compute. Available everywhere.
- **Waymo approach (L4):** Many sensors, HD maps, geo-fenced. Better in constrained areas.
- In 5-7 years, both technologies will be ubiquitous globally. L2++ will become near-free, like nav systems became free with CarPlay/Android Auto.
- Full autonomy will become expected in every car, leading to clear decreases in injuries and deaths.

### 6. Staying Quiet: "Our Best Work is Done Alone and Quietly"
- Qasar intentionally stayed under the radar for ~10 years, focusing on product and customers rather than public presence.
- "Every minute you're writing something for public consumption, you're not focusing your very limited time on your customers and your product."
- He could do this because he was already known in the ecosystem (YC COO, personal network).
- Only recently joined Twitter/X at the urging of Marc Andreessen and Elad Gil to propagate ideas about physical AI and societal change.
- Pragmatic advice: if you don't have a network, building a public following can be a powerful tool for recruiting, investors, and customers.

### 7. Radical Pragmatism and Company Values
- All of Applied Intuition's values reduce to two words: **radical pragmatism**.
- Core values: move fast and move safe, never disappoint the customer, technical mastery, high output matters, laugh a lot, half the work is follow-up.
- Values were derived from observing what made them successful, then codified. Managers are assessed and compensated on adherence to values.
- The company has never spent any money it raised — profitable/sustainable for nearly 10 years with 1000+ engineers.

### 8. China as a Non-Market Competitor
- China cannot be compared apples-to-apples with American companies. Huawei is effectively an extension of the Chinese state, not a profit-driven company.
- ~25% of Huawei employees are Communist Party members. The name "Huawei" means "China's ambition."
- Chinese EV companies are lauded, but they operate without profit constraints. Rivian makes great products but gets hammered by markets for losing money.
- The comparison is fundamentally unfair — American companies are assessed as businesses; Chinese counterparts are not.
- Qasar's view: open and free markets mean everyone can succeed. China's system is different, but that doesn't mean an antagonistic relationship is necessary.

### 9. Founder Advice: Traction, Taste, and Craft
- **Traction:** Good companies tend to show traction early and sustain it for a decade+. If after 2 years the market isn't giving you a more specific path, consider resetting — the foundation (co-founders, market, life phase) may be wrong.
- **First startup as practice:** Treat your first startup as learning the craft. The first table you build will be wobbly. Most successful founders succeed on their 2nd or 3rd attempt.
- **Taste:** Many Silicon Valley CEOs lack taste because they've had narrow life experiences. Never having been an employee, never having backpacked, never having been at the bottom of a large org — these limit judgment.
- **Reading old books:** Time filters out noise. Read pillars of human ideas (philosophy, history, biographies). Qasar recommends: "The Emperor of All Maladies," "Made in America" (Sam Walton), "Guns, Germs, and Steel," "My American Journey" (Colin Powell), "SPQR."
- **Maintenance as craft:** The score takes care of itself. Focus on hygiene, follow-up, and the system. "Half the work is follow-up."

### 10. Listening to Naysayers and Decision-Making
- Create a culture where the best idea wins, regardless of who proposes it. Everyone must speak up.
- Large companies fail because momentum in one direction overwhelms signals that the market is changing.
- Google vs. Facebook example: Google couldn't win social because "a gorilla can't learn to fly by not being a gorilla."
- Emotions are generally not helpful in making rational decisions. Remove the emotional filter to see the "raw image" of the decision.
- But once a decision is made, execute decisively. "Move fast, move safe" holds these tensions: be open AND make decisions quickly.

### 11. AGI and Consciousness
- Qasar is skeptical that AGI is just more intelligence. Consciousness may require emotions, which are far more complex than input-output associations.
- "Technical mastery is mastering the complex; creativity is mastering the simple."
- He acknowledges he could be wrong about AI art (willing to "eat his words").
