## Core Summary

[[Lenny Rachitsky]] interviews [[Max Schoening]], Head of Product at [[Notion]] (formerly PM at Google, design/engineering leader at [[GitHub]] and [[Heroku]]), about how AI is reshaping product-building roles. Schoening's central argument: as AI collapses the cost of implementation, the skill that separates people who thrive from people who stall is not technical ability or job title but **agency** — the disposition to believe the world is malleable and to just make things. He traces Notion's own history of designers and PMs learning to prototype (and increasingly ship) in code, argues for **[[Malleable Software]]** (software that bends to users' interests rather than the vendor's), and lays out Notion's internal product philosophy — "obviously good," "shots on goal," "incremental correctness," and a wariness of "feature creep" in favor of finding each product's one exceptional "tiny core." He also covers taste as a trainable, iterative skill; skepticism about the "SaaS apocalypse" narrative; why token spend shouldn't be a bragging metric; and a closing riff that knowledge work already functions as a kind of universal basic income.

## Key Points

- **Agency over skill/title**: "Before it was very easy to always say, well, I will never be able to do this because [skill issue]... even if you have the skills at your fingertips, the thing that matters is agency. I don't think agency is very evenly distributed in the world." Schoening's advice for building it: just start making things (tinkering, side projects, a "home-cooked meal is a form of tinkering") rather than waiting to be granted permission inside "the machine."
- **Notion's prototyping-in-code origin story**: Schoening and another designer built a deliberately minimal, LLM-friendly "playground" codebase (inspired by Bret Victor's "Stop Drawing Dead Fish") to prototype chat UIs instead of static Figma mockups — the seed of Notion's designers/PMs now writing code, and increasingly contributing to production.
- **Coding for understanding, not for shipping**: Schoening explicitly does not care whether designers/PMs ship code to production — the value of coding is that it forces you to "interrogate the material" (understanding agent loops, not just tweaking UI styles), so you become "a master of the material" rather than "a cog in the delivery mechanism."
- **Role collapse has a cost**: merging engineer/designer/PM roles risks losing specialists and craft/delight at the edges — Schoening uses a hardware-prototyping metaphor (3D-printed prototypes with visible layer lines vs. a precision-manufactured product) to argue "engineering" is now about making something work reliably for 100M+ people, not about how many tokens/features you can ship.
- **Malleable software**: software should work closer to the interest of the people using it than the corporation that made it; most consumer software today is an inflexible, vendor-designed "little square" with no ability to adapt it to your needs. AI plus a platform built for extensibility (Schoening cites [[Ink & Switch]] and [[Jeffrey Litt]]'s work) can let ordinary people reclaim ownership over their computing lives.
- **The "SaaS apocalypse" is overstated**: most people don't want to maintain a full software stack themselves — "software is like a garden" ([[Brett Taylor]]) requiring ongoing tending, and "as a service" pricing is really paying for that maintenance/specialization. Schoening expects general-purpose tools (word processor, spreadsheet, Notion-as-OS) to become more malleable via AI, while specialized tools (security, compliance) remain "as a service."
- **First 10% now free, last 10% still 90% of the work**: AI has made early-stage exploration (demos, first drafts, PRDs-as-prototypes) essentially free, enabling "shots on goal" — sending multiple agents to explore multiple directions in parallel — but the final polish/reliability layer remains the hardest part.
- **Automation vs. augmentation, and a "retina display" ceiling on intelligence**: Schoening is skeptical that ever-smarter frontier models matter for most knowledge work once a "good enough" threshold is reached (like pixels below the resolution of human perception); he's more interested in faster inference and better UX ("exoskeleton") than in raw intelligence gains.
- **Token spend should not be a status metric**: comparing it to bragging about lines of code written; leaderboards (he cites [[Meta]] getting "flack" for one) risk making volume the goal instead of value delivered — echoes the [[Token Budgeting]] theme from other episodes.
- **Tiny core theory of great products**: every great product (GitHub's pull request, Heroku's `git push heroku master`, Dropbox's menu-bar sync icon, Notion's blocks/slash commands, multi-touch on the iPhone) has one exceptionally good, narrow "superpower" — chasing "one more feature" to make a mediocre product finally great "never works."
- **Being first doesn't matter; being right does** — cites AirPods (not the first Bluetooth headphones) and Anthropic (started after OpenAI, less funded, "did the same thing twice" successfully under Dario Amodei) as evidence that durability and quality beat first-mover advantage.
- **Taste is trained like a model**: taste is the ability to run an internal "virtual machine" that predicts how a specific in-group will react to an idea; it's built through repeated cycles of making something, getting feedback, and iterating — analogous to training/backpropagation. Best built via side projects, tinkering, and constant exposure to other people's ideas.
- **Notion's internal quality practices**: "obviously good" (a physical sticker/bar — you know it when you see it, like the first iPhone or ChatGPT), "incremental correctness" (get very good at iterating rather than launching in isolation), and "shots on goal" (increase the number of experiments since experimentation is now cheap) — balanced against a real struggle with proliferating, redundant "automation primitives" that need periodic consolidation.
- **Jobs to be done, used loosely**: Schoening uses the JTBD framing mainly as a discipline to ask "would you actually buy/use the thing you just built," countering the tendency of teams to lose the user's-eye view once they're emotionally invested in what they made.
- **Notion AI agent's success**: attributed to Notion functioning as a connected, permissioned workspace/"operating system" that gives agents rich context to roam — described as resembling the relationship between coding agents and a Unix-like environment.
- **Closing riff on UBI**: "We already have universal basic income. It's called knowledge work" — a semi-joking claim that most knowledge work is not strictly economically necessary and that humanity will keep inventing reasons to stay "in the loop" with agents regardless.
