## Core Summary

Max Schoening, head of product at Notion, argues that in the AI era, agency matters more than job titles. With AI removing skill barriers, the differentiator is whether someone sees the world as malleable and acts on it. He describes how Notion designers and PMs started coding by building a playground that made AI-friendly prototyping approachable, and how this evolved into production contributions. The key skill is not coding per se, but understanding the material you're designing with -- currently code, increasingly agent loops. He warns against losing specialists in the rush to merge roles, and emphasizes that taste (predicting what an in-group will like) is developed through reps, like training a model.

## Key Points

- "Could you drive Notion like it's stolen?" -- agency is about treating the world as malleable
- Agency is not evenly distributed; those who have it will thrive, those who stick to defined roles will struggle
- The first 10% of every project is now free -- it takes almost no effort to build the first version of a startup
- Designers shouldn't necessarily ship production code, but should think and design in the medium (code) to understand the material
- Understanding agent loops is more important than tweaking UI styles
- "Stop drawing dead fish" -- static Figma designs for chat interfaces are useless; you need to feel the AI
- Taste = running a virtual machine in your head that predicts whether an in-group will like something; you develop it through reps
- Warning: don't lose specialists (craft, delight, reliability engineering) in the rush to merge roles
- "One day you wake up and realize the world is made up by people no smarter than you" (Steve Jobs)
- All great products have one tiny core superpower that is exceptionally good
