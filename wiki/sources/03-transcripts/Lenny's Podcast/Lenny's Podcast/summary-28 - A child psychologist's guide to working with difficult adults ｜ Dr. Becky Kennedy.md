## Core Thesis

Dr. Becky Kennedy's "Good Inside" parenting philosophy and frameworks — including repair, boundary-setting, separating behavior from identity, and resilient leadership — translate directly into effective workplace leadership. All humans fundamentally need the same things regardless of age, and the same principles that help a parent raise resilient, confident children also help a leader build resilient, high-trust teams.

## Concepts

- [[Good Inside (philosophy)]] — core philosophy: separate behavior from identity; everyone is good inside regardless of behavior
- [[Repair (psychological)]] — going back after a rupture to take responsibility, acknowledge impact, and commit to doing differently
- [[Connect Before Correcting]] — build a bridge of connection before attempting to correct behavior
- [[Most Generous Interpretation (MGI)]] — tool for shifting from least generous to most generous explanation of someone's behavior
- [[Sturdy Leadership]] — combining boundary-setting with validation; seeing someone's experience as real without being overwhelmed by it
- [[Boundaries (psychological)]] — what you tell someone else you will do; requires the other person to do nothing
- [[Resilience over Happiness]] — optimizing for long-term capability over short-term comfort; "optimizing for happiness in childhood is the quickest way to build anxiety and fragility in adulthood"
- [[I Believe You I Believe In You]] — framework combining validation ("I believe you") with confidence in capability ("I believe in you")
- [[Feelings Overpowering Skills]] — bad behavior = feelings > skills; the core problem is missing skills, not bad character
- [[Job Confusion]] — asking a child (or subordinate) to do your job because you don't want to deal with emotional fallout
- [[Deeply Feeling Kids]] — kids labeled dramatic/oppositional whose core fear is being "too much"
- [[Perfect is Creepy]] — mantra that perfection is neither achievable nor desirable for humans
- [[This Feels Hard Because It Is Hard]] — life motto: difficulty is inherent to the situation, not evidence of personal failure
- [[Curiosity over Judgment]] — curiosity and judgment are mutually exclusive; approaching with curiosity enables productive conversation
- [[Efficiency vs Relationship Building]] — efficiency and relationship building are often in opposition; relationship mode requires intentionality
- [[Agenda-Free Connection]] — connecting with someone without an agenda, even for 30 seconds
- [[We're on the Same Team]] — powerful phrase to start difficult conversations
- [[Separate Behavior from Identity]] — distinguishing who someone is from what they do; prerequisite for effective behavior change
- [[Secure Attachment]] — relationship quality defined by the presence of repair, not the absence of mistakes
- [[Potty Learning]] — framing skill acquisition as learning rather than training; parallels to workplace skill development
- [[Resilient Rebels]] — people (kids or adults) whose core fear is loss of control; highly attuned to domain encroachment
- [[Locatable Leader]] — a leader with a clear point of view whom others can "locate" and trust, even when disagreeing

## Key Takeaways

1. **Repair is the #1 relationship strategy.** Secure attachment is not defined by getting it right all the time — it's defined by the willingness to repair after ruptures. This applies equally to parenting and workplace relationships.

2. **Separate behavior from identity.** The quickest way to have an unproductive conversation is to lose sight of the fact that someone is "good inside." Start with "we're on the same team" and "I know you're a good person."

3. **Boundaries are what YOU will do, not what you ask someone else to do.** A boundary requires the other person to do nothing. "Don't press the buttons" is a request. "I'm going to stand between you and the buttons" is a boundary.

4. **Sturdy leadership = boundaries + validation.** The pilot metaphor: a sturdy leader says "I hear you screaming. That makes sense. And I know what I'm doing." They don't dismiss feelings, nor do they let feelings dictate decisions.

5. **Resilience over happiness.** In both parenting and work, optimizing for short-term comfort builds long-term fragility. The goal is people who can say "this is hard and I can do hard things."

6. **The "I believe you" + "I believe in you" framework.** One foot in the hole with someone (validation), one foot out (seeing their capability). Both are necessary; neither alone is sufficient.

7. **MGI (Most Generous Interpretation).** The story you tell yourself at night becomes the parent/leader you are the next morning. Ask: "What's the most generous interpretation of why this person is behaving this way?"

8. **"A company is just people."** Dr. Becky's biggest surprise as a non-product founder: understanding people is a surprisingly effective leadership skill.
