## Core Thesis

Compensation negotiation is not about confrontation or greed — it's about understanding the value exchange between you and the company, using curiosity and empathy to uncover what the company truly needs, and collaborating to expand the pie rather than fighting over slices. Simple, gentle pushbacks ("What's the chance there's a little more?") can yield 20%+ improvements, while deeper strategic approaches built on information gathering and relationship building can double or triple initial offers. The process starts far earlier than the offer stage — it begins with how you position yourself, what you share (and don't share), and how you conduct every interview conversation.

## Key Takeaways

1. **A simple pushback yields 20%+.** Asking "What's the chance there could be a little more?" almost always improves an offer by 20% — even at high comp levels. Jacob's average client sees 40% movement, with some seeing 100-400% increases.

2. **Negotiation starts far earlier than the offer.** Your LinkedIn profile, headshot, what you tell recruiters, and every interview answer shapes your perceived value. If you're positioned as a commodity, you'll be treated like one.

3. **Don't negotiate over email.** You can't control tone, timing, or the recipient's emotional state. Video calls or in-person conversations let you read body language, adjust in real time, and collaborate rather than confront.

4. **Never share your number first.** Let the company anchor. If you must share, anchor high — those who anchor egregiously high win 75% more than those who are "reasonable." The higher you anchor, the more room you have.

5. **Slow down the process.** Haste equals risk. Taking 2-3 days to respond shows scarcity and thoughtfulness. The longer the process, the more information you gather and the more leverage you build.

6. **Treat interviews like a sales conversation.** Instead of reciting your resume, ask discovery questions: "What's exciting to you about me?" → "What are the biggest challenges?" → "What have you tried?" → "What would success look like in 6 months?" This is the "Selling the Vacation" technique.

7. **Negotiate with the decision maker, not the recruiter.** Recruiters are intermediaries — you're playing telephone. Go to the person who controls the P&L and has skin in the game.

8. **Make it about "we," not "me."** Frame asks as collaborative problem-solving. Example: instead of asking for severance for yourself, propose severance protections for the entire executive team — you become the hero.

9. **Get creative when capped.** When base salary and equity are maxed out, explore performance-based milestones, company cars, severance protections, title upgrades, or other creative compensation structures.

10. **Take ownership when things go wrong.** If you over-negotiate or come across poorly, own it. Authenticity and honesty can bring a rescinded offer back to the table — as demonstrated with a CMO who lost and then regained an $800K offer.
