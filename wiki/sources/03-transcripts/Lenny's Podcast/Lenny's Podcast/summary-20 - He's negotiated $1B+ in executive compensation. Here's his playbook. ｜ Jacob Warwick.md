## Core Summary

[[Lenny Rachitsky]] interviews [[Jacob Warwick]], a professional negotiator who advises senior tech executives, athletes, and Hollywood celebrities behind the scenes ("fingerprintless" — he never negotiates directly, only coaches), and has helped clients secure over $1 billion in additional compensation. Warwick argues a simple, non-aggressive pushback ("what's the chance there could be a little more?") wins roughly 20% more almost universally, and his direct coaching typically achieves ~40% average gains, sometimes breaking salary bands entirely via role up-leveling. His core philosophy reframes negotiation as collaborative value-exchange rather than adversarial "politics": companies already extract far more value from top performers than they pay, so asking for more isn't greedy. Practical tactics include never negotiating over email (tone gets lost), always negotiating with the person who controls the budget rather than a recruiter, choosing a favorable meeting environment ("[[Home Field Advantage]]"), never anchoring to a number too early, and "[[Sell The Vacation]]" — using discovery questions to surface a stakeholder's real pain, then vividly describing the future state after it's solved, with the candidate positioned as the one who delivered it. He warns against reflexively "[[Never Split The Difference|splitting the difference]]," uses "[[Reciprocity Imbalance]]" (unprompted introductions) to build leverage, and frames negotiating power as fundamentally about information and timing. He closes discussing his free-content mission (Execs in the City) and the Cody Dieruf Foundation, motivated by his son's cystic fibrosis diagnosis.

## Key Points

- A simple pushback ("what's the chance there's a little more?") produces ~20% improvement on offers almost universally; direct coaching targets ~40% average, with up to 100-400% increases via role up-leveling.
- Product/engineering/design types tend to negotiate worse than marketing/sales types, despite driving much of tech's value.
- Never negotiate over email — tone is uncontrollable and easily misread by a rushed or stressed reader; negotiate with whoever controls the P&L, not a recruiter.
- "[[Home Field Advantage]]": choose a favorable meeting environment/time; slow down ("haste equals risk") rather than rush to respond.
- "[[Sell The Vacation]]": use discovery questions to surface real stakeholder pain, then describe the resolved future state vividly, positioning yourself as the one who delivered it — beats credential-based pitching.
- Never anchor to a compensation number too early, since role scope often expands mid-process ("scope creep") and an early number gets used against you later.
- "[[Never Split The Difference]]": don't reflexively accept a counterparty's middle-ground offer — cases where clients pushed past the "split" gained substantially more.
- "[[Reciprocity Imbalance]]": proactive, unprompted introductions/favors create social debt that improves negotiation outcomes.
- Negotiating power comes down to information and timing — companies negotiate constantly and know market data at scale; individuals negotiate only a handful of times in their careers (illustrated via the game Werewolf).
- Reframes the job search as a familiar professional process (sales, marketing positioning, PRD-writing) to build negotiation confidence rather than treating it as an alien academic skill.
- Recommends *Influence* ([[Robert Cialdini]]), *You Can Negotiate Anything* ([[Herb Cohen]]), *Negotiation Made Simple* ([[John Lowry]]), and *Radical Candor* ([[Kim Scott]]).
