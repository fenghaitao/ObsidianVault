## Core Summary

Mark Pincus, founder of Zynga and creator of FarmVille and Words with Friends, shares his "Proven Better New" framework for product success. The core insight: your instincts are right 95% of the time, but your ideas are wrong 75% of the time. The framework separates a product into three layers: Proven (copy the best-of-breed, don't innovate on what's already solved), Better (small, universally agreed improvements), and New (the novel idea that gets people to try -- but probably won't work, so have 4-5 ready to test). The key is failing for the right reason: if your "new" fails, your "proven" foundation means the product still works. He also argues that social media has lost its "adrenaline" -- the next innovation is making it "rowdy" again, like a great cocktail party.

## Key Points

- Proven Better New framework: separate your product into what's proven (copy best-of-breed), better (polish), and new (the novel idea)
- "Your instincts are right 95% of the time. Your ideas are wrong 75% of the time"
- "Get your PhD in proven first. You haven't earned the right to innovate until you're the world's leading expert on what already exists"
- Better = 10 out of 10 existing users would say "hell yeah" -- not what you think is better (that's "new")
- If you're asking whether your product is an A, it's not an A. When you have lightning in a bottle, everything works
- Sid Meier's social game failed because he didn't copy Zynga's proven onboarding -- his innovation was never seen
- Social media has "lost the adrenaline" -- people are proud to say they're not on Instagram
- The next social innovation: "make it rowdy" -- like a great cocktail party where you're glad you're there
- "Define your ambition in the eyes of your consumer" -- not in the eyes of your peers
