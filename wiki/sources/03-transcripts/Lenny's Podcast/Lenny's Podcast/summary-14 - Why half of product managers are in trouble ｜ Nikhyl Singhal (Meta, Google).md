## Core Summary

[[Lenny Rachitsky]] interviews returning guest [[Nikhyl Singhal]] (former [[Meta]]/[[Google]] executive, ex-CPO of [[Credit Karma]], founder of the [[Skip]] community) about how the product-management job market has transformed. Singhal's central claim: the industry is splitting into "builders," who love hands-on making and are having a career renaissance (higher comp, more offers, paths to founder/CEO roles), and "information movers," whose core skill of relaying/translating information through an org hierarchy is being obsoleted — see [[Builders Vs Information Movers]]. He predicts 12-24 months of "[[Shed And Rehire]]": mass layoffs of legacy staff paired with much smaller AI-first rehiring. His central career advice is "[[Crossing The Threshold]]" — finding the courage to reinvent one's working style, typically triggered by a personal moment of joy building something with AI, despite the "[[Shadow Superpower]]" psychological trap where the most successful practitioners of the old system resist recognizing it's obsolete. He also observes "[[Modernity Over Brand]]": pedigree at a legacy-process company is becoming a liability rather than an asset in interviews, replaced by demonstrated current-tool fluency. Despite the disruption, he reports the most open PM roles globally in 3+ years, since judgment (deciding what's worth building) becomes more valuable as the volume and pace of AI-driven change increases.

## Key Points

- Roughly half of existing PMs are "information movers" at risk of being obsoleted; the other half are "builders" entering a renaissance. See [[Builders Vs Information Movers]].
- Predicts mass "shed and rehire" cycles over the next 12-24 months as companies replace larger legacy teams with smaller, AI-first cohorts. See [[Shed And Rehire]].
- Core career advice: "[[Crossing The Threshold]]" — actively choosing to reinvent your working style, usually unlocked by a personal moment of joy with a new AI tool.
- "[[Shadow Superpower]]": mastery of the old system makes people resistant to recognizing the new one is needed.
- "[[Modernity Over Brand]]": legacy-company pedigree is losing hiring-signal value relative to demonstrated current-tool fluency.
- Judgment (deciding what to build, evaluating whether a change is good) is the skill that persists and grows in value as building itself becomes near-free — echoing [[Taste]] from earlier episodes.
- Warns the current wave may set back workplace diversity, since the pace of change disproportionately rewards people with more discretionary nights/weekends time.
- Cites [[Amol]] (Anthropic's head of growth, also a Lenny's Podcast guest) for the view that PMs are needed *more*, not less, to track and evaluate the accelerating volume of AI-driven product changes.
- Predicts product leaders will increasingly move into non-product functions (even CHRO roles) as "change agents," given their combination of judgment, communication, and technical fluency.
