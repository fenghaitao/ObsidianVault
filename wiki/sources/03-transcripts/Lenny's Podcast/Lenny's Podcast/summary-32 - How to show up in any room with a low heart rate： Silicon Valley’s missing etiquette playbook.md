## Core Summary

[[Lenny Rachitsky]] interviews [[Sam Lesson]] (Sam Lessin), partner at [[Slow Ventures]], former VP of Product at [[Facebook]], and two-time founder, about why proper etiquette matters for Silicon Valley founders in 2025/2026. Lessin's core framing — repeated throughout — is that etiquette is "a skill for how to show up in a room with a low heart rate" ([[Low Heart Rate]]): as software gets commoditized and trust becomes the scarce resource, founders who show up "abrasive" (a behavior he says Silicon Valley culture rewards) sabotage deals and relationships they don't even realize they're losing. He frames the goal as showing up with an [[Abundance Mindset]] rather than transactional scarcity ("this is my one shot"). The conversation walks through roughly ten social-situation categories from Lessin's etiquette book/course — introductions, conversations, hygiene, dress, dining, small talk/humor, scheduling, communication, meetings, and exiting — each yielding concrete, specific rules (be early but not too early; repeat names back; let a partner's name-forget "hang"; don't order the most expensive item; tip a lot but don't make the tip memorable; napkin in lap, knife blade in; camera on for video calls with an appropriate/closed-closet background; stand to shake hands). In the closing AI Corner and Contrarian Corner segments, Lessin describes his AI-cartoon newsletter aggregator ("[[Lettermeme]]") and argues that seed investors in AI-branded startups will lose enormous amounts of money because such businesses are too capital-intensive for seed-stage economics, while genuinely believing every startup should use AI as infrastructure.

## Key Points

- **Low heart rate as the etiquette thesis**: the goal of etiquette is not politeness for its own sake but projecting calm, trustworthy confidence — "if you show up like a little Energizer Bunny... you're going to project totally the wrong vibe." Founders who burn "mild relationship opportunities" by running too hot create an "unnecessary uphill battle" for themselves, even though raw product excellence can occasionally override all of this ("if you bring mana from heaven... you basically can get away with anything" — but this describes ~0.1% of startups).
- **Introductions**: be early (10-15 min, not 30+); if late, apologize briefly and move on without turning it into "a five-alarm fire"; never make someone else feel bad for being late; strong handshake; repeat the other person's name back when introduced; make eye contact (with grace for neurodivergent founders who are visibly trying); introduce your partner first; if you forget someone's name, "let it hang" by introducing your partner to them so they have to reintroduce themselves; default to "great to see you" over "nice to meet you" since it works whether or not you've met before.
- **Conversations**: index toward asking questions but treat it like a ping-pong game — not six questions in a row, always give something back ([[Give To Get]]); match vocabulary/register to the person, don't force mirroring; leave people wanting more rather than delivering your full "four-minute spiel"; self-deprecating humor is safe, making fun of others requires established familiarity; keep a running, informally ranked list of jokes by "offensiveness"; gracefully exit conversations (state you're leaving, or introduce the other person to someone else) rather than lingering.
- **Hygiene and dress**: scent should be undetectable in either direction ("don't smell like [censored]"); dress one level up from expected, and it's fine to under-formalize afterward (e.g., remove a jacket); fit matters far more than brand/price — "a well-fitting $20 shirt is way better than a misfitting $500 shirt"; a founder showing up with a Rolex reads as "classless"; if unsure of dress code, just ask — asking signals confidence, not weakness.
- **Dining**: don't order the most expensive item or over-order wine; let others order first and set the pace; always offer to pay (you'll typically be declined) but skip the offer for extravagant meals (a $10,000 bottle of wine); tip generously (20% minimum, 30%+ sometimes reads as silly) — "I don't think you want your tip to be memorable"; when eating with someone famous/wealthy who's letting you buy, tip as they would (often near 100%); B (bread) is on your left, D (drink) on your right; napkin goes in the lap; knife blade points inward when set down.
- **Scheduling and communication**: Lessin says he personally "drove most of [Calendly's] growth for the month" via a viral rant about how disrespectful it is to make someone use a scheduling link instead of offering real availability, especially when there's a seniority/business gap; the less senior/busy party should accommodate the other's schedule, not the reverse; check time zones explicitly; extend outsized respect and gratitude to EAs/PAs/servers — "the number one way to look extremely classless is to not respect people who are helping the other person"; in email, avoid emoji in business contexts (they read as either over-familiar or AI-generated), proofread carefully (his own worst etiquette mistake: putting someone in the To: line to check their name's spelling, then hitting send), and read meaning into email ordering — who's first in the To: line, and who's CC'd (a CC line signals "no response expected").
- **Meetings and exiting**: arrive 10-15 minutes early but don't sit in the lobby for an hour; open with brief small talk (described elsewhere in the conversation as "the TCP/IP handshake" or "modem crash" of a meeting) before pivoting to business; camera on for video calls, appropriate/tidy background (closed closets, made beds); clean up after yourself (his partner [[Kevin Colleran]] insists on this even at VC firms working with LPs); stand to shake hands when people arrive/leave; send brief thank-you notes; don't make a production of leaving large gatherings — an "Irish goodbye" is often the most respectful exit.
- **AI Corner**: Lessin built [[Lettermeme]], a personal news aggregator that converts newsletters he doesn't have time to read into daily AI-generated cartoons, built first via vibe coding (Cursor, DigitalOcean, Cloudflare) and later handed to engineer friends to scale.
- **Contrarian Corner**: Lessin argues seed VCs backing "AI-branded" startups will lose enormous money because such businesses are too capital-intensive for seed economics and fundamentally commoditizing; he cites [[OpenAI]]'s seed investors making "only" ~25x on a $500B valuation as evidence that even the defining company of the moment is a mediocre seed outcome given the capital consumed. He distinguishes this from believing every company should use AI as infrastructure (not using AI now is "the equivalent of not using the cloud in 2010"). He frames this as the latest "Terra Nova" (frontier) narrative that generations chase (the American West, the internet, mobile, crypto, now AI), arguing AI is a powerful tool for existing businesses but a weak standalone startup thesis.
- **Lightning round / personal**: recommends *The Ancient City* (via [[Marc Andreessen]]), *Man's Search for Meaning*, *Area 51: An Uncensored History*, and especially *The Lessons of History* by Will Durant; favorite recent show is *Land Man*; pitches the dating app that builds match profiles from ChatGPT chat histories; life motto is "Carthage must burn" (from a Facebook hackathon poster); co-hosts the low-key podcast *More or Less* with his wife [[Jessica Lessin]] (founder of [[The Information]]) and friends [[Dave Morin]] and Brett Morin.
