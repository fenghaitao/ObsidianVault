# Summary: Head of Growth (Anthropic)： Anthropic is automating its own growth

## Core Thesis

**Anthropic's unprecedented growth (1B to 19B+ ARR in 14 months) is driven by a combination of world-class AI models, extreme focus on B2B and coding, and a growth team that operates differently from traditional growth teams — prioritizing larger bets over micro-optimizations and beginning to automate growth experimentation itself with Claude.** Amol Avaisary, Head of Growth at Anthropic, reveals that the company is using Claude to automate the growth experimentation loop (identifying opportunities, building, testing, analyzing results), describes the 70% "success disasters" and 30% proactive work split of his role, and shares deep insights on how AI is reshaping the PM, engineering, and design roles.

## Key Topics

### 1. Anthropic's Unprecedented Growth Trajectory
- Anthropic grew from 1B ARR (start of 2025) to 19B+ ARR (Feb 2026) — 1 to 19 billion in 14 months
- 10x year-on-year revenue growth trend has been consistent since 2023: 0 to 100M (2023), 100M to 1B (2024), 1B to ~10B (2025)
- "It just has not slowed down" — Amol notes the 19B number is already out of date
- Internally, linear charts are "not cool" — everything is shown on log-linear scale
- Amol: "We're really hanging on by the seat of our pants and we're trying to manage the growth"

### 2. How Amol Got the Job — Cold Emailing Mike Krieger
- Amol was a user of Claude and noticed Anthropic didn't have a growth team
- He sent Mike Krieger (CPO) a cold email saying "you guys badly need a growth team. Want to chat?"
- Mike responded and they started talking; Amol was the only PM Mike hired from a cold email
- Cold email tactics: tested subject line copy for high open rate, used personal email (not LinkedIn/work email), kept it short, followed up multiple times
- Rule of thumb: "If I really care about it, I should just keep reaching out until they tell me please stop"

### 3. The Growth Team at Anthropic (~40 People)
- Structured with horizontals (growth platform, monetization) and audience-focused pods (B2B growth, Claude Code growth, knowledge worker growth, API growth)
- Functions: engineers, designers, PMs, data
- **Key difference from traditional growth teams**: indexes much more toward larger swings (70/30 or 50/50) vs. small-to-medium bets. Traditional growth teams do 60-70% small/medium bets
- Reason: "We always talk about the exponential" — product value in 2 years will be 100-1000x what it is today, so capturing small optimizations misses the forest for the trees
- The growth team built the Chrome extension that underpins Claude Code and Co-work use cases

### 4. Success Disasters — 70% Firefighting, 30% Proactive Work
- 70% of Amol's time is spent on "success disasters" — things going so well that other things break
- Categories: acquisition, activation, monetization — all have firefighting from urgent issues
- "All the charts are green, fully up and to the right, and everyone's just like it can be quite tough emotionally still"
- 30% is proactive: which products to put resources behind, long-term pricing/packaging, when to lean into new products like Co-work

### 5. Activation and the Import from ChatGPT
- Activation is a "really big challenge in AI" — capability overhead where models improve faster than products can adapt
- Importing memory from ChatGPT was a clever growth move specific to a moment in time
- Key learning: **the right friction helps** — adding friction to understand who the user is leads to better recommendations and higher conversion
- "Cut friction when it doesn't add to the experience of helping a user understand why the product is for them"
- Examples: MasterClass purchase flow quiz, Calm's quiz, Mercury's onboarding, Anthropic's onboarding — all have friction that tested well

### 6. Mercury Onboarding — Quality Drives Growth
- At Mercury, Amol's team spent a full quarter fixing onboarding quality — "forget metrics, forget growth, forget everything else"
- It was the single most impactful quarter of his career up to that point
- Mercury's onboarding for regulated banking was complex (registered agent vs. legal address vs. physical address)
- Result: significant uplift in onboarding start-to-completion from focusing on quality
- "Quality drives growth" — a core principle Amol brought to Anthropic

### 7. CACHE — Automating Growth Experimentation with Claude
- CACHE = Claude Accelerates Sustainable Hypergrowth (name Amol didn't come up with)
- Led by Alexey Komissarouk (teaches growth engineering at Reforge)
- Started a couple of months ago; only became possible with Opus 4.5/4.6
- Four-part lifecycle being automated: (1) identifying opportunities, (2) building the feature, (3) testing/quality, (4) analyzing data and gathering learnings
- Currently delivering results at a "junior PM, 2-3 years in" win rate — not yet at senior PM level
- "It ultimately prints money" — you can press play and it works
- Human-in-the-loop currently for brand approval, but expected to decrease as models improve
- Missing piece: cross-functional stakeholder management — still needs human brains

### 8. How AI Is Reshaping PM, Engineering, and Design Roles
- Engineering is getting the most leverage from AI right now (e.g., Claude Code)
- PMs and designers are getting less leverage, creating a bottleneck — a team of 5 engineers + 1 PM + 1 designer now effectively has 15-20 engineers' worth of output
- PM and design are "absolutely squeezed" — may need to hire more PMs, or use product-minded engineers as mini PMs
- **Two-Week Rule**: If a project is under 2 weeks of engineering time, the engineer is on the hook to be the PM (talk to security, legal, stakeholders). PM is in advisory capacity. If over 2 weeks, PM drives.
- "Product-minded engineers" — their value goes up "an order of magnitude"
- Counterintuitive insight: "More PMs, fewer engineers might be the future" because engineers are so accelerated
- For larger companies, the PM's highest leverage is not shipping PRs but improving the "why and what" by 5% — that's a "very high leverage hire"
- Amol is averse to PRDs — 60-80% of what they ship has no PRD; just Slack messages back and forth

### 9. Daily AI Automations at Anthropic
- **Data monitoring**: Co-work runs on a schedule, looks at 20-25 charts every morning, flags concerning trends and interesting insights
- **Life admin**: Claude books meeting rooms, clears inbox, files reimbursements and expenses via Brex
- **Manager lens**: Claude looks at direct reports' work, OKRs, discussion transcripts, and suggests key takeaways and feedback
- **Self-coaching**: Amol has Claude model his manager (Amie Vora) based on her public writing, internal communications, and their discussions, then asks "what feedback would Amie have for me this week?"
- **Misalignment detection**: Claude scans Slack weekly to find areas of potential misalignment across projects — "does a really, really good job"
- Scott (enterprise lead) found major misalignment areas that would have caused teams to spin their wheels or do overlapping work

### 10. Anthropic's Culture and Focus
- Extreme focus on B2B and coding from the very beginning — Dan Mad wrote a doc in 2021 (months after founding) arguing they should focus on AI coding
- Coding focus serves dual purpose: huge TAM + accelerates the research loop (better coding → better models)
- "Freedom through constraints": historically the smallest, least well-funded player — constraints forced extreme focus
- Anthropic had Claude before ChatGPT launched but chose not to release it for safety reasons
- Corporate structure: Public Benefit Corporation (PBC), not a Delaware C-corp — legally allows optimizing for public benefit over shareholder value
- Mission: "Make sure the transition to powerful AI goes well and is net beneficial for humanity" — "happy to take a significant commercial hit for"
- Culture described as "secret sauce": mission-driven, no one is checked out, extreme openness (notebook channels where anyone can argue with Dario), talent density like "playing for Real Madrid"
- Notebook channels: everyone has a Slack channel where they share internal thoughts, provocative ideas, and leaders scale their beliefs — also serves as training data for Claude

### 11. Growth Philosophy: Leaving Money on the Table
- Core principle: "Be comfortable forgoing metric impact to prioritize safety, brand, quality bar, and user experience"
- Two types of controversial tests: (1) so controversial you wouldn't ship regardless of results — don't run it; (2) cringey but not a red line — run it with a high bar for return
- "Look beyond the short-term and zoom out — the very best products all operate this way"
- Safety stance will become a "significant competitive advantage" as risks and stakes get higher

### 12. Advice for Thriving in the AI Future
- **Use the tools**: Be on top of Claude Code, Co-work, and each model release — what new things can you do? Test repeatedly as models improve
- **Lean into competitive advantage**: Double down on what you spike in, forget weaknesses. Be the best at one thing
- **Be interdisciplinary**: Engineer who is highly product-minded = unicorn. PM who can design = unicorn. Chances of being let go drop dramatically
- **Be adaptable**: "50, 60, 70% of how you operated in the past, just throw it out the door"
- AI safety is non-negotiable — it's in category 1 (don't run the test)

### 13. Amol's Personal Journey — Brain Injury and Recovery
- Suffered a traumatic brain injury in early 2022 from a kick to the head during Muay Thai sparring
- Spent 9 months off work; first 2-3 months: couldn't walk, look at screens, or listen to music for more than 20 seconds
- Wife did everything for him including texting friends
- Wasn't clear he would ever work again; discussed with his wife what they'd do if he couldn't
- Returned to work after 8 months, then got reinjured a month into joining Mercury (off another 2-3 months)
- Still not 100% healed — has occasional dizziness and headaches
- "One of the best things that could have happened to me" — forced habits: no alcohol, no caffeine, regular breaks, meditation
- "Freedom through constraints" — constraints force adaptation and focus
- "The true freedom in life is learning how to be content when you don't get what you want"

### 14. Failure Corner — Shutting Down His Startup
- Spent 3 years on a mental health startup, raised a couple million, had 7-10 employees
- Had to shut it down and tell investors they lost their money
- Key lesson: send monthly investor updates even when things are bad — "it's just the right thing to do"
- "It took me a number of years to truly get over" — but without that failure, he wouldn't have learned cold emailing, product skills, or been on this career path
- "It's a long game. I'm so grateful that it failed"
