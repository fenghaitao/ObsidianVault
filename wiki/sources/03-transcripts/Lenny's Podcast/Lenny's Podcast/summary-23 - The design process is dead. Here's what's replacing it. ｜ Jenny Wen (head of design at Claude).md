## Core Thesis

The traditional design process (research → diverge → converge → prototype → mock) is dead, accelerated by AI. Engineers' ability to rapidly ship with AI tools forces design to change from a gatekeeping role to a consulting and execution-support role. Design work is becoming stratified into two types: (1) supporting implementation and execution, and (2) creating vision/direction (now 3-6 months out instead of 2-10 years). Designers are spending less time on mocking (down from 60-70% to 30-40%) and more time pairing with engineers and implementing. The future of design favors three archetypes: strong generalists, deep specialists, and craft new grads. Quality is maintained through trust built by speed — shipping early, iterating rapidly, and responding to user feedback.

## Key Concepts

- [[Death of the Design Process]] — The traditional design process (research, diverge, converge, prototype) treated as gospel is dying; was already fading before AI but AI has accelerated it dramatically
- [[Two Types of Design Work]] — Design work is stratifying into: (1) supporting implementation and execution, and (2) creating vision/direction for products
- [[Designer as Consultant]] — A big part of the design role now is helping engineers and teams execute, not just telling them "here's the design"; designers act as consultants and guides
- [[Building Trust Through Speed]] — Quality is maintained by shipping early, iterating quickly, and responding to user feedback; trust degrades when you ship early and never improve
- [[Legibility Framework]] — Evan Tana's 2x2: founders can be illegible or legible, ideas can be illegible or legible; the most interesting opportunities are illegible ideas that designers can help make legible
- [[Three Designer Archetypes]] — Jenny's three hiring archetypes: (1) strong generalist (block-shaped, 80th percentile in multiple skills), (2) deep specialist (T-shaped with exceptional depth in one area), (3) craft new grad (early career, wise beyond years, humble, eager to learn, blank slate)
- [[Pie Chart of Designer Time]] — Designer time allocation has shifted: mocking/prototyping went from 60-70% to 30-40%, while pairing with engineers grew to 30-40%, and implementation is a new slice
- [[Low Leverage Tasks as High Leverage]] — Jenny's management philosophy: "low leverage" tasks (testing product, fixing bugs, making anniversary cards) become high leverage when a senior leader does them because it shows they care deeply
- [[Designers as VCs]] — Part of a designer's role at a frontier lab is spotting illegible ideas internally (like a VC) and trying to understand what's there, then transforming them through storytelling or UX
- [[Research Preview (Launch Strategy)]] — Labeling early releases as "research previews" to set expectations that the product is early and will improve; the promise is continuous iteration and responsiveness
- [[Chatbots as UI Paradigm]] — Chatbots and terminals may persist as UI paradigms because they provide infinite flexibility; specific UIs will still exist for tactile interactions but will increasingly be model-generated
- [[Non-deterministic AI Design]] — Non-deterministic AI models can't be fully mocked up; you have to use actual models underneath and see how people use them to discover use cases
- [[Team Roasting as Safety Signal]] — When team members feel comfortable roasting each other (and their manager), it's a signal of high psychological safety; must be balanced with high standards
- [[Introspection via AI]] — Using AI tools like Co-work to analyze personal notes, interview notes, and memos to extract insights about oneself; Jenny used it to create a design craft evaluation rubric
- [[Psychological Safety]] — Creating an environment where people feel safe enough to roast each other and the manager, combined with high standards; like being a "tough parent" who supports but challenges
- [[Human-AI Collaboration]] — Someone still needs to decide what gets built and what matters; AI can inform but can't resolve disputes between people; human accountability persists
- [[Speed as Advantage]] — Shipping fast and iterating is the new quality paradigm; the product is the worst it will ever be at launch
- [[Dogfooding]] — Senior leaders who deeply test the product, reproduce bugs, and share logs create familiarity and show they care
- [[Convergence of PM Engineer Designer]] — Designers are becoming more PM-shaped and engineering-shaped; the design role is stretching and spanning
