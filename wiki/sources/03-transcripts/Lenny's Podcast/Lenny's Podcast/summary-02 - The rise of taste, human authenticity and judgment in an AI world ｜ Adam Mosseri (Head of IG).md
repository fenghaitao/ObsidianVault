## Core Summary

Lenny Rachitsky interviews [[Adam Mosseri]], Head of [[Instagram]], about how AI is reshaping product teams, careers, and Instagram itself. Mosseri argues that as AI makes building easier, human value concentrates in [[Taste]] (judgment about what to build), vision, and strategy rather than execution. Instagram has reorganized around small, generalist "[[Pods|pods]]" led by a new "[[Product Staff|product staff]]" role that blends PM, design, and data-science skills, with specialists brought in only when work truly demands deep expertise. The conversation also covers how recommendation algorithms actually work (embeddings, not detailed semantic understanding of users), Instagram's stance on AI-generated content (label it, don't filter it, and invest in creators and authenticity as differentiators), token/compute budgeting as a resource-management problem, and Mosseri's personal reflections on handling public criticism, past product failures (Facebook Home, early Reels), and raising digitally literate kids.

## Key Points

- Taste — the instinct for what AI is and isn't good at, and for what's worth building — is becoming the scarcest and most valuable human skill as building itself gets cheaper via AI. See [[Taste]].
- Instagram/Meta product teams are shrinking from ~13-person specialist teams to 4-7 person "[[Pods|pods]]" centered on a generalist "[[Product Staff|product staff]]" role (an evolved PM who absorbs design/data-science work), with senior specialists added only for genuinely novel or high-stakes work.
- Hiring signals Mosseri looks for are stable (grit, quick learning, self-awareness) but the two traits he's newly emphasizing are curiosity and willingness to put yourself out there / make mistakes in public.
- Mosseri distinguishes vision (an articulation of a desired future state) from strategy (an opinionated, necessarily contestable path to it); both are increasingly where human judgment matters most as AI eats execution, and AI strategy advice is only useful when heavily steered with real constraints.
- The [[Centaur And Reverse Centaur|centaur vs. reverse-centaur]] framing (human-in-charge vs. AI-in-charge) captures the core risk Mosseri wants product teams to avoid when delegating strategy work to AI.
- The best product leaders Mosseri has worked with function less as singular "idea machines" and more as curators — of ideas, people, and teams — because good chemistry and complementary skills on a leadership team matter as much as raw talent.
- Instagram's ranking algorithm has much less detailed semantic understanding of users than people assume; it mostly relies on illegible embedding vectors, though a new feature ("[[Your Algorithm|Your Algorithm]]") uses an LLM to describe those embeddings in plain language and let users see/adjust their inferred interests.
- Pure chronological feeds fail at scale because they reward high-volume posting (publishers/professional content crowd out friends) and ignore that recency is only one input to relevance; Meta's own tests show satisfaction drops when users choose chronological-by-default.
- Mosseri expects AI-generated content to be a net tailwind for Instagram: in a world of abundant synthetic content, people will seek out human creativity, point-of-view, and authenticity more, not less — so Instagram plans to label content/accounts as AI or not rather than filter out AI content, and to keep investing in individual creators over institutions.
- On recommendation systems, Instagram has been catching up to [[TikTok]]/ByteDance's exploration-based ranking (surfacing niche/breakout content a user might like but doesn't know it yet) versus pure exploitation-based ranking (showing what's already known to work); Mosseri credits TikTok with pioneering this and says Instagram now leads in some areas.
- Instagram deliberately avoids per-engineer token spend leaderboards; Mosseri treats compute/token budgets like any other constrained resource (GPUs, headcount, opex) and expects a "roller coaster" where usage-driven costs rise before frontier model price competition brings per-token costs down. See [[Token Budgeting]].
- Mosseri's "fail corner" stories: Facebook Home (an Android fork/HTC hardware project) was a "spectacular failure" but a huge personal learning experience; building early [[Reels]] on top of [[Stories]] (2019) starved it of a strong distribution foundation and let [[TikTok]] take off unchallenged during the pandemic.
- Mosseri manages public backlash (Cambridge Analytica, the 2009 News Feed redesign, the 2021 feed/video-viewer test backlash) by trying to communicate proactively about trade-offs, accepting that major product debates (privacy vs. safety, personalization vs. discovery) have no free lunch.
- On parenting: boundaries and earned screen time (not free access), parental approval of apps, and deliberately teaching his 10-year-old to "vibe code" (see [[Vibe Coding]]) so kids build AI literacy rather than falling behind.
