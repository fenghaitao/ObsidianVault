## Core Summary

Adam Mosseri, head of Instagram (3B+ monthly users), describes how product teams are evolving in 2026. Instagram has adopted "pods" -- mini teams of 4-6 generalist engineers plus a "product staff" (evolved PM role that also does design, data science, and research). This replaces the old "baker's dozen" team of 12+ specialists. The key insight: small teams move faster and make better decisions with less design-by-committee. Taste is the critical skill: "In a world where it's easier to build things, it's more important to make sure your time is spent figuring out what you should be building." He's bullish on designers because they have taste, but expects the strongest ones will convert to product staff roles. On AI content: don't filter it, label it -- in a world of synthetic abundance, people will seek authenticity.

## Key Points

- Instagram's "pod" model: 4-6 engineers + 1 "product staff" (PM who does design, data science, research)
- Product staff is an evolution of the PM role -- generalists who can do what specialists used to do
- Small teams are more effective: less coordination, fewer people, faster decisions
- "Taste matters a ton. In a world where it's easier to build things, it's more important to figure out what to build"
- Bullish on designers: "they tend to have taste and that is much more difficult to automate away"
- Strongest designers will convert to product staff roles, expanding beyond traditional design
- AI content strategy: don't filter it out, label it so users know -- people will seek authenticity
- Instagram algorithm: "people assume there's a much more detailed semantic understanding than there is"
- 3B+ monthly users, head of Instagram for 8 years, designed Facebook News Feed before that
- "People who will make the most of AI are those clear-eyed about what it's good at and what it's not"
