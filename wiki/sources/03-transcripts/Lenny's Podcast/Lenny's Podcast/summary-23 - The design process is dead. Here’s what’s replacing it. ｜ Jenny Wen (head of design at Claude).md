## Core Summary

[[Lenny Rachitsky]] interviews [[Jenny Wen]], head of design for [[Claude Cowork]] at [[Anthropic]] (previously Claude/Claude Code; before that director of design at [[Figma]]), on how the traditional design process is dying — forced by how fast AI-accelerated engineering now moves. Wen argues design work is stratifying into supporting rapid implementation (letting engineers scrap-build ideas rather than waiting for polished mocks) and short-horizon (3-6 month) vision-setting, often via a prototype rather than a storytelling deck. She still uses Figma for divergent exploration and fine visual detail, but has shifted roughly half her time toward pairing directly with engineers and implementing code herself. She coined "building trust through speed" — ship early and clearly-labeled, then visibly iterate fast — as the way to preserve trust while shipping unfinished work. She deliberately returned to IC design work for about a year to build hands-on fluency with the shift, applies a VC-style "[[Legibility Framework]]" to spot high-energy-but-inarticulate internal prototypes worth developing (as she did with Claude Cowork), and hires against three archetypes: strong generalists, deep specialists, and "craft new grads." She closes on management philosophy: seemingly low-leverage tasks (dogfooding, small PRs, personal gestures) are often secretly high-leverage, and psychological safety (measured partly by whether a team comfortably "roasts" her) should coexist with high standards.

## Key Points

- The traditional research→diverge→converge design process is dead, forced by engineers "spinning up seven Clauds" — design is splitting into implementation support versus short-horizon (3-6 month) vision/prototype work.
- Time allocation shifted from ~60-70% mocking/prototyping a few years ago to ~30-40% today, with the rest now split between engineer pairing and hands-on implementation.
- Still relies on [[Figma]] for divergent exploration (8-10 directions at once) and fine detail work, since coding tools are comparatively linear/single-direction.
- "Building trust through speed": ship early under a clear "research preview" label, accept flaws, and commit to fast, visible iteration — that's what preserves user trust, not waiting for polish.
- Believes AI's taste/judgment will keep improving, but argues the persistent human role is being accountable for disputed build decisions, not necessarily aesthetic judgment itself.
- Returned to IC design work for ~1 year specifically to stay empathetically fluent with how fast her own discipline is changing, before considering a return to management.
- Applies the "[[Legibility Framework]]" (credited to VC [[Evan Tana]]) to spot internal prototypes with real energy behind them but not yet articulable — the mechanism behind how [[Claude Cowork]]'s design (publicly "10 days," really a much longer exploration) came together.
- Hires for three archetypes: strong generalists ("block-shaped"), deep T-shaped specialists, and "craft new grads" (early-career, humble, fast learners unburdened by legacy process). See [[Design Hiring Archetypes]].
- Management philosophy: "low-leverage" tasks (dogfooding, fixing a small bug, personal gestures) are often secretly high-leverage; psychological safety (a team comfortable "roasting" its leader) should coexist with high standards.
