# Update on Quitting My Job at Meta

For the first time in my career, I made a decision that didn't make any sense on paper. Last year, I started a podcast to practice speaking and shared the software engineering career stories that inspired me. Everything felt new and I was learning a lot. After work, I kept thinking about ways on how to make the podcast better. I dreamed of working on it full-time, but it never really made sense because I'd only ever lost money on it. Then late last year, there was a reorg that felt like a good opportunity to leave Meta early and try it out.

So, I decided to use six months of my savings to see if I could make it work. And at first, it was glorious. I interviewed people like Bejarn Strost, the creator of C++, who I'd looked up to ever since I started learning C++ as my first programming language. And it was a lot of fun studying for every interview and getting into video production. But doing everything myself had an obvious downside. If I stopped, everything stopped.

I put so much time into each episode that even though I was working more than I ever had, I could barely keep up with the goal of one episode per week. And that's because I personally reviewed every second of the footage and I did deep research on each guest before the interviews. Well, four months into my six-month runway, I still hadn't made a dollar, and I'd been reluctant to monetize since finding sponsors felt like time taken away from the fun parts of creating content. But as my bank account got closer to zero, I knew I couldn't put it off much longer. And then I got sick. I caught what felt like a strong flu that had me bedridden for a few days, lying there, drifting in and out of consciousness.

I remember I kept wondering if I'd even be able to publish an episode that Monday. And because I couldn't work, suddenly everything felt fragile for the first time.
